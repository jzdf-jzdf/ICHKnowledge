# Effect of postoperative tranexamic acid on recurrence rate and complications in chronic subdural hematomas patients: preliminary results of a randomized controlled clinical trial

# Abstract  

Chronic subdural hematoma (CSDH) is a frequent neurosurgical disease which mainly affects elderly patients. Tranexamic acid (TXA) has been hypothesized as an oral agent to avoid CSDH progression and/or recurrence. We performed an evaluation to determine whether the postoperative use of TXA reduces recurrence rate. A prospective, randomized, and controlled trial. Patients with unilateral or bilateral chronic subdural hematoma undergoing surgical treatment by burr-hole were randomized as to whether or not to use TXA in the postoperative period. We evaluated image and clinical recurrence of CSDH at follow up of 6 months and potential clinical and/or surgical complications impact of TXA. Twenty-six patients were randomized to the control group $(52\%)$ and twenty-four patients to the TXA group $(48\%)$ . Follow-up ranged from 3 to 16 months. There were no significant difference between baseline data in groups regarding to age, gender, use of antiplatelet or anticoagulants, smoking, alcoholism, systemic arterial hypertension, diabetes mellitus, hematoma laterality, hematoma thickness, and drain use. Clinical and radiological recurrence occurred in three patients $(6\%)$ , being two cases in TXA group $(8.3\%)$ and 1 in control group $(3.8\%)$ . Postoperative complications occurred in two patients during follow-up $(4\%)$ , being both cases in TXA group $(8.3\%)$ , and none in the control group. Although TXA group had a higher recurrence rate $(8.3\%)$ , there was no statistically significant difference between the two groups. Moreover, TXA group had two complications while control group had no complications. Although limited by experimental nature of study and small sample, our current data suggest that TXA should not be used as a potential agent to avoid recurrences of CSDH and might increase complication odds.

# Introduction  

Chronic subdural hematoma (CSDH) is a frequent neurosurgical disease which mainly affects elderly patients [1]. Its incidence has increased considerably due to population aging [2], reaching 58 patients per 100,000 people in those aged 65 years old or more [3]. Most patients have complete remission of symptoms when diagnosed and treated early [4–6].  

Main risk factors for the development of CSDHs are age, gender, alcoholism, and coagulopathies (including the use of anticoagulants or antiplatelets). Treatment can be conservative or surgical, depending on the symptoms and clot size. As a general rule, surgery is indicated for hematomas thicker than $10\mathrm{mm}$ or for symptomatic patients [2].  

In surgical cases, several techniques can be used: one or two trepanations, twist-drill craniotomy, craniotomy, minicraniotomy, and endoscopic trepanation [6]. Although there is no consensus in the literature as to the best surgical technique, burr-hole surgery has been the most used method, and is considered the first-line treatment for CSDH [6–8].  

Recurrence rate after surgical treatment ranges from 5 to $30\%$ , with an approximate average of $10\%$ [7]. Main factors associated with recurrence are male gender, diabetes mellitus, bilateral hematoma, hyperdense hematoma, thickness of the hematoma [9–11].  

There is much debate about the risk factors for recurrence, and no preventive measure has been definitively established so far [9–11]. In order to reduce recurrence rates, some medications have been studied. Trans-4-(aminomethyl)- cyclohexane-1-carboxylic acid, also known as tranexamic acid (TXA), induces anti-fibrinolysis, and it is believed that it may promote hemostasis in microbleeding [12]. There are reports of reduction in recurrence rate with the use of TXA in the postoperative period [13–15]. We conducted a prospective, randomized, controlled study to determine whether the postoperative use of oral tranexamic acid reduces the risk of recurrence after CSDH treatment by burr-hole and to evaluate any associated complications.  

# Method  

# Design of study  

The study was a randomized controlled clinical trial with a parallel design. The allocation ratio was 1:1. Patients were users of the medical facility (one single institution) and met the eligibility criteria. This study was conducted and reported according to CONSORT guidelines.  

# Participants  

Patients with unilateral or bilateral chronic subdural hematoma undergoing surgical treatment by burr-hole. Patients operated on between March 1, 2021, and August 15, 2022, at the Hospital do Servidor Público Estadual de São Paulo, were randomized as to whether or not to use tranexamic acid in the postoperative period, after approval by the ethics committee.  

Criteria for indication of surgery: symptomatic unilateral or bilateral chronic subdural hematoma or thicker than $10\mathrm{mm}$ .  

# Inclusion criteria  

Patients aged over 18 years, diagnosed with unilateral or bilateral chronic subdural hematoma and submitted to surgical drainage by trepanation and consent to participate in the study.  

# Exclusion criteria  

Patients with malignant diseases, history of acute myocardial infarction, stroke, severe peripheral arterial disease, or any other condition that contraindicated the use of tranexamic acid. Patients who needed to be reoperated due to acute rebleeding in the immediate postoperative period (first $12{-}24\mathrm{h})$ were also excluded from the study.  

# Interventions  

After surgery for CSDH, patient was randomized (using the software: miniwebtool-https://miniwebtool.com/br) to the control group (without TXA) or to the intervention group (use of TXA at a dose of $750\mathrm{mg/day}$ in 3 daily doses for 3 months).  

All operated patients underwent control computed tomography (CT) on the first postoperative day. After control CT, if there was no surgical complication, TXA was initiated.  

Recurrence rate was used as the primary outcome, defined as the return of symptoms or the need for surgical intervention due to recurrence of the chronic subdural hematoma. Recurrences were also divided in early (before 1 month) and late recurrences (after 1 month).  

Pre- and postoperative clinical and surgical evaluations were performed by the same care team composed of 8 resident physicians and 3 senior neurosurgeons. Standard surgery adopted by our institution consists of one or two burr-hole surgery. Procedures were performed under general anesthesia in the operating room. Burr-holes were made in the frontal or parietal region or both according to anatomical distribution of CSDH. In all procedures, irrigation with saline solution was performed until cleaning blood clot. A subdural drain was placed when possible, with the tip located in the frontal region.  

A control CT scan was performed on the first postoperative day. Subdural drain was maintained for $24-48\mathrm{~h~}$ , at a level below the head. During the drain maintenance period, the patient was restricted to the bed, without raising the headboard. After removal of the drain, patient was encouraged to walk with the help of the physical therapy team.  

Radiological evaluation of CSDH was done with skull CT in axial and coronal slice, focusing in the thicker section (Fig. 1).  

As morphology of CSDH may differ among patients we used Nakaguchi classification [16] to facilitate understanding and comparing morphological aspects among both groups of patients. Figure 2 illustrates each type of architecture: Type 1 — homogeneous architecture, Type 2 — laminar architecture, Type 3 — separated architecture, and Type 4 — trabecular architecture.

# Primary outcome  

We evaluated image and clinical recurrence of CSDH at follow up of 6 months.  

# Secondary outcomes  

We evaluated the potential clinical and/or surgical complications impact of tranexamic acid during follow-up.  

# Sample size  

Once there are no previous studies concerning the question of this manuscript and defining ideal sample size, and we have no idea of differences between groups and respective variances, we opted to perform a pilot study trying to reach at least 30 patients in each group.  

Initially, we have reached over 60 patients, but after exclusion criteria application, final number was twenty-six (26) patients to control group and twenty-four (24) to experimental group.  

# Randomization  

Assignment of participants was performed after surgery, to avoid intraoperative bias. After standard surgery and following inclusion criteria, patients were then randomized with software application and then immediately started TXA use. There were no blinding procedures once both researcher physicians and patients knew their study groups and medication applied.  

Patients were divided into two groups: TXA group and control group. In the immediate postoperative period as well as 3 and 6 months postoperation, all of the patients underwent a brain CT.

# Statistical methods  

In this study, numerical data are presented as the mean with the range. Categorical data are presented as percentages. To determine the distribution of our data, the Kolmogorov–Smirnov test was performed. Student’s $t$ test for the paired and unpaired groups and Chi-square tests for categorical variables were performed when appropriate. The significance level was established as $p<0.05$ .  

# Results  

# Participant flow  

Seventy-four (74) patients underwent surgery for chronic subdural hematoma along evaluated period. Among patients, twenty-four $(32\%)$ were excluded. Three patients evolved with acute subdural hematoma in the immediate postoperative period and required emergency reoperation by craniotomy and the other twenty-one had contraindications to the use of TXA. Twenty-six patients were then randomized to the control group $(52\%)$ and twenty-four patients to the TXA group $(48\%)$ . Follow-up ranged from 3 to 16 months, with mean of 9.4 months. Full participant flow is shown in flowchart (Fig. 3).  

# Baseline data  

Among patients in TXA group, fifteen $(62.5\%)$ were male, and nine $(37.5\%)$ were female. Mean age was $75.8\pm11.8$ years old. Mean follow-up time was 9.3 months. Three $(12.5\%)$ patients reported chronic use of antiplatelets, and one $(4.2\%)$ reported chronic use of anticoagulants. Fifteen $(62.5\%)$ patients reported previous medical history of arterial hypertension, ten $(41.7\%)$ had diabetes mellitus, one $(4.2\%)$ reported chronic use of alcohol, and four $(16.6\%)$ reported smoking. Glasgow Coma Scale (GCS) at admission ranged from nine to 15, with a mean of 14. Presenting symptoms were: impairment of consciousness in 10 patients $(41.7\%)$ , motor deficits in 12 $(50\%)$ , headache in 9 $(37.5\%)$ , language deficits in one $(4.2\%)$ , and gait disturbance in one $(4.2\%)$ . Twelve $(50\%)$ patients had hematoma width larger than $20~\mathrm{mm}$ in at least one side, with a hematoma width mean of $20.1\mathrm{mm}$ .  

Applying Nakaguchi Classification, there were 12 $(50\%)$ patients with homogeneous hematomas, two $(8.3\%)$ laminar, one $(4.2\%)$ separated, and nine $(37.5\%)$ trabecular. Six $(25\%)$ patients had bilateral CSDH, and eighteen $(75\%)$ patients had unilateral CSDH. Among unilateral CSDH patients, three $(16.7\%)$ patients were operated with a single burr hole, and 15 $(83.3\%)$ were operated with two burr holes. Among patients with bilateral hematomas: four were operated with one burr hole in each side, one patient with two burr holes in each side and one patient was operated with one burr hole on the left side and two burr holes on the right side. Nineteen $(79.2\%)$ patients used drains and five $(20.8\%)$ did not.

# Outcomes  

Clinical and radiological recurrence occurred in three patients $(6\%)$ , being two cases in TXA group $(8.3\%)$ and one in control group $(3.8\%)$ . There was no statistically significant difference between groups regarding the recurrence rate according to Chi-square tests $(p=0.5)$ . Data of CSDH recurrences are exposed in Table 2. Images of recurrence cases are exposed in Fig. 4.  

Postoperative complications occurred in two patients during follow-up $(4\%)$ , being both cases in TXA group $(8.3\%)$ , and none in the control group. There was one case of oligosymptomatic pulmonary thromboembolism (demanding oral anticoagulants), and one wound infection in TXA group, treated with antibiotics. No other clinical or surgical complications were observed including no cases of acute myocardial infarction, peripheral vascular disease, stroke, or allergic reactions. There was one death in TXA group, due to SARS-CoV-2 infection. This subject developed infection after hospital discharge and when was admitted to hospital due to COVID-19 he did not present any new neurological symptoms and head CT showed no recurrence. There was no statistically significant difference between groups regarding the recurrence rate and complications according to Chi-square tests $(p{=}0.5)$ . Data of complications are summarized in Table 3.  

# Discussion  

# Limitations  

Our main limitation is the small sample in each study arm. Surely, we will continue to reach more data to settle a more definitive result. Then, we did not apply blinding methods, which might have increased bias risks. Additionally, we understand the heterogeneity of CSDH patients, which are generally old subjects and using several medications and preexisting medical conditions which might impact in study analysis. However, we believe our randomization could draw two similar groups according to their baseline data, and we believe our results are coherent.  

Another relevant observation is that we only applied TXA after surgery. Thus, according to data from this manuscript, we are not able to make any inferences about TXA effect when used as an option to nonsurgical management of CSDH. This should be addressed in future trials.  

# Generalization  

Chronic subdural hematomas (CSDH) remain a conceptual and management challenge in neurosurgical practice. Their complex and multifactorial pathophysiology [17], divergent treatment strategies, associated morbidities, and high recurrence rates make this an intriguing chapter in neurosurgical portfolio. Several different and respectful institutions follow different guidelines in their approach especially because there is no clear and strong literature evidence of best practice. Once elder population is continuously increasing together with risk factors like trauma and medications, the prevalence of CSDH should also probably increase [9].  

CSDH treatment may be conservative or surgical, depending on symptoms and clot size [2]. Morbidity and mortality are low; however, complications and recurrences are common [2, 4], occurring in up to $30\%$ of cases. Although there is no consensus on the best surgical technique to be employed, burr hole evacuation of hematoma has been the most frequently used procedure and is considered the first option of treatment for CSDH [6–8].

In 2009, Santarius et al. performed a randomized controlled trial evaluating the use of a drain after burr-hole evacuation. They concluded that a drain was effectively associated with a lower rate of recurrence of CSDH and lower rate of patient mortality [7]. However, Gernsback et al. [18], Sivaraju et al. [19], and Heringer et al. [20] have recently reported that their results did not match with those found by Santarius et al [7]. In these cases, the use of a drain did not affect recurrence rates [18–20].  

When patients have normal conscience status, no motor deficits, and/or controlled symptoms like seizures or headache some nonsurgical treatments are being tried. There have been growing reports on the use of statins (HMG-CoA reductase inhibitors) [21–24], tranexamic acid [12–15, 25] (antiplasminogen antifibrinolytic), a platelet-activating factor receptor antagonist (etizolam) [26, 27], angiotensin-converting enzyme inhibitors [28–30], corticosteroids [31–35], goreisan [13, 25, 36, 37], and antiangiogenic endovascular procedures like embolization of the distal middle meningeal artery (MMA) [38–43]. All medications used until now share a potential role on inflammatory processes underlying CSDH formation.

Most studies on pharmacological treatment of CSDH are based on case series and single-center reports. Tranexamic acid is also being evaluated for CSDH treatment as a standalone method or used together with surgery. Kageyama et al. [12] have reported improvement in a case series. Since then, other studies have pointed divergent results. Yang et al. [15] presented animating results in their sample, showing improvement of CSDH patients while using TXA while Yamada et al. [25] carried out a randomized clinical trial with a treatment protocol similar to the one in this study and found no benefit from tranexamic acid in terms of recurrence avoidance [25].

# Interpretation  

In this study, we tried to assess whether TXA can reduce recurrence of CSDH when used after burr-hole evacuation of CSDH. There were no statistically significant differences in baseline data between groups. Furthermore, surgical technique and perioperative care were barely the same for all patients.  

Although TXA group had a higher recurrence rate $(8.3\%)$ , there was no significant difference between both groups. This result is in agreement with the only randomized clinical trial ever published on the subject [25].  

Moreover, TXA group had two complications while control group had no complications. One complication was potentially related to the use of TXA (pulmonary thromboembolism), and the other was more sporadic (surgical site infection). Although there was no statistically significant difference, the presence of complications in this group points to a possible clinically significant difference and the need for enhanced and close surveillance of such patients.  

Although with the limitations exposed above, our current data suggest that TXA should not be used as a potential agent to avoid recurrences of CSDH and might increase complication odds. Continuing current study is mandatory to draw stronger inferences about this theme. Other similar reports from worldwide centers are also welcome to improve discussion.  

# Conclusion  

Our current data suggest that TXA should not be used as a potential agent to avoid recurrences of CSDH and might increase complication odds. However, it should be emphasized that larger sample sizes will be needed to fully answer the clinical question in this study once our samples were small.  

Future multicenter studies with larger sample sizes are expected to confront current literature and address any issues about CSDH nonsurgical treatment.
