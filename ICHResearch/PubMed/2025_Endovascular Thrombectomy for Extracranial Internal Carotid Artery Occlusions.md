# Abstract  

# Background and Objectives  

Although previous trials have established the efficacy and safety of endovascular thrombectomy (EVT) in large ischemic core strokes, most of them excluded patients with extracranial internal carotid artery (e-ICA) occlusion. We aimed to compare outcomes in patients with e-ICA occlusion and large ischemic core infarcts treated with EVT vs medical management (MM).  

# Methods  

This was a secondary analysis of the SELECT2 trial, a randomized controlled trial conducted at 31 international sites. Adult patients with proximal intracranial anterior circulation large ischemic strokes, defined as Alberta Stroke Program Early CT Score (ASPECTS) 3–5 on noncontrast CT or ischemic core $\ge50\mathrm{mL}$ on CT-perfusion/magnetic resonance-diffusion imaging, and concomitant e-ICA occlusion were selected. The primary outcomes were the distribution of modified Rankin Scale (mRS) score at 90-day follow-up and symptomatic intracranial hemorrhage (sICH).

# Classification of Evidence  

This study provides Class II evidence that for patients with large core acute ischemic stroke and concomitant e-ICA occlusion, EVT is associated with better functional outcome at 90 days compared with MM alone.  

# Introduction  

Approximately $15\%$ of patients with acute ischemic stroke (AIS) are related to an extracranial internal carotid artery (e-ICA) occlusion, either isolated or in tandem with an intracranial large vessel occlusion (LVO).1-3 Nevertheless, only $2^{4,5}$ of the initial pivotal endovascular thrombectomy (EVT) trials included patients with e-ICA lesions.4-10 Consequently, there are limited randomized data supporting the safety and efficacy of EVT in this population. Thus, treating physicians may hesitate to use EVT due to perceived technical difficulties and a lack of evidence showing improved functional outcomes without safety concerns. Furthermore, there is uncertainty regarding the best surgical approach—whether to implement immediate, delayed, or no stenting for proximal extracranial lesions. In addition, the risk-benefit analysis of requiring antiplatelet agents in the acute postprocedure phase suggests a potential increase in the risk of hemorrhagic transformation (HT).11-13  

These concerns are amplified in patients with large core strokes, where the presence of intracranial occlusions poses technical challenges and poor collateral flow increases the likelihood of reperfusion injury, leading to worse clinical outcomes compared with patients with small core strokes.14 Overall, these factors dampen enthusiasm for proceeding with EVT and diminish optimism for achieving acceptable outcomes.  

However, recent results from 6 pivotal randomized controlled trials (RCTs) demonstrating the efficacy and safety of EVT in patients with large core strokes due to anterior circulation LVOs have altered our perspectives on the utility of reperfusion in this population.15-20 It is important that 4 of these trials allowed enrollment of patients with steno-occlusive lesions of the e-ICA, allowing for an opportunity to examine utility of EVT in these patients, as well as the optimal approach for treating extracranial lesion in these patients.  

The SELECT2 trial15 enrolled the largest number of patients with e-ICA occlusions among the 4 trials, offering a unique opportunity to further assess the utility of EVT in this highrisk population. This trial allows us to investigate (1) whether EVT is beneficial, (2) if EVT treatment effect differs in large core stroke patients with and without e-ICA occlusions, and (3) functional outcomes in e-ICA EVT with and without immediate stenting. Thus, we aimed to determine whether EVT is associated with better functional outcome at 90 days compared with medical management (MM) alone for patients with large core AIS and concomitant e-ICA occlusion.  

# Methods  

# Trial Design  

The SELECT2 trial (NCT03876457) was a prospective, open-label, RCT with blinded outcome assessment, aimed to evaluate whether EVT leads to better functional outcomes than standard MM in patients with AIS with large ischemic-core volume at 31 sites across the United States, Canada, Europe, Australia, and New Zealand. The methods of SELECT2 have been reported21 as well as the primary results.15,22 Briefly, eligible patients included adults $\tilde{\cdot}18$ years) with no prestroke disability (modified Rankin scale [mRS] 0 or 1), who presented with an AIS due to occlusion of the internal carotid artery (ICA) or middle cerebral artery (MCA)–M1 segment, with a large stroke on noncontrast CT (Alberta Stroke Program Early CT Score [ASPECTS] 3–5), CT perfusion (relative cerebral blood flow $\textless30\%$ volume of $50~\mathrm{mL}$ or greater) and/or magnetic resonance (MR) diffusion/perfusion (apparent diffusion coefficient $<620\times10^{-6}\mathrm{mm}^{\bar{2}}/s$ volume of $50~\mathrm{mL}$ or greater), and a NIH Stroke Scale (NIHSS) score of 6 or more within 24 hours of symptom onset. The SELECT2 trial enrolled patients between September 2019 and September 2022. All patients received standardized neuroimaging evaluation at baseline including a noncontrast CT and vascular imaging. Patients also received a CT perfusion or MR diffusion-perfusion imaging at baseline with automated processing using RAPID software. The study was approved by the local institutional review board at each participating center and by the responsible regulatory authorities. All participants or their legally authorized representatives provided written informed consent. This study followed the Consolidated Standards of Reporting Trials reporting guideline. The trial protocol is provided in eSAP 1.  

# Study Population  

e-ICA occlusions were defined as a complete occlusion $100\%$ ) in the e-ICA. This lesion could be present with an intracranial LVO, specifically the intracranial segments of the ICA, or the M1 or proximal M2 segment of the MCA. The evaluation was conducted using CT/MR angiography, and subsequently confirmed with digital subtraction angiography for patients undergoing EVT. For those undergoing MM, only CT/MR angiography was used, as assessed by the imaging core laboratory. Patients were categorized based on their treatment into 2 groups: those receiving EVT and those receiving MM.  

# Standard Protocol Approvals, Registrations, and Patient Consents  

The study protocol for SELECT2 was approved at local institutional review board for all sites, and the study was  

prospectively registered at ClinicalTrials.gov (NCT03876457). All participants or their legally authorized representatives provided written informed consent before enrollment in the study.  

# Study Treatments and Interventions  

EVT for intracranial occlusions primarily involved the utilization of stent retrievers and/or aspiration devices cleared by the Food and Drug Administration for thrombus removal in patients with AIS within 24 hours of symptom onset. The decision regarding the thrombectomy method, whether primary aspiration or primary stent-retriever with or without aspiration, was at the discretion of the interventionalist.  

Therapeutic strategies for e-ICA occlusions included carotid artery stenting (CAS) with and without angioplasty or angioplasty alone. The choice of treatment for e-ICA occlusions was left to the operator’s preference. Immediate stenting of e-ICA occlusions was discouraged, and delayed CAS (after 72 hours) was preferred to mitigate the presumed risk of HT due to the necessity for antiplatelet therapy/heparin following stent implantation. The use of heparin and dual antiplatelet therapy within 24 hours postprocedure was discouraged. Decisions to proceed with decompressive hemicraniectomy in patients with severe brain swelling were made in accordance with local practices.  

# Study Outcomes  

The primary efficacy outcome was an ordinal analysis of the distribution of scores on the 90-day mRS (a 7-point ordinal score, with 0 representing no residual stroke deficits and 6 representing death). Secondary efficacy outcomes included 90-day functional independence $(0{-}2\mathrm{mRS)}$ , independent ambulation $\left(0{-}3\mathrm{mRS}\right)$ ), successful reperfusion (defined as grade $\scriptstyle2\mathrm{b}-3$ on the modified Treatment in Cerebral Ischemia Scale), early neurologic improvement (defined as a reduction of ${\ge}8$ points in the NIHSS score from baseline to 24 hours after presentation or a score of 0–1), and quality of life as assessed using domain-specific Neuro-QoL measures (mobility, depression, social participation, and cognitive aspects) transformed to respective $\mathrm{T}$ scores (with higher scores indicating better performance in a given domain, except for depression, where higher scores indicate worse outcome).  

The primary safety outcome was symptomatic intracranial hemorrhage (sICH) defined according to the SITS-MOST criteria (adjudicated by the core laboratory) as parenchymal hemorrhage type 2 (PH2) or remote parenchymal hemorrhage associated with an increase of 4 or more points in the NIHSS score at 24-hour follow-up window. We also assessed the rate of ischemic infarct HT defined as no hemorrhage, petechial hemorrhage (hemorrhagic infarction type 1 and type 2), and parenchymal hematoma (parenchymal hematoma type 1 [PH1] and PH2), according to the Heidelberg Bleeding Classification. Secondary safety outcomes were 90-day mortality, any intracerebral hemorrhage, neurologic worsening (an increase of ${\ge}4$ points in the NIHSS score within 24 hours after presentation), and procedural complications.  

# Statistical Analysis  

Descriptive statistics were used to summarize both continuous and categorical variables. For categorical variables, we presented counts and percentages, while for continuous variables, we reported means (SD) or medians (interquartile range [IQR]) as appropriate. To assess the normality of distributions, we applied the Shapiro-Wilk test and examined histograms. In the univariable analysis, we used the Student $t$ tests or Mann-Whitney $U$ test for continuous variables and the $\chi^{2}$ or Fisher exact test for categorical variables, as required by the nature of the data.  

The primary outcome was evaluated using probabilistic index models (PIMs). PIMs differ from parametric ordinal logistic regression models because PIMs estimate the odds of a random patient from one group having better mRS outcome than a random patient from the other group, given the differences in covariates between these 2 patients. Respective associations are reported as adjusted generalized odds ratios (aGenORs) and $95\%$ CIs, with ties split equally between groups. For secondary outcomes, modified Poisson regression models with robust standard errors27 were used for dichotomous variables and linear regression models were used for continuous variables (infarct volume and growth). Models were adjusted for age, coronary artery disease, IV thrombolysis, NIHSS, ASPECTS, ischemic-core volume (larger of the CT-perfusion/MRI core volume and CT hypodensity volume), and last known well-to-randomization time. Forest plot illustrations were used to demonstrate the association of treatment approach and clinical outcomes across selected subgroups based on age ( ${<}70$ vs $\ge70$ years), stroke severity (NIHSS ${<}20$ vs ${\ttz}20\$ ), ischemic core strata based on 70 and $100\mathrm{mL},$ and CT ASPECTS strata $\left(<4\mathrm{~vs~}\ge4\right)$ . Finally, a univariable exploratory analysis was used to compare clinical characteristics and outcomes stratified by the cervical approach, dichotomized by CAS vs non-CAS. Non-CAS involved angioplasty, while in CAS, angioplasty was performed before stent delivery.  

All analyses were performed using STATA 17 and R version 4.2.2. Data were analyzed under the as-treated principle. All hypotheses were evaluated using 2-sided tests. Analyses were considered exploratory, and no adjustment for multiple comparisons was performed. Missing data were not imputed.

# Results  

# Study Cohort and Baseline Characteristics  

Of the 352 patients enrolled in SELECT2, 62 patients $(17.6\%)$ presented with e-ICA occlusions among whom 37 underwent EVT and 25 received MM. Figure 1 shows a flowchart of patients enrolled in the SELECT2 trial. A comparison of the baseline clinical and imaging characteristics of the patients included in this study is presented in Table 1. The characteristics of both groups were comparable, except for the EVT group exhibiting a higher rate of coronary artery disease $27.8\%$ vs $4.3\%$ ; $p=0.038$ ).  

# Efficacy Outcome  

Figure 2 shows the distribution of mRS scores by the treatment group at 90 days. We found a shift in the distribution of the 90-day mRS score toward better outcomes in the EVT group compared with the MM group (aGenOR 2.51; $95\%$ CI 1.43–4.39; $p=0.001\$ ). Independent ambulation at 90 days occurred in $37.8\%$ of the patients in the EVT group and in $8\%$ of the patients in the MM group (adjusted relative ratio [aRR] 4.58; $95\%$ CI 1.18–17.79; $p=0.028)$ . Functional independence at 90 days was observed in $21.6\%$ of the patients in the EVT group and in $8\%$ of the patients in the MM group (aRR 2.16; $95\%$ CI 0.53–8.83; $p=0.285,$ ). Successful reperfusion with EVT was present in 24 of 37 individuals $\left(64.9\%\right)$ . Additional results for the secondary efficacy outcomes are given in Table 2.  

No heterogeneity of EVT treatment effect was observed across the presence of e-ICA occlusions $\overset{\cdot}{p}$ -interaction $=$ 0.248). EVT demonstrated significant improvement in functional outcome (mRS score) at the 90-day follow-up in patients without e-ICA occlusions (aGenOR 1.65; $95\%$ CI 1.28–2.13; $p<0.001\$ ). Similar results were observed for independent ambulation (EVT: $38.0\%$ vs MM: $20.1\%$ ; aRR 1.98, $95\%$ CI 1.40–2.80; $p<0.001$ ; $p$ -interaction $=0.231$ ) and functional independence (EVT: $19.7\%$ vs MM: $6.9\%$ ; aRR 3.28; $95\%$ CI 1.61–6.67; $p=0.001$ ; $p$ -interaction $=0.713\$ ) (eTable 1).  

# Safety Outcomes  

Results for all safety outcomes are given in Table 2. Neither sICH nor PH2 events were observed in either the EVT or the MM group. The overall risk of HT was higher in the EVT group compared with the MM group, notably driven by an increased rate of petechial hemorrhage in the EVT group $72.9\%$ vs $32\%$ ) (Figure 3A). At 90 days, 14 of 37 patients $(37.8\%)$ in the EVT group and 10 of 25 patients $(40\%)$ in the MM group had died (aRR 0.75; $95\%$ CI 0.39–1.45; $p~=$ 0.388). It is important that there were no significant differences in the rates of hemicraniectomy (EVT $19\%$ vs MM $28\%$ , $p=0.54)$ or withdrawal of care during patients hospitalization (EVT $27\%$ vs MM $20\%$ , $p=0.56,$ .  

Procedural complications occurred in 8 patients $\left(21.6\%\right)$ in the EVT group. Complications included dissection (in 2 patients $\left[5.4\%\right],$ ), perforation (in 2 patients $\left[5.4\%\right],$ ), and vasospasm (in 3 patients $\left[8.1\%\right]$ ).  

# Subgroup Analyses  

The results of exploratory subgroup analyses for mRS shift and independent ambulation at 90 days are presented in eFigures 1 and 2, respectively, and align with the findings of the primary analysis. Nonsignificant trends favoring EVT over MM for improved mRS scores at 90 days were present across all subgroups, irrespective of age, NIHSS at presentations, infarct volume, and ASPECTS.

# Classification of Evidence  

This study provides Class II evidence that for patients with large core AIS and concomitant e-ICA occlusion, EVT is associated with better functional outcome at 90 days compared with MM alone.  

# Discussion  

Patients with e-ICA occlusions and large ischemic core stroke constituted $17.6\%$ of the SELECT2 trial’s cohort.9 Key findings demonstrated that EVT resulted in better functional outcomes and independent ambulation at 90 days without an increased risk of symptomatic ICH or PH2. The nature of the study population resulted in a limited number of patients with e-ICA achieving functional independence, which limited our ability to detect a difference, but the proportion was still higher with EVT. Furthermore, EVT was shown to be beneficial in patients without e-ICA occlusions, and no heterogeneity of EVT effect was observed. EVT had a higher risk of HT mainly driven by petechial hemorrhage, which was independent of the cervical treatment approach used. No significant interaction effect was found among stratified key clinical subgroups.  

Historically, patients with e-ICA occlusions or large ischemic cores were excluded from early EVT pivotal trials, resulting in a knowledge gap in their management. Among the current 6 trials focusing on large core strokes, the Large Stroke Therapy Evaluation trial excluded patients with e-ICA lesions with a concomitant intracranial LVO (tandem).20 Conversely, the Randomized Clinical Trial of Endovascular Therapy for Acute Large Vessel Occlusion with Large Ischemic Core (RESCUEJapan LIMIT) trial included tandem lesions but did not explicitly define whether it encompassed extracranial and/or intracranial ICA occlusions.19 In the Thrombectomy for Emergent Salvage of Large Anterior Circulation Ischemic Stroke16 and The Efficacy and Safety of Thrombectomy in Stroke With Extended Lesion and Extended Time Window18 trials, $3.6\%$ and $5.9\%$ of the patients, respectively, presented with e-ICA occlusion, while in the Endovascular Therapy in Acute Anterior Circulation Large Vessel Occlusive Patients with a Large Infarct Core (ANGEL-ASPECT) trial, $16.7\%$ had e-ICA occlusion in addition to the intracranial occlusion.

Our SELECT2 subanalysis, therefore, provides valuable insight into this understudied area, showing promising clinical outcomes and reperfusion rates of EVT in these patients, with e-ICA lesions and large ischemic core strokes, and no significant heterogeneity from those without e-ICA occlusions. As outcomes in MM arms are generally worse in those with e-ICA occlusions as compared with those without e-ICA occlusions, treatment effect estimates were numerically higher in patients with e-ICA occlusions. Similar findings supporting the efficacy of EVT in patients with large-core strokes and tandem occlusions (comprising intracranial LVO and e-ICA occlusion) were observed in a subanalysis of the nonrandomized Endovascular Treatment in Ischemic Stroke (ETIS) registry.28 In this study, tandem patients with ASPECTS 0–5 who were treated with CAS or angioplasty achieved a significantly higher rate of 90-day $0{-}3\mathrm{mRS}$ compared with nontandem patients ( $49.3\%$ vs $39.2\%$ ; adjusted odds ratio 1.51; $95\%$ CI 1.01–2.26; $p=0.042,$ ).28 In addition, a subgroup analysis of the ANGELASPECT trial, which focused on patients with an ipsilateral e-ICA occlusion (lesion in tandem), suggested a trend favoring EVT over MM in functional outcomes (aGenOR 1.28; $95\%$ CI $0.76{-}2.16)$ , although these results did not reach statistical significance, potentially due to underpowering.17  

The results of our exploratory subgroup analyses are consistent with the primary results. Although limited by the sample sizes of the subgroups, the results indicate that the beneficial effect of EVT in patients with e-ICA occlusions and large core infarcts remains consistent. This effect seems to be independent of factors such as age, neurologic severity, ischemic core volume, or ASPECTS, particularly evident in patients with an ischemic core volume below $100\mathrm{mL}$ and those with an ASPECTS score of ${\ge}4$ (eFigures 1 and 2). These results, along with the lack of significant heterogeneity across functional outcomes, support treating these patients with EVT in a manner similar to those without e-ICA.  

Intracranial hemorrhage has been reported in $30\%-40\%$ of cases following revascularization of the e-ICA, attributed to reperfusion injury and the aggressive antiplatelet therapy required during acute CAS.29,30 In our study, neither the EVT or MM group experienced sICH events and, while the EVT group showed a higher risk of any ICH and HT, this difference in HT distribution was primarily due to petechial hemorrhage (Figure 3). Reperfusion injury and blood-brain barrier disruption are well-known mechanisms that contribute to the elevated risk of hemorrhage following EVT.31,32 It is important that in patients with large-core strokes, the presence of large infarct areas leads to more extensive tissue damage, greater disruption of the blood-brain barrier, and an increased likelihood of reperfusion injury.33 Despite these concerns, the safety profile of EVT in e-ICA occlusions with a large ischemic core has also been reported previously in the ETIS registry subanalysis.28

Concerns have been raised regarding the increased risk of hemorrhagic complications with acute CAS. Antithrombotic agents are required to prevent intraluminal stent thrombosis, yet specific recommendations for antiplatelet therapy during the acute phase following CAS are lacking. Recent evidence has demonstrated the safety of acute CAS, regardless of the use of IV thrombolytics or “aggressive” antiplatelet therapy .12,35,36 The controversies surrounding the safety of CAS and the optimal antithrombotic regimen are particularly worrisome in the context of patients with large ischemic core stroke, who are known to be at a heightened risk of ICH following reperfusion therapy, leading to a worse prognosis.37,38 However, our findings suggest that both acute CAS and nonstenting groups demonstrated comparable rates of any ICH and no occurrences of sICH, challenging previous concerns about hemorrhagic risks associated with acute CAS in a specific population of patients with extracranial ICA occlusions and large ischemic stroke. Nevertheless, given the limited sample size, larger cohorts are required to more thoroughly assess the safety implications of CAS in LVO patients with large infarcts at presentation.

We observed a nonsignificant trend toward higher rates of independent ambulation, successful reperfusion, and early neurologic improvement in patients treated with acute CAS compared with a non-CAS approach. Although our primary analysis revealed a lower rate of successful reperfusion $(64.9\%)$ compared with the overall SELECT2 trial cohort (79.8%),15 patients undergoing acute CAS achieved comparable successful reperfusion rates $(75\%)$ to SELECT2 and to the findings from the aforementioned ETIS subanalysis $(79.8\%)$ .28 It is worth nothing that patients with extracranial ICA lesions are known to have a relatively high rate of periprocedural clot migration and embolization, which may partially explain our findings.39,40 However, acute intervention of the extracranial ICA lesion with CAS leads to improved functional outcomes and reperfusion with acceptable safety compared with no stenting .11,13,41 Although our exploratory findings are limited by sample size, they suggest a similar beneficial clinical effect of CAS in extracranial ICA occlusions with large ischemic strokes.  

This secondary analysis has several limitations. The randomization algorithm did not consider the presence of extracranial ICA occlusion for stratification, and the treatment assignment across patients with extracranial ICA was thus unbalanced. The early termination of SELECT2 and resulting small sample size potentially restrict the robustness of our findings, particularly due to its subgroup analysis nature.15 Unmeasured variables such as extracranial ICA lesion etiology, intraprocedural techniques, intraprocedural antiplatelet protocol, and socioeconomic factors could confound results, emphasizing the need for caution in interpretation. In particular, the antiplatelet regimens used during and after the procedure may affect the outcomes for patients treated with EVT and CAS. Similar to the SELECT2 trial, where approximately $20\%$ of the patients received IV thrombolytics, the rate in this secondary analysis was even lower $(17\%)$ , limiting the ability of evaluating the additional effect of IV thrombolytics in this population. As patients in the MM group did not undergo digital subtraction angiography to establish the diagnosis of e-ICA occlusion, it is possible that a proportion of these patients had carotid pseudo-occlusion with intracranial carotid occlusion and no e-ICA occlusion. As the median ASPECTS of our patients in this study was 5, it is uncertain whether we could generalize our findings in patients who present with lower ASPECTS. CAS was discouraged per protocol, which may have influenced the number of patients with e-ICA occlusion who were treated in the trial. Therefore, both the acute CAS and non-CAS approach groups had relatively small sample size, insufficient for proper adjustment for covariates, and cautioning any clinical interpretability.  

Overall, we observed significant improvement in functional outcomes with EVT in patients with e-ICA occlusions and large ischemic core stroke. Furthermore, we did not observe any PH2 or sICH, despite overall higher risk of HT, largely driven by petechial hemorrhages. Although ongoing randomized clinical trials are necessary to definitively determine the benefit of EVT in this specific population, our findings suggest that in the meantime, these patients seem to benefit from EVT with a similar effect size as isolated LVO presenting with large ischemic infarcts.
