# Efficacy and safety of NeuroEndoscopic Surgery for IntraCerebral Hemorrhage: A randomized, controlled, open-label, blinded endpoint trial (NESICH)  

Long Wang1, Tengyuan Zhou1, Pangbo Wang1, Shuixian Zhang1, Yi Yin1, Lin Chen1, Haijun Duan1, Na $\mathbf{\Delta}\mathbf{w}_{\mathbf{u}}\mathbf{2}$ , Hua Feng1 and Rong Hu1  

# Abstract  

Background: Neuroendoscopy is a minimally invasive procedure for clot evacuation in intracerebral hemorrhage (ICH) which may have advantages compared with open surgical evacuation procedures. The application of neuroendoscopy in ICH has attracted increasing attention in recent years. However, it remains unclear whether it could improve outcomes in patients with ICH.  

Objective: The aim of this study is to explore the efficacy and safety of neuroendoscopic hematoma evacuation surgery compared with standard conservative treatment for spontaneous deep supratentorial cerebral hemorrhage.  

Methods: The Efficacy and safety of NeuroEndoscopic Surgery for IntraCerebral Hemorrhage (NESICH) Trial is a multicenter, randomized, controlled, open-label, blinded-endpoint clinical trial. Up to 560 eligible subjects with acute deep supratentorial ICH will be randomly assigned (1:1) to receive either neuroendoscopic hematoma evacuation or standard conservative treatment at more than 30 qualified neurosurgery centers in China.  

Outcomes: The primary endpoint is the proportion of patients with a good functional outcome (mRS score 0–3) in both groups at 180 days after onset. The main safety endpoints include all-cause mortality at 7, 30, and 180 days, rebleeding at 3, 7, and 30 days, and serious complications within 180 days.  

Discussion: NESICH will provide high-quality evidence for the efficacy and safety of neuroendoscopic hematoma evacuation surgery in ICH patients.  

Trial registration: ClinicalTrials.gov NCT05539859.

# Introduction and rationale  

Intracerebral hemorrhage (ICH) is a non-traumatic bleeding in the brain parenchyma due to spontaneous rupture of small arteries, veins, or capillaries in the brain, often caused by rupture of small blood vessels in the brain due to hypertension.1 In Europe and the United States, cerebral hemorrhage accounts for approximately $10\%{-}15\%$ of all cerebrovascular disease, but in Asian countries, it accounts for about $20\%-30\%$ of cerebrovascular

diseases.2,3 Compared to other stroke types, ICH has a very high mortality and disability rate, with a 30-day mortality rate of up to $50\%$ and $70\%$ of survivors left with neurological deficits.4,5  

Surgical treatment has become an important option for ICH with its advantages of rapid hematoma removal, relief of high cranial pressure, and release of mechanical compression. Previous randomized controlled trials (RCTs) for surgical treatment of ICH, such as the STICH I and STICH II (craniotomy for hematoma removal),6,7 and MISTIE III (minimally invasive surgery combined with rt-PA for cerebral hemorrhage treatment), none of them showed positive results that surgery provided better benefit to patients when compared with conservative treatment. The ENRICH trial has been completed this year, but the results have not been fully published yet.9 It is believed that the possible reason for the failure of the STICH studies was that the benefits of the surgery were offset by the brain injury secondary to the surgical procedure. The MISTIE study shows that greater clearance of hematoma has a higher likelihood of positive functional outcomes.10 Therefore, finding a more minimally invasive surgical approach and removing as much hematoma as possible is the future direction for the development of surgical treatment of ICH.  

Our previous work and related studies showed that a minimally invasive neuroendoscopic surgical approach protecting white matter fiber tracts (mainly the corticospinal tract) could effectively improve the neurological outcome of patients with ICH.11 But there are relatively few multicenter clinical studies on it. Accordingly, we plan to conduct a prospective, multicenter, randomized controlled clinical trial, aiming to investigate the safety and efficacy of neuroendoscopic treatment of ICH and to provide medical evidence for the minimally invasive endoscopic treatment.  

# Methods  

# Trial design  

The ongoing NESICH trial is a multicenter, randomized, controlled, open-label, and blinded endpoint trial. The study has a planned duration of 3 years to recruit 560 patients at more than 30 neurosurgery centers in China. NESICH has been approved by the medical ethics committee of the first affiliated hospital of the Army Medical University in China ((A) KY2022103). Written informed consent is obtained from patient or family. The study flow chart is shown in Figure 1.  

# Patient population  

Inclusion criteria  

1.	 Age 18–80 years, either sex.  

2.	 Time from onset to the first diagnostic CT within 24 hours (for those without bystanders and with unknown onset time, use the last known time the patient was in good condition).   
3.	 Deep (external capsule, putamen, internal capsule, and caudate nucleus) supratentorial cerebral hemorrhage with a hematoma volume $\geq25\mathrm{mL}$ .   
4.	 Stability of the hematoma determined by two CT scans at different times after onset. If the hematoma enlarges $5\mathrm{mL}$ , then the stability of the hematoma can be detected by CT again after 6 hours until the randomization time window is closed.   
5.	 Pre-randomization Glasgow Coma Scale (GCS) score of 5–14 and/or NIHSS score of $\geqslant6$ .   
6.	 Pre-onset Modified Rankin Scale (mRS) score 0 or 1.   
7.	 Blood pressure recorded 6 hours prior to randomization consistently controlled at $180\mathrm{mmHg}$ or less.   
8.	 Randomization completed within 24 hours after the first diagnostic CT, and surgical intervention should be performed as soon as possible, no later than 6 hours after randomization, that is to say, surgery should be performed no later than 54 hours after onset.   
9.	 Informed and voluntarily signed informed consent by the patient or family.  

# Exclusion criteria  

1.	 Hemorrhage clinically diagnosed as a result of cerebral aneurysm, cerebrovascular malformation, moyamoya disease, traumatic brain injury, brain tumor, hemorrhagic transformation of a large cerebral infarct, or coagulation dysfunction.   
2.	 Lobar hemorrhages, thalamic hemorrhages, primary ventricular hemorrhages, cerebellum hemorrhages, and brain stem hemorrhages.   
3.	 Hematoma involving the midbrain, with dilated or unresponsive pupils.   
4.	 Hematoma producing life-threatening occupying effects (e.g. CT showing midline deviation of more than $1\mathrm{{cm}}$ , loss of cisterna ambiens) or patients who are extremely unstable and unfit for enrollment.   
5.	 Platelet count $<100\times10^{9}/\mathrm{L}$ , international normalized ratio $(\mathrm{INR})>1.4$ .   
6.	 Hematoma extension to ventricle and completely blocked the third or fourth ventricle.   
7.	 Recent history of cerebral hemorrhage (less than 1 year).   
8.	 Severe hepatic impairment with ALT three times the upper limit of normal, or AST three times the upper limit of normal. Severe renal insufficiency with glomerular filtration rate less than $30\mathrm{mL}/\$ $\mathrm{min}/1.73\mathrm{m}^{2}$ .

# Randomization  

In this study, a total of 560 subjects who meet the eligibility criteria will be randomly allocated 1:1 to either experimental or control group according to the centralized procedure using the minimization, a dynamic randomization, which is adjusted centrally by dynamic assignment against age and hematoma volume as the minimization factors. In practice, the random assignment is achieved using a mobile-based application (“Ericure Random Grouping” WeChat App).  

# Recruitment and consent  

Patients are screened by experienced neurosurgeons at each center based on inclusion and exclusion criteria. Surgeons should help patients understand the risks of both treatment strategies and will obtain their written consent. Informed consent will be obtained from relatives of patients screened for this trial if they are unconscious or mentally impaired due to stroke at admission and from patients if they regain consciousness after treatment. If patients are incapacitated and their relatives cannot be located, these individuals will not be included in this trial. Any individuals directly related to the investigator, such as close relatives of the hospital or hospital staff, will not be included.  

# Treatment  

Control group: standard conservative treatment.  Subjects in the control group will be treated according to the American Heart Association/American stroke Association (AHA/ ASA) 2022 edition of the Guidelines for the Management of Spontaneous Cerebral Hemorrhage, the Chinese Guidelines for the Management of Cerebral Hemorrhage 2019, and the Hypertensive Cerebral Hemorrhage Chinese Multidisciplinary Guidelines 2020.  

Experimental group: NeuroEndoscopic surgery.  Procedure: The location and direction of the puncture are determined using a simple positioning device. The scalp is marked and incised approximately $5\mathrm{cm}$ , followed by tight hemostasis, periosteal stripping and drilling of a $2.5{-}3\mathrm{cm}$ bone flap. After opening the dura mater and initial hematoma decompression, the endoscopic channel is inserted to aspirate the liquid hematoma under the neuroendoscopy. The goal of hematoma removal is to achieve the maximum possible clearance of the hematoma. Hemostasis in the presence of bleeding is achieved by gelatin sponge compression or warm saline flushing, electrocoagulation should be avoided whenever possible except pulsatile arterial bleeding. In addition, placement of a cranial pressure monitoring probe with drainage is considered if available. The dura is then carefully sutured and the bone flap is repositioned and fixed, followed by suturing the scalp. Postoperative management is similar to the control group.  

# Treatment crossover  

Patients in the conservative treatment group who experience deterioration, induced by rebleeding or severe edema, and so on, during treatment that endangers the patient’s life will be transferred to surgery in a manner determined by the local neurosurgeon.  

# Efficacy endpoints  

Primary endpoint.  The primary endpoint is the proportion of patients with a good functional outcome (mRS score 0–3) in both groups at 180 days after onset.  

# Secondary efficacy end points  

1.	 Extended Glasgow outcome scale (eGOS) scores of patients in both groups at 30, 90, and 180 days after onset of disease.   
2.	 Proportion of good outcome (mRS score 0–3) in both groups at 30 and 90 days after onset.   
3.	 Survival rate of patients in the two groups at 30, 90, and 180 days after onset.   
4.	 The number of cases transferred to surgery after conservative treatment and related circumstances.  

# Safety endpoints  

1.	 All-cause mortality in both groups at 7, 30, and 180 days.   
2.	 Incidence of death, rebleeding, and infection in both groups at 3, 7, and 30 days after randomization.   
3.	 Incidence of complications (seizure, hydrocephalus, pneumonia, gastrointestinal bleeding, deep vein thrombosis, cardiovascular, and cerebrovascular events) in both groups within 180 days.   
4.	 The incidence of adverse and serious adverse events in the two groups of patients within 180 days.  

# Data and safety monitoring  

A Data and Safety Monitoring Board (DSMB) has been established to advice on whether to suspend/continue the trial during the implementation phase. The DSMB is composed of experienced neurosurgeons and statisticians, none of whom are involved in the implementation of the trial. At each meeting, DSMB will be presented with group comparisons regarding mortality, occurrence of rebleeding and hemorrhage expansion, unexplained and unexpected surgical complications, and other SAEs. The results of the analysis of all major outcome indicators and adverse events will be reported to the DSMB under strict confidentiality measures.  

# Sample size estimates  

The primary efficacy outcome of the trial is the proportion of patients with a good functional outcome (mRS score 0–3) in both groups at 180 days of onset. To detect a $13\%$ difference of the proportion of patients with a good functional outcome in two groups, assuming $37\%$ patients with a good functional outcome in the control group, 450 subjects randomized 1:1 to experimental versus control group will provide $80\%$ power with a two-sided $\upalpha$ of 0.05. Considering $10\%$ loss of follow-up and $10\%$ cross-over rate, a total of 560 patients (280 patients in each group) are planned to be enrolled in this study.  

# Statistical analysis  

Analysis of the primary efficacy outcome will be analyzed according to the group they are randomized to, referred to as the intent-to-treat (ITT) population. For the primary outcome analysis, the difference and its $95\%$ confidential interval (CI) of the proportions of patients with an mRS score of 0–3 in the treatment and control group will be estimated. The $95\%$ CI will be adjusted for the covariates used in the randomization and for clinically established important prognostic variables.  

For the analysis of the secondary efficacy outcome, we will use the ordinal logistic regression to compare the eGOS scores of patients in both groups at 30, 90, and 180 days after onset of disease separately. The proportions of patients in both groups with an mRS score of 0–3 at 30 and 90 days. Ordinal logistic regression will be undertaken to compare the mRS scores of patients in both groups at 90 and 180 days after onset of disease separately. Kaplan– Meier time-to-event analysis will be used to estimate the survival functions and the log-rank test to compare survival rate of patients in the two groups. These models will be informed by univariate regression and adjusted for important prognostic factors. In addition, the number of cases transferred to surgery after conservative treatment and related circumstances will be reported.  

Subgroup analysis was planned by variables such as age, sex, hematoma volume, GCS score at admission, time from onset to treatment initiation, and so on.  

Safety endpoints will be analyzed using the safety analysis set (SAS), and for most of the safety endpoint events, risk ratios will be calculated and the proportions will be compared using chi-square tests, Fisher’s exact test. Adverse events and serious adverse events will be listed separately. In addition, a detailed description of all deaths and serious adverse events will be provided in a case narrative format. End-of-study follow-up uses descriptive statistics to report data collected at the end of the study visit for each treatment group.  

The statistical significance level for all tests was set at $\upalpha{=}0.05$ for the two-sided test. Statistics and their $95\%$ confidence intervals will be reported when comparing all outcomes in both groups according to the CONSORT statement (http://www.consort-statement.org).  

# Discussion  

This study is an RCT designed for patients with spontaneous deep supratentorial ICH. In the acute treatment of ICH, there is no evidence-based primary surgical option. Traditional surgical treatment options are often accompanied by greater surgical trauma; therefore, the aim of this study is to compare neuroendoscopic surgery with standard conservative treatment options, for which studies have confirmed some of the clinical advantages,12,13 but studies on the functional outcome are lacking. We currently believe that neuroendoscopic surgery provides a better outcome for patients, and this trial aims to provide high-quality evidence to support this hypothesis.  

# Trial status  

The trial was registered at ClinicalTrials.gov on 14 September 2022, and recruitment has begun on 15 November 2022. The planned completion date for recruitment is 1 September 2025, which will include a 6-month follow-up period. The trial is currently in the recruitment phase.
