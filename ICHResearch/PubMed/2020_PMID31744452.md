Abstract: Introduction: Controlling of secondary traumatic brain injuries (TBI) is necessary due to its salient effect on the improvement of patients with TBI and the final outcomes within early hours of trauma onset. This study aims to investigate the effect of intravenous tranexamic acid (TAX) administration on decreased hemorrhage during surgery.  

Methods: This double-blind, randomized, and placebo-controlled trial was conducted on patients referring to the emergency department (ED) with IPH due to brain contusion within $^{8\mathrm{~h~}}$ of injury onset. The patients were evaluated by receiving TXA and $0.9\%$ normal saline as a placebo. The following evaluation and estimations were performed: intracranial hemorrhage volume after surgery using brain CT-scan; hemoglobin (Hb) volume before, immediately after, and six hours after surgery; and the severity of TBI based on Glasgow Coma Score (GCS).  

Results: 40 patients with $55.02\pm18.64$ years old diagnosed with a contusion and intraparenchymal hemorrhage. Although the $\mathrm{(Mean~\pm~SD)}$ ) hemorrhage during surgery in patients receiving TXA $784.21\pm304.162)$ was lower than the placebo group $(805.26\pm300.876)$ , no significant difference was observed between two groups $(\mathrm{P}{=}0.83)$ . The $(\mathrm{{Mean}\pmSD})$ ) Hb volume reduction immediately during surgery $(0.07\pm0.001$ and $0.23\pm0.02\$ ) and six hours after surgery $(0.04\pm0.008$ and $0.12\pm$ 0.006) was also lower in TXA group but had no significant difference $\mathrm{P}=0.89$ and $\mathrm{P}=0.97$ , respectively).  

Conclusion: Using TXA may reduce the hemorrhage in patients with TBI, but this effect, as in this study, was not statistically significant and it is suggested that a clinical trial with a larger population is employed for further investigation.  

Keywords: Cerebral hemorrhage, emergency medicine, intraparenchymal hemorrhage, tranexamic acid, trauma, linical trial study.  

# 1. INTRODUCTION  

More than 10 million people yearly worldwide are being admitted to hospital or die due to traumatic brain injuries (TBI) [1]. About $90\%$ of deaths from these injuries occur in low- or average-income countries [2]. A severe traumatic brain injury can permanently impact the life of an individual and reduce their lives’ quality [3-5].  

The mortality rate is different, depending on the severity of injury [6]. Although the severity of the initial injury cannot be amended after trauma, the improvement of outcomes in patients with traumatic injuries largely depends on the reduction of secondary brain injuries such as the extent of hematoma, cerebral edema, increased intracranial pressure, infection, hypoxia, and coagulation disorders [7, 8]. The brain tissue contains a large volume of thromboplastin. In physical traumatic injuries to parenchyma, this substance is released with high concentration in blood and causes disorder in coagulation process [9]. 13 to 35 percent of patients with severe head trauma face intraparenchymal injuries, though only 20 percent of surgeries due to craniotomy to reduce the space-occupying pressure relate to intraparenchymal injuries [8]. Intraparenchymal hemorrhage often needs no surgery unless it has pressure effect due to injury volume or causes increased intracranial pressure [10]. A big challenge in this hemorrhages is how to control the intraparenchymal hemorrhage growth [11]. At least, 25 percent of patients with intracranial injuries indicate signs of worsening in radiologic or clinical evidences from trauma within 2 to 3 days after the accident, which is largely due to contusion and cerebral edema [12]. Tranexamic Acid (TAX) is a synthetic substance made of lysine amino acid which plays its antifibrinolytic role by binding to plasminogen and preventing its interfering with fibrin, which in turn prevents the clot from solving [13, 14]. This drug as an anti-fibrinolytic agent may constrain fibrinolysis and in turn intracranial hemorrhage [15]. Fibrinolysis is prevalent in TBIs and it has been shown that its volume can be a strong predictor of progressive intracranial hemorrhage [16]. Also, using TAX may limit the activating effect of plasminogen which plays an important role in pre-injury edema [17]. This study aims to investigate the effect of TAX on reduction of hemorrhage volume during neurosurgery and improvement of therapeutic outcomes of patients with brain contusion and intraparenchymal hemorrhage admitted to emergency department (ED).  

# 2. METHODS  

This study was carried out based on consolidated standards of reporting trials (CONSORT) [18].  

# 2.1. Trial Design  

This was a double-blind, randomized, and placebocontrolled trial study which was conducted after obtaining ethics code from ethics committee of Jundishapur University and clinical trial code on patients referring to ED of Golestan hospital in Ahvaz between 2017-2018 within first $^{\textrm{\scriptsize8h}}$ of injury and initial examinations and stabilizations related to traumatic injury to head and confirming the existence of brain contusion with intraparenchymal hemorrhage by brain CT scan.  

# 2.2. Participants  

# 2.2.1. Inclusion Criteria of Study  

It includes patients over the age 18 years referred to hospital within $^{\mathrm{~8~h~}}$ after accident, diagnosed with brain contusion with intraparenchymal hemorrhage by brain CT scan, having no significant extradural hemorrhage such abdominal bleeding, no fracture and/or deformity in membranes, no hematuria and coagulation disorders, and the need for surgery depending on clinical condition and neurosurgeons’ opinion.  

# 2.2.2. Exclusion Criteria of Study  

It excludes pregnant women, the patients taking anticoagulant drugs such as warfarin, heparin as well as aspirin, patients with intracranial hemorrhage (in addition to contusion and intraparenchymal) along with the need for massive transfusion and/or fresh frozen plasma (FFP) injection.  

# 2.3. Effect sizes  

For the independent samples T-test, Cohen's d is determined by calculating the mean difference between two groups and then dividing the result by the pooled standard deviation [19]. Statistical power calculations refer to pre-trial calculations aimed at determining the minimum sample size required for this clinical study. For this, we determined the sample size according to liturcher review [11] and Cohen's d, that 20 patients had a minimum size for each group.  

# 2.4. Therapeutic Intervention and Randomization  

# 2.4.1. Outcomes  

Based on the random number list, the patients in each group were divided into two groups of receiving TAX and placebo. In the TAX group, a package containing 4 tranexamic acid $500~\mathrm{mg}$ injection (manufactured by CASPIAN Co. Iran) and two sterilized syringes $10~\mathrm{cc}$ were considered for each patient. Drug infusion in the form of loading dose (combination of 1g TAX with $500~\mathrm{ml}$ of $0.09\%$ normal saline and intravenous Infusion within $10\mathrm{min}$ ) and maintenance dose (combination of ${1}\mathbf{g}$ TAX with $500~\mathrm{ml}$ of $0.09\%$ normal saline and Intravenous Infusion within $8\mathrm{min}$ ) was administered by the nurse as the research assistant. In the placebo group, only $0.09\%$ normal saline was used which was similar to that in the TAX group regarding dose and conditions. All patients underwent neurosurgery by a fixed surgery team.  

Considering the observance of double-blind situation of this study and unawareness of nurse in charge of injection of the type of study groups and also due to the cases where injecting the solution containing TAX was allowed, all serums of patients were marked to avoid injecting this solution in mixed form with blood or solutions containing penicillin or mannitol.  

Hemorrhage volume during surgery was evaluated using HB comparison, before, immediately after, and six hours after surgery. Density and blood volume poured at the surgery field was measured by a dry scale. The intracranial Hemorrhage volume was estimated after surgery by CT scans after surgery and appropriately upon the patient’s discharge, using the computer-based volumetric method by a fixed neurologist who had no information of patients grouping.  

The severity of TBI was specified according to GCS after head trauma as severe [3-8], moderate [9-12], and minor [13- 15]. A code was dedicated to each patient and the information were recorded and collected by a research assistant who had no information about the study groupings.  

# 2.5. Statistical Methods  

After collecting the patients’ data, the information obtained using SPSS software were analytically analyzed. In this study, the results were reported using descriptive statistics including mean, deviation, frequency, and percentage. Also, in terms of variable distribution normality, the quantitative and qualitative variables were examined by the MannWhitney test or independent t-test, and chi-square test, respectively, to compare the mean value of studied variables in two groups and the significance level was considered less than 0.05.

# 3. RESULTS  

In this study, 40 patients diagnosed with a contusion and intraparenchymal hemorrhage were evaluated based on inclusion and exclusion criteria under two treatment and control groups (20 patients per group) (Fig. 1). The patients aged average between $55.02\pm18.64$ and 35 percent of the population were female. Other basic information of patients has been reported in Table 1.  

The average age of TAX and placebo group had no significant difference $(\mathrm{P}{=}0.91)\$ ). Distribution of patients in terms of gender also had no significant difference between these two groups $(\mathrm{P}{=}0.13)$ but the number of male patients was more than females.  

In both TAX and placebo groups, the number of patients with severe TBI was significantly more than other patients, however, no significant difference in patients’ distribution between two groups was observed in terms of severity of injury among intervention and placebo group and they were consistent regarding the severity of injury $(\mathrm{P}{=}0.85)$ .  

The average hemorrhage during and after surgery among all studied patients was evaluated. Although the average hemorrhage during surgery in the TAX-receiving group was lower than the placebo group, this difference was not reported statistically significant $(\mathrm{P}{=}0.83)$ (Table 2).  

In order to evaluate the Hb reduction volume as a criterion of bleeding volume during surgery of patient, the Hb difference was calculated before, immediately after, and after surgery, showing no significant difference between Hb reduction volume immediately after surgery and six hours after surgery among TAX and placebo group (Table 2).  

In intervention and placebo groups, 3 patients of each group died after surgery with average hemorrhage of 682.19 $\pm\:0.20\$ CC and $572.20\pm0.35\mathrm{CC}$ , and there was no significant difference between studied groups in terms of mortality rate $(\mathrm{P}{=}0.87)$ . No severe side effects from taking TAX and normal saline was observed in any group.  

# 4. DISCUSSION  

This study was designed and performed in order to investigate the effect of TAX on reduced hemorrhage, especially in patients with head trauma, and observing the reduced hemorrhage after surgery and the interesting clinical outcomes in these patients. Using TAX involves lower risk of incidence of side effects than similar drugs [20-24]. On the other hand, however, this drug is not typically used to reduce hemorrhage in heart surgery, bones and joints surgery and hepatic surgeries and it is not common to use it in neurosurgery [25].

Based on GCS, the number of patients with severe brain injury in this study was significantly higher than others; however, there was no significant difference between patient’s distribution regarding the severity of injuries in patients of intervention and placebo groups. In this study, the mean hemorrhage of TAX-receiving patients during surgery was lower than placebo group but no statistically relevant difference was observed between two groups, which are consistent with the results in the study by Kawahara et al. (2011) and Yutthakasemsunt et al. (2013) who had reported better hemorrhage control in the TAX group [26, 27]. In a metaanalysis by Zehtabchi et al. (2014), they reported a significant reduction of ICH hemorrhage in the TAX-taking group which, while confirming the results of the present study, was not consistent with our study in terms of significance (2). In a review study, Cap et al. (2011) reported the safety and effectivity of using TAX drug in reducing hemorrhage after surgery. The difference between the latter and current studies was that several types of surgeries was investigated [28]). There was no significant difference in Hb volume reduction immediately after and 6 hours after surgery between TAX and placebo groups (0.891 and 0.972).  

In the study by Kawahara et al, $11\%$ of patients in the TAX group and $18\%$ of patients in the placebo group died [26]. Although the mortality rate in the placebo group of this study was higher, there was observed no significant difference between these two groups, which is inconsistent with the present study. Results of study by Zehtab et al. also reported decreased mortality in the TAX group but no significant difference was observed, which consists with results of present study, and in the study by Majima et al. (2013) [29], the mortality rate in TAX group had been reported significantly lower than placebo group $(4\%\nus.24\%)$ , What's in line with our results. Also, in a study by Harvey et al. (2014), the mortality rate due to hemorrhage and other factors in the TAX group was significantly lower than the placebo group $(\mathrm{P}{=}0.004)$ , which was not consistent with the present study [30].  

As in the study by Yutthakasemsunt et al.; Zehtab et al. and Majima et al. also observed no severe side effects in any of the patients taking TAX such as Thromboemboli incidence [2, 27, 29].  

# 5. LIMITATIONS  

The limitations of the present study include: the lower number of studied patients in each group, time-consuming process of preparing the patients to perform initial CT scan, and not fulfillment of the criteria of presence within the $^{\textrm{\scriptsize8h}}$ after injury in patients until surgery time, lower number of cases using drug within $^{3\mathrm{~h~}}$ after trauma and also some patients or individuals accompanying them not wanting to participate in the study.  

# CONCLUSION  

The results showed that using TAX may reduce hemorrhage in patients with traumatic injury and subsequently reduce mortality, but this effect in results from this study was not statistically significant and suggestions for further investigation are made for clinical trials with a larger population.
