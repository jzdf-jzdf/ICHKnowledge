# Endovascular treatment versus standard medical treatment for basilar artery occlusion: a meta-analysis of randomized controlled trials  

\*Ying Yu, MM,1–3 Yake Lou, MM,4 Rongrong Cui, MM,5 Zhongrong Miao, MD,1,2 Xin Lou, MD,6 and Ning Ma, MD1,2  

Departments of 1Interventional Neuroradiology and 3Neurology, Beijing Tiantan Hospital, Capital Medical University, Beijing, China; 2China National Clinical Research Center for Neurological Diseases, Beijing, China; 4Department of Cardiology, The Second Affiliated Hospital of Chongqing Medical University, Chongqing, China; 5Department of Neurology, Beijing Daxing District People’s Hospital, Beijing, China; and 6Department of Radiology, Chinese People’s Liberation Army (PLA) General Hospital, Beijing, China  

OBJECTIVE  Acute ischemic stroke caused by basilar artery occlusion (BAO) is devastating, but the optimal treatment for patients with BAO remains controversial. In this study, the authors aimed to investigate the safety and efficacy of endovascular treatment (ET) versus standard medical treatment (SMT) in patients with BAO.  

METHODS  The PubMed, Embase, and Cochrane Library databases were searched for randomized controlled trials (RCTs). The primary outcome was good functional outcome, defined as a modified Rankin Scale (mRS) score of 0–3 at 90 days. The secondary efficacy outcome was excellent functional outcome defined as an mRS score of 0–2 at 90 days. The safety outcomes included mortality at 90 days and symptomatic intracranial hemorrhage (sICH). Subgroup analyses were carried out based on race (Asian or non-Asian).  

RESULTS  Four RCTs of 988 patients (556 in the ET group and 432 in the SMT group) were included in this metaanalysis. The proportion of good functional outcome in the ET group was significantly higher than that in the SMT group $(45.1\%$ vs $29.6\%$ ; number needed to treat 6.45; RR 1.54, $95\%$ CI 1.16–2.06; $\uprho=0.003$ , $1^{\circ}=60\%^{\prime}$ ). The subgroup analysis based on race showed a significant difference between Asian and non-Asian race in the primary outcome $(\mathsf{p}=0.03$ , $|2$ $=78.5\%)$ . Patients in the ET group had a higher rate of excellent functional outcome at 90 days than those in the SMT group $34.9\%$ vs $20.6\%$ ; RR 1.83, $95\%$ CI 1.07–3.12; $\mathsf{p}=0.03$ , $1^{2}=80\%)$ . Patients in the ET group had a lower mortality at 90 days $(35.6\%$ vs $45.4\%$ ; RR 0.77, $95\%$ CI 0.66–0.89; $p=0.0007$ , $|2=0\%$ ) but a higher rate of sICH $5.4\%$ vs $0.5\%$ ; RR 8.29, $95\%$ CI 2.49–27.66; $\uprho=0.0006$ , $1^{2}=0\%$ ) than those in the SMT group.  

CONCLUSIONS  ET may improve the functional outcome and reduce mortality at 90 days but increase the risk of sICH compared with SMT in patients with BAO. This conclusion needs to be confirmed in non-Asian populations in future studies.

B asilar artery occlusion (BAO) is one of the causes of acute ischemic stroke (AIS), and patients with AIS due to BAO usually have severe clinical symptoms and rapid deterioration, presenting as quadriplegia and reduced consciousness due to acute ischemia of the cerebellum, brainstem, or thalamus and poor collateral compensation of perforating branches of the basilar artery.1–4 BAO is a mostly fatal type of stroke, with a mortality of up to $40\%-80\%$ , and patients who survive the acute phase often have poor functional recovery.1,5,6 Early reperfusion for preserving the ischemic penumbra is the key to saving lives and improving functional prognosis.4,7 Endovascular treatment (ET) significantly benefits patients with AIS due to large-vessel occlusion in the anterior circulation within 24 hours of onset and has been recommended by American Heart Association/American Stroke Association guidelines.8  

However, in the past 15 years, the exploration process of ET for BAO has been tortuous. The preliminary prospective registry study, the Basilar Artery International Cooperation Study (BASICS), comparing the efficacy among intra-arterial therapy, intravenous thrombolysis (IVT), and antithrombotic treatment for BAO, did not find a definite advantage of intra-arterial therapy in reducing poor outcome (a modified Rankin Scale [mRS] score of 4 or 5) at 1 month.9 Subsequently, the first randomized controlled trial (RCT), the Acute Basilar Artery Occlusion: Endovascular Intervention vs Standard Medical Treatment (BEST) trial, which investigated the safety and efficacy of ET versus standard medical treatment (SMT) for vertebrobasilar system occlusion, was terminated prematurely because of high crossover rates between treatment arms, and there was no difference between the two treatments in the intention-to-treat analysis.10 A later RCT, BASICS, demonstrated that the proportions of favorable functional outcome (mRS scores 0–3) and mortality at 90 days in the ET group were not significantly different from those in the SMT group.11  

The negative results of these two RCTs may be due to the failure to find the optimal patients for ET. Subgroup analysis of BASICS showed that in BAO patients with a National Institutes of Health Stroke Scale (NIHSS) score $>10$ , ET is superior to SMT. Recently, the Basilar Artery Occlusion Chinese Endovascular (BAOCHE) and Endovascular Treatment for Acute Basilar-Artery Occlusion (ATTENTION) trials compared ET with SMT for strictly selected patients with BAO in China and found that ET was associated with better functional outcome and lower mortality at 90 days, which may be a milestone for the treatment of BAO.12,13 Considering that there is no clear recommendation on the optimal treatment for patients with BAO in the current guidelines, it is necessary to perform a meta-analysis of RCTs to compare the safety and efficacy of ET versus SMT in patients with BAO.  

# Methods  

# Search Strategy  

This meta-analysis was presented according to the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses (PRISMA) guidelines and registered in the PROSPERO database (registration no. CRD42022357718, https://www.crd.york.ac.uk/prospero/).14 The keywords “endovascular treatment,” “endovascular therapy,” “thrombectomy,” “embolectomy,” “medical,” “basilar,” “vertebrobasilar,” “posterior circulation,” and “occlusion” were used in the literature search. The PubMed, Embase, and Cochrane Central Register of Controlled Trials (CENTRAL) databases were searched to identify RCTs comparing ET with SMT in patients with BAO until November 2, 2022. We also performed a manual search of relevant references. We only searched citations published in the English language, and the search details are listed in Supplementary Data 1. Inclusion criteria were 1) RCTs comparing ET with SMT in patients with BAO; 2) inclusion of both efficacy outcomes and safety outcomes at 90 days; and 3) sample size $>50$ patients. Animal studies, retrospective studies, and cohort studies were excluded.  

# Data Extraction and Quality Assessment  

The titles and abstracts of potential articles were independently screened by two authors (Y.Y. and Y.L.), and a third author (N.M.) browsed the full texts to resolve the disagreements. The baseline characteristics and outcomes data of included studies were independently extracted by two authors (Y.L. and R.C.). The risk of bias for the included RCTs was evaluated in accordance with the Cochrane Handbook for Systematic Reviews of Interventions.15,16 Two authors (Y.Y. and R.C.) independently reviewed the articles and divided studies into low risk, unclear risk, and high risk through random sequence generation (selection bias), allocation concealment (selection bias), blinding of participants and personnel (performance bias), blinding of outcome assessment (detection bias), incomplete outcome data (attrition bias), selective reporting (reporting bias), and other bias.  

# Outcomes  

The primary outcome was good functional outcome, defined as an mRS score of 0–3 at 90 days. The secondary efficacy outcome was excellent functional outcome, defined as an mRS score of 0–2 at 90 days. The safety outcomes included mortality at 90 days and symptomatic intracranial hemorrhage (sICH).  

# Statistical Analysis  

The risk ratios and $95\%$ confidence intervals were calculated by Mantel-Haenszel analysis to compare the efficacy and safety of ET with those of SMT. I2 statistics and chi-square tests were used to assess heterogeneity across trials, and $\mathrm{I}^{2}>50\%$ was considered substantial heterogeneity.17 The Mantel-Haenszel fixed-effects model was used when $\mathrm{I}^{2}<50\%$ ; otherwise, the Mantel-Haenszel randomeffects model was used. We assessed publication bias using a visual funnel plot and Begg’s test. Influence analysis was undertaken by the sequential exclusion of one trial. In addition, subgroup analysis was carried out based on race (Asian or non-Asian). All analyses were performed using Stata 15.1 software (StataCorp) and Review Manager (RevMan, version 5.3; The Cochrane Collaboration).  

# Results  

Using the aforementioned keywords, we identified 309 potential references in the PubMed (96 studies), Embase (155 studies), and Cochrane (58 studies) databases; 148 duplicates were excluded manually and using EndNote software. The remaining 161 references were screened by titles and abstracts, from which 53 irrelevant topics, 8 case reports, 48 meta-analyses or reviews, and 12 clinical registries were excluded. The remaining 40 articles were assessed through browsing full texts. After that, we excluded 14 one-arm studies, 9 retrospective studies, 5 protocols, 5 registry studies, and 2 conference reports. One RCT was excluded because the sample size was fewer than 50 patients and the ET was performed using intra-arterial urokinase, which is no longer used for ET.18 The remaining 4 RCTs were eligible for our meta-analysis (Fig. 1).

# Characteristics of Eligible Studies  

In total, 4 RCTs of 988 patients were included in our final analysis.10–13 Among the 988 patients, there were 556 patients $(\bar{5}6.3\%)$ in the ET group and 432 patients $(43.7\%)$  

in the SMT group. The sample size in each study ranged from 131 to 340 patients. In one trial, more than $90\%$ of the patients in the ET group received stent retriever–based mechanical thrombectomy,13 and in the other trials, mechanical thrombectomy with stent retriever and thromboaspiration were used.10–12 The baseline characteristics in each study, including demographic, clinical history, lesion features, and stroke mechanisms, are listed in Tables 1 and 2.  

# Primary Outcomes  

The primary outcome was good functional outcome (mRS scores 0–3 at 90 days). All 4 RCTs reported good functional outcome. As shown in Fig. 2, the proportion of patients achieving a good functional outcome in the ET group was significantly higher than that in the SMT group $(45.1\%$ vs $29.6\%$ ; number needed to treat 6.45; RR 1.54, $95\%$ CI 1.16–2.06; $\mathrm{p}=0.003$ , $\mathrm{I}^{2}=60\%$ ). The subgroup analysis based on race showed a significant difference between Asian and non-Asian patients for the primary outcome $\mathrm{\Delta}_{\mathrm{\tilde{P}}}{=}0{.}03$ , $\mathrm{I}^{2}=78.5\%)$ . ET still had an advantage of a good functional outcome at 90 days in Asian populations $45.5\%$ vs $25.5\%$ ; RR 1.76, $95\%$ CI 1.37–2.26; $\bar{\mathrm{p}}<0.001$ ; I2 $=15\%$ , but there was no statistical difference between ET and SMT in non-Asian populations $(44.2\%$ vs $37.7\%$ ; RR 1.17; $95\%$ CI 0.89–1.54; $\overset{\cdot}{\mathrm{p}}=0.26\$ ).  

# Secondary Outcomes  

The secondary efficacy outcome was excellent functional outcome at 90 days. The proportion of patients who had an excellent functional outcome at 90 days was $34.9\%$ among those in the ET group versus $20.6\%$ among those in the SMT group (RR 1.83, $95\%$ CI 1.07–3.12; $\mathrm{p}=\mathrm{\bar{0}}.03,\mathrm{I}^{2}$ $=80\%$ ). The subgroup analysis based on race revealed that there was no significant difference in excellent functional outcome $(\mathrm{p}=0.\dot{0}7,\mathrm{I}^{2}=68.6\%$ ; Fig. 3).  

# Safety Outcomes  

Regarding safety outcomes, patients in the ET group had a lower mortality at 90 days $35.6\%$ vs $45.4\%$ ; RR 0.77, $95\%$ CI $0.66{-}0.89$ ; $\ensuremath{\mathbf{p}}=0.0007$ , $\begin{array}{r}{\mathrm{I}^{2}=0\%.}\end{array}$ ) but a higher rate of sICH $(5.4\%$ vs $0.\bar{5}\%$ ; RR 8.29, $95\%$ CI 2.49–27.66; $\mathsf{p}=$ 0.0006, $\mathrm{I}^{2}=0\%$ ) compared with those in the SMT group (Fig. 4). The subgroup analysis based on race showed no statistical difference in mortality at 90 days $(\mathtt{p}=0.21,\mathtt{I}^{2}=$ $37.2\%)$ or sICH $(\mathrm{p}=0.81,\mathrm{I}^{2}=0\dot{\%})$ (Fig. 4).

# Publication Bias and Quality Assessment  

Because of the limited number of RCTs included, it is difficult to assess the publication bias using funnel plots (Supplementary Data 2). Considering that blinding to treatment type was quite difficult to achieve, all RCTs had inevitable performance bias due to the difficulty of blinding to treatment type. We assessed the risk of other bias as high in the BEST trial because of early termination.10 The details of quality assessment are shown in Fig. 5.  

# Influence Analysis  

We performed influence analysis by subsequently omitting one study at a time and found that our results were relatively robust (Supplementary Data 3).  

# Discussion  

The present meta-analysis based on the existing RCTs aimed at investigating the efficacy and safety of ET versus SMT in patients with BAO found that ET can significantly improve the functional prognosis and reduce the risk of death at 90 days, although the risk of sICH is increased for

ET patients in comparison with SMT patients with BAO. However, subgroup analysis suggested that the results of good functional outcome at 90 days were inconsistent between Asian and non-Asian populations.  

The primary finding of this study was that ET was superior at improving functional outcomes at 90 days in BAO patients in terms of good and excellent functional outcomes. The reason for this conclusion in the present meta-analysis may be because of the positive results of the recently published RCTs from China.12,13 The previous two RCTs (BEST and BASICS) comparing ET within 8 hours and 6 hours, respectively, versus SMT for patients with BAO found the same treatment effect.10,11 However, these two trials may have potential imperfections, such as high crossover rates between treatment arms, slow enrollment, and major protocol revisions. A high-quality national registry study (Acute Basilar Artery Occlusion Study [BASILAR]) in China reflecting the real-world situation of patients with BAO undergoing ET found that ET was beneficial in improving the prognosis of patients within 24 hours.18 Drawing on previous experience, the recent ATTENTION and BAOCHE trials screened highrisk patients, excluded patients who were not suitable for ET, and rigorously followed the study protocol,12,13 ultimately confirming the findings in the per-protocol and as-treated analyses of the BEST.10 A registry study of 156 patients in Japan demonstrated that patients in the severe group (NIHSS scores $\geq10$ ) benefited from ET compared with SMT, but not those in the mild group (NIHSS scores $<10\AA,$ ,20 which was in line with the best subgroup of patients for ET in the BASICS trial and another German registry study.11,21 The ATTENTION trial only included patients with NIHSS scores $>10$ , providing evidence that ET was more effective than SMT in patients with severe BAO.12  

In addition, both the ATTENTION and BAOCHE trials screened patients using the pc-ASPECTS (posterior circulation Alberta Stroke Program Early Computed Tomography Score), which enabled a more accurate evaluation of posterior circulation infarction.12,13,22 Different from previous RCTs, the ATTENTION and BAOCHE trials extended the time windows to 12 hours and 6–24 hours, respectively, which may have contributed to the low rate of IVT in the included patients. The effect of direct ET versus ET with IVT remains unclear in BAO patients. A recent cohort study showed that ET with IVT was associated with better prognosis compared with direct ET, contrary to the results of the subgroup analysis of the BASICS.11,23 Large RCTs are needed to explore the superiority of direct ET versus ET with IVT. In this meta-analysis, substantial heterogeneity was observed in the results of good functional outcome and excellent functional outcome, which may also be explained by the different inclusion and exclusion criteria of the 4 RCTs.

It is worth noting that rates of good functional outcome in the SMT groups from the ATTENTION trial $(23\%)$ and BAOCHE trial $(24\%)$ were lower than those in the BEST trial $(32\%)$ and BASICS trial $(38\%)$ , possibly because of higher-risk patients enrolled in the former two studies from China.10–13 In addition, the proportion of BAO patients with intracranial atherosclerotic stenosis is higher in China, which may involve a more complex operation and more variable application of devices in the procedure.24 The population in 3 of the 4 trials included in our study was Asian, and the subgroup analysis based on race demonstrated that Asian populations with BAO are more likely to benefit from ET with a good functional outcome at 90 days compared with non-Asian populations. The underlying reason may be the higher proportion of BAO patients with intracranial large-artery atherosclerosis in the Asian population. Moreover, the limited RCTs for non-Asian patients may obscure this benefit of ET for BAO patients. Further studies are needed to investigate whether ET can be further applied to non-Asian populations with BAO.  

According to the data from a meta-analysis of individual patients from 5 RCTs of anterior circulation largevessel occlusion, the mortality of patients with BAO $(35.6\%$ in the ET group and $45.4\%$ in the SMT group in our meta-analysis) is much higher than that of patients with acute large-artery occlusion in the anterior circulation ( $15.3\%$ in the intervention population and $18.9\%$ in the control population).25 From our study, the 4 RCTs had the same trend in mortality, with no significant heterogeneity among trials. It suggested that the benefit of ET in reducing the risk of death compared with SMT is universal, which is in agreement with the previous metaanalysis of both observational studies and RCTs.26 Moreover, the benefit could be extended through more rigorous screening of patients. From another side of safety, one key to ensuring the safety of ET for patients with BAO is to reduce the incidence of operative complications. With the improvement in medical care in recent years, the rate of sICH after ET in the 4 RCTs $(4\%-5.9\%)$ decreased compared with that in the BASICS registry study $(14\%)$ in 2009.9 The incidence of sICH in BAO patients undergoing ET is higher than in those who received SMT $5.\dot{4}\%$ vs $0.5\%$ ), which is clearly different from the data derived from anterior circulation large-vessel occlusions ( $4.4\%$ vs $4.3\%$ , respectively).25 This may imply that patients with BAO may have a higher risk of sICH after ET than patients with anterior circulation large-artery occlusions. Strict preprocedural evaluation and actively promoting the homogenization of the thrombectomy level in different hospitals are particularly crucial to ensure the safety by reducing sICH in the future.

vantages in the original research. First, the small number of included trials may limit our ability to test for publication bias. Second, this meta-analysis is based on a study level instead of a patient level because of a lack of raw data, which hampered further subgroup analysis based on baseline characteristics. Third, only 4 RCTs were included in this study due to the limited number of RCTs published in this area, so more subgroup analyses could not be performed. Fourth, our results may have selection bias, because only articles published in the English language were included. Finally, the BEST trial was terminated early because of the high crossover rates between treatment arms, which may affect the quality of this trial, but the influence analysis with exclusion of the BEST trial suggested robust overall results.  

# Conclusions  

According to the available data, ET may improve the functional outcome and reduce mortality at 90 days but might also increase the risk of sICH compared with SMT in patients with BAO. This conclusion needs to be confirmed in non-Asian populations in future studies.
