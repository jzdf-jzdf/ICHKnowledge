# ABSTRACT  

Introduction: Chronic subdural hematoma (cSDH) is a prevalent neurosurgical condition with an increasing incidence due to the rising life expectancy and the widespread use of anticoagulant and antiplatelet therapies. Insights into the inflammatory origins of cSDH led to the exploration of Middle Meningeal Artery (MMA) embolization as a therapeutic strategy. In recent years the endovascular treatment of MMA has gained momentum. Herein we present the initial experience of a dual trained neurovascular unit implementing this therapeutic technique.  

Methods: This single-center, prospective pilot study aimed to evaluate the feasibility, safety, and efficacy of MMA embolization in the treatment of cSDH. Following ethical approval and informed consent, demographic, clinical, and radiological data were collected. Patients requiring emergent surgical treatment were excluded. The study focused on assessing clinical outcomes, including the Modified Rankin Score (mRS) and volumetric analysis of cSDH, before and after embolization.  

Results: Fifteen patients underwent MMA embolization, with a predominance of males $(80\%)$ and a mean age of 72.4 years. The most common presenting symptom was headache $(53.3\%)$ The average hospital stay was 3.9 days. Various embolization techniques were employed, with DMSO-EVOH being the most frequent. All procedures were successfully conducted without complications. Although not statistically significant, trends suggested better outcomes in patients with homogeneous cSDH on the CT scan, displaying the cotton wool sign on angiography and treated with EVOH-DMSO.  

Conclusion: MMA embolization for cSDH demonstrates promise as a safe and effective treatment, potentially reducing the need for surgical intervention and recurrence rates. This study lays the groundwork for a larger, randomized controlled trial which protocol is herein presented.

# Introduction  

Chronic subdural hematoma (cSDH) is a common neurosurgical entity, with an incidence of 1-5 cases per 100,000 people.1 This incidence increases with age, being two to three folds higher in patients over 65 years.1 The increase in life expectancy added to the emergence of cardiovascular diseases that require antiplatelet or anticoagulant treatments as prophylaxis for subsequent events, suggest that the incidence of cSDH will continue to rise over the next decades. Conventional treatment might consist of a conservative treatment based on analgesics, corticosteroids and statins; or a surgical management based on hematoma evacuation via either one or two burr holes, twist drill or craniotomy; irrigation and subdural or subperiostial drainage.2,3 The clinical course is often benign but reported recurrence rates range from 2 to $37\%$ ,although the actual need for retreatment is certainly lower than the latter figure.4  

Contrary to the traditional pathophysiological explanation of cSDH being caused by trauma and subsequent tearing of bridging veins, Inglis proposed in 1946 an underlying inflammatory mechanism. He suggested that the cells lining the dura mater could act as macrophages, which then transform into fibroblasts, in response to trauma or delamination of dural layers.6 These fibroblasts would create a membrane where new, defective vessels prone to exudation and bleeding may develop, leading to cSDH.6 The foundation of this theory was later extended and demonstrated7,8 improving the understanding of effective medical management and paving the way for new therapeutic strategies such as Middle Meningeal Artery (MMA) embolization. According to this inflammatory theory the extension and recurrence of cSDH could be prevented by blocking the leakage from the capillaries of the neomembrane via embolization of theMMA.  

Pioneered by Mandai et al. MMA embolization for the treatment of cSDH has gained momentum in the last years.9 Actually, the number of publications reporting successful results has escalated while current ongoing clinical trials suggest promising preliminary results.4,10 However, it is yet unclear which subgroup of patients might benefit more from this treatment; whether MMA embolization should be offered as a stand-alone treatment or an adjuvant therapy for the novo or recurrent cSDH; and which embolization material or technique might be optimal.  

Herein, we present our early experience in the implementation of MMA embolization for cSDH in the context of a dual trained neurosurgical department with vast experience in the medical and surgical management of cSDH. Based on the results of this pilot study, a study protocol for a randomized controlled trial is proposed.  

# Methods  

This is a single-center, prospective, pilot study, designed to assess the feasibility, optimal technique, safety and benefits of MMA embolization. The study protocol was approved by the Institutional Ethical Board and all patients signed an informed consent.  

# Pilot study protocol  

Demographic, clinical and radiological variables were prospectively collected. Modified Rankin Score (mRS) was assessed on admission and at first follow up (FU).11 cSDH were classified following Nakaguchi's classification according to their appearance on the initial CT scan.12 Classification was independently performed by SGG and IA and in case of disagreement a consensuswasmade.  

Patients diagnosed with cSDH were initially screened for inclusion. Patients with cSDH causing loss of consciousness or severe focal neurological deficits were surgically treated and excluded from this study. Patients with bilateral cSDH were not included in the analysis. Other exclusion criteria included: cSDH developing with any underlying condition (vascular lesions, brain tumor, spontaneous intracranial hypotension, etc.); Known contraindication for endovascular access, anesthetics, contrast or embolic agents; and patients with short life expectancy or treatment. No other exclusion criteria were considered. Therefore, patients with mild symptoms not requiring emergent treatment were considered for MMA embolization.  

Patients' antiplatelet or anticoagulant medications were recorded. As part of general measures for patients with cSDH antiplatelet therapy was reduced to AAS ${100}\mathrm{mg}$ and anticoagulants were suspended in cases of Atrial fibrillation or switched to prophylactic Low Molecular Weight Heparin (Enoxaparin 4oUI/day adjusted to renal function) in cases of mechanical heart valves or any other condition with high risk of thromboembolic events. If not contraindicated, patientsreceived $6{-}8\mathrm{mg}$ dexamethasone twice a day until the endovascular procedure was performed, which was scheduled in less than 2 days from the date of admission. If an initial clear clinical improvement was observed corticosteroids were suspended and if not, a 2-week tapering course of oral dexamethasonewascontinued.  

Following MMA embolization patients demonstrating clinical improvement underwent a non-contrasts CT (NCCT) scan 2-4 weeks after the procedure. Those patients who didn't show anyearly $(<48\mathrm{h})$ clinical improvement underwent NCCT scan and were surgically treated if the cSDH showed no changes or signs of progression (Fig. 1).  

Volumetric analysis of cSDH was performed by segmenting preoperative and subsequent FU NCCT scans. The segmentation process utilized a semi-automated technique based on an edge-detection snake algorithm as implemented in ITKSNAP (version 4.0).13 Due to the variable timing of NCCT scan control, the temporal evolution of cSDH was analyzed by calculating cSDH volume resorption rate per week.  

Safety was assessed in terms of either local (Groin Hematoma related to the access, Puncture site infection, etc.) or systemic complications (Ischemic or hemorrhagic stroke, Air Embolism, Contrast related allergic reaction, Contrast induced Encephalopathy, Cranial nerve injury, etc.) that could be potentially imputed to the endovascular procedure.  

# Technique  

All procedures were performed using femoral access with a Micropuncture standard Seldinger technique. An $11\mathrm{cm}6$ French femoral sheath was utilized. Generally, 3000 IU of heparin is administered following femoral access. To gain access to the external carotid artery, a diagnostic 4F Simmons-2 or Bentson-1 catheter was employed over a $260\mathrm{cm}$ straight hydrophilic guidewire. The diagnostic catheter was then exchanged fora $95\mathrm{-cm}$ ，0.071-inch catheter (Benchmark, Penumbra, Alameda, CA). Diagnostic series were run to assess the presence of the cotton-wool-like staining of the distal MMA and to determine the optimal device and technique for MMA embolization. The MMA was selectively catheterized with a microcatheter as distally as possible within the MMA. We have tested different liquid embolic agents, including EVOH-DMSO (Squid 12 and 15, Balt, France), n-butyl cyanoacrylate (NBCA) mixed with lipiodol (30:70), and suspended PVA particles. Different microcatheters were used dependingon the liquid embolic agentand the size oftheMMA (Sonic 1.2. Balt; Magic 1.2; Vasco-10; France). The catheter was advanced as far as possible to avoid dangerous anastomoses from internal to external carotid arteries, and gentle embolization was performed. A control series was performed once the embolization was deemed complete.

# Statistics  

Data collection was performed using Microsoft Excel (Microsoft, Redmond, WA, USA; version 16.16.4). Statistical analyses were conducted in Python (version 3.7) using Pandas library, and results were plotted using Matplotlib library. The distribution of quantitative variables across groups was examined using Student's t-test, ANOVA, Wilcoxon rank-sum test, Mann-Whitney U test, or Kruskal-Wallis test, depending on the outcomes of normality tests and the number of categories in qualitative variables. A statistical significance threshold was established at an alpha error of $5\%$  

# Results  

Between January and December 2023, 49 patients were diagnosed with a cSDH of which 15 were surgically evacuated with craniostomy and drainage, 17 were conservatively managed and 17 patients were treated endovascularly. Two of these patients had bilateral cSDH, however, only one side could be successfully embolized being the opposite sides treated conservatively in one case and surgically in the other case. These two patients have been excluded from the analysis. The analyzed sample was composed by $80\%$ of males with a mean age of 72.4 ( $\mathbf{\Psi}_{[\mathrm{QR}=21}^{\prime}\$ [61-82]). Headache was the most common symptom at diagnosis $(53.3\%)$ ， $33\%$ presented some sort of neurological deficit and $20\%$ of patients presented with gait imbalance. $13\%$ of the included cases were a recurrence after a prior surgical procedure. The average stay for embolized patients was 3.9 days ( $\mathbf{\mathrm{[0R=2}}$ [2-4]).  

Patients were treated with different embolization techniques, being DMSO-EVOH the most frequent embolic agent employed $(66.7\%)$ .In 9 patients the cotton wool sign was clearly visible on the initial angiographical series. Mean volume and Midline shift on the initial CT Scan were respectively  

$122,455\mathrm{mm}^{3}$ (SD: 41,320, $95\%\mathrm{CI}$ ：101,544.7-143,365.9) and$7.4\mathrm{mm}$ (SD: $2.9595\%\mathrm{CI}$ : 6-8.9).  

All procedures were successfully conducted (Figs. 2 and 3). There were no local nor systemic complications derived from the procedure. Apart from the excluded case above mentioned, none of the endovascular treated patients needed a surgical rescue. CSH appearance on the initial CT scan, CSH initial volume and volumetric evolution of patients are summarized in Tables 1 and 2 and Fig. 4.  

CSH resorption weekly rates were slightly higher for patients with a positive cotton wool sign; for those in which EVOH-DMSO was used as liquid embolic material and in those with an homogeneuous CSH pattern on the initial CT scan. However, these differences were no statistically significant (Table 3).  

# Proposal for a randomized controlled trial  

# Ouerview  

Unicentric, randomized, prospective, non-inferiority trial of embolization of MMA versus medical management for cSDH with the primary objective to evaluate the benefits of endovascular treatment of cSDH in addition to medical therapy in a cohort of patients with mild to moderate symptoms. Eligible participants will be allocated to either 1) endovascular MMA embolization in addition to medical management or 2) medical management alone.  

# Participant selection  

All patients referred for cSDH evaluation to the neurosurgical department of the aforementioned center will be screened. Each patient will undergo a standard clinical examination and a brain CT scan. Patients who have clinical and imaging findings consistent with a diagnosis of symptomatic cSDH, deemed to benefit from hospitalization and medical therapy, and not requiring emergent surgical treatment will be included.The inclusion and exclusion criteria have been detailed in themethods section.

# Collected data  

Clinical data will be collected preoperatively, intraoperatively andwithin $48\mathrm{h}$ postoperatively, 2 weeks, at 3 months and 6 months. Images will be acquired $^{48\mathrm{h}}$ after embolization in the interventional arm and for all patients at 2 weeks, 3 and 6 months after discharge. All patient clinical information and radiological images are securely stored and researchers are required to sign a confidentiality agreement.  

# Management  

Patients in the interventional arm (IA) (Medical Therap $\mathsf{\Pi}^{\mathsf{N}+\mathrm{MMA}}$ embolization) will receive the same initial medical treatment as patients in the non-interventional arm (NIA). The initial medical treatment consists of conventional analgesics, continuation of statins if previously prescribed, and $8\mathrm{mg}$ Dexamethasone twice a day. Iatrogenic anticoagulation and antiplatelet therapy will be managed as described in the methods section. In the IA, corticosteroids will be withdrawn following successful embolization of the MMA, while in the NIA group, Dexamethasone will be tapered down over the following two weeks. The endovascular technique has been previously described. Based on the results of the pilot study, MMA embolization will be performed under general anesthesia via femoral access, using EVOH-DMSO as the liquid embolic. Any changes to the embolic agent used will be disclosed. The embolization technique may include doublelumen catheter balloons.  

# Blinding  

Blinding is not possible due to the nature of treatment. However, the clinical outcome will be evaluated by a blinded assessor.  

# Cross-over  

Failed MMA embolization will result on patient's cross-over from IA to NIA. Patients in either arm who suffer worsening of their symptoms at any point of the FU will be considered for surgical evacuation and will not be considered cross-overs.

Primaryoutcome   
The primary outcome is the rate of recurrence of ipsilateral cSDH within 6 months.  

# Secondary outcome  

The current study protocol is not powered to compare secondary outcome measures and will be considered exploratory: mRS at 6 months; Mortality at 6 months; cSDH volume reduction at 2 weeks and 3 months; and Hospital length of stay.  

Information regarding Informed consent, Sample size calculation and Statistical analysis is included as Supplementary materialB.  

# Discussion  

In this article, we detail the inaugural experience of a neurovascular unit with dual training in the endovascular management of cSDH. The findings underscore the safety and efficacy of the procedure, as MMA embolization was successfully performed in all intended patients without any complications related to the procedure, yielding positive clinical outcomes. The limited sample size precluded the identification of prognostic factors associated to the success of the treatment. However, we have examined clinical and radiological features that could potentially influence clinical outcomes. A comprehensive methodological analysis of each case is presented herein, aiming to delineate future research directions that may eventually pinpoint the patients who would derive the most benefit from this treatment modality. In addition a protocol for a randomized controlled trial comparing medical management to medical management and MMA embolization is herein suggested.  

Since its introduction over two decades ago, the endovascular treatment of cSDH has experienced a significant increase in its application in recent years.9 Numerous studies have documented its effectiveness in series of patients, and the initial outcomes of ongoing trials underscore the safety and efficacy of this treatment.4,10,14 A primary advantage of MMA embolization is the reduction in cSDH recurrence, which has been reported to be as high as $37\%$ 4,14Although this percentage may indicate the presence of postoperative cSDH remnants, the actual number of these cases requiring treatment for such recurrences is likely substantially lower, especially when intraoperative irrigation and adequate subdural drainage systems are implemented.3 Consequently, while endovascular treatment of cSDH holds a valuable position within the range of therapeutic options, a considerable number of patients will still benefit from conservative and surgical management.

Over the last five years numerous trials have been designed to appraise the efficiency of MMA embolization for cSDH.19-36 These trials vary in the number of arms; patient's profile, distinguishing in some of them between symptomatic or asymptomatic patients; public or private promotion of the study; implemented embolic agent; and the indication of MMA embolization either as a stand-alone therapy or as an adjunctive treatment to surgery. A common exclusion criterion amongst the majority of these trials is the presence of bilateral cSDH and sustain, as primary endpoint, the rate of symptomatic recurrence. The results of three of these randomized, prospective trials were recently unveiled at the International Stroke Conference 2024 meeting.10,23,24 Collectively, these studies corroborate a substantial decrement in recurrence rates of cSDH in those arms including MMA embolization.WhileinEMBOLISE and MAGIC-MT MMA embolization benefited both patients managed conservatively or with surgery, the squid trial(STEM) demonstrated a statistically significant benefit when MMA embolization was exclusively used as a standalone therapy.10,23,24,37 In addition in MAGIC-MT trial a significant reduction in the rate of serious adverse events within three months was observed in the group receiving MMA embolization.10 The outcomes of ongoing trials and published clinical series, like this one, will ultimately establish the true efficacy of MMA embolization. Furthermore, these investigational efforts will enable to define optimal technical nuances and patient's profile. Our proposed randomized controlled trial stands out from the existing ones in this aims to evaluate the efficacy of MMA embolization in a particular subset of patients with mild symptoms who cannot be conservatively managed.  

Endovascular therapy has broadened its therapeutic horizons beyond the treatment of hemorrhagic and ischemic neurovascular conditions. The consolidation of endovascular treatment for cSDH in recent years has been accompanied by the emergence of new potential applications, the efficacy of which remains to be fully validated. These include treatments for hydrocephalus, cerebrospinal fluid-venous fistulas, idiopathic intracranial hypertension, and cerebral neurostimulation, amongst others.14,38-40 The management of cSDH patients is a common neurosurgical practice worldwide. However, the surgical or endovascular resolution of CSH represents one mere aspect of the comprehensive care required by affected individuals. The invaluable insights gained from the care of elderly, multimorbid, and frequently dependent patients should enrich and give qualitative weight to therapeutic decision-making and not be undermined in favor of a new technique often performed by physicians with limited experience in the holistic management of these patients. For this reason, and given the existence of a future of opportunity and challenges, we advocate for the inclusion of this technique within the neurosurgical armamentarium.  

# Limitations  

The present article harbors some limitations that need to be acknowledged. First, the investigation was conducted in a relatively small cohort. The primary objective was to document the preliminary outcomes of a neurosurgical unit integrating a novel technique into its standard protocol, evaluating its safety, feasibility, and potential impact on patient care, as well as its implications for future therapeutic strategies. Second, the suggested variable of resorption rate per week assumes a linear evolution of volume over time which has never been proven. This variable was created to compare the response to the treatment despite different follow up times. Third, the study protocol did not control various factors that caninfluence the evolution and outcome of these patients, including pre- and post-operative corticosteroid usage; patients on antiplatelet, anticoagulant, or statin treatments; and the inconsistent timing of follow-up CT imaging. Nonetheless, our intention is to build upon this preliminary work by undertaking a larger-scale study designed to meticulously control for these factors, thereby providing a more comprehensive understanding of the clinical implications. Despite these constraints, we believe our findings accurately depict the reality that many neurosurgical services introducing this technique will encounter in the initial phases of its implementation.  

# Conclusion  

The endovascular treatment of cSDH is a safe and effective neurosurgical procedure. Given the influence of clinical and radiological variables on its success,further research is imperative to elucidate these factors and delineate the patient profile that could benefit most from this therapeutic modality. The herein presented preliminary experience lay the foundation for a novel randomized controlled trial targeting patients with mild symptoms.
