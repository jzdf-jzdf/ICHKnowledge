# Validation of a Smartphone Application in the Evaluation and Treatment of Acute Stroke in a Comprehensive Stroke Center  

Sheila C.O. Martins, MD, PhD; Gustavo Weiss, MD; Andrea G. Almeida, MD, PhD; Rosane Brondani, MD, PhD; Leonardo A. Carbonera, MD; Ana Claudia de Souza, MD; Magda Carla O. Martins, PsyD; Guilherme Nasi, MD; Luiz A. Nasi, MD, PhD; Carlos Batista, MD; Fabrício B. Sousa, MD, MsC; Márcio A.B.C. Rockenbach, MD; Felipe M. Gonçalves, MD, MBA; Leonardo M. Vedolin, MD, PhD; Raul G. Nogueira, MD  

Background and Purpose—The increasing demand and shortage of experts to evaluate and treat acute stroke patients has led to the development of remote communication tools to aid stroke management. We aimed to evaluate the JOIN App smartphone system—a low-cost tool for rapid clinical and neuroimaging data sharing to expedite decision-making in stroke.  

Methods—Consecutive acute ischemic stroke patients treated at a University Hospital in Brazil from December 2014 to December 2015 were evaluated. The analysis included all patients presenting with acute ischemic stroke who underwent initial evaluation by neurology residents followed by JOIN teleconsultation with a stroke neurologist on call for management decisions. An expert panel of stroke neurologists and neuroradiologists revised all cases using a standard Picture Archiving and Communication System imaging workstation within 24 hours and analyzed the decision made with remote assistance during the emergency setting.  

Results—A total of 720 stroke codes were evaluated with 442 acute ischemic stroke qualifying. Seventy-eight $(18\%)$ patients were treated with intravenous thrombolysis. The main reasons for tPA (tissue-type plasminogen activator) exclusion were symptom onset ${>}4.5$ hours $(\mathrm{n}{=}295$ ; $67\%$ ) and hypodense middle cerebral artery territory area ${>}1/3$ $\mathrm{\tilde{n}}{=}31$ ; $7\%$ ). The agreement rates between Picture Archiving and Communication System versus JOIN-based thrombolysis decisions were $100\%$ for the stroke (unblinded) and $99.3\%$ for the neuroradiologist (blinded) experts. The use of the application resulted in a significant reduction in the door-to-needle times across the pre- versus postimplementation periods (median, 90 [interquartile range, 75–106] versus 63 [interquartile range, 61–117] minutes; $P{=}0.03$ ). The rates of 90-day excellent outcomes (modified Rankin Scale, 0–1) were $51.3\%$ ; 90-day mortality, $2.6\%$ ; and symptomatic intracranial hemorrhage, $3.8\%$ .  

Conclusions—The JOIN smartphone system allows rapid sharing of clinical and imaging data to facilitate decisions for stroke treatment. The remote application-based decisions seem to be as accurate as the physical presence of stroke experts and might lead to faster times to treatment. This system represents an easily implementable low-cost telemedicine solution for centers that cannot afford the full-time presence of stroke specialists. (Stroke. 2020;51:240-246. DOI: 10.1161/STROKEAHA.119.026727.)  

Key Words: Brazil ◼ decision-making ◼ humans ◼ stroke ◼ telemedicine troke is the leading cause of disability worldwide1 and the first or second cause of death in most low- and middle-income countries.2 Despite the fact that the evidence for intravenous thrombolysis dates back to 1995,3 only a small number of patients receive this treatment, with huge disparities on the use of tPA (tissue-type plasminogen activator) for acute ischemic stroke (AIS) among different countries and even across distinct regions within the same country.4 Such inequality is much higher in low- and middle-income countries because of, among other reasons, the lack of neurologists trained to assist acute stroke patients and the scarce economic resources to pay for neurological care in the emergency department (ED) in a full-time schedule 24 hours a day, 7 days a week.5 One of the main challenges to remote acute stroke care is neuroimaging interpretation as it remains one of the most critical parts in the decision-making process.  

Smartphones are widely available, and the mobile applications facilitate the communication within medical teams. Safety concerns were risen for data protection and privacy, demanding the development of applications that use data encryption technology.6 There is a paucity of data in the medical literature regarding the use of applications for medical communication, consultation, and treatment decision in stroke.7,8 The main aim of the current study was to evaluate the use of a Health Insurance Portability and Accountability Act compliant, low-cost smartphone application system (JOIN App; Allm, Tokyo, Japan) for the rapid sharing of clinical and neuroimaging patient data and measure its impact on the quality and speed of the decision-making process in acute stroke care with a particular focus on the challenges typically encountered during the evaluation for intravenous thrombolysis in the resource-limited reality faced by a comprehensive stroke center within the public healthcare system of a developing country.  

# Methods  

Because of the sensitive nature of the data collected for this study, requests to access the dataset from qualified researchers trained in human subject confidentiality protocols may be sent to the corresponding author.  

# Study Setting and Patient Population  

The study was prospectively conducted at Hospital de Clínicas de Porto Alegre (HCPA) over the course of 1 year (December 2014 to December 2015). HCPA is a university hospital located in southern Brazil that has a well-structured Acute Stroke Unit since $2006^{9}$ and a Comprehensive Stroke Unit since 2012. The stroke center is staffed by a formally trained multidisciplinary stroke team including 4 stroke neurologists who provide oversight and support to the neurology residents. This occurs on site during the day and from home via phone overnight. Notably, as most stroke centers in underresourced public healthcare systems, our institution does not have any web-based browsing systems for imaging sharing; thus, the ability for the attending physicians to review images remotely is limited. The neurology residents provide on-site coverage 24 hours a day, 7 days a week, on a rotating schedule and care for all stroke patients since their arrival to the ED all the way to hospital discharge. They also follow the patients in the outpatient stroke clinic. We systematically collected prospective data for all consecutive stroke patients to monitor the quality of stroke assistance. The diagnosis of AIS was confirmed by initial computed tomography (CT) scan. We followed the protocols of acute stroke care from Brazilian Stroke Society/Brazilian Academy of Neurology,10,11 which are, in general, similar to the American Stroke Association3 and European Stroke Organization.12  

# Description of Study Device  

In December 2014, the JOIN App smartphone system was introduced to the entire stroke team including all neurology residents and stroke neurologists as a solution for safe communication of time-sensitive patient information. The JOIN system is comprised of a virtual server located within the hospital network to where Picture Archiving and Communication System (PACS) and other system modalities can export Digital Imaging and Communications in Medicine (DICOM) images into. The server anonymizes the DICOM and sends it to the Amazon Cloud Server through Virtual Private Network from where the images are streamed to the application installed in the physicians smartphones. This system is available on both the iOS and Android platforms and is fully HIPPA compliant. The application is available to be downloaded for free in the Apple store or Google Play but requires a setup by the hospital information technology administrator to be connected to the service. JOIN has the following key functions (Figure 1): (1) chat function, which allows stroke code activation by simultaneously informing the whole stroke team about a new stroke patient in the ED or inpatient units and allowing for subsequent group case discussions among the team members that are in or outside of the hospital. As the system is web-based, there is no distance barrier to the communication; (2) time stamp function to track, monitor, and collect management times and key stroke protocols; (3) DICOM image viewing function (with the possibility to change the window and to magnify the images including CT, CT angiography, magnetic resonance, and magnetic resonance angiography); (4) interhospital exchange of images and information, allowing consultations for other hospitals; (5) an encrypted 2-way video system that allows for videobased teleconsultation as needed. JOIN App is the only smartphone application available in Brazil for this purpose, and HCPA was the first institution to use the JOIN application outside of Japan.  

# Patient Workflow and Treatment Protocols  

At HCPA, all neurologists and residents must be certified in the use of the National Institutes of Health Stroke Scale (NIHSS). Patients are examined by neurology residents in the ED, where the severity of neurological deficit at admission is assessed using the NIHSS. This occurs nearly simultaneously to the neuroimaging acquisition. All the relevant clinical information and DICOM images are then shared in real time with the whole stroke team via the JOIN system.  

Whenever the stroke neurologist is at the hospital, the cases are discussed with the residents in person and the images are assessed in a PACS workstation. Otherwise, the stroke neurologist reviews the clinical and imaging data over the JOIN App to provide guidance to the residents.  

Before the implementation and use of the JOIN App, the resident used to perform the neurological examination, read the images in the PACS, and contact the supervisor neurologist. Instant messaging application videos (eg, WhatsApp) or even screenshots sent by email were used to send the DICOM images, which limited the windowing and spatial resolution. To guarantee the safety for the patient, most of the times, the neurologist needed to go to the hospital and see the images in the PACS too, delaying the treatment decision. After the application, the resident contacted the neurologist who could then immediately review the imaging through the JOIN App. The neurologist evaluated the images and assisted in the decision-making process. As per the Brazilian11 and American Heart Association/American Stroke Association Guidelines,13 our institutional protocol excludes from intravenous thrombolysis any patients with imaging evidence of hemorrhage or irreversible ischemic changes involving ${>}1/3$ of the middle cerebral artery (MCA) territory.  

# Study Design and Outcome Measures  

The current analysis includes all patients presenting with AIS who underwent initial evaluation by neurology residents followed by JOIN system teleconsultation with the stroke neurologist on call for the purpose of decisions regarding intravenous thrombolysis. As a quality control measure, an expert panel of stroke neurologists and neuroradiologists reviewed all cases using a standard PACS imaging workstation within 24 hours. The neuroradiologists were blinded to the JOIN-based decisions, whereas the stroke neurologists were not. The resident had access to the PACS and could discuss imaging features with the neurologist before and after the implementation of the application. Functional outcome was evaluated at 90 days using the modified Rankin Scale (mRS) assessed in person or by telephone by mRS-certified neurologist blinded to the type of treatment decision-making (JOIN based or in person). An excellent outcome was defined as a dichotomized variable, with a score of 0 to 1 at day 90.  

The primary outcome was the rate of agreement between the JOIN and expert group treatment decisions. The secondary efficacy outcomes included (1) the difference in the median door-to-needle (DTN) times between the 12 months of the study period versus the

# Statistical Analysis  

Comparisons between categorical variables were made using $\chi^{2}$ or Fisher exact tests. Student $t$ test or Mann-Whitney $U$ test were used for continuous variables. The interrater agreement was quantified by the Kappa (κ) statistic. A $P$ of 0.05 was considered significant. Data were analyzed using SPSS for Windows, version 20 (SPSS, Inc, Chicago, IL).  

# Ethical Considerations  

All patients with stroke who were admitted to the ED at the HCPA have signed consent form for data collection and analysis. The study protocol was approved by the local institutional review board.  

# Results  

During the study period, 720 patients with suspected acute stroke were evaluated at the HCPA ED. In $84\%$ of the overall cases, patients were initially assessed by neurology residents via JOIN-based teleconsultation with a stroke neurologist to aid in the treatment decision-making process including the indication for intravenous thrombolysis in AIS, surgery in hemorrhagic strokes, and decompressive craniectomy in malignant MCA infarctions. Of the 720 patients, 45 patients presented with transient ischemic attacks, 50 with hemorrhagic strokes, and 92 with stroke mimics or other cerebrovascular diseases. Ninety-one patients were diagnosed with an AIS but were not assessed via JOIN-based teleconsultation. Thus, 442 patients met the study inclusion criteria (Figure 2). The baseline characteristics of 442 AIS patients with JOIN activation are presented in Table  1. Their mean age was $62{\pm}15\$ years; $50\%$ were woman; in $30\%$ , the etiology of stroke was cardioembolic, and $12\%$ showed hyperdense arterial sign (MCA or basilar artery) on the baseline CT scan, all of them identified using PACS or JOIN.  

The main reasons for exclusion from thrombolytic therapy were the time window ${>}4.5$ hours $(67\%)$ and hypodensity involving ${>}1/3$ of the MCA territory $7\%$ ; Table 2). Seventyeight patients of the 422 AIS received thrombolytic therapy corresponding to $18\%$ of the overall AIS and $53\%$ of those patients arriving ${<}4.5$ hours from symptom onset. Among the 147 patients with AIS who arrived ${<}4.5$ hours, the main cause of exclusion was a large territory infarct on CT $(21\%)$ .  

In terms of the primary end point, the decision to treat patients with tPA based on real-time smartphone system image was not different than the expert analysis of the imaging studies in the workstation. In no case, the decision would have been different after a careful review of images in the workstation by stroke neurologists (unblinded to the JOIN-based decision). Only in 1 of the 147 $(0.7\%)$ AIS patients arriving ${<}4.5$ hours from stroke onset, the neuroradiologist (blinded to the JOINbased decision) would have recommended not to thrombolyse the patient due to the extent of the infarcted area (Table 3).

This represented a significant improvement in the DTN times as compared with the 12 months preceding the use of JOIN (median, 90 minutes [interquartile range, 75– 106]; $P{=}0.03$ ).  

Excellent functional outcomes (90-day mRS, 0–1) occurred in $51\%$ of patients who received tPA. The JOIN-based decision approach showed a great safety profile with only 2 of 78 patients $(2.6\%)$ experiencing SICH. There were only 3 deaths within 90 days $(3.8\%)$ , none of which was related to tPA (1 caused by sepsis, 1 related to the malignant middle cerebral infarction in a patient without recanalization, and 1 related to myocardial infarction during the hospitalization).

# Discussion  

Our study showed an excellent agreement between the remote decision to treat a patient with tPA made through the JOIN App versus expert review using the hospital PACS workstation. In addition, we noticed a dramatic improvement in work flow as manifested by 27-minute reduction in DTN times. Finally, our functional outcomes and safety profile were comparable with the literature.  

A pooled analysis of 9 randomized clinical trials of tPA for AIS $(\mathrm{n}{=}6756)$ ; mean baseline NIHSS, 12)15 showed an excellent functional outcome (90-day mRS, 0–1) in $34\%$ of patients. The rate of SICH was $3.7\%$ (SITS-MOST criteria), and the 90-day mortality was $17.9\%$ .16 In a more recent meta-analysis including 8 randomized trials $scriptstyle\mathrm{n=6}136$ ; mean baseline NIHSS, 11.2),17 $44\%$ of patients treated within 4.5 hours from stroke onset achieved excellent functional outcome at 90 days. The SITS registry (Safe Implementation of Treatment in Stroke) analyzed a total of 29 618 patients (median baseline NIHSS, 9) thrombolysed across 732 hospitals in 40 countries. The rate of SICH was $1.8\%$ . At 90 days, $63\%$ of the patients achieved functional independence (mRS, 0–2) while $11\%$ were dead.18 Our results are within the range of the aforementioned intravenous thrombolysis large datasets. This further supports the safety of the JOIN App–based telecommunication approach in the decision-making process of patients with AIS.  

Our study showed a 27-minute reduction in the DTN time for thrombolysis (from a median of 90 to 63 minutes) with the application use. This has bought our performance to levels comparable to much more advanced and resourceful systems. As an example, the median DTN time for thrombolysis in the SITS Registry was 65 (47–87) and 72 (51–120) minutes in patients treated within 3 hours and between 3 and 4.5 hours from symptom onset, respectively.18 Despite our main focus on the assessment for intravenous thrombolysis, the decision-making process behind the indication for surgery in hemorrhagic stroke, decompressive craniectomy in malignant MCA infarction, and evaluation of stroke mimics was also possible through the application.

Conventional telemedicine for stroke is validated and its use endorsed by many recent guidelines as an alternative for delivering stroke care to areas with limited access to specialists.11,13,19,20 In this model, stroke neurologists can remotely perform real-time evaluation of patients with suspected acute stroke through a high-resolution webcam to perform the neurological evaluation including NIHSS, interview the patient and companion, and assess the neuroimage via an encrypted DICOM viewer. The neurologist can then aid in the decision-making process, including the choice of reperfusion therapy as applicable.21 While telemedicine has been shown to be cost-effective,22–24 issues related to poor infrastructure and low funding continue to limit its more widespread use, particularly in low- and middle-income countries.25 To enable stroke treatment in many of these low-resource countries, physicians often have to accept suboptimal standards for telecommunication including the use of low-quality pictures or videos by email, WhatsApp, Message, or other chat applications. This may compromise patients’ safety, privacy, and clinical outcomes.  

Brazil has ${>}207$ million inhabitants, and the whole population has been granted access to a unified public healthcare system since 1988. Only $25\%$ of the population can afford additional private healthcare insurance.26,27 Stroke was the leading cause of death in Brazil for ${>}20$ years. Fortunately, stroke-related mortality has been decreasing over the last years,28 and as of 2011, stroke has dropped to the second cause of death in our country with a total of 100.512 deaths in 2015.29 The lower socioeconomic classes are the most affected by the inadequate control of risk factors so, not surprisingly, $80\%$ of stroke patients in the country are treated in the Public Healthcare System. Despite the great improvement of stroke treatment in Brazil in recent years, following the publication of the National Stroke Policy of the Ministry of Health in $2012^{20}$ , there are still several barriers to overcome. As in many lowand middle-income countries, one of the most critical obstacles we face relates to the difficulty of access to specialists, both in the private and public healthcare systems.4,25 Conventional telemedicine is currently used in only 24 Brazilian hospitals. Unfortunately, the more widespread use of conventional telemedicine in many underresourced countries is limited by its lack of portability, suboptimal ease to use, and more importantly, high costs related to infrastructural setup and personnel. The mobile smartphone technology has evolved tremendously and, given its widespread availability and relative low costs, has emerged as promising solution for many medical applications. Smartphone are equipped with high-resolution screens and great capacity of rapidly receiving and sending data in an encrypted manner. Instead of physicians going to telemedicine devices, smartphone-based cloud systems have been created to enable practitioners to more promptly access telemedicine tools from anywhere and at any time.6  

There are few articles describing the use of applications in acute stroke, with different focuses, none of them applicable to this study.7,8 To our knowledge, this is the only article comparing the quality of imaging and effect on the decision-making process for thrombolysis. In this setting, the JOIN smartphone application emerged as a creative and highly cost-effective tool to improve stroke assistance at our hospital, which, as many other public hospitals in Brazil and across the globe, struggles with the balance between the demands imposed by high patient volumes and the limited human and financial resources. Our scenario is actually a lot better than many other more common realities, where sometimes there is not even a single neurologist in an entire city. In this setting, mobile teleconsultation tools represent an easily implementable and financially viable solution that may help addressing the large global gap we currently face in terms of stroke care.  

Another advantage of this new strategy is that it enabled us to provide a more dynamic and interactive educational experience to our trainees. It is important to acknowledge that there are some potential limitations to the proposed solution as it is highly dependent on the quality of the internet (velocity and capacity) inside the hospital, to upload the images, and in the smartphones to receive the images.  

Our study has some limitations including the lack of a concomitant control group. However, it is unlikely that potential confounding factors such as residents’ learning curve and access to PACS, staff training, or workflow changes have influenced our results since all these factors were unchanged across the whole study period. Moreover, the analysis was performed in only one comprehensive stroke center that had above average degree of expertise and organization including well-trained emergency and stroke teams with full-time onsite coverage by neurology residents. This obviously limits the generalizability of our results. However, after considering the amount of experience from the physicians/residents that were on site during the actual time the study tool was used and the prior inability of our attending physicians to remotely review the images, one may better understand how this initial experience could be potentially translated to other realities including smaller community hospitals without any neurology support and rural sites. We are currently in the process of testing the application in such settings. Another important limitation is that the neurologists assessing the JOIN-based thrombolysis decisions were not blinded. However, the neuroradiology assessment was completely blinded and, despite that, was largely congruent with the other treatment decisions.  

# Conclusions  

In conclusion, the smartphone application effectively and safely connected the stroke team in and outside the hospital aiding in the treatment of acute stroke, including the decision of thrombolytic treatment. This had a dramatic impact in our work flow resulting in an almost half-hour reduction in DTN times. The system could be used to help young neurologists or physicians without experience to make the decisions in acute stroke with low cost, simple hardware and easy implementation. As for other application platforms, new functions can be easily incorporated. The recent addition of HIPPA-complaint video-consultation and real-time ambulance tracking systems may further optimize our stroke work flow in the near future. Telestroke continues to bring neurovascular expertise to resource-restricted areas with advanced virtual communication techniques, optimizing stroke care. Future research should aim at broadening telestroke technology applications, while improving quality and reducing the delivery-associated cost and resources. Furthermore, the use of other imaging modalities including CT angiography, CT perfusion and Magnetic Resonance imaging should be evaluated. Comprehensive multidisciplinary virtual telestroke centers that cover all aspects of stroke management will hopefully become a growing global reality in the near future.
