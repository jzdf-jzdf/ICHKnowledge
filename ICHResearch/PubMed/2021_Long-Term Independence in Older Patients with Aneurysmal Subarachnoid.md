# ABSTRACT  

Objective: Older patients have a higher risk for poor neurological outcomes following aneurysmal subarachnoid hemorrhage (aSAH). This study compares functional independence in older versus younger patients with aSAH, and endovascular coiling to microsurgical clipping in the older cohort. Methods: Patients enrolled in the Barrow Ruptured Aneurysm Trial (BRAT) with a confirmed aSAH were analyzed. Patients were grouped by age: older $\geq65$ years) or younger (<65 years). The primary outcome analyzed was functional independence at long-term follow-up, defined as Barthel Index (BI) $>80$ at the 6-year follow-up. A second analysis was performed comparing functional independence in older patients treated with endovascular coiling versus microsurgical clipping. Results: Of the 405 patients with aSAH enrolled in the BRAT, 77 $19\%$ were ages 65 years or older and 328 $(81\%)$ were less than 65 years of age. A lower percentage of older versus younger patients were functionally independent $(\mathrm{BI}>80)$ at 6-year follow-up ( $42.0\%$ [29/69] vs $82.2\%$ [217/264]; $\mathtt{p}{<}0.001\$ ). A higher percentage of younger patients $69.7\%$ [184/264]) had good neurological outcomes (modified Rankin Scale score ${<}3$ ) at the 6-year follow-up compared with older patients $31.9\%$ [22/69]) $(\mathtt{p}{}{}{}{}^{\angle0.001}\$ ). A greater percentage of older patients treated with microsurgical clipping $(51.0\%$ [18/47]) versus endovascular coiling $(22.7\%$ [5/22]) had functional independence at the 6-year follow-up $(\mathtt{p}{<}0.04)$ .  

Conclusion: Patients 65 years or older with aSAH are at an increased risk for poor neurological outcomes compared with younger patients. Greater independence was observed in older patients after microsurgical clipping than endovascular coiling at long-term follow-up.

# INTRODUCTION  

Aneurysmal subarachnoid hemorrhage (aSAH) is a debilitating neurological event, with an estimated mortality rate of $30\%$ and a permanent disability rate in $50\%$ of survivors.1-4 Although outcomes have improved with the advent of modern neurological treatments, older patients (65 years of age and older) are particularly at an increased risk for severe neurological deficits. Unlike other vascular lesions, such as cerebral arteriovenous malformations, which carry a risk of poor outcome of less than $30\%$ , aSAHs result in an estimated poor neurological outcome of approximately $75\%$ at long-term follow-up in older patient groups.  

Higher Hunt and Hess (HH) and World Federation of Neurological Surgeons grades are associated with an increased risk of poor outcomes in the older aSAH patients. However, these patients are still treated via microsurgical clipping or endovascular coiling to diminish the rate of re-rupture, which is associated with a mortality rate of about $80\%$ .10 Yet controversy exists regarding which treatment modality (clipping or coiling) is superior for improved long-term outcomes.  

The literature on the outcomes of older patients with aSAH, particularly those who are independent in activities of daily living at long-term follow-up, is largely lacking. In this study, we performed a retrospective analysis to compare long-term functional independence in older $(\ge65\$ years) versus younger patients (<65 years) with aSAH. We also compared functional independence after microsurgical clipping to functional independence after endovascular coiling in the older patients.  

# METHODS  

All patients enrolled with a confirmed aSAH in the Barrow Ruptured Aneurysm Trail (BRAT) (registered at ClinicalTrials.gov [NCT01593267]) were retrospectively analyzed. During the initial enrollment in the BRAT, patients gave informed consent. The study protocol was approved by the St. Joseph’s Hospital and Medical Center Institutional Review Board for Human Research (Phoenix, AZ). The complete BRAT protocol is described in the 1-year follow-up report.11  

Patients were categorized by age for comparison, with older patients defined as $\geq65$ years old and younger patients defined as $<65$ years old. Data collected and analyzed in the BRAT included age, HH grade, Fisher grade, aneurysm size, aneurysm location, aneurysm type, treatment, procedures, hospital days, disposition, and neurological outcome at discharge, 1-year follow-up, and 6-year follow-up. Functional independence at long-term follow-up was defined as a Barthel Index (BI) of $>80$ at 6-year follow-up. Barthel Index scoring was conducted by trained research nurses during the initial study. A second analysis was conducted among older patients with aSAH that compared functional independence and neurological outcomes after endovascular coiling to those after microsurgical clipping. The primary outcome was functional independence at long-term follow-up $\mathbf{\epsilon}(\mathbf{B}\mathbf{I}>80\$ at 6-year follow-up). Modified Rankin Scale (mRS) scores at 6-year follow-up were also compared between the groups, with a mRS $<3$ defined as a good neurological outcome.  

The statistical analysis was performed using SPSS Statistics for Windows, version 26.0 (IBM, Armonk, NY). Cohorts were compared via independent $t$ test and $\chi^{2}$ analyses. A $\mathrm{p}<0.05$ was defined as statistically significant. A univariate analysis for predictors of a poor outcome defined as a BI $\mathord{<}80$ was performed in elderly aSAH at 6-year post-hemorrhage and all variables with a p value $<0.20$ were included in a multivariate stepwise logistic regression analysis.  

# RESULTS  

A total of 405 patients with a confirmed aSAH enrolled in the BRAT were analyzed, with 77 $(19\%)$ patients ages $\geq65$ years (older group) and 328 patients $(81\%)$ ages $<65$ years (younger group). Table 1 compares the demographics and clinical characteristics by age group. The mean (SD) patient age was 71.4 (4.8) years in the older group versus 49.9 (9.3) years in the younger group $(\mathtt{p}<0.001)$ . There was no statistical significance in aneurysm size, Glasgow Coma Scale scores on admission, HH scores, aneurysm location, treatment type, mean hospital stay, or external ventricular drain placement between the two cohorts. The 77 older patients had a higher percentage of Fisher grade IV $(\mathrm{n}=71,92.2\%)$ $(\mathtt{p}<0.001)$ and saccular aneurysms $(\mathrm{n}=73,94.8\%)$ $(\mathtt{p}=0.048)$ compared with the 328 younger patients $\mathrm{\Delta}\mathrm{n}=256$ , $78\%$ ; $\ensuremath{\mathbf{n}}=283$ , $86.3\%$ , respectively). Among the younger patients, a higher percentage was discharged home $(\mathrm{n}=154,47.0\%)$ and a lower percentage was discharged to a skilled nursing facility $(\mathtt{n}=30,9.1\%)$ ) compared with the older cohort ( $\mathbf{\check{n}}=8$ , $10.4\%$ ; ${\bfn}=26$ , $33.8\%$ , respectively) $(\mathsf{p}<0.001)$ ). At discharge, the 1-year follow-up, and the 6-year follow-up, the older patient group had a significantly lower mean BI than the younger group $(\mathtt{p}<0.001)$ .  

As shown in Table 2, a lower percentage of older patients versus younger patients had functional independence $\mathbf{\tilde{B}I}>80\$ ) at discharge $9.7\%$ , 7/72 vs $47.7\%$ , 147/308), 1-year follow-up $(59.7\%$ , 43/72 vs $88.7\%$ , 251/283), and 6-year follow-up $(42.0\%$ , 29/69 vs $82.2\%$ , 217/ 264) (p < 0.001). A higher percentage of patients in the younger cohort had good neurological outcomes (mRS scores $<3$ ) at the 6-year follow-up than in the older cohort $(69.7\%$ , 184/264 vs $31.9\%$ , 22/69) $(\mathtt{p}<0.001)$ .  

No significant difference was found in demographic and clinical characteristics of patients with aSAH in the older group when comparing those undergoing endovascular coiling versus  

microsurgical clipping. Barthel Index mean (SD) score at the 6-year follow-up was significantly higher in microsurgical patients (clipping: 54.6 (47.4); coiling: 30.5 (39.4); $\mathrm{p}=0.04,$ ). The discharge location was also more favorable for microsurgical patients $(\mathsf{p}<0.001)$ ) (Table 3).  

Among the older patients, a higher percentage of patients treated with microsurgical clipping $(51.0\%$ , 24/47) had functional independence $(\mathbf{B}\mathbf{I}>80)$ ) at the 6-year follow-up than those treated with endovascular coiling $(22.7\%$ , 5/22) $(\mathtt{p}<0.04)$ . More than one-third $38.8\%$ , 18/47) of the patients treated with microsurgical clipping had good neurological outcomes $\mathrm{(mRS<3)}$ ) at the 6-year follow-up. However, less than one-fifth ( $18.2\%$ , 4/22) of the patients treated with endovascular coiling had good neurological outcomes at 6-year follow-up $(\mathtt{p}=0.11$ ) (Table 4).  

A univariate analysis was performed (Table 5) for risk factors of a poor outcome (defined as $\mathrm{BI}<80\AA,$ at 6-years post aSAH in elderly patients. Of the variables analyzed, only hypertension (OR 2.1, $95\%$ CI 0.8-5.9, $\mathrm{p}=0.14,$ ) and endovascular coiling (OR 3.5, $95\%$ CI 1.1-12, $\mathrm{p}=0.04,$ ) were found to have p values $<0.20$ and were included in a multivariate stepwise logistic regression analysis (Table 6) for predictors of poor outcome in elderly aSAH patients 6-years posthemorrhage. In the multivariate analysis, only endovascular coiling was found to be predictor of a poor outcome in elderly aSAH at 6 years post-hemorrhage (OR 3.5, $95\%$ CI 1.1-11.2, $\mathrm{p}=0.03,$ .  

# DISCUSSION  

Older age in patients with aSAH is associated with an increased risk of mortality and severe neurological disability.12-16 Reports in the literature show a 5-times greater risk, with an $11\%$ increased risk per year of age for a poor neurological outcome in such patients.12, 16 Similarly, we recently found older age in aSAH patients to be a risk factor for poor neurological outcomes on long-term follow-up. In the present study, older patients $\geq65$ years) were found to have decreased odds of functional independence at discharge, the 1-year follow-up, and the 6-year follow-up. However, there was no significant difference between the older and younger patients for Glasgow Coma Scales scores on admission or HH score. In both older and younger patients, functional independence substantially increased by 40 to 50 percentage points from discharge to the 1-year follow-up. However, from the 1-year follow-up to the 6-year follow-up, the drop in the percentage of patients with functional independence was only 6.5 percentage points in the younger cohort $88.7\%$ to $82.2\%$ ) versus 17.7 percentage points $59.7\%$ to $42.0\%$ ) in the older cohort. These findings not only suggest that older patients are less likely to achieve functional independence, but also that the chances decrease on longer follow-up.  

The initial BRAT study was designed to compare neurological outcomes after microsurgical clipping and endovascular coiling.11, 17, 18 At the 1-year follow-up, endovascular coiling was associated with a decreased rate of a poor neurological outcome $(\mathrm{mRS}>2)$ ); however, no significant difference in neurological outcomes was found between the two treatment modalities on long-term follow-up.11, 17, 18 Nonetheless, controversy remains, as other studies, including the International Subarachnoid Aneurysm Trial (ISAT), have found endovascular coiling to have superior neurological outcomes and survival compared to microsurgical clipping. In 2008, Ryttlefors et al.20 analyzed 278 older patients in ISAT and found coiling to be associated with an increased rate of independence (defined as an mRS of 0-2 in the study) at the 1-year follow-up compared with clipping $60\%$ vs $56\%$ ). In 2016, Bekelis et al. analyzed 3210 elderly patients with aSAH and found no significant difference in outcomes between coiling and clipping. However, our findings suggest that microsurgical clipping is associated with better outcomes than endovascular coiling in older patients with aSAH. A significantly greater percentage of patients with microsurgical clipping had functional independence $\mathbf{\epsilon}^{\mathrm{\tiny~BI}}>80\$ at the 6-year follow-up. Furthermore, although the  

difference was not statistically significant, $38.3\%$ of older patients treated with clipping versus only $18.2\%$ of older patients treated with coiling were found to have $\mathrm{mRS}<3$ at the 6-year follow-up. At discharge and the 1-year follow-up, the patients treated with clipping had a higher percentage of functional independence than the patients treated with coiling. This finding is difficult to explain, but the cerebral atrophy present in many elderly patients may facilitate subarachnoid dissection by widening the sylvian fissures, eliminating retractors, and improving visualization of anatomy. Additional room in the intracranial compartment may increase tolerance to cerebral edema resulting either from the initial subarachnoid hemorrhage or from microsurgical manipulation, especially relative to younger patients without cerebral atrophy. Finally, elderly arteries may be stiffened or dilated by atherosclerosis and therefore lessen susceptible to vasospasm induced by SAH or by microsurgical manipulation. The favorable outcomes observed among patients with microsurgical clipping are important, but the advent of contemporary endovascular techniques for aneurysmal occlusion since the completion of the BRAT study may improve upon the endovascular results reported here. This comparison would require a modern randomized controlled trial to explore this question among elderly patients.  

As previously mentioned, the BRAT was designed to compare neurological outcomes after microsurgical clipping and endovascular coiling, and subgroup analyses of the data may lead to biases. The limitations of the BRAT data include the fact that patients were treated as long as 17 years ago, and microsurgical and endovascular techniques and outcomes have improved. A potential confounder in this study is the high percentage of elderly patients in the coiling cohort who had posterior circulation aneurysms, which are associated with poorer outcomes than anterior circulation aneurysms. Furthermore, coils were used in isolation without the more refined endovascular occlusion strategies that are available today.22 Additionally, the neurosurgeons

performing the microsurgical clipping during the BRAT were considered experts in aneurysmal surgery with vast experience, and their outcomes may not be representative of outcomes seen more generally, which may explain the discrepancy between our findings and those of ISAT.  

# CONCLUSION  

Older patients $(\ge65\$ years old) with aSAH are at an increased risk of poor neurological outcomes on long-term follow-up compared to younger patients ( $\mathrm{\mathit{\Omega}}\langle65\mathrm{\mathit{\Omega}}$ years old). However, a small percentage of older patients become and remain functionally independent after treatment. Greater independence and better neurological outcomes were observed in older patients in the long term after microsurgical clipping than after endovascular coiling in the BRAT. Additional studies comparing modern endovascular techniques with microsurgery for aSAH are warranted given the more refined endovascular techniques currently in use.
