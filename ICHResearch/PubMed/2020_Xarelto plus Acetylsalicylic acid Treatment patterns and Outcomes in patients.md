# Xarelto plus Acetylsalicylic acid: Treatment patterns and Outcomes in patients with Atherosclerosis (XATOA): Rationale and design of a prospective registry study to assess rivaroxaban 2.5 mg twice daily plus aspirin for prevention of atherothrombotic events in coronary artery disease, peripheral artery disease, or both  

Keith A. A. Fox, a Sonia S. Anand, b Victor Aboyans, c Martin R. Cowie, d E. Sebastian Debus, e Uwe Zeymer, Danja Monje, g Kai Vogtländer, h Robert Lawatscheck, g and Alain Gay, g  

Abstract Patients with coronary artery disease (CAD), peripheral artery disease (PAD), or both remain at risk of cardiovascular events (including peripheral ischemic events), even when they receive the current guideline-recommended treatment. The phase III COMPASS trial demonstrated that treatment with rivaroxaban vascular dose $2.5\mathsf{mg}$ twice daily plus aspirin (dual pathway inhibition [DPI] regimen) significantly reduced the risk of major adverse cardiovascular events (including peripheral ischemic events) and increased the risk of major bleeding, but not fatal bleeding or intracranial hemorrhage, versus aspirin alone in patients with CAD, PAD, or both. The results of the COMPASS trial supported the regulatory approval of the DPI regimen in several geographic regions. However, it is unclear whether the patients selected for treatment with the DPI regimen in clinical practice will have a similar risk profile and event rates compared with the COMPASS trial population. The prospective post-approval XATOA registry study aims to assess treatment patterns, as well as ischemic and bleeding outcomes in patients with CAD, PAD, or both, who receive DPI therapy in routine clinical practice. Up to 10,000 patients from at least 400 centers in 22 countries will be enrolled and followed up for a minimum of 12 months, and all treatment will be at the discretion of the prescribing physician. The primary objective of the XATOA study will be to describe early treatment patterns, while ischemic and bleeding outcomes will be described as a secondary objective.  

Trial registration number NCT03746275 (Am Heart J 2020;222:166-73.)  

Cardiovascular (CV) diseases are collectively one of the leading causes of death globally.1 Coronary artery disease (CAD) and peripheral artery disease (PAD) are predominantly caused by atherosclerosis.2,3 Currently, guideline-recommended treatment aims to limit the progression of atherosclerotic disease by targeting modifiable risk factors and to prevent thrombus formation in case of atherosclerotic plaque rupture and atherothromboembolism.4-7 Treatment includes lifestyle modifications such as smoking cessation, regular exercise, healthy diet, and weight management, as well as use of medication such as statins, antihypertensive agents, antidiabetic agents and antithrombotics. Until recently, the main antithrombotic agents recommended to patients with atherosclerotic disease were antiplatelet agents.4-6  

Despite receiving guideline-recommended treatment, however, patients with CAD, PAD, or both remain at risk of CV events (including peripheral ischemic events).4-6,8 For example, the continuing risk of CV events in patients with CV disease receiving antiplatelet therapy was approximately $6\%$ per year in the CAPRIE study and approximately $10\%$ over 3 years in the TRA $2^{\circ}\mathrm{P}.$ -TIMI $50~\mathrm{trial}.$ 10 whereas approximately $13\%$ of patients with symptomatic PAD in the EUCLID trial required lower-limb revascularization over 2.5 years despite receiving antiplatelet therapy.11  

In the phase III COMPASS trial, the risk of major adverse CV events (MACE) and major adverse limb events (MALE) was significantly reduced in all patients with chronic CAD, PAD, or both receiving rivaroxaban $2.5~\mathrm{mg}$ twice daily (bid) plus aspirin compared with aspirin alone.12-14 The treatment effect was consistent across virtually all subgroups, and as expected, the absolute risk reduction varied according to baseline risk. The greatest benefit was seen in patients with the highest baseline ischemic risk, including those with polyvascular disease or those with additional risk factors such as diabetes, renal dysfunction, or chronic heart failure.15 The risk of major bleeding was increased with rivaroxaban $2.5\mathrm{mg}$ bid plus aspirin versus aspirin alone, as would be expected with a regimen of intensified antithrombotic therapy. However, most bleeding events occurred within the first year of treatment, and no increase in severe bleeding (i.e. fatal or critical organ bleeding) was observed.12-14,16 The net clinical benefit of dual pathway inhibition (DPI) therapy, therefore, increased over time. As DPI therapy begins to be used in clinical practice, it is unclear which patients physicians will select for initiation of DPI therapy, at which stage of their disease, and what the event rates are likely to be in a ‘real-world’ population.  

XATOA: rationale and objectives  

New therapies may be used in a broader patient population in clinical practice than that enrolled in the phase III clinical trials, which formed the basis for their regulatory approval, and the regulatory approval may also extend beyond the inclusion and exclusion criteria of the trials. This was the case for the rivaroxaban $2.5~\mathrm{mg}$ bid plus aspirin regimen. The results of the COMPASS trial led to the approval of rivaroxaban $2.5~\mathrm{mg}$ bid plus aspirin in patients with CAD, PAD, or both in geographic regions including the EU, Canada,17,18 and Asia; regulatory approval is also anticipated in other regions. An analysis applying the COMPASS inclusion and exclusion criteria to the REACH registry patient population suggested that approximately half of patients with CAD, PAD, cerebrovascular disease, or at least three atherosclerotic risk factors encountered in clinical practice, are likely to be eligible for treatment with the rivaroxaban $2.5~\mathrm{mg}$ bid plus aspirin regimen as used in the COMPASS trial. However, in some countries, the treatment has been approved for use in a broader patient population than that enrolled in the COMPASS study.18  

Post-approval studies can address this gap by investigating the use of a regimen in a broad patient population in ‘real-world’ clinical practice, thereby complementing the information from phase III studies, where treatment regimens are rigorously compared in a controlled population. A post-approval study can provide information on the ‘real-world’ treatment of patients with CAD, PAD, or both with rivaroxaban $2.5~\mathrm{mg}$ bid plus aspirin, and will be valuable for informing treatment decisions, as well as health technology assessment bodies and payers. The prospective post-approval XATOA registry study was, therefore, initiated to provide insights into global treatment practices and outcomes in patients with CAD, PAD, or both who are treated with rivaroxaban $2.5~\mathrm{mg}$ bid plus aspirin in routine clinical practice.  

The primary objective of the XATOA study is to describe treatment patterns, including clinical characteristics, the use of prior and concomitant therapies, the reasons and decision points for introducing or discontinuing rivaroxaban $2.5~\mathrm{mg}$ bid, as well as planned and actual treatment duration. A secondary objective will be to describe ischemic and bleeding outcomes, and analyses of baseline characteristics after approximately 6 and 12 months of enrollment will allow investigation of treatment patterns as they develop over time. Treatment pathways will be investigated in the XATOA study, but some possibilities might include:  

• Transitioning to DPI therapy at the end of a predetermined period of dual antiplatelet therapy (DAPT) after, for example, acute coronary syndrome (ACS), percutaneous coronary intervention, or percutaneous transluminal angioplasty   
• Transitioning from a post-ACS ‘ATLAS’ regimen of rivaroxaban $2.5\mathrm{mg}$ bid plus DAPT by cessation of the $\mathrm{P}2\mathbf{Y}_{12}$ inhibitor   
• Adding rivaroxaban $2.5~\mathrm{mg}$ bid to aspirin therapy due to worsening symptoms or the diagnosis of new co-morbidities that may increase the risk of CV events   
• Directly initiating rivaroxaban $2.5~\mathrm{mg}$ bid plus aspirin therapy in patients with a new diagnosis of chronic CAD, PAD, or both at high risk of ischemic events   
• Directly initiating rivaroxaban $2.5~\mathrm{mg}$ bid plus aspirin therapy before or after an intervention or surgery for PAD  

# Methods  

# Design  

XATOA is an international, multi-center, prospective, single-arm registry study that will be conducted in 22 countries around the world (Figure 1). The study is designed to reflect local clinical practice, and management choices are at the discretion of the local clinician.

Patient population, enrolment, and follow-up  

Consenting patients aged ${\geq}18$ years with a diagnosis of CAD, PAD, or both receiving rivaroxaban $2.5\mathrm{mg}$ bid plus aspirin within 4 weeks before study enrollment will be included. Screening of consecutive patients will be recommended to reduce selection bias. Patients will be documented in an anonymous log file, and reasons for exclusion will be documented. Only countries with both regulatory approval and clinical use of the rivaroxaban $2.5\mathrm{mg}$ bid plus aspirin regimen for patients with chronic CAD, PAD, or both will be allowed to participate in the study. Patients must meet all the inclusion criteria and none of the exclusion criteria (Table I), and will be able to withdraw from the study at any time and for any reason. The inclusion and exclusion criteria of the COMPASS trial are summarized in Supplementary Table 1 for reference.  

The study aims to enroll 8000–10,000 patients from at least 400 centers in Europe (Belgium, Denmark, France,  

Germany, Italy, Luxembourg, the Netherlands, Norway, Slovakia, Spain, Sweden, and the UK), Canada, Israel, Latin America (Argentina, Brazil, Colombia, and Mexico), Asia (South Korea and Thailand), Russia, and the Middle East (Saudi Arabia). Countries were selected to represent a global patient population where treatment with DPI therapy was available. Study centers were chosen with the aim to represent the patient population in each country. Screening of consecutive patients is encouraged to ensure that the proportion of men and women enrolled, as well as other patient characteristics, is representative of the patient population at each site. In addition, the aim is to enroll a minimum of $25\%$ of patients in the study population based on a diagnosis of PAD, although this is not mandated. Enrollment started in November 2018 and is expected to be completed in May 2020. A total of 3598 patients have been enrolled up to 23 January 2020. The follow-up period will be a minimum of 12 months from enrollment up to an expected maximum of 30 months, and follow-up visits will occur during routine practice but are anticipated at ${\sim}1$ month, 3 months, 6 months, and then at 6-monthly intervals thereafter. Followup is expected to be completed in May 2021 and the results will be reported shortly thereafter.

# Drug administration  

Treatment will be at the discretion of the prescribing physician. This includes duration, withdrawal, and interruption of rivaroxaban $2.5~\mathrm{mg}$ bid plus aspirin therapy. In addition, no other specific therapies will be mandated and patients who discontinue treatment prematurely will not be replaced.  

Data management, quality control, and administrative organization  

All investigators will receive training before initiation of the study. A contract research organization (CRO) developed an electronic data-capturing system that included a standardized electronic case report form for data collection. The CRO will also manage quality control, verification of data collection, data analysis, and data transfer to the sponsor. Medical review of the data will be performed to verify the data from a medical perspective for plausibility, consistency, and completeness, and to identify potential issues that could affect the robustness of the data or study progress.  

For quality review, training status will be assessed through standardized telephone interviews and source data verification will be conducted. Telephone interviews will be conducted in $20\%$ of the sites at an early stage. In a second quality review step, data of at least 125 patients will be reviewed at randomly selected sites when up to $80\%$ of patients have completed the study. The documented data will be compared statistically across study sites to identify any sites with patient data that are significantly different from the other sites, and those that differ the most will be selected for a targeted quality review visit. Source verification of a subset of data in each patient enrolled at these sites will be performed.  

In addition, patient documentation across all study sites will undergo medical review on a regular basis to identify and resolve any medical inconsistencies in the documented data. Finally, to ensure consistent reporting, bleeding and thromboembolic events and all events resulting in death will be centrally adjudicated by an independent external adjudication committee (Supplementary Table 2). Throughout the study, national and international data protection laws and regulations on observational studies will be followed.  

Data collection and analysis of baseline characteristics  

Data will be collected at baseline and during the study. The variables that will be collected include demographics and clinical characteristics, including medical history related to CAD, PAD, or both and the use of other secondary prevention and antithrombotic therapies, as well as data required to analyze the primary and secondary outcomes (Supplementary Table 3). To assess patterns in baseline characteristics and treatment that may develop over time, analyses of baseline characteristics will be performed after approximately 6 and 12 months of enrollment or after 1000 and 5000 patients have been enrolled, whichever comes first.  

# Study outcomes  

The primary outcome will be to describe treatment patterns in patients with CAD, PAD, or both receiving rivaroxaban $2.5~\mathrm{mg}$ bid plus aspirin in routine clinical practice. Secondary outcomes include MACE, MALE, thromboembolic events, hemorrhagic events, and mortality, as well as procedures and hospitalization. The study outcomes are listed in Table II. The survival status of patients who permanently discontinue DPI therapy before the end of the study will be documented by the treating physician within 30 days of the end of the study observation period.  

# Management and reporting of adverse events  

Any adverse events (AEs) and serious AEs (SAEs) occurring up to 30 days after the last dose of rivaroxaban or aspirin will be documented and reported to the sponsor. AEs that are not serious will be documented on the AE report form or in the case report form and the sponsor will be notified of these events within 7 calendar days. SAEs and pregnancies will be documented, and the sponsor will be notified of these events within 1 business day. Bleeding events, ischemic events, and deaths that are collected as AEs will be centrally adjudicated by an independent external adjudication committee, and the outcomes of AEs and SAEs will be followed up and documented.

# Statistical analysis  

The aim of the XATOA study is not to test predefined hypotheses, and any statistical analysis will be descriptive and exploratory in nature. The target sample size was based on feasibility and the ability to obtain useful estimates with adequate precision for the study outcomes. For example, if the incidence proportion of approximately $4\%$ for MACE reported in the COMPASS $\mathbf{trial}^{12}$ is similar to that in clinical practice, a minimum sample size of 8000 patients would yield a $95\%$ confidence interval (CI) of $0.8\%$ , ranging from $3.6\%$ to $4.4\%$ .  

Outcomes will be determined according to country and patient subgroups (Table III) if the sample size is large enough. All patients receiving at least one dose of rivaroxaban $2.5~\mathrm{mg}$ bid plus aspirin will be included in the main analysis population, and only treatmentemergent events (occurring $^{\leq2}$ days after permanent treatment discontinuation) will be considered. Descriptive statistics will be used to report the primary outcome variables. For the secondary outcomes, incidence proportions, cumulative incidence, and incidence rates with corresponding $95\%$ CIs will be reported. Antithrombotic treatment patterns after MALE and the duration of hospitalization for stroke, CV reasons, MALE, or bleeding complications will be analyzed descriptively. Total and pain-free walking ability, assessed using the 6-minute walking distance test or a treadmill test, will be recorded for countries where it is performed as part of routine practice. Walking ability at each visit, as well as the change from baseline to the end of the study, will be evaluated.  

# Discussion  

The findings of phase III clinical trials, which include a carefully selected patient population and follow a strict protocol, may not always be equivalent in a broader patient population encountered in clinical practice. Noninterventional post-authorization studies can complement the results of phase III registration studies on non-vitamin K antagonist oral anticoagulants (NOACs) by providing insight into their applicability to clinical practice and informing health technology assessment bodies and payers. For example, the prospective GARFIELD-AF registry study assessed ‘real-world’ treatment patterns, risk profiles, and outcomes in patients with atrial fibrillation (AF) after the approval of NOACs for stroke prevention in patients with AF.20,21 The results of early analyses of the GARFIELD-AF registry showed that patients were not always treated with NOACs according to the guidelines, which highlighted the need to improve patient selection. Patients with a lower risk of stroke were enrolled in the XANTUS study compared with those in the phase III ROCKET-AF trial population and they experienced correspondingly lower event rates.23 In the prospective ATACS registry study (mandated by the European Medicines Agency), outcomes in patients with ST-elevation myocardial infarction receiving a loading dose of clopidogrel or prasugrel prior to primary percutaneous coronary intervention were consistent with the results of the phase III TRITON-TIMI 38 trial. Trends toward a lower risk of death, infarction, and stroke, and a higher risk of major bleeding were observed in the subgroup of patients younger than 75 years and with a body weight of at least $60~\mathrm{kg}$ receiving prasugrel compared with those receiving clopidogrel. Following the results of the phase III COMPASS trial and subsequent approval of the new treatment regimen, the XATOA study will investigate ‘real-world’ treatment patterns and outcomes in patients with chronic CAD, PAD, or both receiving the recently approved regimen of rivaroxaban 2.5 mg bid plus aspirin for preventing CV events (including peripheral ischemic events).  

The XATOA study has a number of strengths and some limitations. The prospective design of XATOA, with predefined outcomes and events, will increase the accuracy and completeness of the data on treatment patterns and outcomes in a clinically relevant patient population. The study outcomes will be adjudicated independently. Independent adjudication is not frequently included in ‘realworld’ studies; this will ensure consistent and robust event reporting and is an important strength of the study. A robust quality review process will improve the identification and correction of problems with data documentation at the study sites, which will increase the accuracy and completeness of the study data. In addition, the study is designed to include a patient population that is representative of clinical practice in a wide range of countries. Screening of consecutive patients can reduce selection bias, and all patients treated with rivaroxaban $2.5\mathrm{mg}$ bid plus aspirin for chronic CAD, PAD, or both, depending on the countryspecific indication, will therefore be considered for enrollment. Because it is unknown which physicians will primarily be involved in decisions about treatment with rivaroxaban $2.5\mathrm{mg}$ bid plus aspirin, a range of treating physicians will participate in the study (e.g. cardiologists, vascular specialists, internists, elderly care specialists, and general practitioners). The large patient population, which is expected to include a significant proportion of patients with PAD, will provide the robust data needed to provide key insights into treatment practices and outcomes in patients with CV disease receiving rivaroxaban $2.5\mathrm{mg}$ bid plus aspirin in clinical practice. The XATOA study is designed to help address important clinical questions about the use of DPI therapy in a ‘real-world’ setting (Table IV).  

Some limitations are inherent to registry studies. For example, patients are selected for treatment in a non-random way based on clinical criteria, and selection bias for enrollment is, therefore, inherent in such studies. However, the study is designed to reflect clinical practice. Limited data may be available on patient medical history, and patient follow-up, which is based on routine care, may vary in different geographic settings and centers. In addition, treatment or event reporting may differ depending on local guidelines. Finally, although XATOA will be a single-arm cohort study with no comparator, the study design will allow the objectives of the study, which are to describe treatment patterns and outcomes in clinical practice, to be achieved.  

# Conclusion  

The XATOA study will provide valuable insight into the implementation of the rivaroxaban $2.5~\mathrm{mg}$ bid plus aspirin regimen in patients with chronic CAD, PAD, or both in routine clinical practice across a range of geographic regions. The anticipated patient population is expected to be broader than that in the COMPASS trial. Insight into how physicians select patients to treat with the new regimen and manage their long-term treatment, as well as the outcomes in patients receiving DPI therapy in clinical practice, will inform treatment decisions in the future.
