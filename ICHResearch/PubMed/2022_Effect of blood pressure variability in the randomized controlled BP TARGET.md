# Abstract  

Background and purpose: The Blood Pressure Target in Acute Ischemic Stroke to Reduce Hemorrhage After Endovascular Therapy (BP TARGET) trial evaluated whether an intensive systolic blood pressure (SBP) target resulted in reduced rates of intracranial hemorrhage (ICH) after successful endovascular therapy (EVT) but did not assess the effect of blood pressure variability (BPV) on functional outcomes and ICH occurrence. We sought to evaluate this question in the BP TARGET trial.  

Methods: We performed a post hoc analysis of the BP TARGET trial and included patients with at least $50\%$ of blood pressure (BP) recordings during the first $24\mathsf{h}$ after EVT. BPV parameters were SBP and diastolic BP (DBP) coefficient of variation (CV), standard deviation (SD), maximum–­minimum (max–­min), successive variation (SV), and time rate.  

The primary outcome was favorable functional outcome (3-­month modified Rankin Scale between 0 and 2); the secondary outcome was the rate of ICH at $^{24\mathrm{h}}$ .  

Results: We included 290 patients (mean number of BP measures $\cdot=30.4$ , $\mathsf{SD}=8.0\$ . BPV parameters $(\mathsf{SBP}_{\mathsf{SD}},\mathsf{SBP}_{\mathsf{max-min}},\mathsf{SBP}_{\mathsf{CV}})$ were higher in the intensive SBP target group. Only DBP BPV parameters were associated with worse functional outcomes in the unadjusted model $\mathrm{(DBP_{SD}}$ , $\mathsf{DBP}_{\mathsf{max-min}}$ , ${\mathsf{DBP}}_{\mathsf{CV}},$ and $\mathsf{DBP}_{\mathsf{SV}})$ , but not after adjustment. Higher ${\mathsf{SBP}}_{\mathsf{max-min}}$ was associated with worse functional outcomes in Thrombolysis in Cerebral Infarction 2B patients (odds ratio $[\mathsf{OR}]=0.62$ , $95\%$ confidence interval $\left[\mathsf{Cl}\right]=0.38–1.02$ ), but not in patients with complete reperfusion $(\mathsf{OR}=1.27,95\%\mathsf{Cl}=0.80\substack{-2.02,p}$ for heterogeneity $(p_{\mathrm{het}}=0.037\$ ). None of the BPV parameters was associated with ICH, regardless of the randomization group or the reperfusion grade.  

Conclusions: BPV was significantly higher in the intensive SBP target group but was not associated with functional outcome or ICH.

# INTRODUCTION  

Endovascular therapy (EVT) is now the reference treatment for acute ischemic stroke (AIS) consecutive to an anterior large vessel occlusion (LVO) [1]. However, despite reperfusion rates now exceeding $90\%$ at the end of the procedure [2], outcomes at 3 months remain poor, with nearly half of reperfused patients having unfavorable outcome [3]. This illustrates that the new challenge in AIS research is beyond reperfusion and ultimately focuses on ischemia-reperfusion lesions [4]. Recently, a large body of evidence has shed light on the role of blood pressure (BP) management after EVT and its association with intracranial hemorrhage (ICH) and worse functional outcome [5-15]. Several works recently highlighted the association between higher systolic BP (SBP) and diastolic BP (DBP) values after successful reperfusion and higher odds of ICH or disability [8-16]. With this as a background, Blood Pressure Target in Acute Ischemic Stroke to Reduce Hemorrhage After Endovascular Therapy (BP TARGET) was the first randomized controlled trial (RCT) performed in the EVT era; it aimed to evaluate whether an intensive SBP target of 100– $129\mathrm{mm}{\mathsf{Hg}}$ reduced the occurrence of ICH after successful EVT, as compared with a standard care SBP target of $130{-}185\mathrm{mmHg}$ [17]. Interestingly, and in contrast to observational studies, an intensive SBP target did not reduce ICH occurrence compared to the standard SBP control [17]. Of note, recent observational studies have described an association between BP variability (BPV) after EVT and functional outcomes or ICH [6,7,11]. However, the effect of SBP and DBP variability within the first $24\mathsf{h}$ after successful reperfusion on functional outcomes and ICH rates was not addressed in the BP TARGET trial.  

In this context, we aimed to evaluate in a post hoc analysis of the BP TARGET trial the effect of BPV after successful EVT on functional outcomes and ICH rates.  

# METHODS  

# Study population  

We performed a post hoc analysis of the BP TARGET trial [17]. Briefly, BP TARGET was a randomized, multicenter, open-label, blind-assessed clinical trial evaluating the efficacy and safety of an intensive BP-lowering strategy after successful EVT in AlS patients consecutive to an anterior LVO [17]. The BP TARGET trial randomly assigned patients to an intensive SBP target $(100{-}129\mathrm{mm}\mathsf{Hg})$ or a standard SBP target $(130{-}185\mathrm{mm}\mathrm{Hg}$ within $^{1\textrm{h}}$ after randomization [17].  

For this post hoc study, we included patients with at least $50\%$ of BP recordings during the first $^{24\textrm{h}}$ after EVT, to better capture the effect of BPV during this period. The study protocol and consent forms were approved by the Comité de Protection des Personnes Sud Méditerranée IV(N°ID-RCB: 2016-AO1884-47, 14 March 2017).  

# Hemodynamic management  

The hemodynamic management among the included centers has been previously published [17]. Briefly, BP was noninvasively measured with an automatic sphygmomanometer on the arm every $15~\mathsf{min}$ for the first $^{2\textrm{h}}$ , then every $30~\mathsf{min}$ for $^{6\textrm{h}}$ and every hour for the remaining $16~\mathsf{h}$ , as recommended in French stroke units [18]. The treatment goal in the intensive SBP target group was to reduce and maintain SBP from 100 to $129\mathrm{~mmHg}$ within $^{1\textrm{h}}$ after randomization and for $24~\mathsf{h}$ . The SBP target was $130{-}185\mathrm{mmHg}$ within $^{1\textrm{h}}$ and for $^{24\textrm{h}}$ after randomization in the standard SBP target. Nicardipine,administeredbyintravenousinfusion,wasthefirst-line antihypertensive agent recommended, as it is the most commonly used treatment in French stroke units. Nonetheless, investigators were free to choose any antihypertensive medication based on their current practice to achieve SBP targets. Of note, vasopressor therapies were not used to control BP, as they cannot be used in stroke units in France, nor are they currently recommended by European guidelines [19].  

We documented BP using SBP and DBP. We assessed several BP parameters to describe BPV after EVT: SBP and DBP standard deviation (SD), maximum–­minimum (max–­min), coefficient of variation (CV; determined by dividing the SD of the SBP and DBP by the mean of the SBP and DBP, respectively), successive variation (SV; defined by the square root of the average of the squared difference between successive BP measurements), and the rate of BP over time (time rate [TR]; ratio of the sum of the BP measures difference as a function of time divided by the number of measures in mmHg/min), as previously published [6,7,12,20,21].  

# Primary and secondary endpoints  

The primary endpoint was favorable functional outcomes, defined as a modified Rankin scale score between 0 and 2 at 3 months, assessed by neurologists or research nurses blinded to study arm, in telephone contact and/or in-person clinical evaluation.The rate of radiographic ICH seen on brain computed tomography (CT) within $24-36~\mathsf{h}$ after reperfusion was chosen as the secondary endpoint. Although parenchymal hemorrhage types 1 and 2 (PH1, PH2) are commonly associated with unfavorable outcomes at 3 months, hemorrhagic infarction types 1 and 2 (HI1, HI2) were also considered, as the BP TARGET trial evaluated all injuries suggestive of reperfusion lesions [17]. ICH was thus classified as petechial hemorrhage or hematoma according to the Heidelberg and European Cooperative Acute Stroke Study III classifications [22,23]. CT scans were forwarded to the core image analysis center, and senior neuroradiologists, who were unaware of the treatment assignments, clinical findings, and time of image acquisition, centrally determined the presence of ICH.  

# Statistical analysis  

Categorical variables were expressed as frequencies and percentages. Quantitative variables were expressed as mean (SD) or median (interquartile range [IQR]) for nonnormal distribution. The normality of distributions was assessed graphically and by using the Shapiro– Wilk test.  

Twenty-fourhoursofBPVparameters following successful reperfusion were compared between BP TARGET randomization groups (intensive SBP target vs. standard) using Student t-test, and the magnitude of between-group differences was quantified using standardized difference; a standardized difference $>20\%$ was interpreted as a meaningful difference [24].  

We studied the association of each 24-h BPV parameter following successful reperfusion with favorable outcomes and radiographic  

ICH using a mixed logistic regression model including the studied BPV parameter as fixed effect and the center as a random effect. The odds ratios (ORs) of each outcome were derived as effect sizes from logistic models and were expressed per 1-sD increase in $^{24-\mathrm{h}}$ BPV parameters. We assessed the shape of associations using restricted cubic spline function, and no deviation in log-linearity assumption was observed [25]. Associations were further investigated after adjustment for prespecified factors (age, mean of SBP or DBP during $24\mathsf{h}$ of measurement, admission National Institutes of Health Stroke  Scale [NIHSS]  and Diffusion-Weighted Imaging-Alberta Stroke Program Early CT Score[DWI-ASPECTS], and reperfusion grade). Finally, we assessed the heterogeneity in the associations of each BPV parameter with favorable outcomes and the occurrence of radiographic ICH according to the randomization group (intensive SBP target vs. standard group) and the reperfusion grade (modified Thrombolysis in Cerebral Infarction ${\mathrm{[m\mathsf{T}lCl]}}=2\mathsf{B}$ vs. 3) by including the interaction term between the studied BPV parameter and the randomization group or reperfusion grade in the previous multivariate mixed logistic regression model. ORs within each randomization subgroup of the reperfusion subgroups were estimated from this model using linear contrast.  

Statistical testing was done at the two-tailed $\upalpha$ level of 0.05. Data were analyzed using the SAS software package, release 9.4 (SAS Institute).

# BPV and ICH  

In the overall BP TARGET population, none of the BPV parameters was associated with ICH in the unadjusted or adjusted models (Figure 4). We also did not find any heterogeneity in the association between BPV and the occurrence of an ICH according to the specific randomization group (standard vs. intensive SBP target; Figure  5). Overall, we also did not find any heterogeneity in the association between BPV and the occurrence of an ICH according to the specific reperfusion grade $({\mathsf{m}}{\mathsf{T}}{\mathsf{ICI}}=2{\mathsf{B}}$ vs. 3; Figure 6). However, the TR of SBP seemed to be associated with higher odds of ICH occurrence in patients with complete reperfusion $\mathrm{\Delta}Q\mathsf{R}=1.21$ , $95\%$ ${\mathsf{Cl}}=0.81{\mathrm{-}}$ 1.82), but not in patients with partial reperfusion $\mathrm{{OR}}=0.78$ , $95\%$ CI $=0.54–1.13$ , $p_{\mathrm{het}}=0.11\$ , and did not reach statistical significance. The association between ${\mathsf{DBP}}_{\mathsf{SV}}$ and ICH occurrence also seemed to differ according to the reperfusion grade but did not reach statistical significance (Figure 6).  

# DISCUSSION  

In this post hoc analysis of the BP TARGET trial, we showed that (i) BPV was significantly higher in the intensive SBP target group; (ii) only DBP BPV parameters were associated with worse functional outcomes in the overall population, but these associations were not significant after adjustment; we did not find significant associations between BPV and functional outcome according to the randomization group, but Pmax–­min was associated with worse outcomes in mTICI 2B patients and not in patients with complete successful reperfusion; and (iii) BPV parameters were not associated with the occurrence of ICH in the overall population, nor according to the randomization group or the reperfusion grade.

Our results are in contrast with recent observational studies that suggested a deleterious effect of high BPV after successful reperfusion. In a secondary analysis of the Blood Pressure After Endovascular Therapy for Ischemic Stroke (BEST) study, all measures of BPV (including the SD, CV, and SV) were associated with worse outcomes, with a more pronounced association for systolic than diastolic BPV parameters [7]. These results were also observed in two retrospective Korean and Chinese registries in which $\mathsf{SBP}_{\mathsf{SV}}$ was also strongly associated with worse outcomes in patients successfully treated by EVT [26,27]. Among the factors possibly responsible for these discrepancies is the modality of BP assessment, with invasive BP readings preferentially recorded in the BEST study, compared to the noninvasive BP assessment in the BP TARGET trial. Despite our inclusion criteria (at least $50\%$ of BP recordings within the first $24\mathrm{~h})$ , it is possible that the intervals between measurements, which increase with time, may have hindered the capture of BPV. Recently, Castro et al. found that high-frequency BP oscillations after successful reperfusion could be harmful and were associated with a decreased likelihood of neurological recovery and favorable functional outcomes [28]. Interestingly, BPV was quantified using spectral analysis with 5-min recordings of beat-to-beat BP in this study [28]. It is therefore possible that the currently recommended intervals between BP measurements are not close enough to capture a clinically relevant BPV. On the other hand, BPV was more pronounced in the intensive SBP target group, highlighting that our approach was still effective at capturing BPV discrepancies between groups.

More importantly, $18\%$ of patients in the BEST study received vasopressors during the first $^{24\textrm{h}}$ , which may have been used predominantly in severe patients with fluctuating BP [7]. These differences in baseline characteristics and study design could partly explain these results.  

To our knowledge, only one study specifically addressed the effect of BPV on ICH occurrence after successful EVT [6]. In this single-center retrospective study, only the TR of SBP was associated with symptomatic ICH among several other BPV parameters. Interestingly, in our study the TR of SBP seemed to be associated with a higher ICH occurrence in mTICI 2B patients, but not in mTICI 3 patients. Similarly, the TR of DBP also seemed to be associated with worse outcomes in patients randomized to the intensive SBP target compared to patients randomized to the standard group. These results remained nonsignificant but point toward the value of this BPV parameter. As explained by Kim et al., the TR of BP variation takes into account the speed of BP fluctuation and could better reflect BP changes in a time order compared to traditional BPV parameters [6]. Patients randomized to the intensive SBP target were mostly treated with intravenous (IV) nicardipine. It is possible that the intermittent use of bolus, discontinuation, and reinitiation of IV nicardipine, partly captured by the TR, account for this result. This was also found in post hoc analysis of RCTs evaluating BP thresholds for acute ICH [29,30]. Further studies are thus needed to improve BP control in the acute phase, evaluating new antihypertensive treatments that may achieve more steady BP levels [29].  

Altogether, the exact mechanisms by which BPV may affect ICH occurrence or functional outcomes remain largely unknown. In contrast to other studies, we found that DBP BPV parameters were associated with worse outcomes. Our group recently underlined the effect of DBP variability during EVT on functional outcomes [31]. As previously hypothesized, this result could be partly explained by the impact of DBP on the cortical cerebral blood flow [31-33]. Other low-resistance organs such as the kidneys rely on the diastolic flow for optimal perfusion of the microvasculature [31]. Future preclinical and clinical studies are needed to fully understand the prognostic value of DBP variability in the setting of AIS.  

A key still unanswered question concerns the value of BP management according to the specific reperfusion grade after EVT (i.e., mTICI 2B vs. 3). Overall, BPV parameters were not associated with the occurrence of ICH according to the reperfusion grade. However, regarding functional outcomes, ${\mathsf{SBP}}_{\mathsf{max-min}}$ was associated with worse outcomes in mTICI 2B patients but not in patients with complete reperfusion. In addition, higher $\mathsf{DBP}_{\mathsf{max-min}}$ max–­min was associated with worse outcomes in mTICI 2B patients but not in mTICI 3 patients. $\mathsf{DBP}_{\mathsf{SV}}$ and the TR of DBP also seemed to be associated with worse outcomes in mTICI 2B patients but not in mTICI 3 patients, but these associations remained nonsignificant. The ongoing discussed hypothesis underlying this result would be the effect of high BP fluctuations (especially hypotension in this setting) on distal occluded arteries for mTICI 2B patients, leading to an infarct growth. Further studies are thus needed to assess whether BPV is associated with increased infarct volumes in mTICI 2B compared to mTICI 3 patients.  

This study has several strengths. This is the first report from a randomized, controlled, multicenter trial of the effect of BPV on outcomes after successful reperfusion by EVT. This point is important, as several biases limited previous studies on the subject due to their observational design, which may partly explain the discrepancies with our results. Second, ICH rates were evaluated by an independent core laboratory. Third, we assessed BPV parameters previously evaluated in observational studies to improve the comparison between studies. Finally, we studied the effect of BPV according to the randomization group. We found that the TR seemed to be associated with worse outcomes in patients randomized to the intensive SBP target group. This result could suggest a potentially harmful effect of the intermittent use of IV antihypertensive drugs, especially in the intensive SBP target group, a hypothesis that has to be tested in a dedicated RCT.  

However, several limitations must be acknowledged. The first limitation is inherent to the BP TARGET design and its noninvasive BP assessment. This modality may have decreased BPV capture, and new modalities of BP assessment are needed in the stroke unit. Second, our study sample was moderate, and further studies or individual patient data meta-analysis will be required to fully understand the effect of BPV on functional outcome and ICH occurrence, especially according to specific subgroups of ICH (ie, HI1, HI2, PH1, PH2). Finally, baseline ischemic core volume, brain edema, and final infarct volumes were not assessed for technical reasons. Therefore, we could not study the association between these parameters and higher BPV after successful EVT, especially in mTICI 2B patients.  

# CONCLUSIONS  

In this post hoc analysis of the BP TARGET trial, BPV was not associated with ICH occurrence. SBP range was associated with worse outcomes in mTICI 2B patients but not in mTICI 3 patients. To date, current European guidelines suggest that neurologists should monitor BP noninvasively and not actively reduce SBP to ${<}130\mathrm{mm}\mathsf{Hg}$ during the first $^{24\mathrm{~h~}}$ after successful EVT. The present findings also argue against BP fluctuation, notably by the intermittent use of bolus, discontinuation, and reinitiation of IV antihypertensive treatments such as nicardipine. Finally, further analyses are needed to clarify the impact of BP management according to the reperfusion status. Current guidelines do not suggest a distinction based on the specific reperfusion status (ie, mTICI 2B vs. 3) regarding BP management after successful EVT.
