# Magnitude of Blood Pressure Change After Endovascular Therapy and Outcomes: Insight From the BP-TARGET Trial  

Mohammad Anadani , MD; Benjamin Maïer , MD, MSc; Simon Escalard , MD; Julien Labreuche , BST; Adam de Havenon , MD; Candice Sabben, MD; Bertrand Lapergue, MD; Benjamin Gory , MD, PhD; Sebastien Richard , MD; Igor Sibon , MD; Jean-Philippe Desilles , MD, PhD; Raphael Blanc , MD; Michel Piotin , MD, PhD; Mikael Mazighi , MD, PhD; of behalf the BP-TARGET Study Group\*  

BACKGROUND AND PURPOSE: To assess the association between systolic blood pressure change (∆SBP) at different time intervals after successful reperfusion with radiographic and clinical outcomes.  

METHODS: This is a post hoc analysis of the BP-TARGET multicenter trial (Blood Pressure Target in Acute Stroke to Reduce Hemorrhage After Endovascular Therapy). ∆SBP was defined as end of procedure SBP minus mean SBP at different time intervals (15–60 minutes, 1–6 hours, and 6–24 hours postprocedure). The primary outcome was the poor functional outcome (90-day modified Rankin Scale score $3\mathrm{-}6,$ ).  

RESULTS: We included a total of 267 patients (130 in the intensive treatment group). Compared with patients with favorable outcome, patients with poor outcome had lower ∆SBP (less SBP reduction) at all times intervals. After adjusting for potential confounders including baseline SBP, both $\Delta\mathrm{SBP}_{15-60\mathsf{M}}$ and $\Delta\mathrm{SBP}_{6-24\mathsf{H}}$ were associated with lower odds of poor outcome (adjusted odds ratio per 5 mm Hg SBP reduction, 0.89 $[95\%$ CI, $0.81\dot{-}0.99]$ , and adjusted odds ratio 0.82 $[95\%$ CI, $0.73-$ 0.92], respectively). Concerning safety outcomes, patients with intraparenchymal hemorrhage had lower ∆SBP at all time intervals. $\Delta\mathrm{SBP}_{15-60\mathsf{M}}$ was associated with lower odds of any intraparenchymal hemorrhage (adjusted odds ratio per $5\mathsf{mmHg}$ SBP reduction 0.91 $[95\%$ CI, 0.83–0.99]). Conversely, ∆SBP was not associated with mortality or neurological deterioration at any time interval.  

CONCLUSIONS: After successful reperfusion, ∆SBP had a linear relationship with poor outcome and the risk of poor outcome was higher with less reduction from the baseline SBP.

However, the causative relationship between high BP and poor outcome after stroke has not been proven and the evidence supporting the benefit from BP reduction after EVT is lacking.8 Although, BP lowering treatment could mitigate reperfusion injury after successful reperfusion, it can potentially aggravate hypoperfusion of penumbral brain tissue, especially in the setting of incomplete reperfusion.2 Therefore, there is still uncertainty on the balance of the safety and efficacy of BP reduction after successful reperfusion. Prior observational studies have also implied a potential benefit from systolic BP (SBP) reduction after EVT with no safety concerns.9,10 However, the recently published BPTARGET trial (Blood Pressure Target in Acute Stroke to Reduce Hemorrhage After Endovascular Therapy) demonstrated neutral results from intensive BP lowering strategy (SBP target $100\mathrm{-}129\mathrm{mm}\mathsf{Hg})$ compared with guideline-recommended BP control (SBP, $<185$ mm Hg) in patients treated with EVT who had achieved successful reperfusion at the end of the procedure.11  

We performed a post hoc analysis of the BP-TARGET trial data to assess the association between ∆SBP at different time intervals with clinical and radiographic outcomes and to assess the variation in the treatment effects with different degrees of SBP lowering in the first 24 hours after successful reperfusion on the risk of hemorrhagic complications and poor outcome.  

# METHODS  

# Cohort  

This is a secondary observational post hoc analysis of the BP-TARGET trial. The BP-TARGET trial protocol12 and main results11 have been previously published. In brief, the trial enrolled patients with acute ischemic stroke due to anterior circulation large vessel occlusion who achieved successful reperfusion with EVT and had $\mathsf{SBP}\geq130\mathsf{mm}\mathsf{Hg}$ at the end of procedure. Patients were randomized in 1:1 fashion to intensive SBP treatment (SBP target $100\mathrm{-}129\mathrm{mm}\mathsf{Hg}$ to be achieved within 1 hour of randomization) or standard SBP treatment (SBP target $130{-}185\mathrm{mm}\mathsf{Hg}$ ). For the purpose of this study, we included patients with ${>}50\%$ of the planned 24-hour SBP measurements. The study protocol and consent forms were approved by the Comité de Protection des th Personnes Sud Méditerranee IV (N°ID-RCB: 2016- AO1884-47, March 14, 2017). Consent was obtained from all patients included in the study. This study reported according to the Strengthening the Reporting of Observational Studies in Epidemiology guidelines.13  

# Blood Pressure  

BP measurement was performed using automatic sphygmomanometers as follows: every 15 minutes for 2 hours after randomization, then every 30 minutes for 6 hours and then every hour for the remaining 16 hours. The choice of antihypertensive medication was left to the treating team’s discretion. However, the most commonly used antihypertensive medication type was calcium channel blocker. SBP change after reperfusion was defined as baseline SBP minus the mean of achieved SBP levels during 3 periods postreperfusion: 15 to 60 minutes $(\Delta\mathrm{SBP}_{15-60M})$ , 1 to 6 hours $(\Delta\mathrm{SBP}_{1-6+1})$ and 6 to 24 hours $(\Delta\mathrm{SBP}_{6-24\mathsf{H}})$ P6–24H). SBP at the end of procedure was considered as baseline SBP.  

# Outcomes  

The primary outcome was poor outcome, defined as modified Rankin Scale (mRS) score of 3–6 at 90 days or an increase in prestroke mRS for patients with baseline mRS score of ${\ge}3$ . As a secondary sensitivity outcome, we defined poor outcome as mRS score of 2–6 at 90-day or an increase in prestroke mRS for patients with baseline mRS score of ${}^{\ge2}$ (Figure S1). Safety outcomes included 90-day mortality, any intraparenchymal hemorrhage at 24 to 36 hours (intracranial hemorrhage), symptomatic intracerebral hemorrhage (defined as evidence of intracranial hemorrhage associated with an increase of 4 or more points on the National Institutes of Health Stroke Scale at 24 hours), and neurological deterioration (defined as 4 or more points worsening in National Institutes of Health Stroke Scale).  

# Statistical Analysis  

Categorical variables were expressed as frequencies and percentages. Quantitative variables were expressed as mean (SD), or median (interquartile range) for non-normal distribution. Normality of distributions was assessed graphically and by using the Shapiro-Wilk test.  

We assessed the association of each ∆SBP with primary and secondary outcomes in overall study population using logistic regression models adjusted for baseline SBP values. ∆SBP were treated as continuous variables as well as categorical variables according to prespecified thresholds $(<10,$ , 10–19, ${\ge}20$ $\mathsf{mm}\mathsf{Hg})$ ; odds ratio (OR) were derived from logistic regression models as effect sizes, expressed per 5 mm Hg SBP reduction or using $\Delta\mathrm{SBP}<10\mathrm{mm}\mathsf{Hg}$ category as reference. We also examined the shape of association by using 3-knots restricted cubic spline regression. Associations were further adjusted for prespecified variables (center, age sex, history of hypertension, time from symptoms onset to reperfusion, baseline National Institutes of Health Stroke Scale and SBP values, occlusion site and reperfusion grade (modified Thrombolysis in Cerebral Infarction 2b versus modified Thrombolysis in Cerebral Infarction 3) by using multivariable logistic regression models, using penalized approach to account smaller number of events for symptomatic intracerebral hemorrhage, all-cause mortality, and neurological deterioration outcomes. We did not adjust for BP treatment arm to avoid multicollinearity. # RESULTS  

# BP Change and Outcomes  

We included a total of 267 patients of whom 130 patients belonged to the standard SBP group and 137 to the intensive group (Figure 1). Baseline characteristics of the intensive and standard arms are reported in Table 1. Poor outcome occurred in $52.3~\%$ and $53.2\%$ in the intensive and standard groups, respectively (Table  1). Compared with patients with favorable outcome, patients with poor outcome had lower ∆SBP at all times intervals (Table 2). After adjusting for potential confounders including baseline SBP, both $\Delta\mathsf{SBP}_{1-6\mathsf{H}}$ and $\Delta\mathrm{SBP}_{6-24\mathsf{H}}$ were associated with lower odds of poor outcome (adjusted OR [aOR] per $5\mathsf{mmHg}$ SBP reduction, 0.89 $[95\%$ CI, $0.81\substack{-0.99}],$ , and aOR per 5 mm Hg SBP reduction 0.82 $[95\%$ CI, $0.73-$ 0.92], respectively; Table 2). Subgroup analyses based on baseline SBP $(<180$ versus $\geq180\mathrm{mm}\mathsf{Hg},$ ), randomization arm, antihypertensive treatment use, and history of hypertension demonstrated no significant heterogeneity in the treatment effect at any time interval (Figure 2). We repeated the analysis considering poor outcome as mRS score of 2–6. The results are overall similar to the main analysis results and shown in Figure S1.

Concerning safety outcomes, patients with intraparenchymal hemorrhage had lower ∆SBP at all time intervals. $\Delta\mathrm{SBP}_{15-60\mathrm{M}}$ P15–60M was associated with lower odds of any intraparenchymal hemorrhage (aOR per 5 mm Hg SBP reduction, 0.91 $[95\%$ CI, 0.83–0.99]). Conversely, ∆SBP was not associated with mortality or neurological deterioration at any time interval (Table 2).  

In a sensitivity analysis, we included all BP-TARGET trial cohort after handling missing BP measurements and outcomes data with multiple imputations. The results are overall similar to the main analysis results and shown in Table S1.  

# Magnitude of BP Change and Outcomes  

Large SBP reduction $(\geq20\mathrm{mm}\mathsf{Hg})$ ) in the first hour after randomization was associated with significantly lower odds of poor outcome compared with minimal SBP reduction (<10 mm Hg; aOR, 0.38 $[95\%$ CI, 0.15–0.92]). Similar results were observed for $\Delta\mathrm{SBP}_{6-24\mathsf{H}}$ (Figure 3).  

Moderate $(10{-}20\mathrm{mm}\mathsf{H}\mathsf{g},$ SBP reduction in the first hour was associated with lower odds of any intraparenchymal hemorrhage (aOR, 0.41 $[95\%$ CI, 0.17–0.93]). Large $(\geq20\mathrm{mm}\mathsf{Hg})$ SBP reduction in 1 to 6 hours after reperfusion was also associated with lower odds of any intraparenchymal hemorrhage (aOR, 0.42 $[95\%$ CI, $0.19\substack{-0.91}].$ ). There was no increased risk of mortality or neurological deterioration with moderate or large SBP reduction at any time interval compared with no or minimal SBP reduction $(<10\mathrm{mm}\mathsf{Hg})$ ).  

To further understand the shape of the relationship between ∆SBP and outcomes, we analyzed the association between ∆SBP and each outcome using restricted cubic spline regression models. In the first hour after successful reperfusion, SBP increment significantly increased the odds of poor outcome, but SBP reduction had minimal impact on outcome (Figure 4). Beyond 1 hour, SBP change had a linear relationship with poor outcome. There was a linear relationship between ∆SBP and any intraparenchymal hemorrhage at all time intervals. There was U-shaped relationship between ∆SBP and mortality at all time intervals (Figure S2).

Elevated BP after successful reperfusion has been linked to higher risk of hemorrhagic complications and poor outcome.4–6,16 Previous studies that have evaluated the degree of SBP reduction in the acute phase after EVT and outcomes have reported conflicting results.9,10,17 A multicenter study of 1361 patients with successful reperfusion implied an association between ∆SBP and lower odds of poor outcome.9 In a subsequent post hoc analysis of the BEST study (Blood Pressure After Endovascular Stroke Therapy), there was no association between ∆SBP and poor outcome.17 In a more recent retrospective study assessing the association between ∆SBP at different time intervals and outcomes, ∆SBP was associated with poor outcome in a linear pattern for all time intervals, although the effect of SBP increment was more substantial than SBP reduction.10  

Inconsistency in the results of prior studies is likely related to differences in study design, patient selection, sample size, and measurement error. The present study demonstrated a linear association between ∆SBP and poor outcome, consistent with the risk of poor outcome being higher if the SBP was reduced less after successful reperfusion. For example, patients who achieved ${\geq}20\mathrm{mm}\mathsf{Hg}$ reduction in SBP in the first hour after successful reperfusion had $62\%$ lower odds of poor outcome compared with those who had no to minimal SBP reduction $(<10\mathrm{mm}\mathsf{Hg})$ . The effect of ∆SBP on poor outcome was consistent in subgroup analyses based on baseline hypertension, baseline SBP and reperfusion status.  

∆SBP had also a linear relationship with intraparenchymal hemorrhage and larger SBP reduction was associated with lower risk of intraparenchymal hemorrhage. We did not find a significant association between ∆SBP and mortality, although a cubic spline model suggested a U-shaped relationship between ∆SBP and mortality. Similarly, we did not find a significant association between ∆SBP and neurological deterioration. However, the analyses of mortality and neurological deterioration should be interpreted with caution due to a small number of events.

However, in preclinical studies induced hypertension in the reperfusion period led to an increase in blood brain permeability and worsening vasogenic edema,18,19 conversely BP lowering led to reduction in infarct expansion.7 The BP-TARGET trial demonstrated neutral results, however, it was limited by a small difference in the achieved mean SBP between the 2 groups (128 and $138\mathrm{mm}\mathsf{Hg}$ in the intensive and standard groups, respectively) which may not have been sufficient to detect a benefit from SBP reduction.20  

Our study highlights the complexity of BP management after successful reperfusion. Although our findings reinforce the potential beneficial effect of rapid lowering of elevated SBP within the first few hours after successful reperfusion, it also suggests a potential deleterious effect of significant alteration of BP after successful reperfusion giving the U-shaped relationship with mortality. Three ongoing trials (URL: https://www.clinicaltrials.gov; Unique identifiers: NCT04140110, NCT04205305, and NCT04116112) are further evaluating the safety and efficacy of intensive BP reduction after successful reperfusion.  

The key strengths of this study are the standardized BP measurement protocols and central adjudication of all outcomes. The key limitation is related to the post hoc nature of the study, which pose a risk of confounding from between-groups imbalances and random error. In addition, the data regarding the infarct extension is not available in the BP-TARGET study. Moreover, our study is likely underpowered to detect an association between SBP reduction and mortality or neurological deterioration. Despite the association between ∆SBP and poor outcome, our study does not prove that SBP alteration after successful reperfusion improves functional outcome and further trials are needed to address this question.  

# CONCLUSIONS  

There was a linear relationship between ∆SBP after successful reperfusion and poor outcome. The risk of poor outcome was higher with less SBP reduction after EVT.
