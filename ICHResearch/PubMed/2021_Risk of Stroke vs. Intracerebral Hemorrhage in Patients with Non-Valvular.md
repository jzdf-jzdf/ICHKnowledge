# Risk of Stroke vs. Intracerebral Hemorrhage in Patients with Non-Valvular Atrial Fibrillation Undergoing Percutaneous Coronary Intervention: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis of Randomized Controlled Trials Comparing Dual vs. Triple Antithrombotic Therapy  

Aaron Desai, MD,\* Cesar Escamilla-Ocanas, MD,\* Deepika Dilip, MPH,\* † Hamidreza Saber, MD, MPH,† and Rahul Damani, MD, MPH\*  

Background: About $15\%$ of patients with non-valvular atrial fibrillation might require percutaneous coronary interventions (PCIs) with stent placement to treat obstructive coronary artery disease. Dual antiplatelet therapy (DAPT) with acetylsalicylic acid (aspirin) and P2Y12 antagonist is recommended after PCI. Patients requiring DAPT also require treatment with oral anticoagulation for atrial fibrillation. We conducted a meta-analysis to identify the antithrombotic regimen associated with the lowest rate of bleeding and thromboembolic events in non-valvular atrial fibrillation after PCI. Methods: We searched PubMed, Embase, Scopus and Cochrane databases to identify randomized trials that investigated the use of dual antiplatelet therapy and vitamin K antagonist and/or Non-vitamin K antagonist oral anticoagulants (NOAC) (triple antithrombotic therapy (TAT)) against single antiplatelet agent and NOAC (dual antithrombotic therapy (DAT)) in the setting of coronary artery disease (CAD) requiring PCI and non-valvular atrial fibrillation. Random-effect models were used to pool data. We used the $I^{2}$ statistic to measure heterogeneity between trials. Results: We found 4 randomized clinical trials (ENTRUST, AUGUSTUS, PIONEER, REDUAL) using different NOACs. Overall, 9241 patients (median age 70 years, $41.4\%$ female, mean CHADS2VASC Score 3.5) were included. We excluded patients in the very low dose rivaroxaban group from the PIONEER AF-PCI trial and low dose dabigatran group from the REDUAL PCI trial as these are not available in the United States. Our metanalysis showed that dual therapy was associated with less risk of intracranial hemorrhage (RR 0.55, $95\%$ $\mathrm{~C}\mathrm{{I}}0.31{-}0.99$ ; $p=0.045$ ; $\mathrm{I}^{2}=42\%$ ) and major bleeding (RR 0.66; $95\%$ $\mathrm{~C~I~}0.55{-}0.79_{,}$ ; $p<0.0001$ ; $\mathrm{I}^{2}=27\%\rangle$ as compared to triple therapy. Further risk of ischemic stroke $(\mathrm{RR}0.94,95\%\mathrm{CI}0.63-1.39;p=0.75;\mathrm{I}^{2}{=}0\%),$ myocardial infarction (RR 1.18, $95\%$ CI $0.94\substack{-1.47}$ ; $p=0.16$ ; $\mathrm{I}^{2}=\mathrm{0}$ ), or stent thrombosis (RR 0.50, $95\%$ C $\mathrm{~T~}0.93-2.41$ ; $p=0.10.$ ; $\mathrm{I}^{2}=0\%$ ) were unchanged. Similar findings were also noted on analysis of NOAC specific DAT vs VKA based TAT. Conclusions: The combination of an antiplatelet and NOACs (dual therapy) is associated with less risk of major bleeding and intracranial hemorrhage, with no significant difference in ischemic events (stroke myocardial infarction or stent thrombosis).

# Introduction  

Non-valvular Atrial fibrillation (NVAF) is the most commonly diagnosed heart rhythm abnormality. In US, it is estimated that approximately 6 million adults have NVAF and this number is expected to rise to 12 million in 2030.1 NVAF is also an independent risk factor for ischemic stroke severity, recurrence, and mortality.2,3 The management of NVAF in patients who have experienced stroke is geared toward the prevention of secondary thromboembolic events with the use of anticoagulation.3 Over the past decade, non-vitamin K antagonist oral anticoagulants (NOACs) have emerged as the treatment of choice in patients with NVAF over warfarin.4  

Estimates suggest that about $20-40\%$ of patients with NVAF also have coronary artery disease (CAD), complicated by acute coronary syndrome (ACS) requiring revascularization using percutaneous coronary intervention (PCI) and stent implantation 5. However, the optimal antithrombotic regimen after PCI in patients with NVAF is unclear. In this patient population, OAC is indicated for prevention of stroke whereas dual antiplatelet therapy (DAPT) is indicated for prevention of stent thrombosis. On the other hand, the combination of OAC and DAPT, a regimen also known as triple antithrombotic therapy (TAT), is associated with a significantly increased risk of major bleeding and mortality. Prior head-to-head trials of antithrombotic therapy in patients with NVAF and CAD with PCI have largely shown that a DAT based therapy reduces the risk of bleeding without causing any adverse increase in thrombotic or cardioembolic events, however, they were not powered statistically to show such differenc e.711 Therefore, we performed a metaanalysis to compare the safety and efficacy of NOAC based DAT and the warfarin-based TAT in patients with NVAF and recent PCI using previously published randomized data. Prior meta-analyses by Gargiulo et al., Galli et al., Shurrab et al, and Ando et al. for the same were performed in Canada and Europe. They included patients who were on low dose dabigatran which we have excluded, since it is not approved by the FDA for use in the United States. Our results indicate that for patients in United States who have Non-valvular atrial fibrillation (NVAF) and Coronary Artery Disease (CAD) with PCI, a dual therapy based approach should be preferred as it lowers the risk of intracranial bleeds while not affecting the rates of ischemic events and ischemic events related mortality.  

# Methods  

We followed the PRISMA (Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses) guidelines (supplementary figure 1). A systematic review was performed by two independent authors (A.D and C.E.O) on PubMed, Embase, Scopus and Cochrane from inception till December 2019 using the following keywords in various combinations: atrial fibrillation, percutaneous coronary intervention, apixaban, warfarin, rivaroxaban, dabigatran, acute coronary syndrome, edoxaban, oral anticoagulation, randomized trial. All references of the articles retrieved were screened to identify and review further studies on this topic. We included randomized controlled studies comparing NOAC based DAT against TAT. Our exclusion criteria were: case series, observational studies, editorials and other non-randomized trials; studies not including DOACs in the DAT arm. After carefully reviewing the full text of the selected studies data was entered from the available manuscripts and online supplemental materials into a standardized form.811 All data was reviewed by corresponding author (R.D) and any discrepancies were addressed by all authors and resolved with consensus.  

# Outcome measures  

We included the Intracranial Hemorrhage (ICH), and International Society on Thrombosis and Hemostasis (ISTH) major bleeding as primary safety outcome. Clinically relevant non-major bleeding (CRNB) was included as secondary safety outcomes. Stroke, myocardial infarction and stent thrombosis were included as primary efficacy outcomes. Death and cardiovascular death as secondary efficacy outcomes. All the outcomes were assessed at six or more months as reported in the clinical trials.  

# Statistical Analysis  

Measures of association and counts from four studies were used in the meta-analysis. Data analysis was performed using the “meta” package in R Statistical Software.12 The principal study measures analyzed were risk ratios, calculated using percentages between the control and experimental groups. Patients receiving triple therapies were designated as the control group while those receiving dual therapies were considered the experimental group. Since the AUGUSTUS trial was a $2\times2$ factorial design study, we performed 2 analyses: First analysis compared all DAT and TAT and a second analysis compared NOAC-based DAT vs. Vitamin K antagonist (VKA)-based TAT from the included trials (by excluding the VKA-based DAT and NOAC-based TAT arms from the AUGUSTUS). Additionally, the analyses were stratified by the primary safety and efficacy outcomes. For each outcome, a pooled risk ratio with a $95\%$ confidence interval along with an $\mathrm{I}^{2}$ statistic to quantify heterogeneity across studies. Both fixed-effects and random-effects models were used to account for heterogeneity between study populations. To account for potential publication bias, we made a series of funnel plots for each outcome (supplementary figure 2). While accounting for the small total of studies analyzed, there were no indications of publication bias presenting a concern for our statistical analyses.

There was no increased risk of myocardial infarction with DAT as compared to TAT in the primary analysis (Fig 3.1b) including all NOAC and VKA based DAT (RR 1.18, $95\%$ C $\mathrm{~I~}0.94\mathrm{-}1.47$ ; $p=0.16;\:\mathrm{I}^{2}=0\%)$ and secondary analysis (Fig 4.1b) specific for NOAC-DAT vs. VKA-TAT (RR 1.12, $95\%$ CI 0.861.45; $p=0.41;\mathrm{I}^{2}=0\%$ ).  

# Stent Thrombosis  

There was no increased risk of stent thrombosis with DAT as compared to TAT in the primary analysis (Fig 3.1c) including all NOAC and VKA based DAT (RR 0.50, $95\%$ CI 0.932.41; $p=0.10;\:\Gamma^{2}=0\%)$ and secondary analysis (Fig 4.1c) specific for NOAC-DAT vs. VKA-TAT (RR $1.45,95\%\operatorname{CI}0.90-2.32;p=0.12;\mathrm{I}^{2}=0\%)$ .  

# Secondary Efficacy Outcomes  

# Death  

There was no significant difference in terms of mortality in the two groups in the primary analysis (Fig 3.2a) including all NOAC and VKA based DAT (RR 1.07, $95\%$ $\mathrm{~C~I~}0.87{-}1.32$ ; $p=0.54;$ $\mathrm{I}^{2}=0\%$ ) and secondary analysis (Fig 4.2a) specific for NOAC-DAT vs. VKA-TAT (RR 1.07, $95\%$ CI 0.841.36; $p=0.57,\mathrm{{I}}^{2}=0\%,$ ).  

# Cardiovascular Death  

There was no significant difference in cardiovascular deaths amongst the two groups in the primary analysis (Fig 3.2b) including all NOAC and VKA based DAT (RR 1.06, $95\%$ CI 0.821.38; $p=0.65$ ; $\mathrm{I}^{2}=0\%$ ) and secondary analysis (Fig 4.2b) specific for NOAC-DAT vs. VKA-TAT (RR 1.07, $95\%$ CI 0.791.44; $p=0.66;\mathrm{I}^{2}=0\%$ ).  

# Discussion  

Our systematic review and meta-analysis compared the dual therapy (DAT) and triple therapy (TAT) for risk of bleeding against rates of ischemic and thrombotic events, with a focus on intracranial hemorrhage and stroke. Our findings indicate that there was a significantly lower risk of bleeding with DAT as compared to TAT (in both primary and secondary analysis after excluding VKA based DAT). There was no difference in efficacy outcomes including ischemic stroke, death, cardiovascular death, myocardial infarction and stent thrombosis. Our results highlight the advantage of using DAT vs TAT in terms of lowering bleeding risk with no loss of benefit in terms of efficacy.  

In the Framingham Heart Study (FHS), NVAF accounted for ${\approx}1.5\%$ of strokes in individuals 50 to 59 years of age and ${\approx}23.5\%$ in those 80 to 89 years of age.13 In order to minimize the risk of bleeding associated with TAT, there have been some trials which gave evidence for the use of NOACs with an antiplatelet agent, also known as dual antithrombotic therapy (DAT). WOEST trial was one of the earlier studies which showed significant reduction in bleeding rates with no increase in the rates of thrombotic events.7 Major drawbacks of this study included inadequate power to detect significant differences in ischemic endpoints, $31\%$ of patients did not have AF and the patients received TAT for one year contrary to the recommended guidelines.14 Also, it only used VKA for anticoagulation. WOEST was followed by four clinical trials each testing a NOAC in the DAT arm against traditional warfarin and DAPT in the TAT arm.811 The advantage of performing a meta-analysis of this sort allows to detect the differences in the outcomes, which were not previously identified since the individual studies are underpowered. The four randomized controlled trials we analyzed all reported no increased risk of ischemic events with no higher risk of bleeding. 811 However, since the rate of ischemic events is less than the bleeding events, the higher risk of bleeding may have not been detected. The first meta-analysis performed by Lopes et al. showed a lower risk of bleeding with no increased rate of ischemic events.15 However, the authors included the WOEST trial

Roule et al similarly performed meta-analysis before ENTRUST AF PCI was published.16 After the results of ENTRUST AF PCI were available, a number of meta-analysis performed by Gargiulo et al., Galli et al., Shurrab et al, and Ando et al. showed increased incidences of ischemic events, particularly stent thrombosis.1720 However, all of these meta-analyses included the dabigatran $110~\mathrm{mg}$ group of RE-DUAL PCI study. Of note, the use of dabigatran $110~\mathrm{mg}$ is an accepted dose for prevention of cardioembolic risk due to NVAF in Europe and Canada but it is not FDA approved in the United States. We performed our analysis after exclusion of this group: our results showed no statistically significant increase in risk of ischemic events, either cardiac or cerebrovascular. This suggests that the inclusion of low dose dabigatran group causes effect modification. Our findings, in contrast to what was reported in the original trials, indicate that not taking aspirin lower the risk of bleeding without increasing the risk of ischemic events. This is especially important in patients who are on DAT for cardiac stents and then later present with acute ischemic stroke secondary to NVAF. While not completely understood, evidence are accumulating that oral anticoagulants, including NOACs, have some antiplatelet effect which help in prevention of both stroke and recurrent MI.15 This is further amplified by $\mathrm{P}2\mathrm{Y}_{12}$ inhibition. Thus, a strategy of DAT with NOACs and $\mathrm{P}2\mathrm{Y}_{12}$ might be beneficial and sufficient for most patients with NVAF and ACS while the addition of aspirin might only increase the risk of bleeding without providing any added benefit in terms of efficacy. However, due to the important differences in design of the trials included in the meta-analysis, it is impossible to conclude whether the reduction in risk of bleeding that was seen with the NOAC was due to the use of the new drug, the discontinuation of aspirin or a combination of both.  

Our study has a few limitations. First, to perform our analysis we used trial-based data and not individual level data to perform our analysis. In this context, it was not possible to perform a subgroup analysis was not possible which might show different results depending on $\mathrm{CHA}_{2}\mathrm{DS}_{2}$ -VASc status. Further, the AUGUSTUS trial had a unique design $(2\times2$ factorial) for which we performed a sensitivity analysis with DOAC based DAT and VKA based TAT group. And lastly, there was significant data heterogeneity noted in the trials analyzed, including trial design, type, duration of antiplatelet therapy, and duration of follow-up. These differences can affect the interpretation of our results. To address these drawbacks, we performed a random effects analysis which showed similar results.  

# Conclusion  

We performed a meta-analysis of four randomized controlled trials each assessing a different NOAC based DAT against a VKA based TAT. Our analysis is unique in the sense that we did not include patients who received low dose NOACs (rivaroxaban and dabigatran) which are not approved by the Food and Drug Administration (FDA) in the United States. Our findings indicate that for patients in United States who have NVAF and ACS with/without PCI, a dual therapy-based approach should be preferred as it lowers the risk of intracranial bleeds while not affecting the rates of ischemic events and ischemic events related mortality.
