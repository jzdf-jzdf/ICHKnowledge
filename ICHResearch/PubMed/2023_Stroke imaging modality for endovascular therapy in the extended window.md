# Abstract  

Background  In anterior circulation large vessel occlusion (LVO) in the extended time window, the guidelines recommend advanced imaging (ADVI) to select patients for endovascular therapy (EVT). However, questions remain regarding its availability and applicability in the real world. It is unclear whether an approach to the extended window EVT that does not use ADVI would be equivalent.  

Methods  In April 2022, a literature search was performed to identified randomized controlled trials (RCT) and observational studies describing 90-day outcomes. We performed a meta-analysis of the proportion of aggregate using a random effect to estimate rates of functional independence, defined as modified Rankin Scale (mRS) score ${\le}2$ at 90 days, mean ${\mathsf{mRS}},$ mortality and symptomatic intracranial hemorrhage (sICH) stratified by imaging modality.  

Results  Four RCTs and 28 observational studies were included. The pooled proportion of functional independence among patients selected by ADVI was $44\%$ $95\%$ CI $39\%$ to $48\%$ ; $1^{2}{=}80\%;$ ) and $48\%$ $95\%$ $\textcircled{\scriptsize{1}41\%}$ to $55\%$ ; $1^{2}=75\%$ ) with non-contrast CT/CT angiography (NCCT/CTA) $(\mathsf{p}{=}0.36)$ . Mean $\mathsf{mR}$ with ADVI was 2.88 $(95\%\mathrm{Cl}2.36\$ to 3.41; $1^{2}{=}0.0\%)$ and 2.79 $(95\%\mathsf{Cl}2.31\$ to 3.27; $1^{2}{=}0.0\%)$ with NCCT $(\mathfrak{p}{=}0.79)$ . Mortality in patients selected by ADVI was $13\%$ ( $95\%$ CI $10\%$ to $17\%$ ; $1^{2}{=}81\%)$ ) and $16\%$ $95\%$ CI $12\%$ to $22\%$ $1^{2}{=}69\%)$ with NCCT $_{\mathrm{p}=\mathrm{0}.\mathrm{2}\mathrm{9}}$ . sICH with ADVI was $4\%$ $(95\%\mathrm{Cl}3\%$ to $7\%$ ; $1^{2}=73\%$ ) and $6\%$ with NCCT/CTA $(95\%\mathrm{Cl}4\%$ to $8\%$ ; $1^{2}{=}6\%$ , $\scriptstyle{\mathsf{p}}=0.27;$ .  

# What is already known on this topic  

$\Rightarrow$ Advanced imaging (ADVI) including CT perfusion and MRI is the standard of care to select patients for endovascular therapy (EVT) in the extended window time for anterior circulation large vessel occlusion (LVO).  

# What this study adds  

$\Rightarrow$ In our meta-analysis, patients selected by ADVI did not have a higher pooled proportion of functional independence at 90 days than those selected by non-contrast CT (NCCT) alone. The mean modified Rankin Scale score, mortality and symptomatic intracranial hemorrhage were also similar.  

# How this study might affect research, practice or policy  

$\Rightarrow$ These data provide support for the current practice in stroke centers that do not routinely use ADVI in the extended time window to select patients for EVT. This is particularly important in non-urban areas and middle-income countries in which LVO treatment rates are already quite low. However, the hypothesis of equivalence between ADVI and NCCT/CT angiography should be verified through future clinical studies.

# Introduction  

Multiple randomized controlled trials (RCTs) have described a robust benefit of endovascular therapy (EVT) for ischemic stroke due to anterior circulation large vessel occlusion (LVO) in the early and extended time windows (6–24 hours from symptom onset).1 2 Current imaging for LVO includes either CT angiography (CTA) or magnetic resonance angiography (MRA) in addition to parenchymal imaging as part of triage for EVT. The current AHA/ASA guidelines state that advanced multimodal imaging is useful for selecting candidates for EVT in the extended time window and no additional imaging is needed in the early time window.3 The recommendation for advanced imaging (ADVI) was based on the imaging modalities employed in the pivotal DAWN and DEFUSE-3 trials.1 2^ However, the optimal modality for selecting patients for EVT remains debatable due to lack of RCTs using no additional imaging in the extended time window and may present a reasonable alternative to ADVI.4  

Several ADVI modalities have been used in clinical trials to select patients for EVT in the extended time window including CT perfusion (CTP), magnetic resonance perfusion (MRP), and MRI with diffusion-weighted imaging (DWI)/fluid attenuated inversion recovery (FLAIR) mismatch.1 2 Although ADVI has demonstrated excellent performance in the pivotal RCTs of extended time window EVT, questions remain regarding its availability and applicability in the real world, and the necessity to perform ADVI in all patients presenting in this window. CTP with automated software, most commonly used in clinical trials, has limitations including overestimation of the ischemic core volume and might exclude patients who would potentially benefit from treatment.5 6 Since ADVI is not broadly available, implementation of clinical protocols using these imaging criteria are limited predominantly to comprehensive stroke centers.4 7 8 It is unclear whether an approach to the extended window EVT that does not use ADVI would be equivalent to the current guidelines recommended strategy. Current evidence from cohort studies report conflicting results, with several studies suggesting superiority of ADVI,9 10 while others showed a similar proportion of functional independence without ADVI.9–17 Furthermore, no RCT compared these two approaches for patient selection during the extended time window in patients with anterior circulation LVO. All the available data come from cohort studies reporting different outcomes or from retrospective registry analyses.  

We sought to evaluate the utility of the guideline-based approach using ADVI versus a pragmatic approach using conventional imaging with CT and CTA to select patients for EVT in the extended time window for acute anterior circulation LVO.  

# Methods  

A systematic review in accordance with the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses (PRISMA) guideline was conducted.18 The protocol was registered on the International Prospective Register of Systematic Reviews (PROSPERO) code: CRD42021236092.  

# Structured question  

Our research question was: “What is the clinical benefit of ADVI (Intervention) in regard to functional independence at 90 days (Outcome) after EVT compared with computed tomography (non-contrast CT (NCCT)/CTA) (Comparator) to select patients for EVT in acute stroke due to anterior circulation LVO in the extended time window (Population)?”  

# Eligibility criteria  

Articles were included in the analysis if they met the following criteria: (1) RCTs, non-RCTs which included open-label trials, real-world studies or observational studies of adults $(>18$ years) with acute ischemic stroke due to anterior circulation LVO; (2) performance of EVT (with second-generation stent retrievers, aspiration techniques, or both) in the extended time window (6–24 hours from symptom onset); (3) clear imaging selection modality for EVT; and (4) outcomes reported at 90 days after stroke. ADVI was defined as the use of CTP, MRP, or MRI using DWI/FLAIR mismatch to select patients for EVT. We excluded the following types of studies: review articles, abstracts, case reports, letters or editorials. No restrictions were made regarding language and geographical area. We also excluded studies that used first-generation stent devices for EVT (MERCI retriever and first-generation Penumbra system). Patients with low Alberta Stroke Program Early CT Score (ASPECTS) or large core were not included in our study.  

# Literature search and study selection  

We carried out a systematic search in five databases: PubMed/ Medline, Scopus, Web of Science, Embase, and Cochrane (via OvidSP) up to April 2022. The search strategy included Medical Subject Title (MeSH) terms for ‘ischemic stroke’, ‘thrombectomy’ and related words for ^non-contrast CT' (complete details of the search strategy are shown in online supplemental table 1). According to the inclusion criteria and after eliminating duplicate articles, four reviewers in two teams screened articles by titles and abstracts to identify potentially relevant articles. Finally, the same reviewers accessed the full-text articles and assessed their eligibility. Disagreements were resolved by discussion to achieve consensus or by a fifth reviewer if there was no consensus.  

# Data extraction  

For each study, two teams of two researchers extracted information related to patient demographics, baseline evaluation (mRS, stroke severity based on the National Institutes of Health Stroke Scale (NIHSS) and ASPECTS score), site of occlusion, characteristics of the intervention (type of ADVI), and outcome measures (proportion who achieved mRS score of 0–2 at 90 days, mean/ median mRS, mortality at 90 days, sICH in the acute phase).  

# Primary and secondary outcomes  

The primary outcome to assess efficacy was the mRS score at 90 days.19 Functional independence was defined as a mRS score of 0–2. The secondary outcome measure for efficacy was the mean mRS score. To assess safety, all-cause mortality at 90 days and symptomatic intracranial hemorrhage (sICH) were evaluated. sICH was defined according to the Safe Implementation of Thrombolysis in Stroke-­Monitoring Study (SITS-­MOST),20 the National Institute of Neurological Disorders and Stroke (NINDS) study,21 and the European Cooperative Acute Stroke Study (ECASS) criteria II and III.22 23  

# Risk of bias  

The revised Cochrane risk-of-bias tool version 2.0. (RoB-2) was used to assess the methodological quality of the $\mathrm{RCTs}^{24}$ and risk of bias in non-randomized studies of interventions (ROBINS-I) for non-randomized studies of interventions (NRSIs).25 According to the Cochrane handbook for systematic reviews of interventions,26 the classification of low risk, some concerns, and high risk of bias for RCTs, and the classification of low, moderate, serious, and critical risk of bias for NRSIs was performed for the primary and secondary outcomes available in each study.  

# Statistical analysis  

We performed a meta-analysis of proportions to estimate the functional independence and pooled mean mRS for all patients in all eligible studies with respect to imaging modality. Freeman– Turkey double arcsine transformation was used to stabilize the proportion variances and we used the random-effect model, according to the DerSimonian and Laird method, due to expected between-study heterogeneity. Moreover, we used the Hartung-Knapp adjustment for random-effect models, which calculated more adequate error rates, especially when the number of studies included was small.27 We present each estimate with $95\%$ CI. Additionally, we compared the pooled proportions for available outcomes of both imaging modalities by performing an effect comparison test using a $\chi^{2}$ distribution with one degree of freedom. Heterogeneity was evaluated using the $\mathrm{I}^{2}$ statistic. Publication bias was evaluated with the Funnel plot and Egger test. The data were processed (by KP-B and MM) using the statistical software Stata v.15 and R 4.1.1, R Foundation for Statistical Computing, Vienna, Austria.  

# Certainty of the evidence  

We assessed the certainty of the body of evidence from eligible studies in quantitative synthesis according to the Cochrane recommendations.28 We used the Grading of Recommendation, Assessment, Development, and Evaluation (GRADE) approach. This critical appraisal was based on considerations such as study design, risk of bias, inconsistency, indirectness, imprecision, and publication bias by GRADE.29  

# Results  

# Study selection  

We identified a total of 9181 documents during the initial search and removed 5263 duplicates. In the review by title and abstract there were 173 potentially eligible documents (online supplemental figure 1). In the full-text evaluation, we excluded 141 documents due to the type of procedure, absence of data/ outcome of interest, or study design (online supplemental table 2). Finally, we included 32 studies which are described in table 1. One study presented separate data from patients selected by NCCT/CTA and $\mathrm{ADVI}^{1\bar{2}}$ and was therefore included in both groups.  

# Characteristics of the studies included  

A total of 4317 patients were enrolled in the 32 studies. Of these, 3176 patients were selected by ADVI and 1141 by NCCT/ CTA. Four studies were RCTs and the remaining 28 were cohort studies.1 2 30 31 The average age of the subjects ranged from 65 to 75 years. The median NIHSS ranged from 13 to 17.5 points. The median ASPECTS ranged from 7 to 9 in the ADVI group and from 8 to 9 in the NCCT/CTA group. In the ADVI group 13 studies reported use of IV-tPA with an overall use of $22.2\%$ and, in the NCCT/CTA group, 10 studies reported use of IV-tPA with an overall use of $25.3\%$ . Successful recanalization (defined as modified Thrombolysis in Cerebral Infarction $\begin{array}{r}{\mathrm{(mTICI)}\ge2\mathrm{b}.}\end{array}$ ) ranged from $57\%$ to $100\%$ in both groups. The calculated successful recanalization rate was $83.8\%$ in the ADVI group and $84.8\%$ in the NCCT/CTA group. Details of each study are included in table 1.  

# Risk of bias of the studies included  

We assessed the quality of 32 studies included (4 RCTs and 28 NRSIs). In the RCTs, most of the studies $(\mathrm{n}{=}3)$ presented some concerns of risk of bias; the domain with the highest risk of bias was the randomization process (some concerns: $75\%$ ). With regard to NRSIs, all 28 studies were at low and moderate risk of bias, with the confounding domain with some concerns (moderate: $57\%$ ; low: $36\%$ ). Details of each tool and domain are shown in online supplemental figures 2 and 3.  

# Efficacy and safety  

Functional independence among patients selected with ADVI was $44\%$ $95\%$ CI $39\%$ to $48\%$ ; $\bar{\mathrm{I}^{2}}\bar{=}80\%\rangle$ and with NCCT/CTA $48\%$ ( $95\%$ CI $41\%$ to $55\%$ ; $\scriptstyle{\mathrm{I}^{2}=75\%}$ , $\mathtt{p}{=}0.36$ , figure 1). When ADVI was used, functional independence with CTP and MRI was $42\%$ ( $95\%$ CI $35\%$ to $48\%$ ; $\mathrm{I}^{2}{=}75.2\%)$ and $53\%$ ( $95\%$ CI $37\%$ to $69\%$ ; $\scriptstyle{\mathrm{I}^{2}=90.5\%})$ , respectively.  

The mean mRS with ADVI was 2.88 ( $95\%$ CI 2.36 to 3.41; $\scriptstyle\mathrm{I}^{2}=0.0\%)$ and with NCCT/CTA 2.79 ( $95\%$ CI 2.31 to 3.27; $\mathrm{I}^{2}{=}0.0\%$ , $\scriptstyle{\mathrm{p}}=0.79$ , see online supplemental figure 4). In patients selected with CTP the mean mRS was 2.77 $(95\%\mathrm{CI}1.8\$ to 3.7; $\scriptstyle{\mathrm{I}^{2}=0.0\%})$ while in patients selected with MRI it was 2.9 ( $(95\%$ CI 1.6 to 4.1; $\mathrm{I}^{2}{=}\dot{3}.2\%\backslash$ ). Online supplemental figure 5 shows the distribution of the mRS scores between patients selected with ADVI and with NCCT/CTA.  

Mortality at 90 days was lower among patients selected with ADVI ( $13\%$ , $95\%$ CI $10\%$ to $17\%$ ; $\mathrm{I}^{2}{=}81\%$ ) than in patients selected with NCCT/CTA $(16\%$ , $95\%\mathrm{CI}12\%$ to $22\%$ ; $\mathrm{I}^{\bar{2}}{=}69\%$ , $\mathrm{p}{=}0.29$ , figure  2). The mortality rates in the CTP and MRI subgroups were $1{5}\%$ $95\%$ CI $10\%$ to $21\%$ ; $\mathrm{I}^{2}{=}78.1\%)$ and $6\%$ $95\%$ CI $1\%$ to $21\%$ ; $\mathrm{I}^{2}{=}92.1\%$ , respectively.  

The rates of sICH were $4\%$ with ADVI $(95\%\mathrm{CI}3\%$ to $7\%$ ; $\scriptstyle\mathrm{I}^{2}=73\%)$ ) versus $6\%$ with NCCT/CTA $(95\%\mathrm{CI}4\%$ to $8\%$ ; $\mathrm{I}^{2}{=}6\%$ , $\mathrm{p}{=}0.27$ , figure  3). Similar sICH rates were found for CTP and MRI with $5\%$ ( $95\%$ CI $2\%$ to $9\%$ ; $\mathrm{I}^{2}{=}73.6\%)$ and $3\%$ $95\%$ CI $1\%$ to $7\%$ ; $\mathrm{I}^{2}{=}24.7\%\backslash$ , respectively.  

# Certainty of the evidence  

The certainty of evidence was assessed for each outcome individually because not all outcomes were reported in every study analyzed. For all the outcomes evaluated, most studies were observational in design and less than $50\%$ of the studies were at low risk of bias. Apart from the mean mRS, all the outcomes had a high heterogeneity with an $\mathrm{I}^{2}>50\%$ . Nevertheless, indirectness and imprecision were not considered serious. Additionally, publication bias was not detected for any of the outcomes, either by the Egger test (online supplemental table 3) or funnel plot. Therefore, the certainty of evidence was determined as moderate for the mean mRS and low for the remaining outcomes, including the outcome of functional independence (online supplemental table 4).  

# Discussion  

Our study showed that functional independence at 90 days was similar in patients selected for EVT by ADVI $(44\%)$ and in those selected with NCCT/CTA $(48\%)$ in the extended time window. The proportion of functional independence is similar to those reported in the intervention groups in the $\mathrm{DAWN}^{1}$ and DEFUSE-­ $3^{2^{\circ}}$ trials ( $45\%$ and $49\%$ , respectively). More importantly, the reported rates of sICH and mortality are similar regardless of the imaging modality used for triage. Both studies used ADVI to select patients with two different paradigms. The DAWN study selected patients who could be treated from 6 to 24 hours based on a clinical mismatch profile that included criteria for age, ischemia core size based on CTP or DWI-­MRI, and the baseline NIHSS score. The DEFUSE-3 study included patients from 6 to 16 hours selected with a target perfusion mismatch profile using MRP or CTP.15  

In the early time window, a pooled analysis of five RCTs showed the proportion of functional independence to be similar to that of our results $(46\%)$ . Most of these patients did not use ADVI but some were treated beyond ${>}6$ hours (33 patients in the ESCAPE trial,30 33 13 in the REVASCAT trial,34 and 208 patients presented ${>}300\mathrm{min}$ from onset to randomization). In a post hoc analysis of one registry,12 patients treated in the extended time window had similar rates of functional independence using CTP or NCCT $(54.7\%$ vs $60.6\%$ ). Several observational and registry studies11–17 35 showed similar results, but with the limitation of an indirect comparison between imaging modality, since their main objective was to compare the early versus extended time window or EVT versus best medical treatment.

In the extended time window, patients with few early changes of ischemia on NCCT usually have a slow progression of infarct size36 due in part to good collateral circulation and a favorable biologic response to ischemia.37 A recent patient data meta-analysis from the AURORA group shows that patients may benefit from EVT compared to medical treatment within 24 hours, suggesting also that patients beyond 12 hours may benefit the most. That could be due to possible over selection. The imaging modality used for selection was CTP or MRI in 437/505 patients included in both groups (EVT and medical treatment). Only two of the six RCTs analyzed allowed NCCT for patient selection.38 Two of the six $\mathrm{RCTS}^{12}$ were included in our analysis but the others were excluded (Supplementary Table 2). The aim of using ADVI is to assess infarct core and salvageable tissue (penumbra)39 to identify suitable patients for EVT. However, there remains wide variation in practice on the utilization of non-invasive imaging modalities to identify these late window patients.40 All the modalities available (including multiphase CTA collateral, perfusion imaging using CTP and MRP) have technical limitations including real-world reproducibility and access issues, and an uncertain ischemic threshold for core infarct.41 42 More importantly, all these modalities are more reflective of the hemodynamic state at a specific time.43 ADVI is expensive both in terms of resources and time, presenting technical barriers and access limitations. Furthermore, due to over selection, ADVI could reduce the overall treatment rates of patients with LVO who would otherwise benefit from EVT. A recent study comparing treatment rates in centers with and without ADVI with CTP showed decreased treatment rates in CTP-screened patients.4 Patients treated at centers without CTP in this report had equivalent outcomes with higher population treatment rates.6 45

Based on available RCT data and the theoretical advantages of ADVI, we expected a higher proportion of functional independence in the ADVI selected group. Our results indicate that there might not be a difference. There was a non-statistically significant higher proportion of functional independence in patients selected without ADVI. One possible explanation could be that patients selected without ADVI were selected for EVT only if they had good scores in ASPECTS. Both DAWN and DEFUSE-3 trials included patients with ASPECTS 5–7. In our cohort there were no significant differences in the baseline ASPECTS scores, similar to a recent post hoc analysis.12 The ASPECTS score is an ordinal variable that needs a large sample to observe significant differences. Clinical core mismatch by ASPECTS has been reported in about $50\%$ of cases in the extended time window.46 In patients with a high NIHSS score with anterior circulation LVO, CT with a high ASPECTS score/minimal early signs of ischemia seems to be common and, as such, may be a simpler measure of core infarct which could allow selection for EVT and also result in better functional outcome s.11 35 39 47–49 Although there is good concordance between these two modalities, $1{5}\%$ of patients with large ischemic core on ADVI may still have a good ASPECTS score.50  

In contrast to our findings, a previously reported matched prospectively collected retrospective analysis found higher rates of functional independence in patients selected using ADVI $(52.9\%$ vs $40.4\%$ ; $\mathrm{p}{=}0.005\$ ). However, this study did not control for important imbalances in complete reperfusion $({\mathrm{TICI}}\geq3)$ ), which was significantly more common in the ADVI group $(54.8\%$ vs $40.1\%$ ; $\mathsf{p}{<}0.001\$ , and this likely skewed the outcome of the results. Examination of this study in comparison to our methodology highlights the strength of this work, which includes a large number of patients, systematic evaluation of bias, and the ability to better control for confounding.  

Despite the strengths of this study design, it is important to acknowledge its limitations. One of the most important limitations of our study is the fact that few studies directly compared outcomes in patients selected by different imaging strategies. Our analysis was based on indirect comparisons of proportions of different cohorts and subgroups sometimes without a matching comparison group in the same cohort. Therefore, due to the nature of the indirect comparison, we were not able to determine if there is a correlation/association between the functional outcome differences observed between the two imaging strategies. Our analysis was not able to assess the number of patients that were undertreated due to a missed diagnosis based on NCCT alone. There is evidence that ADVI could overestimate the ischemic core.42 43 However, underestimation is also possible. A direct comparison between these two imaging selection strategies is required in a large prospective RCT to determine if they are equivalent or if one is superior.  

We planned our meta-analysis to include both RCTs and nonRCTs. This pragmatic approach allowed assessment of realworld practice by including observational and registry studies. Selection bias and heterogeneity were higher in these studies compared with RCTs, but not to the point of invalidating our results. Using this broad range of studies, our systematic review allowed us to describe the state of the research and current practice in stroke centers around the world. In special settings, particularly when few RCTs are available, evidence synthesized based on observational studies could show similar effect estimates with no systematic differences.51  

Our results suggest that a simplified triage protocol does not seem to increase mortality or sICH in this population in the extended time window. These data provide support for the current practice in stroke centers that do not routinely use ADVI in the extended time window to select patients for EVT.52 This is particularly important in non-urban areas and middle-income countries in which LVO treatment rates are already quite low4 and other disparities are present.  

# Conclusions  

Our study suggests that the rates of functional independence may be similar when selecting anterior circulation patients using ADVI or NCCT/CTA for EVT in the extended time window. A simplified triage protocol does not seem to increase mortality or sICH. The limitations of this meta-analysis may pave the way for a definitive multicenter randomized trial evaluating both triage techniques and using structured standardized imaging protocols.
