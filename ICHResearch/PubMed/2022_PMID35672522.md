# Abstract  

Hemorrhagic stroke is a serious clinical condition that requires timely diagnosis. An artificial intelligence algorithm system called DeepCT can identify hemorrhagic lesions rapidly from non-contrast head computed tomography (NCCT) images and has received regulatory clearance. A non-controlled retrospective pilot clinical trial was conducted. Patients who received NCCT at the emergency department (ED) of Kaohsiung Veteran General Hospital were collected. From 2020 January- $1^{\mathrm{st}}$ to April- $30^{\mathrm{th}}$ , the physicians read NCCT images without DeepCT. From 2020May- $1^{\mathrm{st}}$ to August- $31^{\mathrm{st}}$ , the physicians were assisted by DeepCT. The length of ED stays (LOS) for the patients was collected. 2,999 patients were included (188 and 2811 with and without ICH). For patients with a final diagnosis of ICH, implementing DeepCT significantly shortened their LOS $\langle560.67\pm604.93\mathrm{m\Omega}$ in with DeepCT vs. $780.83\pm710.27\mathrm{mit}$ n without DeepCT; $p{=}0.0232)$ . For patients with a non-ICH diagnosis, the LOS did not significantly differ $(705.90\pm760.86\$ min with DeepCT vs. $679.45\pm681.97$ min without DeepCT; $p{=}0.3362^{\cdot}$ . For patients with ICH, those assisted with DeepCT had a significantly shorter LOS than those without DeepCT. For patients with a non-ICH diagnosis, implementing DeepCT did not affect the LOS, because emergency physicians need same efforts to identify the underlying problem(s) with or without DeepCT. In summary, implementing DeepCT system in the ED will save costs, decrease LOS, and accelerate patient flow; most importantly, it will improve the quality of care and increase the confidence and shorten the response time of the physicians and radiologists.

# Introduction  

Intracranial hemorrhage (ICH) refers to bleeding within the skull. It is an important cause of stroke, disability, and even death [1–3]. Although hemorrhagic stroke occurs in only $10\%{-}30\%$ of stroke patients, its severity is emphasized by the high mortality rate $(35\%-52\%)$ and high burden of disability-adjusted life-years (DALYs) [4–6]. A hematoma formed by expansion of a hemorrhagic stroke can reportedly worsen neurologic deficits, and irreversible damage can occur as early as within few hours from onset of hemorrhagic stroke, making a timely and accurate diagnosis crucial for appropriate management of these patients [7, 8].  

Non-contrast head computed tomography (NCCT) is widely used to diagnose brain strokes as it offers the benefits of 24-h availability, rapid image acquisition, lack of absolute contraindications, low cost, and high sensitivity to early signs of a stroke [9]. Therefore, NCCT is the most common imaging modality for ICH diagnosis in an emergency scenario [6, 8–10]. Unfortunately, timely and accurate diagnosis is a large burden to practicing radiologists [10]. On an average, a radiologist currently interprets one image every 3–4 s and one set of NCCT usually has 30–60 images [10].  

Recently, artificial intelligence (AI) has been applied to accelerate and standardize stroke detection. Machine learning (ML) is a subset of AI wherein algorithms learn from the data itself without explicit programming [11]. Deep learning (DL) is part of a broader family of ML methods based on architectures resembling human neural networks. Currently, one of the mainstream technologies in computer vision is through the use of convolutional neural networks (CNNs); this technology has been successfully used in medical research for image segmentation and computer-aided diagnosis [4, 8, 12–14]. Previously, we developed a deep CNN framework (DeepCT, Deep01 Limited, New Taipei City, Taiwan) to detect acute ICH from non-enhanced brain CT scans and obtained US and Taiwan regulation clearance.

Our DeepCT data were developed based on the Taiwanese National Health Insurance system. This system has all medication and diagnostic claims data of 23 million Taiwanese citizens (covers approximately $99.9\%$ of the total population in Taiwan). The system includes patients’ demographic information, number of prescriptions, the brand and generic name of the prescribed drugs, the date of prescription(s), dosage of medication, and diagnosis. The quality and completeness of this system is excellent, and data from this system have been used to conduct several high-quality researches [13, 15–17]. According to a previous report, the sensitivity and specificity of the DeepCT system for detecting ICH from NCCT were 0.9615 and 0.9538, respectively [18]. The area under the curve (AUC) of the receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve of our DL system was 0.9770 (as shown in Fig. 1). The average processing time of the DeepCT system was \~ 10  s using an NVIDIA GeForce GTX 1080 (Nvidia corporate, CA, US) [18]. The process of the DeepCT DL system is illustrated in Fig. 2.  

The hypothesis of this clinical pilot study was that DL implanted NCCT will assist with the detection of ICH and accelerate patient flow and shorten the LOS at the emergency department (ED).

# Methods  

This study was approved by the Joint Institutional Review Board (KSVGH20-CT10-12) of Kaohsiung Veteran General Hospital (KSVGH).  

The medical device used for the test was DeepCT (ver. 5; FDA 510(k) No.: K182875, TFDA license No.: MOHWMD-No. 006649).  



# Study design  

There were two stages in this study. The first stage was a smaller scale feasibility test to validate the diagnostic efficacy of the DeepCT system in a real clinical scenario. After validation, the second stage of the study was commenced.

The second stage of the study was a non-controlled, retrospective pilot clinical trial. From January $1^{\mathrm{st}}$ , 2020 to August $31^{\mathrm{st}}$ , 2020, data on patients who received NCCT at KSVGH emergency department (ED). The DeepCT system was imported and formally online on May $1^{\mathrm{st}}$ , 2020; therefore, the study was divided into two time periods. During the first period (January $1^{\mathrm{st}}$ , 2020 to April $30^{\mathrm{th}}$ , 2020), the physicians read NCCT without DeepCT assistance. Conversely, in the second period (May $1^{\mathrm{st}}$ , 2020 to August $31^{\mathrm{st}}$ , 2020), the physicians read NCCT in the same clinical scenario but with DeepCT assistance. Data from the two time periods were collected for further analysis.  

The length of ED stay (LOS) was defined as the time period from the patient registering at ED triage to the patient leaving ED for whatever reason.  



# Statistical analysis  

All data are expressed as mean $\pm$ standard deviation. Statistical analysis was performed with the statistical software SAS 9.4 ( ${\mathrm{SAS@}}$ Version 9.4, SAS Institute Inc., Cary, NC).  

General linear model (GLM) was used for analysis of variance (ANOVA) and Duncan’s new multiple range test. The result was considered statistically significant at $\mathrm{p}<0.05$ .

# Discussion  

The treatment of hemorrhagic strokes is a race against time. If the doctors make decisions and the pace of order execution accelerates, patients can receive more immediate treatment, thus improving the quality of treatment and further beneficial to the prognosis.  

According to emergent physicians’ interviews, when emergency department is visited by patients suspected as having ICH, emergency physicians should use DeepCT for faster decision-making and quickly executing medical orders. Traditionally, when emergency physicians suspect ICH from CT images, they need to confirm their diagnosis with a radiologist, which is a time-consuming process. Because DeepCT has stable sensitivity, specificity, and accuracy, the system can continuously and steadily provide a second opinion to frontline emergency physicians and radiologists, thus playing a critical role as a clinical decision support system (CDSS). This will not only increase the confidence of emergency teams in making medical decision but also quicken treatment administration and eventually speed up patient flow in the ED.  

According to our results, among patients with a confirmed diagnosis of ICH, those with a DeepCT systemassisted diagnosis had a significantly shorter response time $\cdot560.67\pm604.93\mathrm{~m~}$ in with DeepCT vs. $780.83\pm710.27\$ min without DeepCT; $p{=}0.0232;$ , as shown in Table 2. This suggests that in patients with ICH, implementing the DeepCT system will expedite the diagnosis and increase confidence of emergency physicians while facilitating treatment execution and eventually shortening the LOS. Although the high accuracy of DL-integrated NCCT in ICH diagnosis has been previously reported [8, 14, 19], our study provides evidence that the use of the DeepCT system shortens patients’ LOS at the ED.

From a hospital management perspective, the DeepCT system can significantly shorten ICH patients’ LOS at the ED and increase the turnover rate of emergency beds. According to the literature, more than $31\%$ medical incidents are caused by congestion in the ED [20]. In other words, introducing the DeepCT system in the ED can not only alleviate the congestion in the ED but also help promptly transfer ICH patients to a specialized ward or unit to get faster and better treatment. The calculated personnel costs per patient bed-hour were calculated to be $\$58.20$ foranurbanacademicmedicalcenterEDineasternUS [21].Accordingtoourreport, theLOSshortenedbyapproximately $3.67\mathrm{~h~}$ afterimplementingtheDeepCTsystemintheEDforICHpatients, suggestinganapproximatecostsavingof $\$210$ per patient for the hospital. In summary, implementing this system in the ED for patients suspected as having ICH saves significant amount of time and indirectly represents a lot of cost savings for the hospital; this will be an effective plan for lean healthcare management of the hospital.  

In the emergency diagnostic process, the relationship between CDSS and physicians is not a competition or mutual exclusion. Instead, CDSS assisted physicians in making decisions more quickly and working with the parallel unit (radiology department) to deal with acute patients in need. Similarly, DeepCT does not challenge the profession of radiologists but rather provides additional reference information. The subject will be recognized faster by both parties, and the efficiency of communication between the ED and radiology department regarding patients with acute ICH will be improved. According to previous literature, DLintegrated CDSS reportedly has stable diagnostic quality and high diagnostic speed and facilitates productive collaboration between physicians and radiologists to speed up the diagnosis [22–25].  

Conversely, for patients whose final diagnosis was nonICH, implementing DeepCT did not affect the LOS at the ED. This finding was opposite to that of a previous report [26]. According to the report, for patients without ICH, the LOS significantly shortened from 471 to $425\mathrm{min}$ with DLintreated CDSS. On the contrary, our results showed much longer LOS for non-ICH patients, with or without DeepCT. This contradiction may be explained by a different ED workflow. In our workflow, when ICH-related problems were excluded from a patient’s diagnosis, emergency physicians spend same effort to identify the underlying problem(s) that brought the patient to the ED, with or without the help of DeepCT.  

Despite the promising results of this study, this study has some limitations. First, this was a retrospective study. Although retrospective studies save time and money, they cannot provide information not included in the database. As the data were not collected in a predesigned proforma as per the specific requirements of the study, in most cases, data on some parameters were inevitably missing. To collect valuable information and further understand the impact of implementing the DeepCT system in the clinical scenario, further prospective clinical trials are needed in the future. Second, as this was a single-institutional pilot study, the included patient number and diversity in this study are definitely not comparable to those in a multi-center large-scale clinical trial. Although the DeepCT system has FDA 510(k) and TFDA clearance, further data validation in a large-scale clinical trial will make the diagnostic efficiency of the system more robust. Finally, implementing DeepCT system in the clinical workflow is still in its early stages, and several unknowns need to be clarified. For example, it remains undetermined whether the DL system has psychological or other unforeseen effects on the physicians, radiologists, hospitals, and patients. Further large-scale studies must be conducted to provide a deeper insight and prevent potential problems.  

In conclusion, our report clearly demonstrates that implementing the DeepCT system in the ED workflow saves costs per patient for the hospital, decreases the LOS at the ED, and accelerates patient flow; most importantly, it improves the quality of care given by emergent physicians and radiologists and increases their confidence and shortens their response time.
