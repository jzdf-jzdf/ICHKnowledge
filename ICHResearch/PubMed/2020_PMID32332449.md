# ABSTRACT  

The purpose of this study was to evaluate the impact of implementing a care bundle for preventing periintraventricular hemorrhage (PIVH) in preterm newborns. A longitudinal, quantitative, quasi-experimental study was conducted with preterm newborns from a neonatal unit. The study was divided into 2 stages: the first consisted of a retrospective cohort of newborns (control group) not exposed to the bundle, and the second consisted of 5 practical steps of implementing a care bundle for preventing PIVH in eligible newborns. The results show that a significant reduction in PIVH occurred, from $34.8\%$ before the intervention to $26.3\%$ after application of the bundle. Key Words: cerebral hemorrhage, newborn, premature birth  

P remature birth brings a high economic cost, considering neonatal intensive care and future education. Decreased gestational age (GA) is associated with increased mortality, disability, special educational needs, and continuing healthcare.1  

Worldwide, about 15 million babies are born prematurely each year and $40\%$ of all deaths among children younger than 5 years are due to prematurity. Neonatal deaths could be avoided, especially in low-income countries where intensive care is scarce, by using highquality evidence-based care based on guidelines.2,3  

Peri-intraventricular hemorrhage (PIVH) is the second most frequent cause of death in preterm infants, preceded only by hyaline membrane disease,4 and one of the main causes of neurological damage in preterm newborns (PTNBs), especially those of very low birth weight. The worldwide incidence of PIVH ranges from $3.70\%$ to $44.68\%$ , and it ranges from $26\%$ to $51\%$ in Brazil.4,5  

After the PIVH is established, no specific treatment exists to limit bleeding.6,7 However, PIVH can be prevented by implementing early interventions to maintain the PTNB’s stability and avoid fluctuations in the cerebral blood flow and blood pressure.8 These interventions include central positioning of the head along the midline, adequate respiratory support avoiding high-frequency ventilation, avoidance of physical therapy maneuvers, constant blood pressure maintenance, interventions to minimize pain and stress, caution in the administration of dexamethasone,4,8–10 noise control,11 and respect for the sleep-wake cycle.9  

Measures as the ones cited earlier can be found in care protocols, which are sets of rules and guidelines intended to provide information, based on the current best evidence of clinical and cost-effectiveness regarding therapeutic interventions for given conditions, such as PIVH.12 Care protocols are also called “bundles.” The Institute for Healthcare Improvement defines care bundle as a small set of evidence-based interventions13 aimed at improving the processes of care and patient outcomes.14 Care bundles for preventing PIVH in PTNBs are particularly needed as the literature suggests that babies born before 34 weeks and weighing less than $1500~\mathrm{g}$ are vulnerable to PIVH.15  

The motivation for developing this study arose from the need for simple, low-cost, and evidence-based technologies to reduce the incidence of PIVH in a Brazilian neonatal intensive care unit (NICU). Therefore, we aimed to evaluate the impact of implementing a care bundle for preventing PIVH in PTNBs.  



# METHODS  

A quasi-experimental trial was performed in 2 stages: the first, a retrospective cohort (control group) of newborns not exposed to the care bundle, and the second prospective stage that consisted of a group of PTNBs who received care based on a care bundle for preventing PIVH. The first stage was conducted to raise PIVH rates in a selected NICU before we started the intervention, since these rates were unknown. The research was registered on January 25, 2019, on the Brazilian Clinical Trials Register site (registration: UTN no. RBR-5s4yqh).

# Measures and procedures  



# Study design  

The clinical variables were the factors related to the higher incidence and protection for PIVH: gender (male, female), type of birth (vaginal, cesarean), number of antenatal visits, weight at birth, and GA.4,6,8,16–19  

The study included PTNBs with GA of 34 weeks or less and/or birth weight of $1500~\mathrm{g}$ or less, who underwent BUS in the first week of life, hospitalized in a neonatal unit of a reference maternity hospital in the city of Fortaleza, Brazil. PTNBs with congenital malformations, genetic syndromes, and congenital infections of the TORCHS group (syphilis, rubella, herpes, toxoplasmosis, and cytomegalovirus) and those who died before the first ultrasound examination or were transferred to another institution were excluded.  

The outcome variable of this study is the presence of PIVH, analyzed in grades I, II, III, and IV, according to the classification of Papile et al20: grade I, hemorrhage restricted to the germinative matrix; grade II, intraventricular hemorrhage without ventricular dilatation; grade III: intraventricular hemorrhage with ventricular dilatation; and grade IV, parenchymal  

The retrospective stage or control stage occurred from the analysis of medical records in the Medical and Statistical Archive Service of children born during the period from January to June 2016, with GA 34 weeks or less and/or birth weight $1500~\mathrm{g}$ or less. These newborns received routine NICU care, without the use of protocols, since at that time, no care bundles for preventing PIVH were being used at the hospital.  

The second and prospective stage was carried out during the period from August 2016 to January 2017. In this stage, the care bundle was applied to the intervention group. No randomization was used because the care bundle was applied to all PTNBs according to the study’s inclusion and exclusion criteria.  

A copy of the care bundle was placed in the delivery room and at the entrance of each neonatal unit of the research institution, and a miniature of the care bundle was placed in each incubator to identify the newborns participating in the study. The multidisciplinary team was trained during 6 meetings of 30 minutes each to clarify doubts about the use of the care bundle and to emphasize its importance in the prevention of PIVH. Because of the nature of the intervention, blinding was not possible.  

The care bundle used in this study consisted of 5 actions and was applied to all PTNBs who met the inclusion and exclusion criteria, from birth up to 72 hours of life, and no baby was removed from the sample until the end of the data collection period. The application of the care bundle in the first 72 hours of life is justified by the high incidence of PIVH, which is about $50\%$ in the first day of life and reaching $90\%$ until the third day of life, emphasizing the need for preventive measures during this period.6 The care bundle was conceived on the basis of evidence-based medicine, which aims to bring the best outcomes to the patient through the best evidence (see Table 1).  

We emphasize that no weight measurements were taken during the first 72 hours of interventions, as recommended by the care bundle. To ensure that this and other recommendations such as positioning were being followed, a researcher from the team spent 6 months at the NICU observing how care was being provided to the participating newborns. In addition, all health workers were aware of the use of the care bundle and were instructed to register the occurrence of breaks in the care bundle and the reasons for that in the medical records, so the infant could be excluded from the sample.

Prospective data collection was performed using the daily care bundle checklist and through follow-up of the medical and daily notes of nurses and physiotherapists in the medical records. The intervention was evaluated by the daily bundle checklist in the first 72 hours of intervention and by the medical, nursing, and physiotherapy records to identify adherence to the bundle actions. Adherence to the bundle was established when the team managed to perform all 5 interventions proposed in the bundle in the first 72 hours of a child’s life.  



# Analysis  

Statistical analysis of the results was performed using the IBM SPSS Statistics 20.0 program. The control and intervention groups were compared using Pearson’s $\chi^{2}$ test for the qualitative variables and Student’s $t$ and Mann-Whitney tests to compare the means between the 2 groups. In addition, an analysis of variance was used for comparisons involving more than 2 groups. The $P$ value was considered less than .05 as a level of statistical significance.  



# Ethical considerations  

The research was approved by the Ethics Research Committee of the Maternity School Assis Chateaubriand, and all methods were in accordance with the relevant guidelines and regulations. After explaining the study and confirming the newborns’ eligibility and the parents’ willingness to participate, the researchers obtained informed consent of the children’s parents. The hospital manager allowed the use of the medical records for the retrospective stage.  



# RESULTS  

In the retrospective stage, 198 premature infants were born with GA of 34 weeks or less and/or birth weight of $1500~\mathrm{g}$ or less. Of these, 20 $(10.1\%)$ medical records were not found, 39 $(19.6\%)$ did not undergo BUS during the first week of life, 41 $(20.7\%)$ died before the first BUS, 18 $(9\%)$ were transferred because of overcrowding of the neonatal unit, and 3 $(1.5\%)$ were excluded according to the criteria defined in the research methodology. Thus, 77 $(39\%)$ premature infants comprised the control group.  

During the intervention stage, 186 premature infants were born. Of these, 38 $(20.4\%)$ died before the first BUS, 11 $(5.9\%)$ were transferred to other hospitals because of overcrowding of the neonatal unit, and $5~(2.6\%)$ were excluded according to the criteria defined in the research methodology. Thus, 132 $(71\%)$ premature infants underwent BUS in the first week of life, hence complying with the study’s inclusion criteria, and received bundle protocol application in the first 72 hours of life.  

Regarding the characterization of newborns, the proportion of males and females was equal in both groups $\left(P=.576\right)$ . Regarding birth weight, the mean of the control group was $1310.09\mathrm{~g~}$ $\mathrm{\Delta{\langleSD=405.64\rangle}}$ , and the mean GA was 30 weeks $\mathrm{(SD=2.8)}$ . In the intervention group, the mean birth weight of the study population was $1376.49\mathrm{~g~}$ $\mathrm{(SD=406.20)}$ and the mean GA was 30 weeks 4 days $\mathrm{\check{S}D}=2.3\$ ).  

As for the overall mean, GA and birth weight were not statistically significant different for both groups. The difference was close to significance between the type of birth (vaginal and cesarean), with $P=.060$ , and statistically significant difference between the number of prenatal consultations of the 2 groups, with $P=.005$ (see Table 2).  

Regarding the presence of PIVH in the control group, of the 77 preterm infants who underwent BUS in the first week of life, 69 $(34.8\%)$ were diagnosed with PIVH. In the intervention group, of the 132 PTNBs, 49 $26.3\%)$ were diagnosed with PIVH.  

When weight and GA were stratified, a statistically significant difference was observed for the onset of PIVH in infants with weight from 1000 to $1499\mathrm{~g~}$ and GA from 28 to 31 weeks for both groups (see Table 3).  

As for data of the intervention stage, there was $100\%$ adherence to the bundle protocol, except for the item not performing routine orotracheal tube aspiration, since 59 preterm infants were not intubated within the first 24 hours of life, 67 had not been intubated between 24 and 48 hours of life, and 68 preterm infants had not been intubated between 48 and 72 hours of life. This rate was calculated using the data recorded in the checklist performed daily by the researcher during 6 months of the research (see Table 4).

# DISCUSSION  

The bundle protocol was applied in the intervention group during the preterm infants’ first 72 hours of life. The control group was constituted for comparison with the same characteristics of the intervention group through data collected in medical records in the Medical and Statistical Archive Service.  

The study presented the results of applying a care bundle protocol to avoid PIVH in preterm newborns. The profile of infants who developed PIVH before and after the care bundle was not different regarding sex, weight, and GA. A statistically significant difference was observed in the number of prenatal consultations. However, studies have shown that males are considered at a higher risk for the development of PIVH than females.6,21  

When weight and GA were stratified, a statistically significant difference was observed for the onset of PIVH in infants with weight from 1000 to $1499\mathrm{~g~}$ for both groups and GA between 25 and 29 weeks for the intervention group and between 30 to 34 weeks for the control group. Prematurity and low birth weight are the main risk factors for PIVH.22 In a retrospective cohort study conducted in the United States between January 1999 and May 2004 involving 574 preterm infants with body weight of $1000~\mathrm{g}$ or less, the authors concluded that with each weeklong increase in GA, the probability of presenting severe PIVH decreased by $19\%$ .22  

Data on the birth weight and PIVH of the intervention group were closer to those reported in the international literature, confirming the statistically significant difference in which grade I PIVH represents $10\%$ of the cases evaluated in newborns with birth weight between 401 and $1500~\mathrm{g}$ .23  

Being born outside a perinatal tertiary center and normal delivery are considered risk factors for PIVH, whereas cesarean delivery is considered protective for PIVH.21 These data may justify the higher rates of cesarean delivery in the infants being investigated. The difference was close to statistically significant regarding the type of delivery in this study.  

The number of visits alone was not associated with preterm birth, but a significant association was found between the content and timing of care and preterm birth.24 In the present study, there was a statistically significant difference between the number of prenatal consultations between the control and intervention groups.

Regarding the adherence to the bundle protocol, in the 5 selected points, adherence almost reached $100\%$ . Adherence to the bundle is measured using “all or nothing,” that is, if at least one of the steps is not followed, the patient no longer receives the best care possible and so the adherence must be greater than $95\%$ .13  

Regarding the outcome variable, the present study showed a reduction in the incidence of PIVH of $8.5\%$ after application of the care bundle protocol and $12.1\%$ in its most severe forms (grades III and IV), in which neurological sequelae were compromised (see Table 5) and cerebral palsy occurred $(P=.000)$ . In the control group, in which there was no application of the care bundle, there was an incidence of $34.8\%$ of PIVH cases, with an incidence of $18.8\%$ in its most severe forms (grades III and IV). In the intervention group, after application of the care bundle, the incidence of PIVH was $26.3\%$ , with only $6.1\%$ of PIVH in its most severe forms (grades III and IV).  

We have found no studies in literature that analyzed the effects of this care bundle on PIVH incidence in PTNBs, which limited our discussion.  



# Limitations  

Although the application of the bundle has shown improvement in the quality of care for preterm infants in the studied neonatal unit, the incidence of this condition is still considered high $(37\%)$ compared with the rates of PIVH in American preterm infants $(20\%-25\%)$ . In hospitals linked to the Brazilian Neonatal Research Network, the incidence was $28.8\%$ in 2015.8,26  

Regarding the severity of PIVH, we found a predominance of grade I PIVH in the intervention group with $93.9\%$ of the cases, and in the control group with $81.2\%$ of the diagnosed cases. The results can be considered similar to previous studies, in which there is a predominance of grade I and II PIVH.27–29 Therefore, it is necessary to expand the study for implementation in larger samples to reduce the high incidence of this pathology in Brazil.  



# Clinical implications  

By understanding the importance of the fact that promotion of qualified care and patient safety for the maintenance of children’s health, the study presented how the use of a low-cost tool and easy operationalization can help prevent hospitalized preterm newborns. The study findings suggest that children benefited using bundle will present fewer complications associated with prematurity and hospitalization, contributing to a better quality of life.  



# CONCLUSION  

Based on the results of this study, a care bundle with 5 practical actions of the multidisciplinary team aimed at preventing PIVH can be directly related to quality improvement, guaranteeing safe care for the preterm newborn.
