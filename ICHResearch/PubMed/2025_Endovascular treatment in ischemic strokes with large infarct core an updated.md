# Endovascular treatment in ischemic strokes with large infarct core: an updated systematic review and meta-analysis of randomized controlled trials  

Marco Longoni1 $\circledcirc$ · Sebastiano Giacomozzi1 $\cdot$ Leonardo Pantoni2 · Simone Vidale3  

Received: 27 May 2024 / Accepted: 17 September 2024 / Published online: 30 September 2024   
$\circledcirc$ Fondazione Società Italiana di Neurologia 2024  

# Abstract  

Background  Endovascular treatment (EVT) is recommended for acute ischemic stroke due to large-vessel occlusion (LVO) and an ASPECTs $\geq6$ . Recent randomized controlled trials (RCTs) have become available on effect of EVT in patients with LVO-related large core infarct stroke (ASPECTS 0–5). We conducted a systematic review and meta-analysis of trials on patients with large core infarct treated with thrombectomy compared to best medical therapy (BMT).  

Methods  The study followed PRISMA guidelines. Primary endpoint was functional independence at 90 days (modified Rankin Scale $\mathrm{mRS}<3.$ ). Secondary endpoints were 3-month moderate disability $\mathrm{(mRS<4)}$ , excellent outcome $\mathrm{(mRS<2)}$ ) and change in mRS (shift analysis). Safety outcomes were: symptomatic intracranial hemorrhage (sICH) and 3-month mortality. Results  Seven RCTs were included with a total of 1964 patients. Functional independence was significantly more frequent in EVT vs BMT group ( $19.4\%$ vs $8\%$ ; $\mathrm{OR}=2.72\$ , $95\%\mathrm{CI}=2.06{-}3.61$ $=0.08$ ; I2: $47\%$ ). Moderate outcome was also more prevalent in EVT group $\mathrm{\DeltaOR}=2.00$ ; $95\%\mathrm{CI}=1.61-2.48$ Pheterogeneiy $=0.17$ ; I2: $46\%$ ) as well as excellent outcome (OR: 1.54, $95\%\mathrm{CI}=1.07-2.22$ Pheterogeneisr $=0.13$ ; I2: $40\%$ ). Shift analysis was also significant with OR 1.59 $(\mathrm{CI}=1.33-1.82$ and $\mathrm{p}<0.001\$ ). Finally sICH, that occurred in 68 patients, was more frequent in EVT $\mathrm{\DeltaOR}=1.63\$ , $95\%\mathrm{CI}=0.99-2.69$ , Pheterogenity $=0.68$ ; I2: $0\%$ ) while $3\textrm{m}$ mortality was reduced in EVT ( $31\%$ vs $37\small{,}1\%$ , OR $0.76\:\mathrm{CI}{=}0.62{-}0.92)$ .  

Conclusions  This updated pooled data show that, in LVO-stroke patients with a large core infarct, EVT plus BMT (as compared to BMT alone) increases significantly the chances of achieving a good functional outcome at 90 days and reduces the 3- month mortality despite a marginal increase in acute sICH.  

Keywords  Large ischemic stroke $^{\ast}$ Mechanical thrombectomy $\cdot$ Meta-analysis $^{\ast}$ Randomized controlled trial  

# Introduction  

In patients with large vessel occlusion (LVO), endovascular treatment (EVT) with or without intravenous thrombolysis. (IVT) is effective in improving functional outcome [1, 2] The efficacy has been clearly demonstrated in patients with small and medium infarct core, while the majority of pivotal thrombectomy RCTs excluded patients with large core LVOrelated strokes. Indeed, in the HERMES collaboration, the median ASPECTS of treated patients was 8 (IQR 7– 9) [3] Considering the consistent proportion of stroke presenting with an ASPECT ${<}6$ and the poor prognosis associated with standard treatment, many RCTs were designed to compare BMT vs. EVT revealing a benefit of EVT in terms of functional outcome [4–9]. As RCTs became available over the last months [4–9], and were not fully included in previous meta-analyses [10, 11], we aimed to provide an updated synthesis of high-quality data.  

We performed a systematic review and meta-analysis of RCTs investigating the efficacy and safety of EVT of large core infarct stroke.  

# Methods  

# Search strategy and inclusion criteria  

The methods of this study-level systematic review and meta-analysis followed PRISMA [12] guideline and study protocol was deposited with OSF (https://doi.org/ 10.17605/OSF.IO/CPW97). Two reviewers systematically searched Pubmed, EMBASE and Cochrane Central register of Controlled Trials for studies investigating the efficacy and safety of EVT in large core ischemic strokes and published between January 1990 and February 2024. Search strategy included the combination of the following terms: “stroke”, “cerebrovascular disorder\*”, “thrombectomy”, “large infarct” or “large core”, as either keywords or MeSH terms (Supplementary Material). Reference lists and cited articles were also reviewed to increase the identification of relevant studies. In this pooled analysis, we included only RCTs comparing the clinical efficacy and safety of EVT or combined treatment with intravenous thrombolysis among adult $\stackrel{\rightharpoonup}{\hphantom{.}}$ years) patients with acute ischemic stroke due to LVO and with established large core infarct (ASPECTS score $<6$ ). All the RCTs found were written in English. The interventional group comprised patients treated with EVT with or without IVT, while the control group was represented by patients treated only with best medical treatment (BMT) endovenous thrombolysis included.  

# Outcomes  

The primary endpoint was functional independence at 90 days from stroke onset, defined as modified Rankin Scale (mRS) 0–2. Secondary outcome endpoints were (i) moderate $(\mathrm{mRS~}0{-}3)$ and (ii) excellent functional (mRS 0–1) outcomes (iii) ordinal shift analysis for mRS scores (reported for the available studies). Secondary safety outcomes were: (i) symptomatic intracranial haemorrhage (sICH), defined according to trial-specific adjudication criteria, (ii) mortality at 3 months. Two reviewers independently extracted data concerning baseline and outcome characteristics of each included study. We reported the lack of data on outcome, when appropriate. Risk of bias was assessed and reported according to the recommendations of the Cochrane Handbook for Systematic Reviews of Intervention [13].  

Heterogeneity was evaluated with Cochrane’s Q test and $\mathrm{I}^{2}$ , with fixed and random-effects models applied accordingly. We pooled data from intervention group and control group, reporting results by means of odds ratio (OR) and $95\%$ confidence interval (CI) for all outcomes, and using forest plots for graphical representation. Data analysis was performed using Review Manager (version 5.3, The Cochrane Collaboration 2012, Copenhagen, Denmark). We also calculated the size effects with CIs for subgroups of patients treated or not with EVT using ProMeta (version 3.0 – Internovi, 2015).

Low risk of bias emerged in two RCTs [4, 5] in relation to missing data on patients lost to follow-up (Table 1). Similarly, significant publication bias were not detected as it is shown in the Funnel plot analysis (Fig. 1 in Supplementary Material). No significant differences emerged for cardiovascular risk factors distribution across EVT vs BMT groups (Table 1 Supplement).  

After pooling the results from the included studies, primary endpoint was observed in $19.4\%$ of cases in EVT group and $8\%$ of cases in BMT group $\mathrm{{(OR=2.72}}$ , $95\%\mathrm{CI}=2.06{-}3.61$ , Chi2: 11.33, Pheterogeneity $=0.08$ , I2: $47\%$ ; Fig. 2A). Excellent functional outcome rates were higher in EVT group ( $8\%$ for EVT vs. $5.2\%$ for BMT group; $\mathrm{OR}=1.54\$ ; $95\%\mathrm{CI}=1.07{-2.22}$ : Chi2: 9.94, Pheterogeniy $=0.13$ , I2: $46\%$ ; Fig. 2C). The distribution of mRS scores at 90 days followup showed a significant benefit of EVT compared to BMT (Fig. 2D) with OR 1.59 of shifting to a better mRS in EVT group $(\mathrm{CI}=1.33-1.82\$ and $p<0.001\$ ).  

Pooling data from 6 studies, sICH was marginally more frequent in EVT vs BMT group ( $4.4\%$ vs $2.8\%$ ; $\mathrm{OR}=1.63\$ , $95\%\mathrm{CI}=0.99-2.69$ Chi2: 3.14, Pheterogeneity $=0.68$ ; I2: $0\%$ ; Fig. 3). Mortality at 3 months was inferior in EVT group ( $31\%$ vs. $37\small{,}1\%$ , OR $0.76\:\mathrm{CI}=0.62{-}0.92$ ).  

We finally evaluate the overall effect size of EVT therapy for the primary endpoint in thrombolysed patients respect to patient treated only with EVT depicting a stronger effect of treatment in the latter patients (overall E.S.: 0.36; $95\%\mathrm{CI}$ : 0.17–0.54; $\mathrm{p}<0.001;$ in respect of patients treated with a bridge approach (E.S.: 0.19; $95\%\mathrm{CI}$ : 0.05–0.34; $p$ : 0.008; Fig. 2 Supplement).  

# Discussion  

Our study represents the first updated pooled analysis including only RCTs that compared EVT vs. BMT in large core ischemic strokes.

We might assume that, in patients with large core infarct and LVO, EVT plus BMT as compared to BMT alone carries a significant increase in chances of achieving good functional outcome at 3 months.  

Indeed, EVT was associated with a 2.7-fold higher chance of achieving a good functional outcome and a twofold higher chance to be able to walk at 3 months and finally a 1.5-fold higher chance to achieve an excellent functional outcome.  

This latter result is in line with previous meta-analysis [15] and might be considered cautiously since the amount of patient reaching an $\mathrm{mRS}=\mathrm{0}{-}1$ at 3 months is very limited (80 vs 51) highlighting a ceiling effect for EVT in large core infarct and the need to extend outcome evaluation at least at 12 months [16].  

The probability of sICH tended to increase in EVT group, probably due to hemorrhagic transformation in a successful reperfused large ischemic tissue. Nevertheless the clinical impact of sICH seems not relevant if we consider data on functional outcome and mortality at 3 months.  

Indeed a significant reduction of mortality was revealed, thus confirming that vessel recanalization and tissue reperfusion, despite increasing the occurrence of intracerebral hemorrhage are pivotal for good outcome and survival also in this subset of large ischemic core patients.  

Although we found slightly differences in treatment window, baseline NIHSS and ASPECTS entry criteria (Table 1S), heterogeneity encompassing trial results was relatively low thus emphasizing the robustness of the treatment effect across studies.  

Moreover, it is not surprising that the overall effect size of EVT for the primary endpoint was more relevant in patients not treated with intravenous thrombolysis respect to rT-PA patients considering the higher risk of intracerebral hemorrhage in large core infarction. Our results add and put into context previous studies and meta-analysis, which were limited by the availability of RCTs [17–19] and/or by the quality of available observational studies [10, 19, 20]. Data included in previous meta- analysis derived from studies with consistent variability in neuroradiological criteria for inclusion, ranging from $\mathrm{ASPECTS}=6$ , with clear indication to treatment, to $\mathrm{ASPECTS}=0$ , therefore unlikely to have any benefit from intervention [9, 18, 19]. A previous meta-analysis of RCTs [10] including only three trials (RESCUE-JAPAN, SELECT2, ANGEL-ASPECT) [6–8], although estimating a positive treatment effect for EVT regarding mRS 0–3, did not provide data on excellent outcome (mRS 0–1) and showed a non-significant increase in sICH rates in EVT arm [9]. As the overall sample size nearly doubled in this metaanalysis compared to the latter, there now seems to be robust data supporting a positive treatment effect for mRS 0–1, mRS 0–2 and mRS 0–3, despite an increase in sICH rates.  

Evolution in time metrics and devices might explain the higher rates of good functional outcome revealed by recent RCTs [4–8] compared to older [10]. Moreover, it is important to underline that all the trials included in our analysis were conducted in comprehensive stroke centers with large experience in EVT techniques and relatively fast transit from diagnostics to interventional suite. Therefore, the caseload and experience may have played a role in determining a rate of sICH as low as $4.7\%$ in EVT-treated individuals with large core [4–8, 10].  

Finally, we might consider that these pooled results might support a paradigm shift in LVO patients’ selection, leading to more pragmatic protocols counting on non-contrast CT and Angio CT only also in late window treatment.  

However, it is worthy to underline the limitations of our results. First, this is a study-level meta-analysis, therefore, no individual patient data analysis was available from published studies. Second, we could not evaluate treatment effect based on single-point ASPECTs, as per-ASPECT group data were not available. Third, it should be underlined that all trials had the same limitation regarding treatment blinding, an issue that seems hard to limit given the very nature of the condition. Fourth, because we also included IMS-III RCTs [13], our estimates for benefit from EVT may appear slightly reduced as such RCTs counted on time metrics and devices dating back to the 2014 era.  

As a conclusion of this study, we suggest considering that in LVO low ASPECTs stroke patients EVT therapy is feasible, safe and can, at best, provide higher chance of recovery, but hardly bring the patient back to their functional status before the stroke.
