# Introduction  

Progressive neurological deterioration (ND) occurs about $10{-}30\%$ in patients with acute intracerebral hemorrhage (ICH) [1–3]. Because ND could cause a high mortality and a poor functional outcome [1, 2], identifying patients at high risk of ND is crucial for improving their prognosis. The majority of ND is considered to develop at a very early stage after the onset of hemorrhage [2], thus early ND (END; ${\leq}24\mathrm{~h~}$ ) has been extensively examined in previous studies [1–5]. Meanwhile, ND also can occur in subacute and chronic phases [6]. Late ND (LND) appears to have a major influence on the prognosis of ICH [6]; however, there are few prospective data on the LND.  

To elucidate the risk factors of LND in subjects with spontaneous ICH relative to those with no LND and to examine whether intensive systolic blood pressure (SBP) lowering treatment can prevent the LND, we performed exploratory analyses of data from a large multicenter randomized controlled trial, the Antihypertensive Treatment of Acute Cerebral Hemorrhage 2 (ATACH-2) [7]. In addition, we assessed the association between LND and the clinical outcome.  

# Materials and Methods  

The study design and primary results of this international, multicenter, open-label, randomized controlled trial (ATACH-2) have been described in previous publications [7, 8]. Briefly, the study included 1,000 participants with spontaneous supratentorial ICH and elevated $\mathrm{SBP}{\geq}180\mathrm{mmHg}$ who were eligible to be randomized within $4.5\mathrm{~h~}$ of ICH symptom onset. The patients were enrolled from 110 sites in 6 countries and randomly assigned to either intensive (target SBP: $110{-}139\mathrm{mm}\mathrm{Hg}$ ) or standard antihypertensive treatment (target SBP: $140{-}179\mathrm{mmHg}$ ). The respective hourly minimum SBP target was to be achieved primarily with nicardipine for $24\mathrm{~h~}$ . The study protocol was approved by the appropriate Ethics Committee at each participating site, and all participants or their legally authorized representative provided written informed consent.  

Demographic and clinical characteristics were recorded at the time of enrollment. Noncontrast computed tomographic (CT) scans were obtained at baseline prior to and at $24\mathrm{~h~}$ after randomization. Baseline and $^{24-\mathrm{h}}$ CT scans were forwarded to the core imaging analysis center. The central reader, blinded to the treatment and clinical data, reviewed all CT scans and recorded findings on a case report form including: (1) the site of hemorrhage; (2) the presence or absence of blood in the ventricles; and (3) the volume of the parenchymal hematoma, using a computerized system for image analysis according to the method described previously [9]. ND was reported by the site personnel and defined as a decrease of ${\ge}2$ points on Glasgow Coma Scale (GCS) or increase of ${\ge}4$ points on National Institutes of Health Stroke Scale (NIHSS) score from baseline that was not related to sedation or hypnotic use and was sustained for at least $^{8\mathrm{h}}$ [10]. In this exploratory analysis, the primary outcome of interest – occurrence of LND – was defined as ND occurring after $24\mathrm{h}$ to 7 days after stroke onset. NDs were captured on the adverse events case report form. All adverse events were reported through 7 days post-onset or discharge (whichever was earlier). Afterward, only serious adverse events were reported.  

# Statistical Analysis  

We compared baseline characteristics between subjects with and without LND using Wilcoxon two-sample test for continuous variables not normally distributed, $t$ test for normally distributed continuous variables, and $\chi^{2}$ test for categorical variables. Logistic regression analysis was performed to examine the association between the putative risk factors and LND. We selected 7 variables (past history of stroke, END, treatment assignment, baseline hematoma volume, hematoma volume increase in $24\mathrm{~h~}$ , presence of intraventricular hemorrhage [IVH], and lobar hemorrhage) for inclusion in the adjusted regression model on the basis of previous research and the results of the univariate analysis of association with LND at the two-sided alpha level of 0.20. Hematoma volume increase in $24\mathrm{~h~}$ was categorized as increase versus decrease/no change, and baseline edema volume was $\log_{2}$ transformed due to poor model fit of the continuous variables on the original scale. The relationship between LND and the dichotomized (0–3 vs. 4–6) 90-day modified Rankin Scale (mRS) was examined with the logistic regression model. All $\boldsymbol{p}$ values were two-sided, with $p<0.05$ considered statistically significant since these analyses are exploratory in nature. All data were analyzed with the use of SAS version 9.4 (SAS Institute, Cary, NC, USA).  

# Results  

In ATACH-2 study, 3 subjects died within 1 day and 21 between 2 and 7 days after stroke onset. Baseline and 24-h CT scan data were available in 876 subjects. Among the 1,000 subjects randomized, 82 $(8.2\%)$ developed END, and 64 $\left(6.4\%\right)$ had LND. Seven subjects had both END and LND. Of the 159 ND events, 44 were confirmed with GCS and NIHSS scores; the remainders were based on adverse events reports. The timing of ND is shown in Figure 1. Over $40\%$ (58/144) of the subjects had ND within the first $12\mathrm{~h~}$ . The number of subjects with events then decreases until a second smaller peak appears in $60{-}72\mathrm{h}$ after stroke onset.  

A comparison of pretreatment characteristics between subjects with and without LND is shown in Table 1. Intensive SBP lowering treatment did not reduce the risk of LND. LND occurred in $7.4\%$ (37 of 500) in the intensive treatment group and $5.4\%$ (27 of 500) in the standard hypertensive treatment group (OR 1.40, $95\%$ CI 0.84–2.34, $p=0.198)$ . Similarly, mean minimum SBP in the first $2\mathrm{{h}}$ was not different between 2 groups. From the univariate analyses, past history of stroke (OR 2.30; $95\%$ CI 1.31– 4.05), baseline hematoma volume (OR per $1{\mathrm{-cm}}^{3}$ increase 1.04, $95\%$ CI 1.03–1.06), any hematoma volume increase in $24\mathrm{{h}}$ (OR 2.35, $95\%$ CI 1.32–4.17), baseline edema volume (OR per 2-fold increase 1.28, $95\%$ CI 1.07–1.54), the presence of IVH at baseline (OR 2.50, $95\%$ CI 1.49–4.20), and the presence of lobar hemorrhage (OR 2.03, $95\%$ CI 1.05–3.94) were selected as putative risk factors to be considered in the multivariable analysis model. # Discussion  

The main findings of the present study are as follows: (1) baseline hematoma volume, the presence of IVH, and hematoma volume increase in $24\mathrm{h}$ were independent risk factors of LND; (2) intensive SBP lowering treatment during first $24\mathrm{{h}}$ did not mitigate the development of LND; and (3) LND was a strongly associated with poor 90-day outcome.  

It has been reported in previous retrospective studies that about $10{-}30\%$ of patients with ICH developed LND [1, 6, 11]. In the present study, the incidence of LND was only $6.4\%$ , and lower than those in the previous reports. Possible reasons for this discrepancy are that inclusion criteria of ATACH-2 trial were much stricter than the observational studies. Patients with severe consciousness disorder with a GCS score ${<}5$ were excluded in this trial, possibly lowering the incidence of LND. Furthermore, the randomized subjects in ATACH-2 tended to be milder cases with $72\%$ having a baseline GCS scores of 14 and 15 and with mean baseline hematoma volume of 13.7. In addition, the fact that the definition of ND differs considerably among the studies may explain the low incidence of LND in our study. We determined ND prospectively according to strictly defined criteria. Meanwhile, prior studies collected the ND data in reference to each physician’s description, and the incidence of ND might be overestimated. On the other hand, as a limitation, GCS score and NIHSS score were not collected after $24\mathrm{{h}}$ in the ATACH-II trial and all ND was based on adverse events reported by the site. Reporting bias may have contributed to an underestimation of LND in this study.  

Baseline hematoma volume and the presence of IVH have been associated with both END and LND in previous studies [1, 4, 5, 11, 12]. Our data from the present study are consistent with these previous findings. In the comparison between patients with late ND and those with early ND, the prevalence of past stroke was higher in patients with late ND. The plasticity of neuronal connections, which can be negatively affected by previous stroke, may be related to the development of late ND. In addition, our results show that hematoma volume increase in the first $24\mathrm{{h}}$ was another independent factor of LND. Hematoma volume expansion has been reported as a predictive factor of poor prognosis in patients with ICH [2, 13]. Our result indicates that early hematoma volume expansion can affect LND independent of baseline hematoma volume. Early hematoma expansion may induce delayed brain edema which can lead to LND. In addition, not surprisingly, LND is a strong predictor of poor outcome.  

Although previous observational studies reported that high SBP in acute ICH was associated with ND and poor outcome [2, 14, 15], our results indicate that intensive reduction in the SBP level $(<140\mathrm{mmHg},$ ) does not mitigate the development of LND. In this study, the mean minimum SBP in the first $2\mathrm{~h~}$ was $128.9\mathrm{mm}\mathrm{Hg}$ in the intensive-treatment group and $141.1\mathrm{mm}\mathrm{Hg}$ in the standardtreatment group [7]. Thus, strict lowering of SBP in the standard-treatment group may attenuate the relative effect of intensive treatment in this study.  

The strength of this study is its multisite prospective design with a large number of subjects and the completeness of its data collection. About $88\%$ of participants completed hematoma volume measurements at both baseline and $24\mathrm{-h}$ follow-up. Our study also has limitations. As mentioned above, LND was reported on the adverse event reports, not all with NIHSS or GCS scores. In addition, $28\%$ of subjects were discharged from the participating hospitals on or before 7 days post onset. Although these subjects are often mild and therefore not likely to develop ND, these conditions may have contributed to the lower incidence of LND than expected.  

In conclusion, this study supports that initial hematoma volume, hematoma volume expansion in $24\mathrm{{h}}$ , and the presence of IVH are risk factors of LND after acute ICH.
