# Association of Stroke Subtype With Hemorrhagic Transformation Mediated by Thrombectomy Pass: Data From the ANGEL-ACT Registry

BACKGROUND: The role of stroke etiology subtype in patients with acute large vessel occlusion on the occurrence of hemorrhagic transformation (HT) after endovascular treatment is poorly studied, and which factors mediate their relationship remains largely unknown. We utilized nationwide registry data to explore the association of stroke subtype (cardioembolism versus large artery atherosclerosis) with HT and to identify the possible mediators.  

METHODS: A total of 1015 subjects were selected from the ANGEL-ACT registry (Endovascular Treatment Key Technique and Emergency Work Flow Improvement of Acute Ischemic Stroke)—a prospective consecutive cohort of acute large vessel occlusion patients undergoing endovascular treatment at 111 hospitals in China between November 2017 and March 2019—and divided into large artery atherosclerosis $(\mathsf{n}{=}538)$ ) and cardioembolism $\scriptstyle(\mathsf{n}{=}477)$ according to the Trial of ORG 10172 in Acute Stroke Treatment criteria. The types of HT included any intracerebral hemorrhage (ICH), parenchymal hematoma, and symptomatic ICH within 24 hours after endovascular treatment. The association between stroke subtype and HT was analyzed using a logistic regression model. Mediation analysis was done to assess how much of the effect of stroke subtype on HT was mediated through the identified mediators.  

RESULTS: Stroke subtype (cardioembolism versus large artery atherosclerosis) was associated with increased risk of any ICH $(29.8\%$ versus $16.5\%$ ; odds ratio, 2.03 $[95\%$ CI, 1.22–3.36]), parenchymal hematoma ( $14.3\%$ versus $5.4\%$ ; odds ratio, 2.90 $[95\%$ CI, 1.38–6.13]), and symptomatic ICH $9.9\%$ versus $4.7\%$ ; odds ratio, 2.59 $[95\%$ CI, 1.09–6.16]) after adjustment for potential confounders. The more thrombectomy passes in cardioembolism patients had a significant mediation effect on the association of stroke subtype with increased risk of HT (any ICH, $15.9\%$ ; parenchymal hematoma, $13.4\%$ ; symptomatic ICH, $14.2\%_{\cdot}$ respectively).  

CONCLUSIONS: Stroke subtype is an independent risk factor for HT within 24 hours following endovascular treatment among acute large vessel occlusion patients. Mediation analyses propose that stroke subtype contributes to HT partly through thrombectomy pass, suggesting a possible pathomechanistic link.

he stroke etiology subtype contributes to the functional outcome of patients with acute ischemic stroke (AIS) undergoing endovascular treatment (EVT). Generally speaking, patients with stroke of cardioembolism have worse functional outcomes than patients with stroke due to large artery atherosclerosis (LAA).1,2 Patients with cardioembolism had significantly worse 90-day outcomes (modified Rankin Scale score 3–6, $60.6\%$ versus $26.3\%$ ) than those with LAA receiving EVT with stent retrievers.1 Later, the MR-CLEAN (Multicenter Randomized Controlled Trial of Endovascular Treatment for Acute Ischemic Stroke in the Netherlands) team also found that cardioembolism was associated with higher median modified Rankin Scale at 90 days (4 versus 3) compared with LAA.2 However, the effect of stroke subtype on the risk of hemorrhagic transformation (HT) following EVT is less well understood.  

In this study, we hypothesized that stroke subtype (cardioembolism versus LAA) plays a role in the HT of patients with AIS who underwent EVT and patients with cardioembolism have an increased risk of HT than those with LAA. We explored this hypothesis in a large sample of subjects receiving EVT for AIS due to acute large vessel occlusion (LVO). We further assessed whether the presumed association of stroke subtype with HT was mediated through some specific factors.  

# METHODS

# Reporting Guideline  

This manuscript follows the STROBE reporting guideline (freely available on the web site http://www.strobe-statement.org).  

# Study Population  

Data were extracted from ANGEL-ACT (Endovascular Treatment Key Technique and Emergency Work Flow Improvement of Acute Ischemic Stroke)—a prospective nationwide registry of 2004 consecutive adult patients with AIS and initiation of EVT in 111 hospitals in China between November 2017 and March 2019. Complete methods of the registry, such as inclusion/exclusion criteria and data collection standards, have been reported earlier.3 The protocol was approved by the ethics committees of all centers, and all participants (or legal representatives) provided written informed consent. The study procedures were in accordance with the 1964 Declaration of Helsinki and its later amendments.  

Of the 2004 patients registered, 211 were excluded from the ANGEL-ACT database (83 with isolated extracranial LVO, 98 without evidence of LVO on digital subtraction angiography, and 30 with missing baseline data). Among the remaining 1793 patients, we additionally excluded those who did not undergo thrombectomy $\left(\mathrm{n}\mathrm{=}186\right)$ ) and those with tandem lesions $\scriptstyle(\nmid=318)$ ), other determined etiology $\left(\mathfrak{n}{=}40\right)$ ), and undetermined etiology $\scriptstyle({\mathsf{n}}=203$ ), as well as those without available imaging within 24 hours after the procedure $(\mathfrak{n}{=}31$ ). Finally, a total of 1015 patients were eligible for this analysis (Figure 1).  

# Stroke Subtype  

Local neurologists or site investigators made decisions based on the 1993 version of the Trial of ORG 10172 in Acute Stroke Treatment (TOAST) standard.4 All evaluators were trained by committee-assigned stroke specialists before initiating patient enrollment and received a manual that included a detailed description of the TOAST subtyping system and operation guidelines in determining etiological subtype. Following a standard diagnostic process, the trained evaluators reviewed the patients’ clinical, imaging, and laboratory features and categorized patients into different causative subtypes according to the TOAST system. The TOAST system stratified patients of the ANGEL-ACT registry into 4 subtypes: LAA, cardioembolism, other determined etiology, and undetermined etiology.  

# HT Classification  

The HT events analyzed in this study were any intracerebral hemorrhage (ICH), parenchymal hematoma (PH), and symptomatic ICH within 24 hours after EVT according to the Heidelberg Bleeding Classification.5 Any ICH was identified by the follow-up imaging regardless of clinical deterioration. Both PH1 and PH2 were considered as PH. An independent imaging core laboratory and clinical events committee adjudicated all HT complications.  

# Statistical Analysis  

Data were displayed as median (interquartile range) or frequency (percentage). A comparison of baseline characteristics between patients with LAA and cardioembolism was performed using the Mann-Whitney $U$ test or Pearson $\chi^{2}$ test. A multivariable logistic regression model examined the association of stroke subtype (cardioembolism versus LAA) with HT (any ICH, PH, and symptomatic ICH). All baseline variables with a significant difference of $P{<}0.10$ between both groups except for the mediators as potential confounders were adjusted. The adjusted odds ratio with a $95\%$ CI was calculated to measure the strength of the association.

Mediation models were used to examine whether the association of stroke subtype and HT was mediated by the following factors (National Institutes of Health Stroke Scale score, occlusion site [anterior versus posterior circulation], prior use of anticoagulants, type of thrombectomy [stent retriever alone, stent retriever plus aspiration], thrombectomy pass, and emergency angioplasty/stenting).6,7 Detailed methods of mediation analysis are available in the Supplemental Material.  

All statistical analyses were conducted with the SAS software, version 9.4 (SAS Institute, Inc, Cary, NC). Two-sided $R{<}0.05$ was considered statistically significant.  

# RESULTS  

# Baseline Characteristics of Patients With LAA Versus Cardioembolism  

Of 1015 patients included in this study, 538 with LAA and 477 with cardioembolism were compared for their baseline characteristics. As shown in Table  1, there were significant differences in many baseline variables between the LAA and cardioembolism groups. As compared with the LAA group, the cardioembolism group was 6 years older (69 versus 63), had 1 point higher National Institutes of Health Stroke Scale score (17 versus 16) and 2 points higher Alberta Stroke Program Early CT Score (10 versus 8), had a larger proportion of women $(52.2\%$ versus $22.7\%$ ) and anterior circulation stroke $M7.8\%$ versus $70.3\%$ ), more often received anticoagulants before the procedure $8.4\%$ versus $1.5\%$ ) and thrombectomy with stent retriever plus aspiration catheter $(23.1\%$ versus $16.7\%)$ ), and received one more pass number of thrombectomy (2 versus 1). However, the cardioembolism group less frequent received antiplatelet agents before the procedure ( $12.2\%$ versus $16.9\%$ ), thrombectomy with stent retriever alone $74.2\%$ versus $80.9\%$ ), emergency angioplasty/stenting ( $4.4\%$ versus $38.9\%$ ), intra-arterial thrombolysis ( $1.9\%$ versus $4.3\%$ ), and tirofiban during the procedure $(35.2\%$ versus $72.1\%$ ) and experienced 67-minute shorter onset-to-puncture time (270 versus 337) than the LAA group. No significant difference was found between the 2 groups regarding intravenous thrombolysis pretreatment, types of anesthesia, use of heparin during the procedure, puncture-to-recanalization time, successful recanalization after the first/final attempt or complete recanalization after the final attempt.

# Association Between Stroke Subtype and HT  

As presented in Table  2, stroke subtype (cardioembolism versus LAA) was independently associated with increased risk of any ICH ${\bf\nabla}_{29.8\%}$ versus $16.5\%$ ; adjusted odds ratio, 2.03 $[95\%$ CI, 1.22–3.36]), PH ( $14.3\%$ versus $5.4\%$ ; adjusted odds ratio, 2.90 $[95\%$ CI, 1.38–6.13]), and symptomatic ICH $9.9\%$ versus $4.7\%$ ; adjusted odds ratio, 2.59 $[95\%$ CI, $1.09\substack{-6.16}]$ ), after adjustment for the potential confounders. Similar results were observed in the unadjusted model.  

# Mediation Analysis of Stroke Subtype on HT  

We tested which factors mediated the observational association between the stroke subtype and risk of HT. Except for thrombectomy pass, other factors (National Institutes of Health Stroke Scale score, occlusion site, prior use of anticoagulants, type of thrombectomy, and emergency angioplasty/stenting) did not have a significant mediation effect on the association of stroke subtype with HT (Table S1). The mediation effect of thrombectomy pass on the stroke subtype HT association was further analyzed in an adjusted mediation analysis (Figure  2). The more thrombectomy passes for cardioembolism than LAA explained $15.9\%$ , $13.4\%$ , and $14.2\%$ of the total effect of stroke subtype on any ICH, PH, and symptomatic ICH, respectively.  

# DISCUSSION  

In line with our hypothesis, after multivariable adjustment, we found stroke subtype (cardioembolism versus LAA) was an independent risk factor for HT in acute LVO patients undergoing EVT. Further mediation analyses suggested that the effect of stroke subtype on HT was partly mediated through more thrombectomy passes in patients with cardioembolism compared with LAA.  

Similar to the findings of our study, data from the ACTUAL registry (Endovascular Treatment for Acute Anterior Circulation Ischemic Stroke) showed that the rate of symptomatic ICH $20.0\%$ versus $11.7\%$ $P{=}0.004.$ ) was notably higher in the cardioembolism group than that in the LAA group.8 We may expect that cerebral ischemia caused by a sudden cardiac embolus coming to an unprepared brain can progress faster than ischemia due to progressive extracranial or intracranial LAA stenosis where there may be time to develop collaterals and potential ischemic preconditioning.9 Because of rapid arterial occlusion, cardioembolism stroke typically has a smaller penumbra and a more extensive core infarct,10 which may increase the risk of HT.11,12 Although the concomitant use of anticoagulants may potentially increase the bleeding risk, results of mediation analysis in this patient cohort (Table S1) did not support anticoagulation treatment as a significant cause for the increased risk of HT after EVT in patients with cardioembolism. However, caution should be taken when interpreting these data as some findings have been contradictory, with reports of no significant relationship between cardioembolism and ${\mathsf{HT}}^{1,2}$ or an association of LAA with an increased risk of HT.13 These conflicting findings on the association of stroke subtype with HT could result from population differences,1,2,13 uncorrected baseline imbalance,8 and small sample sizes.1,13  

Of note, we found that cardioembolism was associated with a higher number of thrombectomy passes than LAA. Several factors might account for this finding. Stroke subtype may differ in thrombus composition. Cardioembolic thrombi tend to be composed of platelet aggregates interspersed with dense fibrin fibers, whereas atherosclerotic thrombi mainly consist of red blood cell masses with thin fibrin fibers.14–16 Previous studies indicated that stickier fibrin-rich clots were associated with an increased number of thrombectomy maneuvers compared with red blood cell–rich clots.17–19 In addition, cardiogenic thrombi are often older, with a more dense and compact structure compared with atherothrombotic clots, thereby making thrombectomy more difficult.20 Last but not the least, nearly half (268/538) of LAA strokes in this cohort were caused by intracranial atherosclerotic disease—a prevalent cause of stroke among Asians.21 Current mechanical thrombectomy devices were primarily designed for embolic occlusion rather than in situ thrombosis.22–24 Repeated thrombectomy alone often leads to reocclusion at the site of intracranial atherosclerotic disease,25,26 with risks of vessel wall damage and HT.27,28 Hence, optimal EVT strategy for acute LVO due to intracranial atherosclerotic disease may be different from that resulting from embolism. For patients with intracranial atherosclerotic disease, operators preferred to perform rescue angioplasty/stenting after only one thrombectomy pass or even direct angioplasty/stenting rather than multiple thrombectomy passes.29 More thrombectomy passes may cause vascular injury and blood-brain barrier disruption, both of which were associated with increased HT risk.12,30

Although our study is based on a large prospectively collected nationwide registry, there are some possible limitations. While the TOAST criteria are widely used in clinical research to categorize stroke subtypes, it is limited by only moderate interrater reliability (κ,  

0.42–0.64).31,32 Although stroke etiology was based on the judgment of a local neurologist or site investigator in this study, both rater training and standardized medical record review have been shown to improve this problem.33,34 Second, we found that only ${<}20\%$ of the total effect of stroke subtype on HT was mediated through thrombectomy pass. Thus, the relationship between stroke subtype and HT could be associated with other pathways such as collateral status and its relationship with HT in cardioembolism-related stroke.2,12,35,36 Unfortunately, detailed data on collateral score were unavailable for more than half (563/1015) of the patients in our study. Third, the median Alberta Stroke Program Early CT Score was higher in the cardioembolism group than in the LAA group, possibly because patients with cardioembolism had a shorter onset-to-treatment time (Table  1) and acute ischemic lesions were not yet fully visualized on computed tomography or magnetic resonance imaging. Therefore, we could not assess the mediating effect of infarct volume in this study. Fourth, aspiration-only thrombectomy rarely was used in comparison to stentriever-only thrombectomy or combined aspiration plus stentriever thrombectomy in our study. Since the risk of bleeding complications may vary with different thrombectomy modalities, this may limit the generalizability for other centers that use an aspiration-only approach. Finally, the number of thrombectomy passes was likely lower in the LAA group since there was operator preference for angioplasty/stenting in this group instead of multiple thrombus retrieval passes, which would also limit the generalizability of the present findings to other centers or populations.  

# CONCLUSIONS  

In patients with AIS who underwent EVT because of acute LVO, stroke subtype (cardioembolism versus LAA) was associated with increased risk of any ICH, PH, and symptomatic ICH. The observed association might be partially attributable to the difference in the pass number of thrombectomy between cardioembolism and LAA. Therefore, further studies are needed to understand the mediators between stroke subtype and HT and determine the optimal technical approach for patients with different stroke subtypes, focusing closely on high-risk individuals to prevent HT occurrence after EVT.
