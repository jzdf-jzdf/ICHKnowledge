# Detrimental Effect of Acute Hyperglycemia on the Outcomes of Large Ischemic Region Stroke

BACKGROUND: Although endovascular therapy (EVT) is effective for large ischemic region strokes, the impact of hyperglycemia remains unclear.  

METHODS AND RESULTS: We analyzed data from the RESCUE-Japan LIMIT (Recovery by Endovascular Salvage for Cerebral Ultra-Acute Embolism-Japan Large Ischemic Core) trial, which randomized stroke patients with Alberta Stroke Program Early Computed Tomography Score of 3 to 5 to EVT versus medical management. Outcomes were compared among patients with normoglycemia $(<140\mathrm{mg/dL})$ , moderate hyperglycemia $\left(\geq140\right)$ , <180 mg/dL), and severe hyperglycemia $(\geq180\mathrm{mg}/\mathrm{dL})$ on admission. Among 200 patients (median age 76.5 years, median glucose level 131 mg/dL, EVT $50\%$ ), diabetes prevalence was $10.0\%$ , $18.4\%$ , and $71.0\%$ in the groups with normoglycemia $(\mathsf{n}{=}120)$ , moderate hyperglycemia $(\mathsf{n}{=}49)$ , and severe hyperglycemia $(\mathsf{n}{=}31)$ , respectively. Achievement of modified Rankin Scale score 0 to 3 at 90 days was less frequent in the group with severe hyperglycemia $(6.5\%)$ than in the groups with normoglycemia $(25.0\%)$ and moderate hyperglycemia $(24.5\%)$ , with adjusted odds ratios (aOR) of 1.48 ( $95\%$ CI, 0.59–3.72) for moderate and 0.17 $95\%$ CI, 0.03–0.95) for severe hyperglycemia relative to normoglycemia. The risk of symptomatic intracranial hemorrhage was higher for moderate hyperglycemia $(6.1\%$ ; aOR, 2.86 $[95\%$ CI, 0.42–19.71]) and particularly for severe hyperglycemia $(25.8\%$ ; aOR, 18.59 $[95\%$ CI, 2.47–139.87]) compared with normoglycemia $(2.5\%)$ . Symptomatic intracranial hemorrhage rates were similar for EVT and medical management in normoglycemia ( $1.6\%$ versus $3.4\%$ ) and moderate hyperglycemia $(5.0\%$ versus $6.9\%$ , but for severe hyperglycemia, the rate was higher in patients with EVT $(36.8\%)$ ) than in those without $(8.3\%)$ .  

CONCLUSIONS: Severe hyperglycemia was associated with worse functional prognosis and increased symptomatic intracranial hemorrhage in large ischemic region strokes.

# CLINICAL PERSPECTIVE  

# What Is New?  

In patients with large ischemic region stroke by large vessel occlusion, severe hyperglycemia $(\geq180\mathsf{mg}/\mathsf{dL})$ on admission was associated with worse functional prognosis and increased symptomatic intracranial hemorrhage and decompressive craniectomy. Endovascular therapy for large ischemic region stroke was more likely to achieve modified Rankin Scale score 0 to 3 at 90 days than medical management in patients with normoglycemia (<140 mg/ dL) and moderate hyperglycemia $\tt\left(\geq140\right)$ , <180 mg/ dL), but endovascular therapy lost its effectiveness in patients with severe hyperglycemia. Regarding safety, symptomatic intracranial hemorrhage and decompressive craniectomy were more frequent in patients with endovascular therapy than those with medical management in the group with severe hyperglycemia.  

# What Are the Clinical Implications?  

•	 It is unclear from the present analysis whether correction of hyperglycemia in patients with large ischemic region stroke leads to improved prognosis. The negative impact of severe hyperglycemia on outcomes after endovascular therapy for large ischemic region stroke needs independent confirmation.

yperglycemia is a common manifestation in patients with acute ischemic stroke with the reported prevalence of approximately $40\%$ and is associated with worse clinical outcomes in both human and animal studies.1,2 Hyperglycemia is suggested to aggravate ischemic brain damage via multiple mechanisms, including endothelial dysfunction, increased oxidative stress, disruption of the blood– brain barrier, and exacerbation of the thromboinflammatory cascade.2–4 As an effective intervention for acute ischemic stroke, mechanical thrombectomy has been established and widely implemented, and data reporting the association between admission hyperglycemia and outcomes after mechanical thrombectomy has accumulated.5-10 A meta-analysis showed that compared with normoglycemia, admission hyperglycemia significantly reduced the achievement of modified Rankin Scale (mRS) score 0 to 2 at 3 months and increased the risk of symptomatic intracranial hemorrhage (ICH) after mechanical thrombectomy.8 In an analysis of pooled individual patient data from randomized trials of endovascular therapy (EVT) versus medical management for large vessel occlusion (LVO) stroke, higher admission blood glucose was associated with a decreased benefit of EVT over medical management.11  

Several randomized trials recently provided evidence on the efficacy and safety of EVT for acute stroke with large ischemic region (Alberta Stroke Program Early Computed Tomography Score [ASPECTS] ${}^{\leq5}$ ). However, in this patient group with severe stroke, the mRS score 4 to 6 rate was approximately as high as $60\%$ to $370\%$ even in the patients who underwent EVT.12–15 Additionally, symptomatic ICH was more frequently seen in the EVT-treated participants in the large ischemic region trials than in the previous trials, which essentially included patients with ASPECTS ${\ge}6$ .12,14 Although EVT is generally effective in patients with the large ischemic region, the risk of symptomatic ICH is relatively high, increasing the need to understand prognostic factors that contribute to more appropriate treatment selection in this population. The relationship between admission hyperglycemia and outcomes in patients with LVO stroke may also be observed in patients with the large ischemic region, but this hypothesis has never been evaluated.  

The present study analyzed data from the RESCUEJapan LIMIT (Recovery by Endovascular Salvage for Cerebral Ultra-Acute Embolism-Japan Large Ischemic Core Trial), a randomized trial of EVT versus medical management alone for patients with acute stroke and a large ischemic region, to evaluate the impact of acute hyperglycemia on the outcomes after such severe strokes.12,16  

# METHODS  

RESCUE-Japan LIMIT was registered in ClinicalTrials. gov (Unique identifier: NCT03702413). The data supporting the findings of this study are available from the corresponding author on reasonable request. This study is reported according to the Strengthening the Reporting of Observational Studies in Epidemiology guideline. The institutional review board or the ethics committee at each participating site approved the trial protocol, and all participants or their legally authorized representatives provided written informed consent.  

# Study Population  

The RESCUE-Japan LIMIT was a randomized,2- group, open-label trial conducted in 45 hospitals in Japan to determine the effectiveness of EVT in patients with acute ischemic stroke caused by LVO and a large ischemic region. A detailed description of the protocol has been provided elsewhere.12 Briefly, enrolled patients were randomized in a 1:1 ratio to receive EVT with medical management or medical management alone if they had acute ischemic stroke, were 18 years of age or older, had a score of at least 6 on the National Institutes of Health Stroke Scale (NIHSS) at admission, had a score of 0 to 1 on the mRS before the onset of stroke, had an occlusion of the internal carotid artery or M1 segment of the middle cerebral artery, and had an ASPECTS of 3 to 5, as determined on computed tomography or magnetic resonance imaging. The method of EVT was selected by the treating physicians and could include stent retriever, aspiration catheter, balloon angioplasty, intracranial stenting, and carotid-artery stenting. Successful reperfusion was defined as modified Thrombolysis in Cerebral Infarction score ${\ttz2b}$ . Other aspects of medical care in both groups were in line with the guidelines from the American Heart Association and the American Stroke Association.  

Blood glucose level was measured as part of screening laboratory data on admission and was followed-up at 48 $(\pm24)$ hours after randomization. There were no regulations regarding blood glucose measurement in relation to time with food intake. In the present study, normoglycemia was defined as blood glucose level <140 mg/dL. Moderate hyperglycemia was defined based on blood glucose levels $\ge140$ to ${<}180\mathsf{mg}/$ dL and severe hyperglycemia based on blood glucose levels ≥180 mg/dL.17 We also defined hypoglycemia as glucose levels $<80\mathsf{mg}/\mathsf{dL}$ .17 Diabetes was diagnosed if the hemoglobin A1c was $26.5\%$ at admission or patients were taking antidiabetic agents approved in Japan for blood glucose lowering before index stroke or at discharge.  

# Outcomes  

The primary efficacy outcome was a score of 0 to 3 on the mRS at 90 days after the stroke onset. The secondary efficacy outcome was an mRS score of 0 to 2 at 90 days. Safety outcomes were any ICH within 48 hours, symptomatic ICH within 48 hours after randomization, decompressive craniectomy within 7 days, and death within 90 days after the onset. Symptomatic ICH was defined as parenchymal hematoma type 2 in combination with worsening of NIHSS score by at least 4 points within 48 hours.18 The scores on the mRS at 90 days were determined by physicians or physical therapists who were trained to obtain this score and were unaware of the trial-group assignments.  

# Statistical Analysis  

We compared baseline characteristics and outcomes among the 3 patient groups according to the blood glucose level on admission: normoglycemia, moderate hyperglycemia, and severe hyperglycemia. The Mann– Whitney $U$ test or Kruskal–Wallis test for continuous and ordinal variables and the Pearson $\chi^{2}$ test for categorical variables were used to compare the patient groups. Distributions of the mRS scores at 90 days among the patient groups were compared using the Jonckheere–Terpstra test for trend.  

Logistic regression models were created to elucidate the association of outcomes with blood glucose level. Age, sex, diabetes, white blood cell count, NIHSS score, internal carotid artery occlusion, ASPECTS, time from stroke onset to randomization, and treatment assignment were included as covariates for multivariable adjustment. Crude and adjusted odds ratios (OR) with a $95\%$ CI were computed for moderate hyperglycemia and severe hyperglycemia, using the group with normoglycemia as a reference and for a 1 mg/dL increase in blood glucose on admission. Comparisons between patients with and without severe hyperglycemia on admission were also performed.  

Comparisons between patients with and without diabetes were conducted to assess confounding. The following covariates were included in the logistic model to evaluate the association between diabetes and outcomes: age, sex, admission blood glucose, white blood cell count, NIHSS score, internal carotid artery occlusion, ASPECTS, time from stroke onset to randomization, and treatment assignment.  

The interaction between glucose and treatment assignment on the outcomes were assessed by adding a multiplicative term of glucose level-by-treatment assignment into the multivariable model for the association between outcomes and blood glucose level. Predictive marginal probabilities for the outcomes in patients with EVT and in patients with medical management alone were computed according to admission glucose levels using the multivariable logistic model.  

Analyses were also made according to the temporal changes in blood glucose levels (glycemic pattern). Glycemic pattern was defined as no severe hyperglycemia if glucose levels were $<180\mathrm{mg/dL}$ both on admission and at 48 hours and transient severe hyperglycemia if glucose levels were $2180\mathsf{mg}/\mathsf{dL}$ on admission and <180 mg/dL at 48 hours. Glycemic pattern of ≥180 mg/dL both on admission and at 48 hours were defined as persistent severe hyperglycemia, and that of <180 mg/dL on admission but $2180\mathsf{mg}/\mathsf{dL}$ at 48 hours were defined as delayed severe hyperglycemia.  

All statistical computations were performed using the Stata/MP statistical package (version 17.0; Stata Corp LP, College Station, TX). Missing data were handled using a pairwise-deletion approach. All tests were 2 sided, and a $P$ value ${<}0.05$ was considered significant.  

# RESULTS  

Among the 203 patients randomized in RESCUEJapan LIMIT, 1 patient assigned to EVT withdrew consent. In this analysis, 2 patients were excluded due to a lack of available data on the admission blood glucose (Figure  1). Thus, 200 patients (90 women, median age 76.5 [interquartile range (IQR), 71–84] years, 100 receiving EVT) were eligible for the present analyses. Median admission NIHSS score, ASPECTS, and infarction volume were 22 (IQR, 17–26), 3 (IQR, 3–4), and 104.5 (IQR, 66–142) and median time from last known well to randomization was 217.5 (IQR, 144.5–407) minutes. Diabetes was present in 43 patients $(21.5\%)$ . Median admission glucose was 131 (IQR, 116–156) mg/dL and median hemoglobin A1c level was $5.9\%$ (IQR, $5.6\substack{-6.3\%}$ , $\mathsf{n}{=}159.$ ).  

Of the 200 analyzed patients, 120, 49, and 31 patients were classified into the groups with normoglycemia, moderate hyperglycemia, and severe hyperglycemia, respectively. The group with severe hyperglycemia more frequently had diabetes $(P{<}0.001)$ and were admitted with lower ASPECTS $(P{=}0.020)$ , higher white blood cell count $(P{=}0.020)$ , higher C-reactive protein level $(P{=}0.003)$ , and higher hemoglobin A1c $(P{<}0.001)$ compared with the groups with normoglycemia and moderate hyperglycemia (Table 1). The group with normoglycemia included 2 patients with admission hypoglycemia (admission glucose levels of 77 and 78 mg/ dL). EVT was assigned in $50.8\%$ , $40.8\%$ , and $61.3\%$ of the groups with normoglycemia, moderate hyperglycemia, and severe hyperglycemia, respectively $(P{=}0.20)$ . Almost all EVT procedures were mechanical thrombectomy, and there was no difference in the type of procedure between the 3 groups. Reperfusion of modified Thrombolysis in Cerebral Infarction score ${\ttz2b}$ was achieved in $88.5\%$ , $85.0\%$ , $78.9\%$ in each group, respectively $(P{=}0.57),$ .  

The mRS scores at 90 days tended to be higher in the groups with moderate and severe hyperglycemia than in the group with normoglycemia $(P$ for trend $=0.003$ ; Figure 2). The primary outcome (mRS score 0–3) was achieved in $25.0\%$ for normoglycemia, $24.5\%$ for moderate hyperglycemia, and $6.5\%$ for severe hyperglycemia $(P{=}0.075)$ . The adjusted OR of mRS score 0to 3 were 1.48 ( $95\%$ CI, $0.59-$ 3.72) for moderate and 0.17 $95\%$ CI, 0.03–0.95) for severe hyperglycemia with the group with normoglycemia referenced (Table 2). The adjusted OR of mRS score 0 to 3 for each $1\mathrm{mg/dL}$ increase in admission glucose levels was 0.98 $95\%$ CI, 0.97– 0.99). The rate of mRS score 0 to 2 tended to be lower in the group with severe hyperglycemia than in the other groups $(P{=}0.17),$ , with no statistically significant odds reduction for severe hyperglycemia compared with normoglycemia. Any ICH was seen in $38.3\%$ for normoglycemia, $44.9\%$ for moderate hyperglycemia, and $71.0\%$ for severe hyperglycemia $(P{=}0.005)$ . The rate of symptomatic ICH was $25.8\%$ for severe hyperglycemia, which was higher than those for normoglycemia $(2.5\%)$ and moderate hyperglycemia $(6.1\%$ ; $P{<}0.001$ ). Decompressive craniectomy was also more frequent in the group with severe hyperglycemia $(29.0\%)$ compared with the groups with normoglycemia $(6.7\%)$ and moderate hyperglycemia $(14.3\%)$ $(P{=}0.002^{\cdot}$ ). Death within 90 days was more common in the group with severe hyperglycemia $(41.9\%)$ than in the groups with normoglycemia $(16.7\%)$ and moderate hyperglycemia $(18.4\%)$ $(P{=}0.008)$ . The odds of any ICH (adjusted OR, 4.14 $[95\%$ $\bigcirc$ , 1.42–12.09]), symptomatic ICH (adjusted OR, 18.59 $[95\%$ CI, 2.47–139.87]), decompressive craniectomy (adjusted OR, 5.04 $[95\%$ CI, 1.21–20.99]), and death (adjusted OR, 3.31 $[95\%$ CI, 1.07–10.23]) were significantly higher in the group with severe hyperglycemia compared with the group with normoglycemia, with no significant change in the odds for moderate hyperglycemia.  

Compared with patients without severe hyperglycemia on admission $(<180\mathrm{mg/dL}$ , $\mathsf{n}{=}169,$ ), patients with severe hyperglycemia $\left({\geq}180{\bmod{}}\right)$ , $\scriptstyle\mathsf{n}=31$ ) showed a significantly lower odds of the primary outcome (adjusted OR, 0.15 $[95\%$ CI, 0.03–0.81]) and higher odds of any ICH (adjusted OR, 3.75 $[95\%$ CI, 1.33–10.57]), symptomatic ICH (adjusted OR, 12.93 $[95\%$ CI, $2.08-$ 80.31]), decompressive craniectomy (adjusted OR, 3.68 $[95\%$ CI, 0.97–13.91]), and death (adjusted OR, 3.59 $[95\%$ CI, 1.22–10.58]; Table S1).

# Patients With and Without Diabetes  

Compared with patients without diabetes $(\mathsf{n}{=}157)$ ), patients with diabetes $(\mathsf{n}{=}43)$ more frequently had hypertension $(81.4\%$ versus $66.2\%$ , $P{=}0.056)$ and dyslipidemia $(46.5\%$ versus $18.5\%$ , $P{<}0.001$ ; Table  S2). The rate of mRS score 0 to 3 was not different in patients with $(20.9\%)$ versus without diabetes $(22.3\%$ , $P{=}0.85$ ; adjusted OR, 2.19 $[95\%$ CI, $0.76\substack{-6.31]}$ ; Table  S3). Compared with patients without diabetes, symptomatic ICH was more frequent in patients with diabetes ( $14.0\%$ versus $5.1\%$ , $P{=}0.044.$ ), but the difference was not statistically significant after multivariable adjustment (adjusted OR, 0.61 $[95\%$ CI, 0.12–3.21]). Decompressive craniectomy was also more common in diabetes patients $(20.9\%$ versus $9.6\%$ , $P{=}0.042,$ ) with no statistically significant difference in the multivariable model (adjusted OR, 0.98 $[95\%$ CI, 0.28–3.38]). With or without diabetes, the relationship between admission blood glucose levels and outcomes was generally similar to the main analysis (Table S4).

# Analysis by Treatment Assignment  

Achievement of mRS score 0 to 3 was more frequent in patients with EVT than in those without for normoglycemia $(36.1\%$ versus $13.6\%$ , $P{=}0.004.$ ) and moderate hyperglycemia $40.0\%$ versus $13.8\%$ , $P{=}0.036)$ , but the difference was not seen for severe hyperglycemia $(5.3\%$ versus $8.3\%$ , $P{=}0.73$ ; Figure 3A). The predictive marginal probability of mRS score 0 to 3 decreased with higher admission blood glucose in patients with EVT, and the decrease was relatively small in patients with medical management $P$ for interaction $_{1=0.28}$ ; Figure 3B).  

The rate of symptomatic ICH was similar between EVT and medical management for normoglycemia $(1.6\%$ versus $3.4\%$ , $\scriptstyleP=0.54)$ and moderate hyperglycemia $(5.0\%$ versus $6.9\%$ , $P{=}0.79.$ ), whereas the rate was higher in patients with EVT $(36.8\%)$ than in those without $(8.3\%$ , $P=\mathrm{0.077}.$ ) for severe hyperglycemia (Figure  3C). The incline of the increase in predictive probability of symptomatic ICH with elevating glucose levels was greater in patients with EVT compared with those with medical management $P$ for interaction $\scriptstyle=0.133$ ; Figure 3D).  

Decompressive craniectomy was less frequent in patients with EVT than in those without for normoglycemia $(1.6\%$ versus $11.9\%$ , $P{=}0.025)$ and moderate hyperglycemia ( $10.0\%$ versus $17.2\%$ , $_{P=0.48,}$ , whereas for severe hyperglycemia, decompressive craniectomy became more frequent in patients with EVT $(36.8\%$ versus $16.7\%$ , $P{=}0.23$ ; Figure 3E). The predictive probability of decompressive craniectomy tended to be smaller in patients with EVT than in those with medical management alone in the normoglycemia to moderate hyperglycemia range, but the relationship was reversed in the severe hyperglycemia range $\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\Delta}}P$ for interaction $_{1=0.085}$ ; Figure 3F).  

# Glycemic Patterns  

The number of patients with available data on blood glucose levels at 48 hours after randomization was 157; blood glucose levels at 48 hours were significantly higher in the group with severe hyperglycemia (median $200\mathrm{mg/dL}$ than in the groups with moderate hyperglycemia $(130\mathsf{mg}/\mathsf{dL})$ and normoglycemia (116 mg/dL) $(P{<}0.001$ ; Table 1). Glycemic pattern was no severe hyperglycemia in 124 patients, transient severe hyperglycemia in 6, delayed severe hyperglycemia in 10, and persistent severe hyperglycemia in 17 (Figure 4). EVT was assigned to $51.6\%$ of no severe hyperglycemia pattern, $83.3\%$ of transient severe hyperglycemia pattern, $60.0\%$ of delayed severe hyperglycemia pattern, and $52.9\%$ of persistent severe hyperglycemia pattern $(P{=}0.48)$ ; the rate of successful reperfusion by EVT was not different among the patterns $(89.1\%$ versus $80.0\%$ versus $83.3\%$ versus $77.8\%$ , respectively: $P{=}0.75$ ; Table S5). Compared with patients with no severe hyperglycemia pattern, those with the remaining 3 glycemic patterns tended to achieve mRS score 0 to 3 less frequently $(29.0\%$ versus $0\%$ versus $0\%$ versus $0\%$ versus $11.8\%$ , respectively: $P{=}0.044.$ ), more frequently developed symptomatic ICH $3.2\%$ versus $16.7\%$ versus $10.0\%$ versus $23.5\%$ , respectively: $P{=}0.008$ ), and more frequently underwent decompressive craniectomy $(9.7\%$ versus $33.3\%$ versus $0\%$ versus $29.4\%$ , respectively: $P{=}0.024.$ ).

# DISCUSSION  

In this study, we analyzed data of 200 patients from the RESCUE-Japan LIMIT trial of EVT versus medical management for LVO stroke with large ischemic region in order to evaluate the association between admission blood glucose levels and clinical outcomes. Compared with patients with admission normoglycemia $(<140\mathrm{mg/}$ dL), those with severe hyperglycemia $\left({\geq}180\mathsf{mg}/\mathsf{dL}\right)$ were less likely to achieve mRS score 0 to 3 at 90 days and showed significantly increased rates of symptomatic ICH, decompressive craniectomy, and death. This relationship between hyperglycemia and outcomes was similarly observed in the stratified analysis by presence or absence of diabetes, and there was no significant effect of diabetes itself on worsening outcomes. Additionally, the impact of admission hyperglycemia on the outcomes of EVT versus medical management was evaluated, and EVT was more likely to achieve mRS score 0 to 3 than medical management in patients with normoglycemia and moderate hyperglycemia $\tt(\geq140$ <180 mg/dL), but EVT lost its effectiveness in patients with severe hyperglycemia. Regarding safety, symptomatic ICH and decompressive craniectomy were more frequent in patients with EVT than those with medical management in the group with severe hyperglycemia.

There are no reports evaluating the association between admission blood glucose and outcomes in large ischemic region stroke. An analysis of pooled individual patient data from mechanical thrombectomy trials for LVO stroke showed adjusted OR for mRS score 0 to 2 achievement of 0.93 per 1 mg/dL increments in admission blood glucose.11 Compared with this value, the decrease in odds for mRS score 0 to 2 or 0 to 3 by glucose increments in the present analysis was smaller in its magnitude. A single-center study of anterior circulation LVO strokes treated with EVT reported an adjusted OR of 0.52 for mRS score 0 to 2 for admission hyperglycemia of ≥140 mg/dL compared with normoglycemia, and the OR was further reduced to 0.38 in the subgroup of patients with moderate/good collaterals and mismatch.19 Hyperglycemia can be a factor that negatively affects penumbra, as past studies have suggested that it promotes loss of salvageable tissue, infarct progression, and deterioration of collateral flow.20–23 In strokes with large ischemic regions, the effect of hyperglycemia on outcomes may be relatively small because the damage caused by brain ischemia itself is severe and the penumbral volume tends to be small. However, the impact of severe hyperglycemia on worsening functional prognosis and increasing symptomatic ICH in this study would be clinically significant. Blood–brain barrier impairment associated with hyperglycemia may be a pathway for ICH development as well as cerebral edema, which may explain the increase in symptomatic ICH and decompressive craniectomy in the group with severe hyperglycemia.2,24,25 Acute hyperglycemia is an important issue in patients with stroke and large ischemic regions, both in terms of prognosis prediction and therapeutic strategy development.  

Poorer functional and safety outcomes after EVT versus medical management alone by increase in admission blood glucose have implications for future research on treatment decisions for large ischemic region stroke. However, the nature of this study as a subanalysis should be well understood when interpreting the present results; the results do not necessarily suggest that EVT should be withheld for large ischemic region stroke with severe hyperglycemia. Hyperglycemia may have become more severe in patients with stroke who originally had a poor prognosis because of the disease stress, which may have linked hyperglycemia to a worse prognosis.26–28 The negative results of the previous randomized trials of treating hyperglycemia in patients with acute stroke may reflect the possibility that acute hyperglycemia in stroke patients is a stress response.29,30 The higher levels of white blood cell count and C-reactive protein in the group with severe hyperglycemia in the present analysis may indicate that the stroke itself was severe with poor prognosis.31 Nevertheless, the median admission glucose level of 131 mg/dL in the present study was not particularly high compared with the previous mechanical thrombectomy trials of nonlarge ischemic region stroke.6,7,32–35 Further investigation is needed to elucidate the clinical relevance of hyperglycemia in the prognosis after EVT for acute ischemic stroke.

Although it is necessary to be cautious in interpreting the present results by directly associating them to the issue of optimizing acute glycemic control in large ischemic region strokes, patients with severe hyperglycemia on admission still had a median glucose level of 200 mg/dL at 48 hours. The blood glucose levels of 10 patients who did not have severe hyperglycemia on admission became ≥180 mg/dL at 48 hours; compared with patients who experienced no severe hyperglycemia during the course, those with transient, delayed, and persistent severe hyperglycemia tended to show a less achievement rate of mRS score, a higher symptomatic ICH rate, and a higher rate of decompressive craniectomies. Current stroke guidelines suggest treating hyperglycemia to achieve blood glucose levels in the range of 140 to 180 mg/dL (7.8–10.0 mmol/L) with close monitoring to avoid hypoglycemia.36,37 However, the evidence for this recommendation is significantly limited.29,30,38  

It should be noted that the sample size was not large enough to fully elucidate the association between blood glucose levels and outcomes. The findings of this study should be considered exploratory and need to be verified by an independent study with enough statistical power. In addition, the data set of RESCUE-Japan LIMIT does not contain information on fasting blood glucose, the glucose-lowering medications used before or at the onset of index stroke, or the methods of glucose lowering therapy used during the acute stroke management. Lastly, it is unclear from the present analysis whether correction of hyperglycemia in patients with large ischemic region stroke leads to improved prognosis.  

# CONCLUSIONS  

Severe hyperglycemia was associated with worse functional prognosis and increased symptomatic ICH and decompressive craniectomy in patients with large ischemic region strokes. The negative impact of severe hyperglycemia on EVT outcomes needs independent confirmation.
