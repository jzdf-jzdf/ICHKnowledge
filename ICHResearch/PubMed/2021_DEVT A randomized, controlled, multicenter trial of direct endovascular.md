# Abstract  

Background: Eight randomized controlled trials have consistently shown that endovascular treatment plus best medical treatment improves outcome after acute anterior proximal intracranial large vessel occlusion strokes. Whether intravenous thrombolysis prior to endovascular treatment in patients with anterior circulation, large vessel occlusion is of any additional benefits remains unclear.  

Objective: This study compares the safety and efficacy of direct endovascular treatment versus intravenous recombinant tissue-type plasminogen activator bridging with endovascular treatment (bridging therapy) in acute stroke patients with intracranial internal carotid artery or middle cerebral artery-M1 occlusion within $4.5\mathrm{h}$ of symptom onset.  

Methods and design: The DEVT study is a randomized, controlled, multicenter trial with blinded outcome assessment. This trial uses a five-look group-sequential non-inferiority design. Up to 194 patients in each interim analysis will be consecutively randomized to direct endovascular treatment or bridging therapy group in 1:1 ratio over three years from about 30 hospitals in China.  

Outcomes: The primary end-point is the proportion of independent neurological function defined as modified Rankin scale score of 0 to 2 at 90 days. The primary safety measure is symptomatic intracerebral hemorrhage at $48\mathrm{h}$ and mortality at 90 days.  

Trial registry number: ChiCTR-IOR-17013568 (www.chictr.org.cn).

# Introduction and rationale  

Intravenous thrombolysis (IVT) with recombinant tissue-type plasminogen activator (rt-PA) within $4.5\mathrm{h}$ of symptom onset has demonstrated the benefits for acute ischemic stroke, irrespective of age and stroke subtype, and despite an increased risk of intracerebral hemorrhage (ICH).1 However, in large vessel occlusion (LVO), its efficacy is limited by a short therapeutic window and the low recanalization rate.2 Recently, eight landmark endovascular treatment (EVT) trials have shown EVT in addition to best medical treatment (with or without intravenous[IV] rt-PA) was superior to best medical treatment alone for acute ischemic stroke (AIS) due to LVO in the anterior circulation.3,4 However, the number of patients in these trials who did not receive prior IVT was very small, and therefore it remains unclear whether pretreated with IV rt-PA provides any additional benefits to the patients undergoing EVT.  

Compared with EVT alone, administration of IV rtPA before EVT has several potential benefits: IVT can be initiated earlier; it might contribute to higher recanalization rates, may help recanalize thrombi in small vessels and in the microvascular structure, inaccessible for endovascular devices.5 However, IVT also has potential limitations: IVT is inefficient in the patients of LVO with a large thrombus load; the therapeutic window for IV rt-PA is short; administration of IV rt-PA might delay EVT in some patients; IVT may increase the probability of intracerebral or systemic hemorrhage. In addition, IVT limits the administration of anticoagulants or antiplatelets after permanent implantation of a stent, and increases the healthcare costs.6 Moreover, IVT before EVT is associated with increased incidence of clot migration, leading to increased proportion of clot inaccessibility by EVT.7  

Recently, available clinical evidence from several single-center studies and multicenter studies demonstrates that in patients with anterior circulation LVO stroke, direct endovascular mechanical thrombectomy initiated within an IVT time window is equally effective and not inferior to bridging thrombolysis.8 Thus, direct EVT is potentially a reasonable treatment strategy in patients with acute anterior circulation LVO with a large thrombus load.  

Therefore, we aimed to investigate the differences in efficacy and safety between direct EVT and bridging therapy in acute stroke patients who are IV rt-PA eligible and have proximal LVO in the anterior circulation. We hypothesize that direct EVT initiated within $4.5\mathrm{h}$ of stroke onset is not inferior to bridging therapy in acute stroke patients with a proximal LVO in the anterior circulation.  

# Methods  

# Study design  

Direct endovascular treatment versus standard bridging therapy in large artery anterior circulation stroke (DEVT) trial is a multicenter, prospective, randomized, open-label controlled clinical trial with blinded endpoint evaluation (PROBE design) (Figure 1). It is an academic trial designed by the principal investigators and a steering committee consisting of experts in cerebrovascular diseases and interventional neuroradiology. # Patient population  

![](images/e56de055d2363a416f9e38d41662f74f0f598fe1a85f66033065a32099b27afc.jpg)  

Inclusion criteria  

1. Aged 18 years or older.   
2. Presenting with AIS symptom within $4.5\mathrm{h}$ .   
3. Eligible for IV rt-PA.   
4. Occlusion of the intracranial internal carotid artery (ICA) or M1 segment of the middle cerebral artery (MCA) confirmed by CT or MR angiography (CTA or MRA).   
5. Randomization no later than 4 h 15 min after stroke symptom onset.   
6. Informed consent obtained from patients or their legal representatives.  

# Exclusion criteria  

1. CT or MR evidence of hemorrhage (the presence of micro-bleeds is allowed).   
2. Contraindications of IV rt-PA.   
3. Pre-morbidity with a modified Rankin scale (mRS) score of $\geq2$ .   
4. Currently in pregnant or lactating or serum beta HCG test is positive on admission.   
5. Contraindication to radiographic contrast agents, nickel, titanium metals, or their alloys.   
6. Arterial tortuosity and/or other arterial disease that would prevent the device from reaching the target vessel.   
7. Patients with a preexisting neurological or psychiatric disease that would confound the neurological functional evaluations.   
8. Subjects with occlusions in multiple vascular territories (e.g. bilateral anterior circulation, or anterior/posterior circulation).   
9. CT or MRI evidence of mass effect or intracranial tumor (except small meningioma).   
0. CT or MRI evidence of cerebral vasculitis.   
1. CTA or MRA evidence of intracranial arteriovenous malformations or aneurysms.   
2. Any terminal illness with life expectancy less than six months.  

13. Unlikely to be available for 90-day follow-up.   
14. Current participation in another clinical trial.  

# Participating center eligibility  

To be fully eligible for participation in this trial, study centers were required to have performed at least 80 endovascular procedures annually, including at least 50 thrombectomy procedures with the stent-retriever devices. Moreover, all neurointerventionists should have more than five years’ experience in cerebrovascular intervention and at least 10 cases of mechanical thrombectomy with stent retriever devices annually.  

# Randomization  

Subjects will be randomly assigned in a 1:1 fashion to receive direct EVT or IV rt-PA plus EVT (bridging therapy). Randomization occurs immediately after baseline (at the EVT institution) CT/MR brain imaging and CT/MR angiography via a real-time, dynamic internet-based, minimal sufficient balance randomization method (www.ratjin.com). The randomization was stratified by participating centers.  

# Treatments  

Patients are assigned to receive either direct EVT or bridging therapy. In the bridging therapy group (control group), subjects will receive a single rt-PA dose of $0.9\mathrm{mg/kg\mathrm{IV}}$ (maximum dose: $90\mathrm{mg}$ ), with $10\%$ given as a bolus, followed by continuous IV infusion of the rest dose within $^{1\textrm{h}}$ . Simultaneously, EVT preparation should be initiated with or as soon as IV rt-PA administration. While in the direct EVT group (active group), subjects will receive EVT directly without prior IV rtPA. Subjects in both groups will undergo rapid EVT. EVT consisted of mechanical thrombectomy, thromboaspiration, balloon dilation, stenting, intra-arterial thrombolysis, or various combinations of these approaches. The choice of technique is left to the discretion of the treating neurointerventionist. Additionally, stenting of the extracranial or intracranial artery is permitted when absolutely necessary to obtain access to distal occlusion or to prevent acute re-occlusion. This may require the use of thrombolytic agents to prevent acute stent thrombosis. After recanalization of the target artery, all patients will get stroke unit care and postoperative management follows the current American Heart Association/American Stroke Association guidelines.9  

The use of conscious sedation or general anesthesia for the procedure to ensure the comfort and safety of patients is at the discretion of the individual site neurointerventionalist. The steering committee will make recommendations for dosages of thrombolytic agents, procedures, and for devices that will be considered in the trial based on proposals by the executive committee or local investigators. The requirements for a device to be considered in the trial should be approved by the China Food and Drug Administration or National Medical Products Administration.  

# Efficacy end-points  

Primary end-point. The primary end-point is the proportion of independent neurological function defined as modified Rankin scale score of 0 to 2 at 90 days. Primary end-point assessments at 90 days will be conducted by two separate certified physicians who will be blinded to the initial treatment information. To ensure the reliability, evaluability, and traceability of the mRS score, we keep patients’ video version of follow-up at 90 days except those who die or refuse to take a video.  

# Clinical secondary end-points  

1. Proportion of mRS score 0 to 1 at 90 days.   
2. Shift in the distribution of mRS scores at 90 days in direct EVT versus bridging therapy (ordinal shift analysis).   
3. Successful recanalization proportion immediate after EVT. Successful recanalization is defined as a modified treatment in cerebral infarction score of 2b (substantial perfusion), 2c (near-complete perfusion) or 3 (complete reperfusion) in the post-procedure angiography.10   
4. Vessel recanalization rate evaluated by CTA or MRA within $48\mathrm{{h}}$ .   
5. The change of the National Institutes of Health Stroke Scale (NIHSS) score at $24\mathrm{{h}}$ from baseline.11   
6. The change of the NIHSS score at five to seven days or discharge if earlier from baseline.   
7. European Quality five dimensions (EQ-5D) scale score at 90 days.  

# Safety end-points  

1. Symptomatic intracerebral hemorrhage (sICH) rate within $48\mathrm{{h}}$ . ICH will be evaluated according to the Heidelberg Bleeding Classification.12 sICH was diagnosed if the new observed ICH was associated with any of the following conditions: (1) NIHSS score increased more than 4 points than that immediately before worsening; (2) NIHSS score increased more than 2 points in one category; (3) Deterioration led to intubation, hemicraniectomy, external ventricular drain placement, or any other major interventions. Additionally, the symptom deteriorations could not be explained by causes other than the observed ICH.  

2. Mortality at 90 days. 3. Procedure-related complications such as arterial perforation, arterial dissection, and embolization in previously uninvolved vascular territory. 4. Incidence of serious adverse events.  

# Blinding and masking  

Each site will designate one or more physician(s) to perform the follow-up evaluation at $24\mathrm{{h}}$ , five to seven days or discharge if earlier and at 90 days who cannot be involved in care of the subjects and must remain blinded to treatment assignment of each subject.  

Regarding the NIHSS examination at baseline, $24\mathrm{{h}}$ , five to seven days or discharge if earlier and the primary end-point, first, a local independent neurologist, not involved in the trial patient management, will evaluate the scores in a face-to-face clinical visit, recording the examination by video with the consent of patient or the legal representative; and second, two experienced and certified physicians will centrally evaluate the score by using the video recording. For cases with disagreement between the two assessors, decisions were made by the third experienced neurologist.  

All neuroimaging end-points including recanalization within $48\mathrm{{h}}$ , Alberta Stroke Program Early CT Score (ASPECTS) score, collateral circulation classification, and hemorrhage will be determined by the $\mathrm{{CT}/}$ MR core laboratory, which will be also blinded to treatment allocation. Another independent angiographic core lab will review angiographic images from the procedure to determine clot location and recanalization.  

Serious adverse events (SAEs) and procedure-related complications will be reviewed and adjudicated by two individuals of the independent clinical events committee who will be unblinded to treatment allocation.  

# Data safety monitoring board  

The independent DSMB will be composed of an experienced neurologist, an interventionalist, and a biostatistician, which are not involved in the trial. The DSMB will meet at least once a year, and is provided with structured unmasked reports, prepared by the trial statistician, for their reference only. DSMB is responsible for recommendations to the executive committee regarding stopping or extending the trial. In addition, the DSMB will review the occurrence of SAEs and make recommendations to the executive committee regarding safety of the trial.  

# Imaging core laboratory  

Centralized imaging core laboratories will be used in this trial to provide consistent assessment of all the images. CT/MR and angiographic images will be independently reviewed by two independent central imaging core laboratories. CT/MR core laboratory will review CT/MR images obtained at baseline and within $24\mathrm{{h}}$ for confirmation of inclusion criteria, ASPECTS score, collateral circulation classification, and presence/absence of hemorrhage. Angiographic core laboratory will review angiographic images from the procedure to determine clot location and recanalization. CT/MR core laboratory will be independent from the angiographic core laboratory to ensure the $\mathbf{CT/MR}$ core laboratory is blinded to the treatment allocation.  

# Clinical events committee  

The CEC will be comprised of three expert physicians independent of the investigational sites. This committee will validate all the complications that occur over the course of the study and categorized for severity and relatedness according to the definition in the Adverse Event section in the CEC Manual of Operations. The CEC can request any additional source information and images supporting the adverse events to assist with the adjudication.  

# Sample size estimates  

According to the previous study data,4,6,13,14 we hypothesis that the 90-day follow-up proportion of independent functional outcome is $43\%$ both in the direct EVT group and bridging therapy group. The clinically relevant non-inferiority margin $\Delta$ was $-10.0\%$ . To maintain the alpha, Pocock Analog Alpha Spending Function is used. Sample size and power are computed incorporating a five-look groupsequential analysis plan with a one-sided $\propto$ at 0.025, 918 cases provide $80\%$ power for testing the primary hypothesis of this trial; assuming the attrition rate is $5\%$ for the primary end-point, the total sample size is up to 970. The evaluable sample size is 194 at each interim analysis. Therefore, in each interim analysis, 97 cases should be enrolled in each treatment group.  

# Statistical analyses  

Non-inferiority test will be used to test the primary hypothesis that the proportion of patients with independent functional outcome will be non-inferior in the direct EVT group compared to the bridging therapy group. We desired a maximum of five looks when approximately 20, 40, 60, 80, and $100\%$ of the total sample size finish the follow-up, monitoring, and data cleaning processes. A group-sequential test strategy was designed to have reasonable chances of stopping as early as possible, either because of efficacy or safety reasons. The independent DSMB may recommend stopping the trial either for effectiveness, or safety in case the stopping boundaries are crossed at interim analysis. For shedding cases, follow-up will be performed until the end of the study, and the results will be included in the final analysis. Data will be analyzed under the principles of intent-to-treat with the per protocol set as reference. Statistical analysis will be performed on the SAS 9.3 system.  

# Ethical considerations  

This research followed the ethical principles of the Helsinki Declaration. To make sure the subjects fully understand about this trial, the investigators must provide the patients or their legal representatives with detailed information about the clinical trial, including the purpose of the trial, possible benefits and risks, and the rights/obligations. Subjects have the right to withdraw from the study at any time if they wish to do so. The privacy protection of subjects has to be ensured. The patients or their legal representatives give their written informed consent prior to the study. Each patient must leave contact information to the investigator of the participating center. At the same time, the investigator must leave his own phone number to the patient so that the patient can find the investigator at any time. Ethical approval for the study was obtained by the Ethics Committee of the participating centers.  

# Study organization  

DEVT trial is an investigator-initiated study which is organized by the second affiliated hospital of the Third Military Medical University and conducted in about 30 comprehensive stroke centers in China.  

# Discussion and conclusion  

The DEVT trial is designed to compare the safety and efficacy of direct EVT with IV rt-PA plus EVT for acute stroke patients with a proximal LVO in the anterior circulation within $4.5\mathrm{h}$ of stroke onset.  

According to the prior studies,15,16 relevant recanalization rates of small arteries after IVT and before EVT in bridging patients are much higher than that of large vessels. DEVT is only enrolling patients with occlusion sites that are significantly responsive to EVT, including intracranial ICA and M1 MCA lesions that respond poorly to IV rt-PA and excluding M2 and more distal MCA lesions that frequently benefit from IV rt-PA alone.  

Time has a crucial impact on the rate of good outcomes. Previous study demonstrates that less than $15\%$ of patients will recanalize within $2\mathrm{{h}}$ of IV rt-PA so waiting to image and thus delaying EVT for all patients to prevent diagnostic angiography in less than $15\%$ seems inadvisable.17 In the bridging therapy arm of our study, the preparation for EVT will start immediately after randomization for IV rt-PA within $4.5\mathrm{h}$ after acute stroke onset, with no need to wait for the 1 h rt-PA infusion.  

We do not restrict the use of multiple EVT modalities per patient. This is a limitation of the trial design, because it will restrict the possibilities of comparing different treatment modalities and only allows us to give a global judgment. In addition, in order to be as practical as possible, the design of this study allows new mechanical devices and therapeutic strategies into the trial, even after the start of the study. Moreover, for a decrease in the occurrence of procedure-related complications during the conduct of the trial, we required a certain amount of experience with EVT from each device and the number of procedures done by the neurointerventionist.  

DEVT enrolled its first patient on 20 May 2018. When completed, DEVT will suggest that in patients with anterior circulation LVO stroke, direct EVT initiated within an IVT time window carries similar effectiveness to bridging therapy. Our trial provides novel evidence that direct EVT may be an alternative for bridging therapy. As we know, there are five other similar studies, MR CLEAN NO IV (ISRCTN registry ISRCTN80619088), DIRECT SAFE (ClinicalTrials.gov Identifier: NCT03494920), SWIFT DIRECT (ClinicalTrials.gov Identifier: NCT03192332), SKIP,18 and DIRECT MT,19 investigating the same issue. We will contact these research groups. A meta-analysis of these trials for direct EVT may be conducted.
