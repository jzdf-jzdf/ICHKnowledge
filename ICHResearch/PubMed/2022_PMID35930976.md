# ABSTRACT  

Background: Recent studies suggest only a third of posterior circulation stroke patients have a good functional outcome with a high mortality after mechanical thrombectomy. To mitigate mortality rates and increase func­ tional outcomes, we investigated the safety and efficacy of high-flow, normobaric oxygen (NBO) after endo­ vascular recanalization in posterior circulation stroke.   
Methods: This is a prospective randomized controlled study. Eligible patients were randomized to receive highflow NBO by a Venturi mask $\mathrm{(FiO}_{2}50\%$ , flow $15\mathrm{L}/\mathrm{min}$ or routine low-flow oxygen supplementation by nasal cannula (flow $3~{\mathrm{L/min}}.$ after vessel recanalization for $^\textrm{\scriptsize6h}$ . Patient demographics, procedural metrics, compli­ cations, functional outcomes, symptomatic intracranial hemorrhage (sICH), and infarct volume were assessed. Results: While we assessed 122 patients for eligibility, 87 patients were randomly assigned (44 patients to the NBO group). Post operatively there was no significant difference in distribution of global disability scores on the mRS at 90 days or functional independence between the two groups. We did observe a trend suggesting reduced mortality at 90 days with reduced infarct volume in the NBO group, however this was not statistically significant. No significant differences were seen in the rate of sICH, pneumonia or urinary infection between the two groups. When comparing our results with the BASICS and BEST study, our study did reveal a significantly better prog­ nosis after endovascular therapy.  

Conclusion: Our results indicate that high-flow adjuvant NBO therapy was safe. However, the current study does not provide evidence for a significant neuroprotection effect in posterior circulation stroke patients after endovascular recanalization.  



# 1. Introduction  

Endovascular therapy has improved functional outcomes in acute ischemic stroke (AIS) patients with large vessel occlusion (LVO) in the anterior circulation [1,2]. In contrast, accumulated data remain less definitive for patients with posterior circulation LVO. The BASICS and BEST randomized controlled trials (RCTs) both failed to demonstrate efficacy of mechanical thrombectomy for basilar artery occlusion [3,4], despite other studies such at BASILAR with positive results post thrombectomy [5]. Although successful reperfusion can be achieved via mechanical thrombectomy in posterior circulation stroke, only a third of these patients had a good functional outcome. One third of these pa­ tients also expired.[6].  

Combining neuroprotection with intravenous or intra-arterial reperfusion therapy was considered to be an important next step in the development of acute stroke therapies [7]. Oxygen supplementation has long-been suggested to reduce secondary injury to penumbral tissue, minimize damage and promote neural recovery and plasticity in AIS [8–12]. Normobaric oxygen (NBO) has gained substantial attention for its clinical ease and feasibility and has been recognized as a potential adjuvant neuroprotective therapy in AIS [13]. Following endovascular recanalization in anterior circulation stroke patients, our previous clinical study has suggested high-flow NBO therapy decreased mortality and infarct volumes. However, no study has been reported on the use of NBO as an adjuvant neuroprotective strategy in posterior circulation AIS after endovascular recanalization [14]. In order to improve prognosis of posterior circulation stroke after mechanical thrombectomy, additional adjunctive neuroprotective treatment strategies may help mitigate mortality rates and increase good functional outcomes.  

Given reperfusion can been successfully reestablished by endovas­ cular therapy, this study was the first to our knowledge to determine whether the administration of NBO post vessel recanalization conferred a safe and clinical benefit in posterior circulation stroke patients.  



# 2. Methods  



# 2.1. Participants  

We conducted a prospective randomized controlled trial to assess the safety and efficacy of NBO administration in AIS patients with posterior circulation LVO after mechanical thrombectomy. One hundred and twenty-two stroke patients with LVO in the posterior circulation were screened consecutively in the Stroke Intervention and Translational Center (SITC), Beijing Luhe Hospital Capital Medical University. The protocol was approved by the Beijing Luhe Hospital Research Ethics Committee. The participants gave a fully informed written consent or this was provided by a legal representative if they did not have the ca­ pacity to consent. The study has been registered on ClinicalTrials.gov (ChiCTR-INR-17013685).  

Included patients met the following criteria: 1) age between 18 and 80 years, 2) clinical diagnosis of AIS, 3) large vessel occlusion in the posterior circulation demonstrated with CTA, MRA or DSA, 4) a baseline National Institutes of Health Stroke Scale (NIHSS) score $\geq6$ , 5) ${<}6$ h from stroke onset to groin puncture. Exclusion criteria were as follows: 1) Rapid spontaneous improvement in neurological status to an NIHSS ${<}6$ or evidence of spontaneous vessel recanalization, 2) No vessel recanalization (TICI $\mathrm{score}=0.$ ) after mechanical thrombectomy, 3) Symptoms that rapidly improved after vessel recanalization, 4) Brain imaging revealing an bilateral extended brainstem ischemia or large area of bilateral cerebellar ischemia, 5) pre-stroke modified Rankin scale $(\mathrm{mRS})>2,6)$ active and chronic obstructive pulmonary disease, such as chronic obstructive pulmonary disease, obstructive sleep apnea and acute respiratory distress syndrome, 7) Use of an oxygen face mask or ventilator for the purpose of maintaining appropriate oxygen saturation levels, 8) General anesthesia or intubation, 9) Severe arterial blood pressure $>185/110\mathrm{mmHg},$ 10) Blood glucose $<2.7$ or $>22.2\mathrm{mmol/L}$ , 11) Laboratory evidence of coagulopathy (platelet count ${<}40\times10^{9}/\mathrm{L}$ , $\mathbf{APTT}>50\mathbf{s}_{:}$ , or INR ${>}3.0\$ ), 12) Pregnancy, 13)Patients being enrolled or having been enrolled in other clinical trial within 3 months prior to this clinical trial, 14) Informed consent was not obtained.  



# 2.2. Randomization  

During the recruitment period, participants were allocated 1:1 to two groups by computer-generated, randomization procedures. To avoid the possible bias from different visual cues between mask and nasal cath­ eter, patient variables and outcomes, before and after oxygen supple­ mentation, were assessed by 2 groups of independent physicians who were either blinded to the treatment and patient evaluation/data collection. Specifically, the first group was responsible for initial patient screening and performing 6-h oxygen administration. Following oxygen supplementation, case observation, data collection and statistical anal­ ysis were carried out by the second group. Clinicians, data collectors in the second group, participant families, adjudicators and data analysts were all blinded to the study.  



# 2.3. Intervention  

Patients who were eligible for intravenous thrombolysis received recombinant tissue plasminogen activator (rt-PA) at a dose of $0.9\mathrm{mg/kg}$ $10\%$ of the dose was given as a bolus within 1 min followed by a $60\mathrm{min}$ total infusion) while endovascular intervention was simultaneously being prepared. All endovascular procedures were performed under local anesthesia with/without sedative agents. Interventional strategies were left to the discretion of the treating interventionalist, including the choice of stent retrievers, aspiration, stenting or other necessary devices. Following the procedure, as described previously by us [14], patients received supplemental high-flow NBO therapy by a Venturi mask $(\mathrm{FiO}_{2}$ $50\%$ , flow $15~\mathrm{L/min}.$ ) or routine low-flow oxygen supplementation by nasal cannula (flow $3~\mathrm{L/min}.$ after vessel recanalization as soon as possible, which was continued for $^{6\textrm{h}}$ .  



# 2.4. Outcomes measures  

All participants had standard assessments of demographic charac­ teristics, medical history, stroke severity and interventional procedures. The primary outcome is distribution of global mRS score at 90 days. The mRS is a graded interval scale (range, 0 [no symptoms] to 6 [death]) for the assessment of neurologic functional disability [15], which was assessed by trained personnel who were blinded to the treatment-group assignments in the study. The secondary outcome including clinical ef­ ficacy outcome [functional independence (mRS scores of 0 to 2), mor­ tality (mRS score 6), NIHSS score at $24\mathrm{~h~}$ and infarct volume] and clinical safety outcome [symptomatic intracranial hemorrhage (sICH), fatal ICH, pneumonia and urinary infection].  



# 2.5. Imaging assessment  

A brain computed tomographic (CT) scan was obtained at baseline and immediately after endovascular intervention to assess ischemic stroke burden and ICH. Magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) was routinely performed at $24\mathrm{~h~}$ after vessel recanalization. If MRI could not be completed or there was any sign of neurological deterioration within $24\mathrm{~h~}$ , another head CT scan was performed. Infarct volume was measured on a Siemens Syngo.Via workstation with diffusion-weighted MRI (https://www.siemens healthineers.com/magnetic-resonance im­ aging/advanced-imaging  

applications/syngo-via). Lesions were outlined on each individual slice using the image tool in the workstation to calculate area. The infarct volumes were calculated by multiplying the area of each slice-byslice thickness. Target vessel recanalization was assessed by the modi­ fied Thrombolysis in Cerebral Infarction (mTICI) scale [16]. TICI scoring was assessed by 2 neuroradiologists blinded to clinical presentation and treatment group.  



# 2.6. Statistical analysis  

Sample size was estimated from one previous NBO study [17] based on the difference in mRS score at 90 days between NBO treated and control group. A total of 180 patients were calculated as necessary to show a favorable outcome within NBO groups at 90 days after posterior vessel recanalization in AIS. The study was terminated early because of difficulties with patient recruitment and a total of 90 patients were enrolled at termination. All analysis was performed in the intention-totreat population. The common odds ratio of the mRS score at 90 days was estimated by using ordinal logistic-regression model. The rate ratio of mRS 0–2, mRS 6 and adverse events were estimated by using modified poisson regression approach [18]. Beta coefficient for infarct volume and NIHSS score were estimated by using multiple linear regression. Estimates of effect were adjusted for age, sex, baseline NIHSS score, location of occlusion, status with respect to intravenous alteplase treatment, the number of passages of retriever stent, time from

# 3. Results  



# 3.1. Patients  

A total of 122 patients were screened and 90 patients were enrolled and randomized in the study. Three patients were lost to follow-up and a total of 87 patients were finally analyzed (44 in NBO group and 43 in the control group) (Fig. 1). Baseline demographic and characteristics of the patients are summarized in Table 1. Age, sex ratio, baseline NIHSS, vascular risk factors, location of stroke, IV rt-PA, stenting or balloon dilatation utilization, time from onset to recanalization, time from treatment to recanalization, the number of passages of retriever stent, TICI score, GCS (Glasgow Coma Scale) and SBP (systolic blood pressure) were not significantly different between the NBO and control groups (Table 1).  



# 3.2. Primary outcome  

There was no significant difference in the distribution of global disability scores based on the mRS at 90 days (adjusted common odds ratio, 0.95; $95\%$ CI, 0.38 to 2.35; $p=0.900.$ ) (Fig. 2 and Table 2).  



# 3.3. Secondary outcomes  

There was no significant difference between the two groups in the proportion of patients who were functionally independent. Meantime, only a trend of reducing mortality at 90 days was found in the NBO group when compared to the control group, with an absolute difference of 9.67 percentage points (adjusted risk ratio, 0.46; $95\%$ CI, 0.21–1.01). This was not statistically significant $(p=0.053)$ . Additionally, at $24\mathrm{~h~}$ after stroke onset, a slight reduction in infarct volume of cerebellum and total infarct volume as determined by MRI was found in the NBO group, the mean infarct volume was $9.1\mathrm{ml}$ compared with $14.9~\mathrm{ml}$ in the control group (adjusted beta coefficient, $-3.87$ ; $95\%$ CI, $-11.87–4.13;$ , although this was not statistically significant $(p=0.34)$ . (Table2 and Fig. 3). There also was no significant difference between two groups in the rate of ICH, symptomatic ICH, pneumonia and urinary infection (Table 2).

# 4. Discussion  

This study was the first, to our knowledge, to reveal the safety of administration of high-flow NBO adjuvant therapy after endovascular recanalization in AIS patients with posterior circulation LVO. No increased risk of ICH, pneumonia and urinary infection were observed following this therapy. We noted a trend of decreasing mortality at 90 days after stroke onset and reducing cerebellum infarct volume, however, a significant effect of NBO in posterior circulation AIS patients was not found after successful endovascular recanalization.

# 4.1. Safety of NBO administration  

Many studies have shown NBO administration to be safe in AIS pa­ tients [19]. However, there are conflicting reports in the literature, wherein some studies have reported NBO as a harmful adjuvant in AIS with or without endovascular therapy [20,21]. Recently, our previous study demonstrated the safety of NBO administration after endovascular recanalization in anterior circulation AIS patients [14]. No studies to our knowledge have focused on the efficacy of oxygen supplementation in posterior circulation stroke, especially after endovascular therapy. This study indicated that the NBO adjuvant therapy was safe in posterior circulation stroke patients after endovascular recanalization, with no significant benefit detected at this time.  



# 4.2. Inconsistency of NBO efficacy between anterior and posterior circulation patients  

Administration of NBO in posterior circulation stroke patients after endovascular recanalization did not demonstrate the same benefit seen in anterior circulation patients [14]. Several reasons may be considered for the different outcomes. First, anterior circulation patients have been shown to exhibit a larger ischemic penumbral region as compared to posterior circulation patients [22] and NBO would be expected to play a more protective role in anterior circulation stroke patients given the larger volume of at-risk tissue. Second, neuronal density is substantially higher in posterior circulation regions and the resultant functional zone is highly concentrated in the brain stem [23]. Therefore, the degree of neurological impairment in posterior circulation stroke patients is dependent more on infarction location than total size. Furthermore, collateral status in anterior circulation strokes is far better with anterior cerebral artery (ACA) to middle cerebral artery (MCA), posterior cere­ bral artery (PCA) to MCA collaterals etc. able to help with NBO distri­ bution. Posterior circulation collaterals are typically much less robust especially for “end vessels” such as pontine perforators in the mid basilar segment. Because of the above reasons, the strategy combining neuro­ protection with reperfusion therapy may be limited in posterior circu­ lation stroke. Thus, it is very difficult to demonstrate any additional therapeutic value of neuroprotection derived from NBO in the posterior circulation stroke. Our study design allowed for a favorable rate of $50\%$ $\mathrm{mRS~}0{-}2$ achieved by current technique in posterior circulation, this could mask any suggested NBO benefit given we selected non vented patients, however this is less likely. On the other hand, because a trend in decreased mortality and decreased infarct volume in the cerebellum was observed with a relatively small sample size here, the efficacy of NBO in posterior circulation AIS need be further explored in a future study.  



# 4.3. Implication from the comparison with BASICS and BEST study  

BASICS and BEST studys did not provide evidence of a favorable neurological outcomes in patients treated with endovascular therapy in posterior circulation stroke due to difficulty with patient recruitment and implementation during the study [3,4]. As comparing with BASICS and BEST studies, our study details a significantly better prognostic outcome after endovascular therapy. In those two studies, only $30\%$ of favorable prognosis was observed, as compared to $50\%$ in our study. Additionally, there appears to be a difference in mortality rate in our study, which was reduced by about $10\%$ as compared to BASICS or BEST. The reason for our favorable outcome may be due to a shorter vessel recanalization time (272 min vs. $400\mathrm{min}$ in BEST) and higher rate of successful recanalization $90.2\%$ vs. $72\%$ in BASICS and $71\%$ in BEST) in our study. Thus, we speculate that the favorable outcome in the present study was due to more optimal patient selection and better technical success. The criteria of RCT on the efficacy of endovascular therapy for posterior circulation LVO patients may need be reconsidered in the future. Nevertheless, our results indicate that compared to the natural history of basilar artery occlusion, mechanical thrombectomy is worthwhile.  



# 4.4. Limitations  

The study was terminated early because of difficulties in patient recruitment with only 90 patients being enrolled in the end. It warrants a multicenter study with large sample to explore the efficacy of NBO in posterior circulation stroke patients after endovascular therapy.  



# 5. Conclusion  

The current study demonstrated the safety of high-flow NBO therapy but did not provide evidence for its neuroprotection in posterior circu­ lation AIS patients after endovascular recanalization. Basilar throm­ bectomy in this randomized study performed better in terms of outcome at 90 days, and mortality than previous randomized controlled studies.
