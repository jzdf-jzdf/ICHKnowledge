Single versus double burr hole for drainage of chronic subdural haematoma:  A randomized controlled Study  

Introduction  

Chronic subdural haematoma is one of the most common neurosurgical emergencies.1-6 Surgical techniques such as craniotomy, burr holes have been used for its drainage.7,10 A review of the various treatment modalities of CSDH concluded that burr hole craniostomy has the best cure rate.2 Most neurosurgeons currently drained CSDH through single or double burr holes, however there are few studies that have compared this 2 approaches to drainage.8-,14 Most of this studies were done outside sub-Saharan Africa and few suffers from one or more methodological flaws.  

The aim of this study is to compare the recurrent rate following double burr holes and single burr hole for chronic subdural haematoma in our practice.  

Methodology  

This is a randomized controlled study over a period of 5 years from January 2015 to December 2019 in a neurosurgical unit in Kaduna Nigeria.  

All patient with imaging diagnosis of subacute or chronic subdural haematoma were enrolled into the study  

A simple random allocation using random numbers table was used to categorized patients into either a single or the double burr hole arm. Numbers were allocated to each patient after the decision to operate was made. Random sequence were initially generated for each treatment arm and concealed.  

The surgery was done either under local anaesthesia or with local anaesthesia and conscious sedation depending on how the patient tolerated the procedure.   
Incision for Single burr hole was placed at the mid parietal region while that for the double burr holes were placed at posterior frontal and posterior parietal regions. Periosteal elevator was used to elevate the scalp from the skull and the wound was opened with mastoid retractors. This also helps in controlling the bleeding from the scalp. The burr holes were performed utilizing a perforator, dura was coagulated with bipolar diathermy, incised with size 11 knife, and dural leaflets coagulated. Visible membrane was coagulated and CSDH allowed to drain. The subdural space was then irrigated with normal saline until the subdural effluent is clear. A size 18F feeding tube connected to a drainage bag is placed as a closed subperiosteal drain and the wound was closed in the standard way.  

The drain was removed after 48 hours in all the patients.  

The patients were followed up for 6 months after surgery to assess for clinical features of recurrence which is the primary outcome measure. Secondary outcome measure is the resolution of presenting features.  

The clinical parameters used for recurrence include; reappearance of the initial presenting symptoms and signs, altered level of consciousness, headaches, convulsions or neurological deficits.  Only patients with recurrent symptoms had brain CT scan for confirmation Data was analyzed using SPSS version 25 for Windows.  Variables considered in the statistical analysis included sex, age, antiplatelet or anticoagulant therapy, bilateral haematoma, and type of surgery. These factors were evaluated to assess their independent effects on the recurrence rate using univariate analysis. Fisher's exact test was performed to compare the two treatment groups. A two-sided P value ${<}0.05$ was considered statistically significant.  

Result  

A total of 223 patients had burr hole for CSDH during the study period, however only 192 reported for follow up for up to 6 months giving an attrition rate of $13.9\%$ .  Of the 192 that were considered for analysis, 99 were in the single burr hole group and 93 in the double burr hole group.  

163 were male and 29 were females (Male female ration is 5.5:1). The mean age of patients was 52.99 with a standard deviation of 10.133. Table 1 showed that the demographic and clinical characteristics of the patients were comparable. The observed differences among these characteristics were not statistically significant.  

The overall recurrence rate in this study was $2.6\%$  

The recurrence rate in the single arm was $3\%$ and double arm was $2.2\%$ . This was not statistically significant $(\mathsf{p}=1.000)$ .  

# Discussion  

The male-female ratio of 5.6:1 in this study differs from most reports in the literature.12,13  

The mean age of 59 is slightly lower than findings in most other studies, this may be due to low life expectancy in our study area. CSDH occurs commonly in the elderly population, especially in men, is frequently associated with falls and anticoagulation or antithrombotic therapy.14 In our study the use of low dose aspirin was documented only in a very small number of patients however most of our patients had history of trauma ranging from fall to road traffic accident.  

We found no statistically significant difference in the resolution of symptoms, duration of hospital stay and recurrence rate between a single and double burr hole drainage for the treatment of CSDH. Both techniques proved to have equal efficacy in patient outcome and risk of recurrence. This finding is similar to most reported study in the literature.1,4,8,12 However, one study found single burr hole to have higher recurrent rate.3 Taussky et al evaluated 97 patients and reported that one burr hole drainage was associated with statistically significant higher rate of recurrence, longer average hospitalization length and higher wound infection rate. Their multivariate regression analysis showed that the number of burr holes had an independent prognostic power.3 In a retrospectively analysis of  267 patients with CSDH that were drained through either single or double burr hole without the use of post-operative drain Kansal et al found no statistically significant differences in terms of recurrence.1 Han et al. in a study of 180 cases of CSDH compared the postoperative recurrence rates between one and two burr craniostomy with closed-system drainage for CSDH.12 The authors found that CSDH treated with two burr holes had higher recurrence rate; however, the difference was not statistically significant.1 In another study of the treatment of CSDH with one burr-hole craniostomy with closed-system drainage with or without irrigation, two burr-hole craniostomy with closedsystem drainage with irrigation and small craniotomy with irrigation and closed-system drainage system, The authors found that small craniotomy was associated with significantly lower complications and recurrence rate.4  

In our study the operation time was longer in double burr hole which was statistically significant. This was similar to findings by Lee et.4  

Limitations of this study include low sample size and non-consideration of the volume of haematoma and presence and thickness of membrane.  

# Conclusion  

We conclude that a single burr-hole is as efficacious as a double-burr-hole in terms of relieve of symptoms and recurrence and the duration of surgery is significantly shorter for single burr hole.  

We recommend further study with higher sample size and taking care of other factors such as volume of haematoma, presence of membrane and thickness of the membrane.
