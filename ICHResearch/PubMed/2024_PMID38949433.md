# Abstract  

Platelet glycoprotein (GP) IIb/IIIa antagonists have been employed in selective patients after endovascular therapy (EVT) for acute ischemic stroke (AIS), yet application in patients without EVT is debated. This meta-analysis of randomized controlled studies on AIS patients without EVT assessed the effectiveness and safety of platelet GP IIb/IIIa antagonists compared with traditional antiplatelet or thrombolysis therapy. Articles were retrieved from databases, including PubMed, Web of Science, EMBASE, and Cochrane. The risk of bias and certainty level of evidence were assessed. Fifteen studies were included. GP IIb/IIIa antagonists increased the proportion of patients with modified Rankin Scale (mRS) 0–1 (odd ratio [OR] 1.37, $95\%$ confidence interval [CI] 1.04–1.81, $p{=}0.03.$ ), $\mathsf{mRSO}{-}2$ (OR 1.27, $95\%$ CI 1.12–1.46, $p{=}0.0004.$ ), and Barthel Index (BI) 95–100 (OR 1.25, $p{=}0.005^{\prime}$ ; decreased the proportion of stroke progression within 5 days (OR 0.66, $p{=}0.006;$ ; and lowered the mean mRS score at 90 days (mean difference [MD] $-0.43$ , $p{=}0.002$ ) and the National Institute of Health stroke scale score at 7 days (MD $-1.64$ , $p<0.00001$ ) compared with conventional treatment. Proportions of stroke recurrence within 90 days (OR 1.20, $p{=}0.60,$ ), any intracranial hemorrhage (aICH) (OR 1.20, $p{=}0.12$ ), symptomatic intracranial hemorrhage (sICH) (OR 0.91, $p{=}0.88^{\prime}$ ), and death (OR 0.87, $p{=}0.25$ ) had no statistical difference between both groups. This meta-analysis finds that compared with traditional antiplatelet or thrombolysis therapy, GP IIb/IIIa antagonists administered within 24–96 h of ischemic stroke onset significantly improve functional prognosis of patients with AIS not receiving EVT, as indicated by mRS and BI at 90 days, and do not increase the incidence of aICH, sICH, and death.

# 1  |  INTRODUCTION  

Ischemic stroke, constituting nearly $80\%$ of all strokes, ranked as the world's top cause of mortality and disability.1 The primary focus of acute ischemic stroke (AIS) treatment is restoring blood flow through recanalization of intracranial occlusion and averting infarct spread.2 Intravenous thrombolytic therapy (IVT) and endovascular therapy (EVT) stand as the top two techniques for restoring blood flow in patients with AIS.3 However, about $54\%-70\%$ of patients following IVT and $30\%$ of patients following EVT experience unsuccessful reperfusion.4-6 Whether an occlusion artery can be re-opened makes a critical difference to the prognosis of an individual patient and how to improve the recanalization rate is an urgent problem to be solved.7  

Platelet glycoprotein (GP) IIb/IIIa antagonists, including abciximab, tirofiban, and eptifibatide, serve as strong, rapid, and specific blockers of the GP IIb/IIIa complex existing on the platelet's surface.8,9 These agents function by obstructing the ultimate shared route of platelet aggregation and are capable of hindering platelet clumping triggered by all agonists including adrenaline, collagen, thrombin, and others.9,10 Platelet function can be restored within 4 h after discontinuation of tirofiban and eptifibatide, and within 24–48 h after discontinuation of abciximab.11,12  

As a result of breakthroughs that platelet GP IIb/IIIa antagonists have made in myocardial infarction treatment,13–15 increasing attention has been paid to the use of these agents in AIS. Some earlier exploratory trials have demonstrated the safety of using GP IIb/IIIa antagonists within $24\mathsf{h}$ of AIS.16–18 Some studies have demonstrated that the effectiveness of platelet GP IIb/IIIa antagonists on patient therapy of AIS was more advantageous than that of traditional antiplatelet drugs,19–22 whereas other studies failed to prove the efficacy of platelet GP IIb/IIIa antagonists in AIS.23,24  

All of these studies above have their drawbacks. The number of patients in each group was less than 50 in two studies.19,20 Two groups performed their studies in a retrospective way.21,22 Two other studies included both patients receiving EVT and patients without EVT.23,24 Meta-analyses evaluating the efficacy of GPIb/lla antagonists in AIS patients who did not receive EVT were infrequently conducted and the results were disappointing.10,25 The first meta-­ analysis on efficacy and safety of tirofiban on patients with AIS who did not receive EVT found that tirofiban did not increase the rate of intracranial hemorrhage and mortality.25 However, the analysis did not draw a conclusion of improvement in functional recovery.25 Use of platelet GP IIb/IIIa antagonists in the early phase of AIS therapy is not well established.3 In consideration of these controversial results and several recently published randomized controlled trials (RCTs) evaluating the use of GP IIb/IIIa antagonists in patients with AIS not receiving EVT,26-28 it is necessary to perform this meta-analysis of RCTs to evaluate platelet GP IIb/IIIa antagonists in patients with AIS not receiving EVT to provide high-level evidence regarding their efficacy and safety.  



# 2  |  METHODS  

The protocol for the comprehensive review and meta-analysis was officially recorded on October 23, 2023 with PROSPERO (registration number: CRD42023472031). Our research adheres to the guidelines of Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Metaanalyses29 and follows the Cochrane manual for systematic reviews.30  



# 2.1  |  Eligibility criteria  

RCTs that fulfilled all of the following criteria were incorporated: (i) population: adult patients diagnosed with AIS, for whom time from onset of symptoms to enrollment in the study was less than 72 h, (ii) intervention: platelet GP IIb/IIIa antagonists, (iii) control: traditional antiplatelet therapy (aspirin, clopidogrel, ozagrel) or IVT, (iv) outcome: the modified Rankin Scale (mRS) score after 90 days, the National Institute of Health stroke scale (NIHSS) score at 7 days, stroke progression during the first 5 days, stroke recurrence within 90 days, any intracranial hemorrhage (aICH), symptomatic intracranial hemorrhage (sICH), and death, (v) study type: only double-arm RCTs, and (vi) English language. The exclusion criteria were: (i) studies on coronary artery disease; (i) participants who received EVT (local Intra-arterial Fibrinolysis,percutaneous transluminal angioplasty, and mechanical thrombectomy)31; (ii) repeated studies; (iv) single-arm tests, cohort studies, conference abstracts, review and meta-analyses, case reports, and nonhuman studies; and (v) articles that were unable to yield accurate information.  



# 2.2  |  Searching databases  

The research articles were sourced from four distinct databases (PubMed, Web of Science, EMBASE, and Cochrane) until September 30, 2023. The keywords below were employed in our study: platelet glycoprotein IIb/IIIa antagonists, tirofiban, aggrastat, eptifibatide, abciximab, ischemic stroke, cerebrovascular accident, and brain vascular accident.  



# 2.3  |  Studies screening  

Articles downloaded from the databases searched were imported into the Zotero software (version 6.0.30) and replicated articles were removed by the software. A tripartite screening process, adhering to specific eligibility standards, was employed to examine the gathered studies. Initially, a review was conducted on the titles and abstracts of the studies that were collected. Next, a thorough examination of the complete texts of pertinent studies was conducted to determine their suitability. Ultimately, a hands-on examination of the references from the trials was conducted. Two researchers independently executed each phase. Discrepancies were settled through discussions with a third author.  



# 2.4  |  Data extraction  

After screening of the studies, information from eligible studies was gathered. The encompassed data are divided into two primary types: (i) general features of the enrolled trials and patients and (ii) information involving the outcomes specified below. Two separate authors executed this procedure independently. Discrepancies were addressed by engaging in discussions or consulting with a senior researcher. The encapsulated overview of the incorporated research included: country, trial registration number, inclusion criteria, intervention approaches, key results, and conclusions. In addition, the baseline characteristics of included participants were represented, which included group assignment, the number of samples, age, gender, the proportion taking antiplatelet drugs, occlusion location, stroke etiology, risk factors, and the proportion of receiving IVT.  



# 2.5  |  Outcome measure  

The efficacy indicators were measured by the mRS score after 90 days, Barthel Index (BI) after 90 days, the NIHSS score at 7 days, stroke progression during the first 5 days, and stroke recurrence within 90 days. Two main outcomes measured for efficacy were identified: the proportions of excellent and favorable functional outcome, which were determined by an mRS score of 0–1 and 0–2 at 3- month follow up, respectively.27,32 The safety outcomes comprised rates of aICH, sICH, and death. Only aICH and sICH occurring within the first 7 days were incorporated. Patients who died during the first 3 months were calculated into the death rate.  



# 2.6  |  Quality assessment  

Three researchers undertook the evaluation of risk of bias using Version 2 of the Cochrane risk-of-bias tool for randomized trials (RoB 2).33 When results of risk of bias were inconsistent, a fourth researcher was to review the results and give a final conclusion. The certainty level of evidence was assessed by three authors using Grading of Recommendations Assessment, Development, and Evaluation (GRADE)34 and if confronted with disputes, the three authors managed to reach mutual agreement by discussion. Ten outcomes of clinical significance were established and presented in the GRADE table showing the level of evidence for the main outcomes. In cases where data from the studies could not determine our results or evaluate their general quality, we contacted authors directly involved to gather the required details.  



# 2.7  |  Data analysis  

Data analyses were conducted using RevMan Web (Version 5.4, The Cochrane Collaboration, UK) and Stata Software Package (Version 18.0, StataCorp, Texas, USA). For continuous data, the mean difference (MD) and the $95\%$ confidence interval (CI) were utilized. For binary data, the odds ratio (OR) and $95\%$ CI were utilized. $p\leq0.05$ identified statistical significance. In cases where median and quartiles were presented as summary statistics for continuous data, the quantile estimation method was utilized to determine the average and standard deviation.35–37 Heterogeneity was assessed using the Chi-square test and the $I^{2}$ index, as used in most meta-­ analyses.30 If $p$ was ${<}0.1$ with $1^{2}>50\%$ , indicating heterogeneous results,a random-effect model was chosen as the statistical method for effect measure. If $I^{2}$ was $<50\%$ , indicating homogeneity, a fixedeffect model was applied as the statistical method. Sensitivity analysis was done for all outcome indicators simply by omitting a study in turn to verify the stability of pooled estimates. Publication bias was evaluated by inspecting whether the funnel plots are symmetrical.  



# 3  | RESULTS  



# 3.1  |  Overview of outcomes from database search and study selection  

After deduplication, we identified 1525 articles published through September 30, 2023 from the various databases searched (Figure 1). In total, 1477 studies underwent full-text screening. After removing ineligible studies for different reasons, 15 RCTs were included in our meta-analysis.16-19,26-28,328-44 Figure 1 illustrates the PRIMA flowchart for searching databases and selecting studies. Of these studies, eight studies were conducted in China, five in the United States, and two in European countries.  



# 3.2  |  Characteristics of the included patients  

Our study encompassed 4537 participants from 15 RCTs, with 2479 undergoing treatment with platelet GP IIb/IIIa antagonists and 2058 receiving traditional antiplatelet or IVT treatment. Patients in the study were predominantly male $(58\%)$ , averaging 67.3 years in age. Eleven studies including 3907 patients provided information on the geographical distribution of patients.19,26–28,32,38,40–44 Two thousand six hundred and thirty-two patients were from China, 756 from Europe, 476 from North America and South America, and 43 from others. Patients in 10 studies18,27,28,38–44 did not received IVT, while all patients in four studies16,17,19,26 received IVT. In one study,32 $12.6\%$ of patients were administered IVT. One thousand two hundred and seventy five patients $(28.1\%)$ in three studies38,41,42 were administered a bolus of 0.15, 0.2, or $0.25\mathsf{mg}/\mathsf{kg}$ abciximab, with or without subsequent infusion of abciximab at a rate of $0.125\upmu\mathrm{g/kg/min}$ for $^{12\mathrm{h}}$ . Two hundred and twenty patients (4.8%) in two studies16,17 received a $75\mathrm{-}\upmu\mathrm{g}/\upk\mathrm{g}$ or $135{\cdot}{\upmu}{\mathrm{g}}/{\upk}g$ bolus of eptifibatide followed by a 2-h infusion of $0.75\upmu\mathrm{g/kg/min}$ . Ten studies including 3042 patients $(67.0\%)$ reported administration of tirofiban and the quantity and duration of the drug exhibit a slight discrepancy. Patients in eight studies18,26–28,32,39,40,44 were given an infusion of tirofiban at a rate of 0.4 or $0.6\upmu\mathrm{g/kg/min}$ for $30\mathsf{min}$ followed by a $0.1{\cdot}{\upmu}{\mathrm{g}}/{\upk}{\mathrm{g}}/{\upm}{\mathrm{in}}$ or $0.15{-}{\upmu}{\mathrm{g}}/{\upk}{\uptheta}/{\upmin}$ infusion for 24, 48, or $72\mathsf{h}$ . Patients in one study43 received a $3{-}4\upmu\mathrm{g/kg/h}$ infusion of tirofiban until discharge. Patients in one study19 received $5{\upmu}{\upg}/{\upkg}$ of tirofiban as an intravenous bolus dose, followed by a continuous intravenous infusion of $0.1\upmu\mathrm{g}/\mathrm{kg}/\mathrm{min}$ for a duration of $24\mathsf{h}$ . Tables 1 and 2 outline the summary of general features of included studies and baseline patient characteristics. Certainty of evidence for the ten outcomes based on GRADE is shown in Table 3.

Nine RCTs reported the mean $\mathsf{mRS}$ score after 90 days of patient enrollment with a total of 3385 patients (1717 in the platelet GP IIb/IIIa antagonist group and 1668 in the control group; Figure  3C). The average mRS score for patients treated with the platelet GP IIb/IIIa antagonist was lower compared to those treated with traditional drugs (mean difference − 0.43, $95\%$ CI $-0.71$ to $-0.15$ , $p{=}0.002\$ ). Substantial heterogeneity $(p<0.00001$ $1^{2}=84\%$ ) was detected across studies. The level of evidence was high ranked by GRADE (Table 3).  



# 3.4.2  |  The NIHSS score at 7 days  

The NIHSS score at 7 days is reported in three RCTs with 540 patients (270 in the platelet GP IIb/IIIa antagonist group and 270 in the control group; Figure 3D). Baseline NIHSS scores in both groups are comparable in all three studies and $11.1\%$ patients received IVT. The NIHSS score for patients treated with the platelet GP IIb/IIIa antagonist was notably less compared to the control group (mean difference − 1.64, $95\%$ CI $-2.18$ to $-1.10$ , $p<0.00001$ ). Betweenstudy heterogeneity was insignificant $(p{=}0.13$ , $1^{2}=51\%)$ . The level of evidence was high (Table 3).  



# 3.4.3  |  Stroke progression during the first 5 days  

In six randomized controlled trials involving 1746 participants, stroke advancement was noted in the first 5 days, affecting 221 patients in total (94 in the GP IIb/IIIa antagonist group and 127 in the control group; Figure 3E). Pooled estimate revealed that the incidence of stroke progression during the first 5 days significantly declined in the GP IIb/IIIa antagonist group in comparison with the control group (OR 0.66, $95\%$ CI 0.49–0.89, $p{=}0.006,$ . Heterogeneity across studies was low $_{,p=0.94}$ , $I^{2}{=}0!$ . The level of evidence was low as optimal information sizes (OIS) were not met (Table 3). Upon visual examination, the findings indicated an absence of bias in publication.

# 3.4.5  |  BI after 90 days  

Six studies enrolling 4051 patients reported the proportion of BI 95–100 with a total of 2666 patients, with 1431 in the platelet GP IIb/IIIa antagonist group and 1235 in the control group. A pooled analysis showed a significant increase in the proportion of BI 95– 100 after 90 days (OR 1.25, $95\%$ CI 1.07–1.47, $p=0.005$ ; Figure 4A). Heterogeneity across studies was low $:p{=}0.44$ , $I^{2}{=}0;$ . The level of evidence was considered high (Table 3).  



# 3.5  |  Safety outcomes  



# 3.5.1  |  The rate of aICH  

This result was documented in 14 different studies. Within the group of platelet GP IIb/IIIa antagonists, 216 of 2280 suffered aICH, in contrast to 150 of 1871 in the regular treatment group who experienced aICH. Two hundred and sixteen patients $(11.5\%)$ in the control group and 452 patients $(19.8\%)$ in the GP IIb/IIIa antagonist group received IVT, respectively. There was no significant difference in the incidence of aICH (OR 1.20, $95\%$ CI 0.95–1.51, $p{=}0.12$ ; Figure 4B) between groups. The pooled result showed homogeneity $(p{=}0.13$ , $1^{2}=33\%$ ). Using GRADE, the evidence level was moderate given significant difference in point estimates among various studies and limited overlap in confidence intervals (Table 3). Upon visual examination, the outcome showed an absence of bias in publication.  



# 3.5.2  |  The rate of sICH  

This result was documented in 13 studies. sICH only occurred in patients in 10 studies. Of the 10 studies, five studies17,38,41–43 used the same definition, that is, symptomatic hemorrhage was diagnosed when there was evidence of neurological deterioration and imaging revealed the presence of hemorrhage in the brain with a necessary causal relationship between the two findings. Two studies32,40 defined sICH in accordance with Heidelberg Bleeding Classification.45 One study used type 2 parenchymal hematoma exceeding $30\%$ of the infarct area as the criterion of sICH, while two studies16,19 did not specify the definition of sICH. Patients receiving platelet GP IIb/IIIa antagonists showed no increase in the rate of sICH when compared to those receiving regular therapy (OR 0.91, $95\%{\mathsf{C}}{\mathsf{I}}0.29-$ 2.86, $p{=}0.88$ ; Figure 4C). The data were heterogeneous $\scriptstyle(p=0.004$ , $1^{2}=65\%)$ . Using GRADE, the evidence level of the sICH outcome was low because of significant difference in point estimates among various studies and limited overlap in confidence intervals, and as OIS was not attained (Table 3). The outcome showed an absence of bias in publication upon visual examination.

# 4  |  DISCUSSION  

In this meta-analysis, we found that platelet GP llb/llla antagonists used within 24–96 h can significantly increase the proportions of the favorable and excellent functional outcomes compared with traditional drugs in patients with AIS without endovascular therapy. Patients in the platelet GP IIb/IIIa antagonists group also had a lower risk of stroke progression during the first 5 days and a lower NIHSS score, which indicated better recent functional outcomes. There was no statistical difference in aICH, sICH, and the mortality rate between the platelet GP IIb/IIIa antagonist group and the traditional therapy group, which confirmed the safety of platelet GP IIb/IIIa antagonists.  

The mRS offers a simple and swift evaluation of disabilities within a societal framework, and has been widely applied for appraising recovery from stroke and as a primary end point in RCTs evaluating AIS treatments.46–48 The NIHSS stands as a broadly recognized and extensively applied grading framework for evaluating and measuring stroke severity.49 Our findings indicate that administration of platelet GP IIb/IIIa antagonists reduces the $\mathsf{mR}$ score after 90 days, the NIHSS score at 7 days, and increases the proportions of favorable and excellent functional outcomes. The NIHSS represents early prognostication and can be scored in a serial manner, demonstrating continuously developing process of stroke. We chose the NIHSS score at 7 days for analyzing because most of the included studies had the scores at this time point and patients were still hospitalized. Patients in the platelet GP IIb/IIIa antagonist group had a lower NIHSS score at 7 days compared with those in the control group, and subsequently, had a lower mRS score after 90 days, which supported the desirable efficacy of platelet GP llb/llla antagonists in AlS.A meta-analysis found that tirofiban markedly raised the count of patients exhibiting an mRS 0 score compared to the control group, and reduced the NIHSS score at 7 days.50 However, it failed to infer that tirofiban significantly increased the proportion of favorable functional outcome in patients with AIS.51 Contradictory findings probably resulted from the former meta-analysis comprising studies published recently which selected patients with more severe baseline symptoms and studies that had larger sample sizes.18,27,28,40  

It has always been the principle that for patients with AIS receiving IVT, antiplatelet treatment is postponed until $24\mathfrak{h}$ later.3 This principle is based on aspirin.52,53 However, the advent of platelet GP IIb/IIIa antagonists is challenging the convention. Five studies including668patients in our meta-analysisfocused on patients who were prescribed platelet GP IIb/IIIa antagonists within $24\mathsf{h}$ and received IVT simultaneously.16,17,19,26,32 Platelet GP IIb/IIIa antagonists rapidly, selectively, and reversibly prevent platelet aggregation through disrupting the fibrin binding to receptors.54 Increasing studies confirmed the safety of early application of platelet GP IIb/IIIa antagonists in AIS.55,5 6 Our meta-analysis also certified that patients in the GP IIb/IIIa antagonist group had a similar risk of aICH, sICH, and death as compared with those in the control group. Our meta-analysis included tirofiban, eptifibatide, and abciximab in the analysis and drew the conclusion that abciximab did not increase the incidence of ICH. Guidelines from 2019 American Stroke Association affirmed that abciximab should not be used in combination with alteplase considering the unacceptable increase in ICH, which is largely influenced by the results of Abciximab in Emergency Treatment of Stroke Trial (AbESTT-l).3,42 Emerging studies of administration of a small dosage of abciximab found that abciximab may not cause increase in ICH and may be safe.57–59 So, in clinical practice, abciximab should be used with more caution and tirofiban and eptifibatide can be the preferential choice.  

The types of GP IIb/IIIa antagonists and doses varied in the included studies. There are three studies on abciximab, two studies on eptifibatide, and 10 studies on tirofiban. In the 10 studies evaluating tirofiban, involving 3042 patients $67\%$ of the total population), nine studies adopted the regime of a loading dose and then a maintenance dose of 0.1 or $0.15\upmu\mathrm{g/kg/min}$ , which was consistent with the dosage utilized in the treatment of myocardial infarction.60 The remaining study used a smaller maintenance dose of tirofiban (0.050 to 0.06 $7\upmu\mathrm{g/kg/min)}$ . In the two studies evaluating eptifibatide, involving 220 patients ( $4.8\%$ of the total population), the dose of eptifibatide used was less than half that used in the management of myocardial infarction.61 In the three studies evaluating abciximab, involving 1275 patients $28.1\%$ of the total population), the maintenance dose of abciximab was the same as the dose used in myocardial infarction.62 In summary, the doses of GP IIb/IIIa antagonists used in AIS are the same or smaller than those used in myocardial infarction in consideration of the higher risk of aICH in patients with AIS.  

There are multiple limitations associated with this meta-analysis. First, the studies incorporated exhibited significant diversity of study design such as if patients were receiving IVT, different subtypes of AIS, and various dosage regimens. Also, 10 of 15 enrolled studies including $67\%$ of the total population focused on tirofiban, leaving two studies on eptifibatide and three studies on abciximab. Second, 8 of 15 included studies were registered, enrolled, and conducted in China and most of the population socioeconomically have limited access to posthospitalization care and rehabilitation, which limits the generalizability of the results.  



# 5  |  CONCLUSION  

In this review, platelet GP IIb/IIIa antagonists administered within 24–96 h, as compared with traditional antiplatelet or thrombolysis therapy, are beneficial benefit to the functional recovery of patients who receiving conservative therapy (no EVT) after attacks of ischemic stroke, but do not influence the incidence of stroke recurrence within 90 days, aICH, sICH, and death. The conclusion is mainly based on trials evaluating tirofiban, which made up $67\%$ of the total number of study participants. It should be pointed out that included studies harbored large heterogeneity across studies and high-quality RCTs involving more countries and regions are needed.
