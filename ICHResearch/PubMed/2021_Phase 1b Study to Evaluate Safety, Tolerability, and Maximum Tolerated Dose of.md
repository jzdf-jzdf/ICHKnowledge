# Phase 1b Study to Evaluate Safety, Tolerability, and Maximum Tolerated Dose of PF-05230907 for Intracerebral Hemorrhage  

Yolanda Silva Blas, MD; Michael N. Diringer, MD; Benjamin Lo, MD; Jaime Masjuan, MD; Natalia Pérez de la Ossa, MD; Matthew Cardinal, ME; Florence Yong, PhD; Tong Zhu, PhD; Gang Li, PhD; Steven Arkin , MD  

BACKGROUND AND PURPOSE: This study aimed to determine the maximum tolerated dose and to evaluate the overall safety and tolerability of single doses of PF-05230907 in subjects with acute intracerebral hemorrhage.  

METHODS: Individuals presenting with intracerebral hemorrhage were enrolled in a phase 1, multicenter, open-label clinical trial. A Bayesian modified continual reassessment method design based on treatment-emergent thromboembolic or ischemic events was adopted. Sequential dosing, an external data monitoring committee, and prespecified stopping rules were incorporated as safeguards.  

RESULTS: Twenty-one subjects received PF-05230907. The mean $(\pm\mathsf{SD})$ age in years and intracerebral hemorrhage volume in mL at baseline were 62 $(\pm9)$ and 18 $(\pm11)$ , respectively. Two treatment-emergent thromboembolic or ischemic events occurred (deep vein thrombosis and cerebral ischemia), in the $30\upmu{}\mathfrak{g}/\upk{}9$ dose group. There were no other clear drug-related toxicities at dose levels ranging from 5 to $30~{\upmu\mathrm{g}}/{\upk\mathrm{g}}$ . At the time of study termination, the maximum tolerated dose was estimated to be $24\upmu{\mathfrak{g}}/\upk{\mathfrak{g}}$ , with a mean fitted dose-toxicity estimate of $11.9\%$ $95\%$ CI, $1.2\%{-}27.4\%)$ ).  

CONCLUSIONS: Single doses of PF-05230907 appeared to be tolerated across a range of doses in the intracerebral hemorrhage population, with thrombotic events observed only at the highest dose level tested. Recruitment within the recommended therapeutic window of opportunity remains a challenge.

# METHODS

# Trial Design  

Bayesian adaptive design with modified continual reassessment method (mCRM) was employed to continuously update the dose-toxicity relationship based on the end points of thromboembolic or ischemic events (TIEs) through 7 days postdose,5 defined as any of the listed events at the severity grade indicated in Table I in the Data Supplement.  

Key inclusion criteria were aged 18 to 79 years; baseline ICH volume ${>}5\mathrm{m}L$ and ${<}60~\mathsf{mL}$ based on the ABC/2 method and able to receive study treatment within 6 hours of symptom onset or last known normal. Key exclusion criteria were Glasgow Coma Scale score of 3 to 5, modified Rankin Scale score of ${>}3$ before ICH onset, and known history of ischemic, vaso-occlusive, or thrombotic events or conditions within 6 months before screening or known prothrombotic disorders (Methods in the Data Supplement). Ethics approval was obtained at each study site, and each subject (or a legal guardian, in countries where acceptable) provided written informed consent before participation.  

A background rate for TIEs of ${\approx}5\%$ to $10\%$ was determined based on studies of eptacog alfa in ICH and on placebo-treated subject-level data sets obtained from the Virtual Interactive Stroke Trial Archive for 4 studies with similar key eligibility criteria.6–8 A target $15\%$ TIE rate was employed for the MTD determination, reflecting acceptable benefit risk in the high-morbidity, high-mortality indication of ICH.  

Subjects were enrolled in cohorts of 3, with an initial dose of $5~{\upmu\mathbb{g}}/{\upk\mathbb{g}}$ . Starting with the second cohort, subjects were assigned to a dose that was closest to, but not exceeding, the currently predicted MTD, based on a parametric dose-toxicity model. The model was updated according to the frequency of TIEs observed in each completed cohort.  

Sequential dosing of study subjects was implemented in each cohort evaluating a dose level exceeding previously administered dose levels, maximum allowed increment from the highest dose previously studied was 2 dose levels, and safety biomarker criteria (Methods in the Data Supplement) were implemented that would end skipping of dose levels with such dose escalations. Enrollment of the next cohort did not proceed until the safety review was completed for the preceding cohort (Figure 1).  

Monitoring for TIEs was through the use of ECGs, thrombosis clinical probability scores, Doppler lower extremity ultrasonography (day 4 posttreatment), cardiac troponin I, and computed tomography or magnetic resonance imaging scan at day 4. Adverse events that may have reflected thromboembolic events or manifestations of consumption coagulopathy were evaluated as potential TIEs based on prespecified thresholds for severity (Table I in the Data Supplement). Events of cardiac troponin elevation were evaluated for clinical significance based on the Third Universal Definition of Myocardial Infarction.9  

Safety and tolerability of PF-05230907 were also assessed by treatment-emergent adverse events and serious adverse events, safety laboratory tests, physical examinations, and vital signs. Subjects were followed for TIEs and adverse events through day 8, and for serious adverse events up to day 43.  

Supportive care was in alignment with published guidelines.1,10 Exploratory end points included absolute change in ICH volume from baseline to 24 hours based on computed tomography scans. National Institutes of Health Stroke Scale and modified Rankin Scale were assessed at baseline and at 24 hours, 72 hours, day 43, and day 91.  

# Statistical Methods  

Bayesian analyses were conducted to update the one-parameter hyperbolic-tangent dose-toxicity model. After TIE data from the first cohort of $5~{\upmu\g}/{\upkg}$ became available, the prior uniform distribution of an unknown model parameter was updated into a posterior distribution through Bayesian Markov Chain Monte Carlo simulations in Adaptive Design Explorer. As TIE data accumulated at the end of each cohort enrollment, the modelbased MTD estimate was used to guide the next dose assignment. Descriptive statistics were used to summarize data for each cohort and for the overall study.  

# RESULTS  

Twenty-one subjects (Table II in the Data Supplement) received a single intravenous dose of PF-05230907 ranging from 5 to $30~{\upmu\mathrm{g}}/{\upk\mathrm{g}}$ (Figure 2). Time from ICH onset to start of treatment ranged from 3 to 6 hours. One subject ( $19\upmu{\mathfrak{g}}/\upk{\mathfrak{g}}$ treatment cohort) withdrew consent during the follow-up period.  

# Safety  

Two subjects from the $30{-}\upmu{}9/\upk{}9$ treatment group experienced TIEs (cerebral ischemia and deep vein thrombosis [DVT]) through day 8. Only 1, an serious adverse event of DVT, was considered to be treatment related (Table III in the Data Supplement). During the active study reporting period, 3 deaths, all unrelated to study drug, occurred and 8 serious adverse events were reported in 6 patients (Table III in the Data Supplement). No discontinuations due to treatment-emergent adverse events were reported.

In total, 13 out of 73 treatment-emergent adverse events were reported as treatment related in 5 subjects across the 6 treatment groups through day 8 (Table IV in the Data Supplement). Ten subjects each had a maximum grade 3 severity adverse event and 1 subject had a grade 4 severity adverse event (pneumonia) reported from the various treatment groups through day 8; none of these adverse events was treatment related. With the exception of the 2 treatment-emergent TIEs, no apparent difference in the frequency of treatment-emergent adverse events across the respective dose groups was observed.  

There were no instances of excessive reduction in platelet counts or excessive reduction in fibrinogen based on prespecified criteria (Methods in the Data Supplement). One subject from the $19~{\upmu\mathrm{g}}/{\upk\mathrm{g}}$ treatment group met the safety biomarker criteria (prothrombin time $>$ baseline $+4\mathrm{~s~}$ ), which limited the increment of dose escalation in subsequent cohorts. Ten individuals had isolated instances of cardiac troponin I elevation; none were associated with clinical symptoms, ECG findings, or a rising and falling pattern that would suggest myocardial infarction. Each was adjudicated by the external data monitoring committee cardiologist as not representing a myocardial ischemic event.  

At study day 8 or discharge, 3 subjects, 1 in each of the PF-05230907 12, 24, and $30~{\upmu\mathrm{g}}/{\upk\mathrm{g}}$ treatment groups, had DVT clinical probability scores of ${}^{\ge2}$ . None of these subjects had DVT on Doppler ultrasound. No subject had a pulmonary embolism clinical probability score of ${\ge}2$ at day 8 or discharge.  

# Dose Finding  

All 21 subjects were evaluable for the TIE analysis (Figure 2). At the time of study termination, the mCRM algorithm estimated an MTD at the $24{-}\upmu{}\mathrm{g}/\upk{}9$ dose level, which had been evaluated in only 3 subjects; therefore, the MTD estimate was limited in precision. The mean fitted dose-toxicity estimate was $11.9\%$ , with a $95\%$ CI of $1.2\%$ to $27.4\%$ (Figure I in the Data Supplement).  

# Pharmacodynamics  

For all dose levels, similar values for the median maximum decrease of activated partial thromboplastin time were observed at 5 minutes postdose (Figure II in the Data Supplement).  

# Exploratory End Points  

There did not appear to be a dose-response with regard to changes from baseline in ICH volume, National Institutes of Health Stroke Scale over time, mortality, and modified Rankin Scale outcome across the various cohorts (Table V in the Data Supplement).  

# DISCUSSION  

This study provides insights into the frequency of toxicity and the coagulation pharmacological effect from PF-05230907. Results indicate that pharmacological effect was achieved over a broad range of dose levels, and the drug appeared to be tolerated at dose levels ${\le}24$ μg/kg. The clinical significance of the isolated elevations of cardiac troponin I that were observed in a substantial subset of study subjects remains to be determined. However, such elevations are often associated with concomitant events that were present in the study population, including hypertension, stroke, and critical illness.11

The mCRM approach used in this study differed from that used by Selim et al12 in their study of ICH. It included an explicitly defined toxicity end point (TIEs), a much lower threshold for target toxicity ( $15\%$ versus $40\%$ ), and a planned sample size of 12 at the MTD that, based on prestudy simulations, would ensure greater precision in the estimation of the MTD relative to more traditional study designs. Bayesian analyses continuously updated the dose-toxicity model to recommend the next dose assignment approaching the MTD, enabling a small sample size at the respective dose levels during the dose-finding process, while assigning more subjects in the vicinity of the MTD. Small increments in dose escalation and use of sequential dosing, with additional safety biomarker limitations on the magnitude of dose escalation, provided assurances against exposing individuals to excessive risk of thrombotic events while enabling assessment of potential thrombogenicity of the study drug relative to the substantial background rate of TIEs in this population. The $15\%$ TIE rate defining the MTD for PF-05230907 was justified based on phase 3 results for eptacog alfa in ICH (FAST study [Factor Seven for Acute Hemorrhagic Stroke]), which, had it demonstrated efficacy, would have been a useful addition to the therapeutic armamentarium. In that study, the $80\upmu{}\mathfrak{g}/\upk{}9$ eptacog alfa dose level, was associated with respective frequencies of $5.4\%,3.4\%,7.4\%,$ and $2.7\%$ for external data monitoring committee-adjudicated events of myocardial infarction, cerebral ischemic event, DVT, and pulmonary embolus.7 Each of those respective manifestations of thrombosis is contained within our composite TIE end point, and the collective frequency of those events exceeded our target toxicity rate of $15\%$ . Treatment effect on hematoma expansion across the various dose levels was not pursued by the sponsor as a primary end point for several reasons. Intracerebral hemorrhage is a challenging condition to treat, and to maximize chances for demonstrating hemostatic effect, it would be prudent to first determine the highest dose level with acceptable safety in this phase 1b study. Based on a 6-hour window for therapeutic intervention, the frequency of individuals who will experience substantial growth in hemorrhage is anticipated to be ${\approx}25\%,^{13}$ necessitating a large sample size to test for hemostatic effect. In contrast, under our study design, all subjects were able to contribute to the model-based determination of the MTD. Adequately characterizing safety at the respective PF-05230907 dose level(s) is also ethically warranted before subjecting large numbers of individuals to treatment in a phase 2 study with ICH growth as the primary end point.  

This study was prematurely terminated by the sponsor. In principle, a hemostatic agent should be administered in individuals with ongoing intracerebral hemorrhage to achieve a reduction in growth of ICH. Mayer et $a|^{14}$ previously proposed optimized criteria for inclusion in a hemostatic intervention study that includes treatment within 2.5 hours of ICH onset. While recruitment with a 6-hour window from the ICH onset proved feasible for this study, despite an emphasis on early treatment, the majority $(76\%)$ of the study participants started treatment more than at least 4 hours after ICH onset. Results from the recently published study of tranexamic acid in spontaneous ICH reflect a similar experience. Despite efforts to enroll patients rapidly, the majority of participants were enrolled ${>}4.5$ hours after ICH onset.15 These observations called into question the operational feasibility of any subsequent study in a patient population optimized to evaluate the efficacy of a hemostatic intervention and provided the rationale for early termination of this study. Future innovations in patient management, such as implementation of mobile stroke care units, may create conditions permissive for conduct of such studies.
