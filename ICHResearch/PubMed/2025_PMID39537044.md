# Milrinone Versus Sildenafil in Treatment of Neonatal Persistent Pulmonary Hypertension: A Randomized Control Trial  

Safaa S. Imam, MD, PHD,\* Rania A. El-Farrash, MD, PHD,\* Amr S. Taha, MBBCh,† and Ghada A. Saleh, MD, PHD\*  

Abstract: Persistent pulmonary hypertension of the newborn (PPHN) is a condition caused by failure of pulmonary vascular adaptation at birth, resulting in severe hypoxia. Several therapeutic modalities are being tried in developing countries where established therapies (inhaled nitric oxide and extracorporeal membrane oxygenation) are widely unavailable. This study aimed to assess the efficacy of milrinone versus sildenafil as available alternative therapeutics in treating PPHN. Forty neonates $(>34$ weeks) admitted to neonatal intensive care units with evidence of PPHN were randomly allocated to receive either oral sildenafil $(0.5{-}2\mathrm{mg}/\mathrm{kg}/6$ hours) or intravenous milrinone $(0.25{-}0.75\mathrm{mic/kg/min})$ . Primary outcomes included improvements in systolic pulmonary artery pressure and oxygen saturation index (OSI) at 24 and 48 hours after treatment. Secondary outcomes included the duration of hospitalization and mechanical ventilation. The ClinicalTrials identifier is NCT04391478. Both groups showed significant improvement in the post-treatment hemodynamic variables compared with pretreatment levels $(P<0.05$ for all parameters). Systolic pulmonary artery pressure and OSI values significantly improved in both study groups compared with baseline $(P<0.001\$ ). The 24-hour and 48-hour posttreatment OSI values were much lower in the milrinone group than those in the sildenafil group $(P<0.05\$ ). The length of hospital stay was significantly shorter in the milrinone group than that in the sildenafil group $(P<0.05\$ . There were no significant differences in the duration of mechanical ventilation, incidence of intracranial hemorrhage and pulmonary hemorrhage, or mortality between the 2 groups $(P>0.05$ ). In conclusion, milrinone and sildenafil are effective and well-tolerated in neonates with PPHN, particularly when inhaled nitric oxide and extracorporeal membrane oxygenation are not available. Milrinone is superior to sildenafil in improving oxygenation without lowering blood pressure parameters.

# INTRODUCTION  

Persistent pulmonary hypertension of the newborn (PPHN) is a condition caused by failure of pulmonary vascular adaptation at or soon after birth, resulting in early and often severe hypoxemia.1 Its incidence varies from 0.4 to 6.8 per 1000 live births with a reported mortality ranging from $4\%$ to $33\%$ .2  

At birth, pulmonary endothelial nitric oxide, cyclic guanosine monophosphate, and vascular smooth muscle cyclic adenosine monophosphate are among several vasoactive products that play a primary role in the neonatal cardiopulmonary transition at birth by causing pulmonary vasodilatation.1,3 However, their effect abolished once degraded by phosphodiesterases.4  

Multiple therapeutic agents are being tried for the treatment of neonatal PPHN. Although inhaled nitric oxide (iNO) is the preferred first-line therapy, its availability is limited in developing countries either because of its high cost or because of logistic requirements.5 The high concentrations of phosphodiesterases (PDE) in the pulmonary vasculature led to the trial of phosphodiesterase inhibitors, such as sildenafil or milrinone.6,7  

Milrinone is a selective PDE3 inhibitor that increases cyclic adenosine monophosphate levels in arterial smooth muscle cells and cardiac myocytes, leading to pulmonary vasodilation, enhanced myocardial contractility, and improved myocardial relaxation.8,9 Milrinone is used in infants with PPHN failing to respond to iNO which results in better oxygenation and improvement of right and left ventricular dysfunction.9,10  

Sildenafil is a selective PDE5 inhibitor that selectively reduces pulmonary vascular resistance by decreasing cyclic guanosine monophosphate degradation.11,12 Sildenafil serves as an alternative or adjunctive pulmonary vasodilator in neonates.7  

This study was conducted to assess the efficacy of milrinone versus sildenafil, as available alternative therapeutics in treating PPHN in a low-resource setting.  

# Patients and Methods  

This randomized controlled study included 40 neonates with a gestational age ${>}34$ weeks, whose color doppler echocardiography showed evidence of PPHN and were admitted to neonatal intensive care units at Ain Shams University Hospital in Cairo, Egypt. Patients with PPHN secondary to structural congenital heart disease or congenital diaphragmatic hernia and those requiring any early major surgical interventions were excluded.  

Mothers of the eligible newborns were informed about the purpose of the study and provided with a written consent to participate. The study protocol was approved by the Ethical Committee of Ain Shams University Hospital. The trial is registered at ClinicalTrials.gov; identifier: NCT04391478. Reporting of the study conforms to the CONSORT 2010 statement13 with a checklist (see Table 1, Supplemental Digtal Content, http://links.lww.com/JCVP/A856).  

# Randomization and Blinding  

During the study period, 63 patients were assessed for eligibility. Twenty-three were excluded: 6 neonates had ductdependent congenital heart disease, 5 with other major congenital anomalies, 6 died before treatment was started, and 6 mothers declined to participate. Forty patients were randomized (ratio 1:1) to either receive enteral sildenafil or intravenous (IV) milrinone as the primary pulmonary vasodilator agent for treatment of PPHN (Fig. 1).  

Randomization was by simple allocation of presealed numbers. IV sildenafil is not available in Egypt, the pharmacy prepared the oral formulations in the same containers as for IV milrinone, and the medication had a sealed code for identification.  

Parents of the studied neonates, data collectors, outcome adjudicators, and echocardiologists were blinded. The study nurses, pharmacist, and neonatologist were aware of the nature of the intervention but played no role in the conduct of the trial. The management plan, apart from the study intervention, was entirely determined by the treating clinicians.  

# Initial Assessment and Diagnosis of PPHN  

Full perinatal history and detailed neonatal examination were fulfilled. All included neonates received standard care including continuous monitoring of oxygenation, blood pressure, and perfusion; maintaining normal body temperature; correction of electrolytes/glucose abnormalities and metabolic acidosis; minimal stimulation/handling of the newborn; minimal use of invasive procedures (eg, suctioning); and nutritional support as per local protocols. Routine laboratory investigations were performed for all neonates as indicated. Plain chest and heart X-rays, posteroanterior view were performed on admission and repeated as needed.

# Echocardiographic Assessment  

On clinical suspicion of PPHN by the attending neonatologists, echocardiographic measurements were performed according to the recommendations of The American Society of Echocardiography14 by the same observer (only during the day shift), using a Vivid 3 Pro ultrasound scanner (GE Medical Systems, Milwaukee, WI) with an ${8{-}10~\mathrm{MHz}}$ (10S) probe. The scan was repeated 24 hours after the start of treatment and as indicated.  

# OSI  

The oxygen saturation index (OSI) is a modality for estimating the severity of hypoxia using pulse oximetry as an alternative to the oxygenation index (OI).15 We calculated OSI before treatment and at 24 and 48 hours after treatment using the following formula: OSI $=$ airway pressure multiplied by the fraction of inspired oxygen multiplied by 100 divided by peripheral capillary oxygen saturation obtained using pulse oximetry.16,17  

# Study Drugs’ Administration  

The enrolled neonates were randomly assigned to receive either sildenafil or milrinone. Neonates in the sildenafil group received enteral sildenafil at a dose of $0.5-$ $2~\mathrm{mg/kg/6}$ hours by the nasogastric tube. The sildenafil solution was prepared by crushing a $50~\mathrm{mg}$ tablet in $25~\mathrm{mL}$ of distilled water to make a concentration of $2~\mathrm{mg/mL}$ (Viagra, $50~\mathrm{mg}$ sildenafil citrate/tablet, Pfizer, Egypt).18,19 Neonates in the milrinone group received IV milrinone infusion at a dose of $0.25{-}0.75\mathrm{mic/kg/min}$ (Primacor, $1~\mathrm{mg}$ milrinone lactate/ mL saline, Sanofi, Egypt).10,20  

Our NICU protocol for pulmonary vasodilators dosing titration is to start with the minimum required dose, and then, the treating neonatologist titrates them further according to the clinical response ie, if the OSI did not improve provided that the mean blood pressure remains stable after the previous dose. Both groups were monitored for possible direct side effects eg, hypotension, bleeding, and/or skin rash.

# Outcome Measures  

Primary outcomes were systolic pulmonary artery pressure (SPAP) and OSI improvement 24 hours posttreatment. Secondary outcomes were the duration of hospitalization and duration of mechanical ventilation. Patients were followed till discharge or death.  

# Sample Size Justification  

The sample size was calculated using EpiInfo $\textsuperscript{\textregistered}$ version 6.0, setting the type-1 error $(\alpha)$ at 0.05 and the power $(1-\beta)$ at 0.80. A sample size of at least 17 neonates in each group was sufficient to detect a significant decrease in pulmonary pressure.21 Assuming a loss of $15\%$ , the sample size used was 20 neonates in each group.  

# Statistical Method  

Data were analyzed using the Statistical Package for Social Science $\mathrm{IBM@}$ $\mathrm{SPSS@C}$ version 22 (IBM $\copyright$ Corp, Armonk, NY, USA). The normality of numerical data distribution was examined using the D’Agostino–Pearson test. Normally distributed numerical variables were presented as mean $\pm$ SD, and intergroup differences were compared using the unpaired Student $t$ test. Within-group (paired) differences were compared using the paired Student $t$ test. Repeated measures Analysis of Variance (ANOVA) was used to compare variables with more than 2 paired measures in the 2 groups.  

Skewed numerical variables were presented as median (interquartile range), and between-group differences were compared nonparametrically using the Mann–Whitney test. Categorical variables were presented as frequency, and percentage and differences were compared using the $\chi^{2}$ test or Fischer’s exact test when frequencies were below 5. A $P$ value of $<0.05$ was considered the cutoff value for significance in all analyses.  

# RESULTS  

During the course of this study, 4 neonates died in the milrinone group: 1 had early-onset sepsis, 2 died secondary to disseminated intravascular coagulation and pulmonary hemorrhage, and 1 died due to intracranial hemorrhage (ICH). In the sildenafil group, a total of 6 neonates died: 1 due to ICH, 3 secondary to septicemia, and 2 due to disseminated intravascular coagulation and pulmonary hemorrhage (Fig. 1).  

Baseline maternal, clinical, and hemodynamic characteristics of the studied groups showed no significant differences between the groups (Tables 1 and 2).  

Table 2 shows significant improvement in the posttreatment SPAP compared with the pretreatment levels in both groups $(P<0.001$ for both). The effect size for both treatments was between medium and large, with a larger size for milrinone than that for sildenafil $(\upeta^{2^{\circ}}=0.713^{\circ}$ ).  

The heart rate significantly increased post-treatment in the milrinone group as well as in the sildenafil group $(P=$ 0.004 and $P=0.04$ , respectively). Systolic blood pressure (SBP) and diastolic blood pressure (DBP) showed significant rise after treatment in the milrinone group $\it{\Omega}^{\prime}P=0.004$ and $P<$ 0.001, respectively), whereas SBP and DBP significantly decreased in the sildenafil group $(P=0.004\$ and $P=0.030$ , respectively). All measures before and after therapy were still within normal levels.  

OSI calculations dropped significantly in both study groups compared with baseline $(P<0.001$ with the effect size for both therapies ranged between small and medium; $\mathtt{\Delta\eta}^{2}=0.475$ ) (Fig. 2).  

All patients were mechanically ventilated with synchronized intermittent mandatory ventilation mode. Table 3 shows that the post-treatment maximum peak inspiratory pressure and positive end expiratory pressure were significantly lower in the milrinone group compared with the sildenafil group $(P<0.001\$ and 0.005, respectively) with no significant difference in the duration of mechanical ventilation between both groups $(P=0.134)$ ).

The length of hospital stay was significantly shorter in the milrinone group compared with the sildenafil group $(P=$ 0.029) (Fig. 3). There were no significant differences in the incidence of ICH, pulmonary hemorrhage, or mortality between the groups (data not shown).  

# DISCUSSION  

The current prospective randomized control trial was conducted to assess the efficacy of milrinone versus sildenafil —as alternative therapies—in treating PPHN in a setting where iNO and extracorporeal membrane oxygenation are not available.  

In this study, both IV milrinone and oral sildenafil improved oxygenation and decreased pulmonary pressure significantly. In the view of their effect on cardiovascular and hemodynamic parameters, we observed that the 24-hour posttreatment SBP and DBP were both significantly higher in the milrinone group and significantly lower in the sildenafil group than those at the baseline. This finding suggests a possible added value of milrinone in the management of PPHN without compromising the systemic blood pressure.  

Both sildenafil and milrinone are among several therapeutics that have been tried in the treatment of PPHN. Sayed and Bisheer22 reported improvement in 21/27 infants with PPHN treated and had sildenafil, and had a $6.3\%$ decrease in OI noted from the first dose. Furthermore, in 2017, Kelly et al7 concluded in a Cochrane review that sildenafil improves oxygenation and decreases the mortality of PPHN in settings where standard therapy is not available. Their work was followed by a cost utility study by Evers et ${\sfal}^{5}$ who found sildenafil to be a cost-effective intervention in severe PPHN in high-income but not low-income and middle-income countries (LMICs). In 2006, a pilot randomized blinded study by Baquero et $\mathrm{al^{19}}$ on 13 patients with severe PPHN, all being critically ill and on $100\%$ oxygen, proved the feasibility of using oral sildenafil, as $6/7$ babies in the sildenafil group survived versus only $1/6$ babies in the placebo group. Moreover, Chaudhari et ${\mathrm{al}}^{23}$ presented an interesting case of a full-term neonate with severe PPHN secondary to impaired alveolarization and plexiform pulmonary arteriopathy, who only improved on adding oral sildenafil to iNO and milrinone. She survived the severe pulmonary hypertensive crisis, and full recovery was achieved on long-term therapy with sildenafil.  

Regarding the effect of milrinone on oxygenation and hemodynamics, Jain et $\mathrm{al}^{24}$ used milrinone in neonates during postcardiac surgery. Their results indicated that neonates in the milrinone group had better oxygenation, less inotropes, and lower ventilation failure. Milrinone has the advantage of exhibiting inotropic and lusitropic effects in addition to its pulmonary dilatory effects.25 Furthermore, McNamar et al,9 in a retrospective case series, reported a rapid improvement in oxygenation of 9 neonates with severe PPHN who received milrinone as an add-on therapy after having poor initial response to iNO. These babies had a trend toward improved blood pressure, although it was not statistically significant. Moreover, Lakshminrusimha and Steinhorn26 concluded that milrinone might be the pulmonary vasodilator of choice in the presence of PPHN with left ventricular dysfunction. In another small retrospective case series of preterm neonates with pulmonary hypertension and right ventricular dysfunction, James et $\mathrm{al}^{27}$ reported that the use of milrinone was associated with a decrease in the OI and iNO dose, suggesting that the usefulness of milrinone in PPHN might be more prominent in babies with cardiac dysfunction by maintaining the systemic blood pressure.

In a recent study comparing sildenafil and/or milrinone in the treatment of PPHN, El Ghandour et $\mathrm{al}^{21}$ found that the effect of sildenafil on improving SPAP was not statistically significant on day 1–2 but was significant in the latter course of the therapy. In their study, milrinone showed an initial decrease in the OI but was not effectively sustained in the latter course of the study. Furthermore, their pairwise comparison with the dual therapy group showed a significant improvement of SPAP in both the early and later phases of the therapy. The investigators attributed this finding to the slow and consistent effect of sildenafil compared with the rapid initial response of milrinone but with the development of tolerance later.  

Regarding the secondary outcomes in the present analysis, the sildenafil group had a significantly higher maximum peak inspiratory pressure and positive end expiratory pressure during the first 24 hours of therapy than the milrinone group. The duration of hospital stay was significantly shorter in the milrinone group than that in the sildenafil group. All these findings might suggest the superiority of milrinone over sildenafil as an available alternative treatment for neonates with PPHN in LMIC.  

In the current study, mortality was collectively $25\%$ . There were no significant differences in mortality or incidence of complications among both groups. Mortality from PPHN remains high in LMIC compared with developed countries. In a study enrolling 385 neonates with PPHN from 12 tertiary units in the United States before the era of iNO, Walsh et $\mathrm{al}^{28}$ reported mortality was almost the same $(11\%)$ between units. Currently, data show that mortality ranges from $4\%$ to $33\%$ in developed countries versus $25\%-48\%$ in developing countries.29 Although the FDA previously issued a warning that the chronic use of higher doses of sildenafil might be associated with mortality and hypotension,30 this has not been proven in infants yet.31 In a review of 49 studies, including 140 infants of 625 children, SamieeZafarghandy et $\mathrm{a}\bar{1}^{31}$ assessed the safety and dosing of sildenafil in children with PPHN. They concluded that sildenafil is a valuable and well-tolerated therapeutic option.  

The limitation of the current analysis is that the unavailability of the IV form of sildenafil made it impossible to completely blind the studied medication. Instead, blinding included parents of the studied neonates, data collectors, outcome adjudicators, and the echocardiologist. Generalization of the results to other low-resource settings might be compromised if one of the administration routes is not possible or contraindicated (eg, in babies kept NPO).  

We focused our analysis on milrinone and sildenafil, two therapies that are widely accepted and available in LMIC. To the best of our knowledge, this is the first analysis to assess the use of milrinone versus sildenafil and subsequent secondary outcomes in neonates with PPHN. Our analysis is exploratory in nature and is designed to generate hypotheses to be tested in future trials.  

In conclusion, both milrinone and oral sildenafil are effective and well-tolerated in neonates with PPHN, particularly when iNO and extracorporeal membrane oxygenation are not available. Milrinone seems to be superior in improving oxygenation and reducing pulmonary vascular resistance while maintaining blood pressure levels. Further randomized trials are warranted to evaluate the effect of combining medications to improve the outcome measures including intermediate and longer-term measures of pulmonary and neurodevelopmental morbidity.
