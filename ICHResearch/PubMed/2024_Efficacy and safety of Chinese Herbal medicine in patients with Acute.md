Intracerebral hemorrhage (ICH) is the most serious and least treatable form of stroke and a major cause of global disease burden [1]. ICH accounts for approximately $10\%$ of strokes in high-income countries, but this figure is much higher in low- and middle-income countries due to the high prevalence of hypertension and other risk factors. Overall, ICH accounts for nearly half of all stroke deaths, worldwide [1]. Most interventions being investigated for the treatment of acute ICH have targeted the reduction of hematoma expansion and secondary brain injury, including through early intensive blood pressure (BP) control [2–4], surgical removal of the hematoma [5, 6] (DF Hanley et al.; Mendelow AD et al., 2013), hemostatic therapy [7], use of hypothermia and neuroprotective agents [8, 9]. The results have been variable regarding any improvement in the conventional primary outcome of functional recovery according to the standard patient-centered measure of scores on the modified Rankin scale (mRS).

FYTF-919 comprises four Chinese herbs – Renshen (Panax ginseng), Dahuang (Radix et Rhizoma Rhei), Sanqi (Radix Notoginseng), and Chuanxiong (Rhizoma Ligustici Chuanxiong) – all of which have potential therapeutic actions on the pathophysiology of ICH [10, 15–17]. Several small, single-center, clinical trials have shown FYTF-919 to be safe and potentially efficacious in ICH [18, 19], and there is an indication of improved functional outcome when these studies are combined in a meta-analysis [17]. However, given the limitations of study design and heterogeneous methodologies, this evidence is insufficient to provide firm recommendations on the use of FYTF-919 in ICH in China, potentially more broadly. We designed the Chinese herbal medicine in patients with Acute INtracerebral hemorrhage (CHAIN) study to determine the efficacy and safety of TCM in patients with moderate-severe ICH and a greater likelihood of cerebral edema and neurological and systemic inflammation.  

Stroke has been a recognized disease in the Chinese medical system for thousands of years, and the use of traditional Chinese medicine (TCM) remains an important component of contemporary treatment for these patients in China. Pharmacological studies suggest the popular herbal TCM FYTF-919 (Zhong Feng Xing Nao [ZFXN] prescription) can reduce intracranial pressure  

# Methods  

# Design  

CHAIN is a multicenter, prospective, randomized, doubleblind placebo-controlled trial that aims to determine the efficacy and safety of FYTF-919 in patients with acute ICH at 20–30 hospitals in China. A total of 1,504 subjects will be randomly allocated in a 1:1 ratio to receive either oral FYTF-919 or matching placebo over 28 days. While the primary outcome will be assessed at the conventional 90-day time point postrandomization, all subjects will be requested to undergo a further follow-up at 180-day time point to assess the sustainability of any early treatment effect and degree of recovery at 6 months (Fig. 1). Data will be collected at seven time points from the onset of ICH until the end of follow-up, and all these data will be used for the assessment of outcomes or safety (Table 1). The study is conducted in compliance with local regulatory and ethical requirements, with approvals being obtained from the Ethics Committee at each participating hospital prior any trial activities being undertaken.  

Patient Population Inclusion Criteria Inclusion criteria include the following:   
1) Age ${\ge}18$ years;   
2) Diagnosis of spontaneous ICH, confirmed by brain imaging;   
3) Presentation within $48\mathrm{~h~}$ of symptom onset (or last seen well);   
4) Meeting severity criteria of either (a) National Institute of Health Stroke Scale (NIHSS) ${\ge}8$ or (b) Glasgow coma scale (GCS) ${\leq}14$ ;   
5) Providing written informed consent by patient (or approved surrogate).

Exclusion Criteria The following are the exclusion criteria:  

1) ICH secondary to a structural abnormality in the brain (e.g., cerebrovascular malformation, arterial aneurysm, tumor, moyamoya disease, trauma, or previous ischemic stroke), or secondary to cerebral amyloid angiopathy, or secondary to reperfusion treatment for ischemic stroke, or secondary to anticoagulant treatment, or secondary to antiplatelet treatment;   
2) Unlikely to potentially benefit from therapy (e.g., advanced dementia) or judged by responsible treating clinician to have a high likelihood of early death irrespective of treatment;   
3) Other medical illness that will interfere with outcome assessments and follow-up (e.g., known significant pre-stroke disability [mRS scores 3–5], advanced cancer and renal failure);   
4) Known definite contraindication to the Chinese herbal medicine;  

5) Women who are known to be pregnant or lactating; 6) Currently participating in another trial which would interfere with outcome assessments.  

# Randomization  

After patient eligibility is confirmed, randomization is via a central internet-based system according to a block grouping method stratified by site, neurological severity (NIHSS scores ${<}15$ vs. ${\ge}15\mathrm{.}$ ), and hematoma location (basal ganglia $^+$ lobe vs. thalamus $^+$ cerebellum $^+$ brain stem $^+$ ventricle) to ensure these key prognostic factors are balanced between groups.

# FYTF-919 Group  

The FYTF-919 (ZFXN prescription) is in accordance with Chinese good manufacture practice of medical product standards (GMP). As a type of plant compound, FYTF-919 is composed mainly of Renshen (P. ginseng), Dahuang (Radix et Rhizoma Rhei), Sanqi (Radix Notoginseng), and Chuanxiong (Rhizoma Ligustici Chuanxiong), with sorbic acid and polysorbate 80 as auxiliary materials. The quality control of FYTF-919 was applied by ultrahigh performance liquid chromatography coupled with a Q Exactive hybrid quadrupole-Orbitrap mass spectrometry system. A total of 30 chemical profiles were detected in different batches of FYTF-919 (online suppl. Table 1; for all online suppl. material, see https://doi.org/10.1159/000534761), and contents of representative compounds as well as chromatographic fingerprint of FYTF919 in each batch have demonstrated good stability (online suppl. Fig. 1; online suppl. Table 2). It is prepared as a brown-yellow liquid specifically in a $100~\mathrm{mL}$ per bottle, and the method and frequency of delivery are determined by the level of consciousness and swallowing ability of patients. Patients, who are awake and have a normal swallow, take warm FYTF-919 orally, at a dose of $33\mathrm{mL}$ from $30\mathrm{min}$ after a meal. For patients with swallowing dysfunction, insufficient oral intake, unconsciousness, or critical condition, a dose of $25~\mathrm{mL}$ every $^\textrm{\scriptsize6h}$ is usually given via a nasogastric tube. For this study, patients are to take FYTF-919 immediately after randomization and until 28 days.  

Control Group (Placebo Treatment)  

The placebo is a plant-flavored solution, composed mainly of soybean peptide and black sugar syrup combined with an edible essence and maltodextrin, salt, and monosodium glutamate as auxiliary materials, and it has no drug activity. According to the safety guarantee from the manufacturer, manual consistency testing shows the placebo has good consistency in appearance, smell, taste, and other aspects, with FYTF-919. It will be administered in a similar manner: oral liquid placebo $33~\mathrm{mL}$ t d.s for 28 days or as a dose of $25~\mathrm{mL}$ via a nasogastric tube 6-hourly over 28 days, according to conscious level and swallowing ability.  

# Routine Treatment  

All participants enrolled in this study will receive FYTF-919 treatment or placebo treatment. Furthermore, standard ICH treatment was also allowed as a basic treatment in accordance with Chinese guidelines for diagnosis and treatment of ICH [20]. It includes general treatments such as continuous vital sign monitoring, nervous system evaluation, oxygen inhalation and respiratory support, BP regulation in patients with elevated BP, blood glucose monitoring and management, dehydration treatment in patients with elevated intracranial pressure, application of neuroprotective drugs, and surgical hematoma evacuation. We list the recommendations in the above guidelines for the management of BP, glucose, intracranial hypertension, neuroprotective drugs, and surgical evacuation of hematoma in online supplementary Table 3. Notably, the investigational drug in this study was Chinese medicine, so patients were required not to take other Chinese medicine or Chinese patent medicine throughout the study. # Outcomes  

Primary outcome. It includes utility-weighted mRS (UW-mRS) at 90 days [18, 19]. Secondary outcomes. These will include the UW-mRS at 180 days: the following at 28, 90, and 180 days – an ordinal analysis of the mRS; death or disability, defined as mRS scores $4{-}6;$ separately on death and disability (mRS 4–5); health-related quality of life on the EuroQol EQ-5D-5L measure and basic activities of daily living according to the Barthel index; NIHSS at $24\mathrm{~h~}$ and 7 days; hematoma volume and cerebral edema volume at $24\mathrm{~h},$ , 7 days, and 14 days (or hospital discharge, if sooner); and clinical pulmonary infection score on days 0, 3, and 7 after stroke-associated pneumonia [21].  

# Safety  

All SAEs and presumed FYTF-919-related adverse events, including diarrhea and an abnormal laboratory test, were the safety measures in all randomized patients during follow-up. Abnormal laboratory tests mainly included abnormal liver function, renal function, coagulation function, and some significant routine tests (Table 2).  

# Data and Safety Monitoring Board  

An independent expert Data and Safety Monitoring Board (DSMB) is responsible for reviewing the safety and ethics for the study. The DSMB is governed by a charter outlining the responsibilities, procedures, and confidentiality for members. The main responsibilities include monitoring the accumulating data for early benefits or potentially harmful effects and providing reports to the steering committee on recommendations to continue or temporarily halt recruitment to the study. The DSMB reviews unblinded data on recruitment, adherence to the protocol, primary and secondary outcomes, and SAEs, at regular intervals, generally 6-monthly.  

# Sample Size  

The sample of 1,504 patients is estimated to provide $90\%$ power (α 0.05) to detect a difference in average scores for better outcome between the active and placebo groups of ${\ge}20\%$ (i.e., 0.59 vs. 0.65; $\mathrm{SD}=0.32;$ ), assuming equal group participations, $6\%$ non-adherence rate, and $10\%$ lost to follow-up. The calculation was based on data from the second Intensive Blood Pressure Reduction in Acute Cerebral Hemorrhage Trial (INTERACT2) where the mean UW-mRS was estimated as 0.59 in control “less intensive BP control” group [2].  

# Statistical Analysis  

Data will be analyzed both according to intention-to-treat principle and in the per-protocol set. The primary endpoint of UW-mRS will be calculated from mRS and EQ-5D scores using the algorithm illustrated in our previous publication [18]. For the primary endpoint, a generalized linear model will be performed to analyze whether FYTF-919 is superior to placebo and similarly for continuous outcomes. Heterogeneity of the treatment effect on the primary endpoint will be assessed in pre-defined subgroups defined as age $(<65$ vs. ${\ttz}65\$ ), time to randomization ( $_{<12}$ vs. ${\geq}12\mathrm{~h~}$ ), degree of neurological impairment (NIHSS ${<}15$ vs. ${\ge}15$ ), baseline hematoma volume $_{<15}$ vs. ${\geq}15\mathrm{mL}$ ), and hematoma location (basal ganglia $^+$ cerebral lobe vs. thalamus $^+$ cerebellum $^+$ brainstem $^+$ ventricle). Shift of 7 levels of mRS will be analyzed by ordinal logistic regression with tests as to whether the proportional odds assumption is violated. Categorical outcomes will be analyzed in logistic regression model. All analyses will be a priori specified in a full statistical analysis plan.  

# Progress to Date and Timeline  

This study is registered at ClinicalTrials.gov (NCT05066620). Patient recruitment began on November 22, 2021. As of June 30, 2023, 1,197 patients have been randomized at 27 hospital sites. The DSMB had held its third meeting on April 19, 2023. We estimate that recruitment will be completed by December 2023 and follow-up completed by June 2024.  

# Discussion  

We describe the protocol for an investigator-initiated and conducted multicenter, prospective, randomized, doubleblind placebo-controlled clinical trial to examine the effectiveness and safety of the TCM herb, FYTF-919, in patients with acute moderate-to-severe ICH. A previous study of FYTF-919 has shown that it is safe for use within the broad time of $72\mathrm{h}$ after the onset of symptoms without any increase in the risk of rebleeding [22]. Pharmacological studies suggest FYTF-919 attenuates neurologic damage through antiinflammatory, anti-oxidative, anti-excitotoxic, and neuroprotective mechanisms [23]. These effects appear largely driven by the components of $P.$ ginseng and Radix Notoginseng [24], with the major effective components of Chuanxiong being ligusticolide and ligustilide, inhibiting the toll-like receptor 4 and nuclear factor-kappa signaling pathways around the hematoma that appear key components of the inflammatory cascade [12]. Chrysophanol, an effective component of rhubarb, can regulate the overexpression of glial fibrillary acidic protein, which can protect the integrity of blood-brain barrier, prevent an increase in inflammation, and create a microenvironment for nerve regeneration [15].  

Most clinical trials of FYTF-919 have had design limitations. The CHAIN study is the first large-scale, prospective, randomized, double-blind clinical trial of TCM with a patient-centered outcome in ICH. We hope our efforts will encourage others to investigate the use of TCM in clinical trials that have a similarly rigorous design. We have followed the Standard Protocol Items: Recommendations for Interventional Trials (SPIRIT) guidelines, followed ICH-GCP principles, and used an experienced operational team to ensure high quality over implementation of the protocol. The placebo is manufactured to GMP standard, which includes monitoring with microbiological testing and evaluations for consistency in appearance, smell, taste, and other aspects to FYTF-919, before commencement of the study. These features will ensure that the results are reliable and guide their interpretation in recommendations in the use of this TCM in clinical practice.  

In summary, the CHAIN study is on target to provide reliable assessment of an ancient therapy applied to contemporary clinical practice in China. It may also lead to a wider evaluation of the use of this strategy in other regions of the world, where there are still limited proven therapies for ICH, which contrasts greatly with what is available for acute ischemic stroke.
