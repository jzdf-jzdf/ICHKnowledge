# Edoxaban versus warfarin in patients with atrial fibrillation in relation to the risk of stroke: A secondary analysis of the ENGAGE AF-TIMI 48 study  

Joris R. de Groot, MD, PhD a, Christian T. Ruff, MD, MPHb, Sabina A. Murphy, $\mathbf{MPH}^{\mathbf{b}}$ , Rose A. Hamershock, MAb, Jim T. Vehmeijer, MD, PhDa, Anton J.M. Oude Ophuis, MD, $\mathbf{PhD^{c}}$ , Laura Grip, MScb, Hans Lanz, ${\bfMD^{d}}$ ， Michele F. Mercuri, MD, PhD e, Elliott M. Antman, $\mathbf{MD}^{\mathrm{b}}$ , and Robert P. Giugliano, MD, $\mathbf{s}\mathbf{M}^{\mathbf{b}}$ Amsterdam, The Netherlands; Boston MA; Nijmegen, The Netherlands; Munich, Germany; Edison, NJ  

Introduction The efficacy and safety of the oral factor Xa inhibitor edoxaban compared to warfarin stratified by ${\mathsf{CHA}}_{2}{\mathsf{DS}}_{2}\forall{\mathsf{ASc}}$ scores have not been described.  

Methods The ENGAGE AF-TIMI 48 trial randomized patients with atrial fibrillation to once-daily edoxaban or warfarin. We classified patients based on $\mathsf{CHA}_{2}\mathsf{DS}_{2}\mathsf{VASc}$ score and compared pharmacokinetics (edoxaban concentration), pharmacodynamics (anti-factor Xa [FXa] with edoxaban, time-in-therapeutic range for warfarin), efficacy (stroke or systemic embolism [SSE]), safety (major bleeding [MB], intracranial hemorrhage), and cardiovascular mortality, for the approved edoxaban regimen vs warfarin.  

Results The distribution ${\mathsf{CHA}}_{2}{\mathsf{DS}}_{2}\forall{\mathsf{ASc}}$ score were: ${\le}3$ , $N{=}4159$ $129.6\%)$ ; 4, $N{=}4066$ $(28.9\%)$ ; 5, $N{=}3165$ $(22.5\%);$ and ${\ge}6$ , $N=2681$ $(19.1\%)$ . Increasing rates of SSE (1.05 to $2.99\%$ /year) and MB (2.27 to $4.66\%$ /year) were observed in the warfarin arm as the ${\mathsf{CHA}}_{2}{\mathsf{DS}}_{2}\forall{\mathsf{ASc}}$ score increased. The hazard ratios per unit increase of $\mathsf{CHA}_{2}\mathsf{DS}_{2}\mathsf{VASc}$ score were 1.29 (1.21-1.38) and 1.26 (1.17-1.36) for SSE, and 1.20 (1.13-1.27) and 1.19 (1.12-1.27) for MB, with warfarin and edoxaban, respectively. Time-in-therapeutic range in warfarin-treated patients was similar and high (median $68\%.69\%)$ across ${\mathsf{CHA}}_{2}{\mathsf{DS}}_{2}\forall{\mathsf{ASc}}$ scores, whereas edoxaban trough concentration, exogenous anti-FXa activity and %inhibition of endogenous FXa were higher at increasing ${\mathsf{CHA}}_{2}{\mathsf{DS}}_{2}\forall{\mathsf{ASc}}$ scores. Edoxaban reduced SSE, MB, intracranial hemorrhage, and cardiovascular mortality vs warfarin to a similar degree across the range of ${\mathsf{CHA}}_{2}{\mathsf{DS}}_{2}\forall{\mathsf{ASc}}$ scores $(P\mathrm{-}\mathrm{i}\mathsf{n}\mathsf{t}=0.90$ , 0.96, 0.21, and $0.37$ , respectively). Because of higher event rates the number of events prevented with edoxaban tended to be greater in patients with higher ${\mathsf{CHA}}_{2}{\mathsf{DS}}_{2}\forall{\mathsf{ASc}}$ scores.  

Conclusion The benefit and safety of edoxaban versus warfarin is maintained across ${\mathsf{CHA}}_{2}{\mathsf{DS}}_{2}\forall{\mathsf{ASc}}$ scores. While the relative risk reductions remain similar, edoxaban provides incrementally larger absolute reductions in outcomes over warfarin in patients with higher ${\mathsf{CHA}}_{2}{\mathsf{DS}}_{2}\forall{\mathsf{ASc}}$ scores. (Am Heart J 2021;235:132–139.)  

In patients with atrial fibrillation (AF), non-Vitamin K antagonist oral anticoagulants (NOACs) significantly reduced stroke or systemic embolism (SSE) intracranial hemorrhage (ICH), and mortality compared to warfarin. 1  

However, patient risk varied widely within and across trials. 1 An analysis from a US health care network in $>120{,}000$ AF patients concluded that warfarin provided a higher intensity of anticoagulation resulting in fewer ischemic strokes than NOACs, a particular concern for patients with higher $\mathrm{CHA}_{2}\mathrm{DS}_{2}$ -VASc scores.? Previous analyses from the large randomized trials exploring the relative efficacy and safety of NOACs vs warfarin across risk strata showed divergent results.3.4 The statistical analysis plan of the Effective Anticoagulation with Factor Xa Next Generation in Atrial Fibrillation-Thrombolysis in Myocardial Infarction (ENGAGE AF-TIMI 48) trial prespecified an ancillary analysis according to $\mathrm{CHADS}_{2}$ score, as at the time the trial was designed the $\mathrm{CHA}_{2}\mathrm{DS}_{2}$ -VASc score was not yet commonly used for stroke risk estimation.

# Methods  

ENGAGE AF-TIMI 48 was a phase 3, multinational, double-blind, double-dummy, randomized trial, comparing two once-daily edoxaban regimens $\mathrm{60mg}$ , 30 mg) with warfarin in 21,105 patients age ${\geq}21$ years with AF and $\mathrm{CHADS}_{2}$ scores ${\geq}2$ over 2.8 years median follow-up.7-8 The edoxaban dose was halved (60 to $30~\mathrm{mg}$ , 30 to $15~\mathrm{mg})$ in patients with weight $\le60$ kg, or creatinine clearance $\leq50\mathrm{mL}/\mathrm{min}$ , or concomitant use of strong P-glycoprotein inhibitors. Here, we compared the approved edoxaban regimen $(60/30\mathrm{mg})$ to warfarin.  

The primary efficacy endpoint was SSE. The principal safety endpoint was major bleeding, 8 assessed in patients who received ${\geq}1$ dose of study drug while on-treatment (last dose $+3$ days).

In patients receiving warfarin, the international normalized ratio (INR) was measured at least monthly with an encrypted point-of-care device and an individual patient time-in-therapeutic range (iTTR; the percentage of days with INR 2.0-3.0) was calculated. Peak and trough edoxaban concentration, exogenous antiFXa-activity were measured at day 29. Exogenous antiFXa assays measure the in vitro capacity of edoxaban to inhibit the activity of bovine FXa that is added to plasma samples. As such, exogenous anti FXa activity reflects a pharmacokinetic effect and serves as a surrogate measure for edoxaban plasma concentrations.9.10 Endogenous FXa activity was measured at baseline, and percentage of inhibition of baseline endogenous FXa were measured at day 29.9,10 Endogenous FXa activity is measured spectrophotometrically as FXa activity in plasma following conversion of FX to FXa by Russel viper venom. Thus, the inhibition of endogenous FXa activity reflects the pharmacodynamics effect of edoxaban (ie, its anticoagulant action), which is a clinically more relevant parameters because it is measured after activation of the patients’ own FX. 10  



# Statistical analysis  

Baseline characteristics were compared with the Kruskal-Wallis and chi-square tests for continuous and categorical variables, respectively. Patients with a $\mathrm{CHA_{2}DS_{2}VASc}~\leq~3\$ or with a $\mathrm{CHA_{2}DS_{2}VASc}\geq6$ were pooled. Efficacy analyses were performed in the intention-to-treat study population and bleeding outcomes in the on-treatment safety population. Event rates are expressed per 100 patient-years. Hazard ratios (HRs) with $95\%$ confidence intervals (CIs) comparing the relative efficacy and safety of edoxaban versus warfarin by $\mathrm{CHA_{2}DS_{2}VASc}$ score subgroups were calculated with Cox proportional hazards models with treatment as a covariate along with the stratification factor of dose adjustment status. Effect modification was assessed by including an interaction term in the model. A two-sided $P-$ value ${<}0.05$ was considered statistically significant, without adjustments for multiple comparisons. Analyses were performed with use of Stata/SE version 16.1 (Stata Corp., College Station, Texas) or SAS version 9.4 (SAS Institute, Inc, Cary, NC).

Permanent AF and most cardiovascular risk factors were more prevalent in patients with higher $\mathrm{CHA_{2}DS_{2}VASc}$ scores (Table 1).

Patients with higher $\mathrm{CHA_{2}DS_{2}VASc}$ scores were more likely to be enrolled in North America or Eastern Europe. Endogenous FXa levels prior to the 1st dose of study drug were similar across patients stratified by CHADSVASc score. Median iTTR was $68.4\%$ (IQR $56.5\%{-}77.4\%)$ with warfarin, with no significant differences in iTTR or in the percent of days sub-therapeutic $(17\%{-18\%})$ , or supra-therapeutic $(10\%{\cdot}11\%)$ across $\mathrm{CHA_{2}DS_{2}VASc}$ groups (Table IM). Edoxaban concentration, exogenous anti-FXa activity and percentage of endogenous FXa inhibition at day 29 were higher in patients with higher $\mathrm{CHA_{2}DS_{2}VASc}$ scores, as these patients tended to have lower body weights and worse renal function – factors known to increase edoxaban exposure (Table I1).10.11  

The rates of SSE (from $1.05\%$ to $2.99\%$ /year) and major bleeding (from $2.27\%$ to $4.66\%$ /year) increased with increasing $\mathrm{CHA_{2}DS_{2}VASc}$ score in warfarin-treated patients (Figure 1). The HRs for SSE per point increment of the $\mathrm{CHA_{2}DS_{2}VASc}$ score were 1.29 $(95\%\mathrm{CI}$ 1.21-1.38, $P<0.001)$ and 1.26 $(95\%\mathrm{CI}$ 1.17-1.36, $P<$ 0.001), in warfarin- and edoxaban-treated patients, respectively $\mathcal{P}$ -interaction $=0.56\$ . For each $\mathrm{CHA_{2}DS_{2}VASc}$ strata, edoxaban was associated with numerically lower rates of SSE than warfarin (HR 0.81-0.95, $P\cdot$ interaction $=0.90$ ，Figure 2，Table IIl. The number of prevented events was between 4 and 52 per 10,000 patient-years across the range of $\mathrm{CHA_{2}DS_{2}VASc}$ scores.  

The HRs for major bleeding per $\mathrm{CHA_{2}DS_{2}VASc}$ point increment were 1.20 $(95\%\mathrm{CI}$ 1.13-1.27, $P~<$ 0.001), and 1.19 $(95\%\mathrm{CI}1.12–1.27P<0.001)$ for patients receiving warfarin and edoxaban respectively, $(P-$ interaction $_{1=0.94}$ ). Edoxaban reduced the risk of a major bleed regardless of $\mathrm{CHA_{2}DS_{2}VASc}$ score $(\mathrm{HR}0.76-\$ 0.85, $P$ -interaction $=0.96$ ), which translated into 42-89 prevented events per 10,0oo patient-years (Figure 2, Table III).  

While the HR for ICH in warfarin-treated patients increased with increasing $\mathrm{CHA_{2}DS_{2}VASc}$ scores (1.25; $95\%\mathrm{CI}$ 1.13-1.38 per point increment, ${P_{\mathrm{~\scriptsize~<~}}0.001}\$ ), no such increase in ICH was present in edoxabantreated patients $(95\%\mathrm{CI}$ 1.03; 0.87-1.22, $P{=}0.73$ , $P\cdot$ interaction $=0.046$ ). Edoxaban significantly reduced ICH with HRs between 0.29 and 0.77 across the risk groups, resulting in 10-94 prevented events per 10,000 patientyears. Similarly, edoxaban reduced cardiovascular mortality (HR 0.74-0.98, 13-83 fewer events per 10,000 patientyears) across the spectrum of risk (Figure 2, Table II). Figures S1 and S2 show similar or lower risks for various secondary efficacy and safety endpoints with edoxaban vs warfarin across $\mathrm{CHA_{2}DS_{2}VASc}$ categories (each P-interaction $\geq0.10\AA,$ , with the exception of major gastrointestinal bleeding, which was increased with edoxaban, without evidence of interaction with $\mathrm{CHA_{2}DS_{2}VASc}$ score.

# Discussion  

In patients with AF treated with anticoagulation, the $\mathrm{CHA_{2}DS_{2}VASc}$ score predicts both major bleeding and stroke. 4 In the warfarin group of ENGAGE AF-TIMI, the iTTR and proportion of time patients spent in subtherapeutic or supratherapeutic range were similar across $\mathrm{CHA_{2}DS_{2}VASc}$ scores. Trough edoxaban concentration, exogenous anti-FXa activity, and the percentage of endogenous FXa inhibition were higher in patients with higher $\mathrm{CHA_{2}DS_{2}VASc}$ scores, who were older, had lower body weight and creatinine clearance. Patients treated with edoxaban experienced numerically fewer ssE, and significantly fewer major bleeding events, ICH, and cardiovascular deaths across $\mathrm{CHA_{2}DS_{2}VASc}$ categories compared to warfarin. For ICH, the benefit of edoxaban over warfarin significantly widened as the $\mathrm{CHA_{2}DS_{2}VASc}$ increased due to a flat rate of ICH across the risk spectrum with edoxaban in contrast to increasing ICH rates with warfarin as $\mathrm{CHA_{2}DS_{2}VASc}$ scores increased. Since patients with increasing $\mathrm{CHA_{2}DS_{2}VASc}$ score had incrementally higher rates of major bleeding, ICH, and cardiovascular death with warfarin, the number of these events prevented by edoxaban was greater as $\mathrm{CHA_{2}DS_{2}VASc}$ scores increased. Thus, despite higher edoxaban concentrations and greater inhibition of exogenous and endogenous FXa activity at higher $\mathrm{CHA_{2}DS_{2}VASc}$ scores, the relative (and greater absolute) benefit of edoxaban vs warfarin was present without increased bleeding.  

An analysis that compared dabigatran with warfarin across $\mathrm{CHADS}_{2}$ scores strata showed that the $110~\mathrm{mg}$ dose reduced bleeding but not SSE, while the $\mathrm{150~mg}$ dose reduced SSE but increased major bleeding in the highest risk groups relative to warfarin. 12 Both doses of dabigatran reduced ICH across risk groups. There were no significant treatment interactions between risk groups and outcomes of SSE and bleeding in the trials comparing dabigatran or apixaban with warfarin.3,13 Our analysis extends beyond these reports with other NOACs by providing pharmacokinetic and pharmacodynamic data and results for 21 clinical endpoints stratified by $\mathrm{CHA}_{2}\mathrm{DS}_{2}$ VASc score for edoxaban.  

The trial design and data collection in ENGAGE AFTIMI 48 precluded accurate assessment of the HASBLED score as the “L” (Labile INR) could not be assessed. Forty percent of the patients randomized to warfarin were naïve to VKA at study enrollment, and the data on INR control prior to the trial were not collected in the remaining $60\%$ . Although the $\mathrm{CHA_{2}DS_{2}VASc}$ score predicts both bleeding and stroke, and shares determinants with the HASBLED score, use of a dedicated risk score for bleeding risk is preferred. 4  

In ENGAGE AF-TIMI 48, the $\mathrm{CHADS}_{2}$ score was ${\geq}2$ , all patients were treated with anticoagulation, and those with major noncardiac comorbidities were excluded, which limits extrapolation to other populations. Addition of biomarkers and clinical characteristics has been proposed to more accurately predict stroke.14, 15  



# Conclusion  

The favorable efficacy and safety profile of edoxaban compared to well-managed warfarin is maintained across $\mathrm{CHA_{2}DS_{2}VASc}$ scores. The rates of ICH with edoxaban are flat across $\mathrm{CHA_{2}DS_{2}VASc}$ scores ${\geq}2$ . Because stroke, embolic events, bleeding and cardiovascular mortality rates are higher with increasing $\mathrm{CHA_{2}DS_{2}VASc}$ score, the absolute number of prevented events is greater with edoxaban compared to warfarin in patients at highest risk.
