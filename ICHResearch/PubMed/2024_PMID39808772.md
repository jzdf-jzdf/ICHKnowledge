# Omaha System-Based Extended Nursing Care in Hypertensive Cerebral Hemorrhage: A Randomized Study  

Yamei Xiao, BScN $\circledcirc$ ■ Xiaohui Wang, BScN  



# BACKGROUND:  

Traditional nursing care often fails to meet the complex needs of hypertensive cerebral hemorrhage patients. Limited evidence exists on the efficacy of structured nursing frameworks such as the Omaha System in postoperative care for these patients.  



# OBJECTIVE: METHODS:  

This study aims to evaluate the efficacy of Omaha-based extended nursing care in improving patients’ outcomes.  

This randomized controlled trial included 53 patients with hypertensive cerebral hemorrhage admitted to the neurosurgical department of a large tertiary hospital in Chengdu, China, from March to September 2023. Participants were randomly assigned to either the traditional nursing care $.n=26)$ or the Omaha-based nursing care in addition to the traditional nursing care $(n=27)$ ). Outcomes included self-health management abilities, anxiety and depression levels, quality-of-life, and functional independence. Data were collected before nursing care and 1- and 3-months post-nursing care.  



# RESULTS:  

Omaha-based nursing care demonstrated improvements in self-health management abilities, with cognition scores increasing from a baseline of 31.65 (4.37) to 57.32 (5.11) at 3-months (mean difference: 25.67, $95\%\mathrm{C}$ [23.45, 27.89], $p=.01.$ ). Anxiety levels decreased in the Omaha-based nursing group, with SAS scores reducing from 45.71 (3.48) to 28.26 (2.74) at 3-months (mean difference: $-17.45$ , $95\%$ CI [−19.02, −15.88], $p<.01)$ . Quality-of-life scores improved in the Omaha-based nursing group, increasing from 6.3 (1.2) to 7.9 (0.9) at 3-months (mean difference: 1.6, $95\%$ CI [1.12, 2.08], $p=.03)$ . Functional independence scores were also higher in the Omaha-based nursing group at 3-months (mean difference: 9.2, $95\%$ CI [6.11, 12.29], $p=.03)$ .  



# CONCLUSION:  

Our findings support the integration of the Omaha System into postoperative care protocols for this patient population. However, further validation is warranted by larger trials.

# OBJECTIVE  

This study aims to evaluate the efficacy of Omahabased extended nursing care in improving the clinical outcomes of patients with hypertensive cerebral hemorrhage.  



# METHODS  



# Study Design and Population  

This parallel, randomized study involved patients diagnosed with hypertensive cerebral hemorrhage admitted to the Department of Orthopedics at the Affiliated Hospital of Chengdu University and West China Hospital of Sichuan University between March and September 2023. Out of 119 eligible patients, 66 were excluded (61 did not meet the eligibility criteria, and 5 refused to participate). The remaining 53 participants were randomly allocated to either a control group receiving traditional nursing care $(n=26)$ or an intervention group receiving Omaha-based extended nursing care in addition to traditional nursing care $(n=27)$ (Figure 1). The study protocol was approved by the Medical Ethics Committee of the West China Hospital (registration code: PJ2023- 001-10; date of approval: 01/24/2023), and informed consent was obtained from the patients’ families. This research followed the recommendations set by the Declarations of Helsinki. No amendments to the in-priori protocol were made after the commencement of the study.  



# Randomization and Blinding  

Patients were randomly assigned to either the traditional nursing care or the Omaha-based nursing care groups using a computer-generated random number sequence. This method ensured that each patient had an equal chance of being allocated to any study group, thereby minimizing selection bias. Due to the nature of the intervention, it was not feasible to blind the patients or the nursing staff to the group assignments. However, the outcome assessors were blinded to the group allocations to reduce assessment bias.  



# Eligibility Criteria  

Participants were selected based on the following criteria:  

1. Age: $\ge60$ years old.   
2. Confirmed hypertensive cerebral hemorrhage via imaging examination (e.g., computed tomography), with hematoma volume and location suitable for surgical intervention.   
3. Good overall health without severe dysfunction in major organs such as the heart, lungs, liver, or kidneys, allowing them to tolerate surgery.   
4. Availability of complete medical records and followup information for postoperative efficacy assessment.  

Exclusion criteria included:  

1. Presence of severe organic lesions (e.g., significant heart, lung, or hematological diseases) that could affect surgical outcomes and patient safety. 2. Secondary cerebral hemorrhage due to trauma, vascular abnormalities, coagulopathy, systemic diseases, or malignancies. 3. Failure to regain consciousness postoperatively. 4. Withdrawal from the study due to complications, personal reasons, or other factors.  



# Nursing Care Protocol  



# Traditional Nursing Care  

Patients received routine postoperative monitoring and basic nursing care. Nursing staff monitored blood pressure, assisted with regular repositioning, and provided interventions such as back massage to prevent pressure ulcers. Oral hygiene was maintained using $0.9\%$ sodium chloride solution to prevent ulcers. Once the patient’s condition stabilized, early bed exercises and physiotherapy were initiated, with close monitoring of consciousness and muscle strength (X. Liu et al., 2019; Wu et al., 2021).

# Omaha-Based Nursing Care  

In addition to the standard care provided to the traditional nursing care group, Omaha-based nursing care group patients received Omaha-based continuation nursing (Supplemental Digital Content File 1, available at:). Standard nursing care involves routine postoperative monitoring and basic interventions, such as blood pressure monitoring, regular repositioning, and preventive measures against pressure ulcers. However, Omaha  

Based Extended Nursing Care extends beyond these basics by incorporating a more holistic approach. This includes a detailed initial assessment of the patient’s environment and health-related issues, followed by developing a tailored care plan. The plan is personalized to address not only the patient’s physical but also the psychological and educational needs, which are often overlooked in standard care. Additionally, the Omaha System emphasizes regular follow-ups, health education, and ongoing support, including technology platforms for continuous patient engagement.  

The Omaha System encompasses problem categorization, intervention planning, and effect evaluation. The initial assessment involved evaluating the patient’s environment and 42 health-related items. Based on this assessment, a tailored care plan was developed, including health education, treatment guidelines, case management, and regular evaluations. Effectiveness was measured using a comprehensive five-point Likert scale assessment of cognition, behavior, and status, with higher scores indicating better rehabilitation outcomes (Huang et al., 2018; Topaz et al., 2014).  

During the continuation care phase, nursing teams identified nursing problems using the Omaha System, conducting comprehensive assessments via telephone and regular follow-up visits. These assessments evaluated patients’ disease knowledge, recovery progress, treatment adherence, adverse reactions, and psychological status. Personalized health education and guidance were supplemented by ongoing consultation and support through platforms such as microblogging groups. Prior to discharge, patients received an action plan manual, demonstrations of rehabilitation exercises, and training in symptom and sign monitoring (Yen et al., 2020).  

Patients were encouraged to adhere to prescribed medications and document treatment progress and any issues encountered. Nursing teams monitored adverse reactions, conducted further evaluations during followup visits, and provided timely feedback to attending physicians. In special circumstances or when treatment uncertainties arose, nursing teams sought superior support and professional advice to ensure comprehensive patient care. These measures were maintained for up to 8 weeks to promote rehabilitation and health maintenance (Luehr, 2016).  



# Outcome Measures  



# Health Self-Management Ability Scale  

Self-health management ability was assessed using the Health Self-Management Ability Scale before and after 3 months of nursing care, encompassing three dimensions and 38 questions, each rated on a fivepoint Likert scale, ranging from 1 (strongly disagree) to 5 (strongly agree). The total possible score ranges from 38 to 190, with higher scores indicating better self-management (Cramm et al., 2012). This scale has been validated across various populations, showing strong internal consistency (Cronbach’s alpha ranging from 0.70 to 0.89 across subscales) and construct validity (C. Chen et al., 2019; Wild et al., 2018; Zhang et al., 2020).  



# Self-Assessment Scale for Anxiety (SAS) and Depression (SDS)  

Psychological state was evaluated using the SAS (Chiesi et al., 2011) and SDS scales (Yue et al., 2020). Each scale contains 20 items, with responses rated on a four-point Likert scale, where $1=a$ little of the time and $4=most$ of the time. The scores of each item are summed to produce a total score, which is then multiplied by 1.25 to obtain a standardized score. For SAS, a total score of 50 or higher indicates clinically significant anxiety, while a total score of 53 or higher was used for SDS to determine clinically significant depression. Higher scores indicate more severe symptoms. Both SAS (Dunstan & Scott, 2020) and SDS (Zung et al., 1965) scales have been validated in numerous studies, showing high internal consistency.  



# Quality-of-Life Index (QLI)  

Quality of life was measured using the QLI scale, a standardized tool to measure an individual’s overall quality-of-life. The QLI scale evaluates various aspects of life quality, including health, psychological well-being, and social relationships. The QLI consists of 36 items, with responses on a six-point Likert scale, where $1=\nuery$ dissatisfied and $6=$ very satisfied. The total possible score ranges from 36 to 216, with higher scores indicating a better quality of life (Smith et al., 2000). The QLI has demonstrated strong internal consistency (Cronbach’s alpha $>~0.90$ ) and construct validity across various patient populations (Can et al., 2010; Yip et al., 2012).  



# Functional Independence Measure (FIM)  

Self-care ability was assessed using the FIM scale, an established tool used to assess patients’ ability to perform activities of daily living independently. The FIM scale includes items that measure motor and cognitive functions (Kidd et al., 1995). The FIM consists of 18 items divided into motor and cognitive subscales. Each item is rated on a seven-point Likert scale, where $1=$ total assistance and $7=$ complete independence. The total score ranges from 18 to 126, with higher scores indicating greater independence in daily activities. The FIM has demonstrated strong reliability (Cronbach’s alpha $>0.95$ ) and validity in numerous studies and is widely used in rehabilitation settings (Dodds et al., 1993).  



# Sample Size Calculation  

The sample size was determined based on the primary outcome measure, improving self-health management ability. Assuming a medium effect size (Cohen’s $d=0.5$ ) (Larner, 2014), a power of $80\%$ , and a significance level of 0.05, a sample size of 53 patients was calculated to be sufficient to detect a statistically significant difference between the groups. Given the lack of similar studies in the literature, these calculations were not based on previous research.  



# Statistical Analysis  

Data were analyzed using SPSS 25.0 statistical software. Mean (standard deviation) values were used for continuous variables, and t-tests were conducted to compare group differences. Categorical data were described using frequencies and percentages, with the chi-square test used to assess differences between groups. A $\smash{p<.05}$ was considered statistically significant, indicating that the observed differences were unlikely due to chance, thus holding research significance.

# DISCUSSION  

This study aimed to evaluate the efficacy of Omahabased extended nursing care compared to conventional nursing care in patients with hypertensive cerebral hemorrhage. These patients face multiple challenges ranging from the complexity of the condition to psychological care, rehabilitation care, nutritional intervention, and drug management. Effective nursing care involves not only monitoring vital signs and preventing complications, but it also includes rehabilitation training and psychological support, all aimed at enhancing patients’ compliance and promoting functional recovery and quality-of-life (Li et al., 2023; X. Liu et al., 2019). Our findings highlight the significant advantages of the Omaha-based approach over standard nursing care. Unlike standard care, which tends to focus on immediate postoperative interventions, the Omaha System’s comprehensive approach addresses patients’ broader and more complex needs. This is achieved through continuous assessment, personalized care planning, and regular follow-up, which together foster greater self-health management abilities, alleviate anxiety and depression, and lead to improved quality of life and functional independence. These differences underscore the value of integrating structured nursing frameworks such as the Omaha System into standard care protocols to achieve better patient outcomes.  



# Self-Health Management Ability  

The significant improvements observed in selfhealth management abilities across cognition, behavior, and environment dimensions underscore the effectiveness of the Omaha-based nursing care model. Before the intervention, there were no significant differences between traditional and Omaha-based nursing care groups, suggesting that both groups had comparable baseline capabilities. However, post-intervention assessments revealed substantial gains in the Omaha-based nursing care group, particularly at the 3-month mark. These findings align with previous research indicating that structured nursing interventions can significantly bolster self-management skills in patients with chronic conditions (Jiang & Wang, 2023; X. Liu et al., 2019; Xiong et al., 2020). The Omaha System’s comprehensive approach, which includes health education, case management, and regular evaluations, likely contributed to these positive outcomes by empowering patients with the knowledge and skills necessary for effective self-care (Martin et al., 2011).  



# Anxiety and Depression  

The reduction in anxiety and depression scores in Omaha-based nursing care highlights the psychological benefits of Omaha-based nursing care. While both groups started with similar levels of anxiety and depression, significant improvements were seen in the Omahabased nursing care as early as 1-month post-intervention. By 3 months, the Omaha-based nursing care group exhibited markedly lower scores in both SAS and SDS scales than the traditional nursing care group. These results are consistent with the literature, suggesting that ongoing support and education can mitigate psychological distress in patients undergoing medical treatment. However, these improvements were limited to epileptic children (Zhuang et al., 2021), mid-to-latestage lung cancer (Bai et al., 2024), and those undergoing urinary calculus surgery (J. Liu et al., 2021). The personalized health education and continuous support provided through the Omaha System likely played a crucial role in alleviating anxiety and depression, offering patients a better understanding of their condition and more robust coping mechanisms (Yanyan et al., 2023).  



# Quality of Life and Functional Independence  

Quality of life and functional independence are critical outcomes for patients recovering from hypertensive cerebral hemorrhage (Christensen et al., 2009; Huang et al., 2021). Our study found that the Omahabased nursing care group experienced significant improvements in both QLI and FIM scores 3 months post-intervention. These findings align with other studies demonstrating the positive impact of comprehensive nursing care on patient outcomes, specifically in acute settings such as patients undergoing percutaneous coronary interventions (Erci, 2012; Yin et al., 2022). The Omaha System’s structured intervention framework, which includes tailored rehabilitation exercises, symptom monitoring, and continuous support, likely contributed to these enhancements (Thompson et al., 2012). By addressing both the physical and psychological aspects of recovery, the Omaha-based nursing care model helps patients achieve higher levels of independence and overall well-being (Topaz et al., 2014).  



# Previous Randomized Trials on the Omaha System  

Our findings are consistent with previous studies exploring the Omaha System’s efficacy in various patient populations. Table 3 summarizes published randomized studies comparing the Omaha System to standard care in different patient populations.  

These studies collectively reinforce the efficacy of the Omaha System in improving various health outcomes across different patient populations. For instance, Ning et al. (2021) reported significant reductions in fatigue scores among lung cancer patients using the Omaha System, while Xiao et al. (2019) found marked improvements in knowledge, behavior, and status among angina patients. Yin et al. (2022) demonstrated enhanced medication compliance and overall health in CHD patients, and J. Liu et al. (2021) observed reduced anxiety, depression, and complications among patients with retained double J ureteral stents. Zhao et al. (2020) reported better nutritional status and laboratory parameters in CKD patients undergoing peritoneal dialysis, and Z. C. Liu et al. (2020) showed significant health improvements in rheumatoid arthritis patients with comparable readmission rates compared to the standard of care. These observations suggest the potential benefits of the Omaha System in postoperative care in various settings; however, these observations are limited to the short-term (below 6 months of follow-up), with no randomized trials conducted explicitly in patients with hypertensive cerebral hemorrhage.  



# Implications for Nursing Care and Future Directions  

The results of this study have several implications for nursing practice. First, they underscore the importance of integrating comprehensive, structured nursing interventions, such as those provided by the Omaha System, into standard care protocols for patients with hypertensive cerebral hemorrhage. The significant improvements across all outcomes observed in the Omaha-based nursing care group highlight the potential benefits of such an approach. Nursing practitioners should consider adopting similar models to enhance patient outcomes. Second, the findings suggest that ongoing education and support are crucial to effective nursing care. The continuous assessment and personalized interventions provided in the Omaha System likely played a significant role in the observed improvements. This indicates that nursing care should focus on immediate postoperative needs and provide long-term support and education to help patients manage their condition effectively.

Future research should explore the long-term effects of Omaha-based nursing care on patient outcomes. Further research is needed to understand how the Omaha System achieves its benefits, which could inform the development of even more effective nursing interventions.  



# LIMITATIONS  

This study has several strengths. This is the first randomized study to assess the efficacy of the Omaha System in patients with hypertensive cerebral hemorrhage. It employed a randomized controlled design, which accounted for both measured and unmeasured confounders. The sample size was adequate to detect significant differences between the groups, and the follow-up period was sufficient to observe meaningful changes in patient outcomes. Additionally, the use of validated scales for assessing self-health management ability, anxiety, depression, quality of life, and functional independence adds robustness to the findings.  

However, there are also some limitations to consider. First, the study was conducted in a single hospital, which may limit the generalizability of the findings. Future research should aim to replicate these results in different settings and with larger, more diverse populations. Second, while the outcome assessors were blinded to group allocations, the nature of the intervention made it impossible to blind the patients and nursing staff, which could introduce bias. Finally, the study only followed patients for 3 months; more extended follow-up periods are needed to assess the sustainability of the observed benefits.  



# CONCLUSION  

Our study demonstrates that Omaha-based nursing care significantly improves self-health management abilities, reduces anxiety and depression, and enhances quality of life and functional independence in patients with hypertensive cerebral hemorrhage. These findings suggest incorporating comprehensive, structured nursing interventions such as the Omaha System into standard care protocols could benefit this patient population. Further research is warranted to explore such models’ long-term and cost-effective implementation in diverse health care settings.
