# Tenecteplase versus alteplase after acute ischemic stroke at high age  

Bente Thommessen1 $\circledcirc$ , Halvor Næss2,3, Nicola Logallo4, Christopher E Kvistad $2,5\textcircled{\scriptsize{1}}$ , Ulrike Waje-Andreassen2, Hege Ihle-Hansen $6,7_{\textcircled{1}}$ Hakon Ihle-Hansen,8, Lars Thomassen2,5 and Ole Morten Rønning1,8  

# Abstract  

Background: Stroke prevalence is increasing with age. Alteplase is the only agent approved for thrombolytic treatment for patients with ischemic stroke, including patients $\ge80$ years. In the present study, the aim was to compare efficacy and safety of tenecteplase and alteplase in patients $\ge80$ years.  

Methods: Data from the Norwegian Tenecteplase Stroke Trial, a randomized controlled trial comparing alteplase and tenecteplase, were assessed.  

Results: Of the 273 patients ${\ge}80$ years included, mean age was 85.5 years.   
In the intention-to-treat analyses, $43.1\%$ receiving tenecteplase and $39.9\%$ receiving alteplase reached excellent functional outcome (modified Rankin Scale score $_{0-1}$ ) after 3 months (odds ratio (OR) 1.14, $95\%$ confidence interval (CI) $0.70-$ 1.85, $\mathsf{p}{=}0.59$ ). No significant differences among patients in the two treatment groups regarding frequency of symptomatic intracranial hemorrhage during the first $48\mathrm{h}$ were identified (11 $(8.5\%)$ in the tenecteplase group, 10 $(7.0\%)$ in the alteplase group, OR 1.23, $95\%\subset10.50–3.00,\textsf{p}0.65)$ ). Death within 3 months occurred in 18 patients $(14.3\%)$ in the tenecteplase group and in 21 $(|5.3\%)$ in the alteplase group $\left(\mathtt{p}0.84\right)$ . After excluding stroke mimics, the proportion of patients with excellent functional outcome was $44.1\%$ in the tenecteplase group and $34.4\%$ in the alteplase group (OR $1.50\mathsf{Cl}0.90\mathsf{-}2.52,\mathsf{p}0.12)$ .  

Conclusion: No differences in the efficacy and safety of tenecteplase versus alteplase in patients ${\ge}80$ years were identified.  

Trial registration: Clinicaltrials.gov (NCT01949948)

# Introduction  

Stroke prevalence and severity is increasing with age, and the burden of stroke-related disability is expected to increase with prolonged life expectancy. Alteplase is the only agent approved for thrombolytic treatment for patients with ischemic stroke, including patients $\ge80$ years.2 The original approval for intravenous thrombolysis was restricted to patients $<80$ years with acute ischemic stroke, and even in recent years patients ${>}80$ years have often been excluded or underrepresented in randomized trials. On the other hand, in one smaller study on outcome after thrombolysis with alteplase in patients ${\tt>}90$ years, the patients receiving alteplase did not have a better functional outcome compared to the patients not treated with thrombolysis, and in a trial on large vessel occlusion (LVO), poor outcome was associated with patients $>80$ years who had been treated with alteplase.7  

Since alteplase has shortcomings, and favorable outcomes might be less frequent in older patients, it is important to study if other agents may have better efficacy with the same safety.8,9  

Tenecteplase is a modified tissue plasminogen activator with a longer half-life and higher fibrin specificity than alteplase, and appears promising as an effective fibrinolytic agent, with greater reperfusion, major vessel recanalization, and fewer bleeding complications than alteplase in pilot studies.10  

In 2017, the results from the Norwegian Tenecteplase Stroke Trial (NOR-TEST), a large phase III trial, were published. It was a randomized controlled study assessing safety and efficacy of tenecteplase in a general stroke population,11 and the results showed that tenecteplase had similar effect and safety profiles as alteplase.  

# Aims  

The present study is a subgroup analysis from NORTEST where the aim was to compare efficacy and safety of tenecteplase and alteplase in patients $\ge80$ years.  

# Methods  

The NOR-TEST trial was a multicenter, randomized, open-label, blinded endpoint phase 3 trial conducted in 13 stroke units in Norway, and 1100 patients were included.11 Patients with clinically suspected acute ischemic stroke with acute focal dysfunction, ${>}18$ years, admitted within $4.5\mathrm{h}$ from symptom onset, and judged eligible for intravenous thrombolysis according to Norwegian clinical guidelines12 which are similar to European guidelines were included. Patients ${>}80$ years and patients with minor neurological deficits were allowed.  

Patients were allocated to treatment with either tenecteplase $0.4\mathrm{mg/kg}$ or alteplase $0.9\mathrm{mg/kg}$ . Study design, participants, randomization, procedures, and data collection are described in detail previously, and a full protocol of the study has been published.13  

In the present study, only patients $\ge80$ years were included into the analyses.  

The primary endpoint was excellent functional outcome defined as ‘‘No symptoms’’ or ‘‘No significant disability, able to carry out all duties’’ according to modified Rankin Scale (mRS) 0–1 at 3 months. mRS was assessed at 7 days (or at discharge if earlier), and at 3 months post-stroke.  

The secondary outcomes were symptomatic ICH occurring within $24-48\mathrm{h}$ , death within 3 months, major neurological improvement at $24\mathrm{{h}}$ defined as either National Institute of Health Stroke Scale (NIHSS) score 0 or a reduction of NIHSS of at least 4 points at $24\mathrm{{h}}$ compared with baseline score. Brain computed tomography (CT) and CT angiography were done before thrombolytic treatment and study inclusion. Follow-up imaging consisted of either brain magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) or brain CT at $24-$ $48\mathrm{{h}}$ after treatment. Intracranial hemorrhage after thrombolysis was described according to the European Cooperative Acute Stroke Study (ECASS) morphological definition, and symptomatic ICH was defined on the basis of ECASS III criteria.14,15  

NOR-TEST was performed in accordance with the guidelines for Good Clinical Practice and the Declaration of Helsinki. Written informed consent was obtained from every patient or from close relatives if the patient was not able to sign. The trial was reviewed and approved by the Regional Committee for Medical and Health Research Ethics and the Norwegian Medicines Agency.  

# Statistics  

Sample size for the sub-study comprising patients $\ge80$ years was not calculated, but for the whole population included in the main study according to the hypothesis that tenecteplase was superior to alteplase.11  

The main analysis of outcomes was an intention-totreat analysis, while the per-protocol analysis excluded patients with stroke mimics.  

Descriptive methods were used to characterize the study population. Categorical variables were tested with $\chi^{\bar{2}}$ test, and Fisher’s exact test when appropriate.  

Logistic regression analyses were performed with outcome variables as dependent variables, and tenecteplase versus alteplase as independent variables. We assessed for interaction of age ${>}80$ with treatment by creating an interaction term between age and treatment (age\*treatment). The results are presented as odds ratios (OR) with confidence intervals (CI).  

# Results  

Among 1100 patients included into NOR-TEST, 273 $(24.8\%)$ patients were $\ge80$ years; of these, 130 $(47.6\%)$ were randomized to treatment with tenecteplase and 143 $(52.4\%)$ to alteplase. Mean age was 85.5 (SD 4.16) years in the tenecteplase group, and 85.4 (SD 4.21) years in the alteplase group. Baseline characteristics are shown in Table 1. There were no significant differences between the two treatment groups in demographic and baseline characteristics.  

# Intention-to-treat analyses  

In the intention-to-treat analyses, including stroke mimics, 56/130 $(43.1\%)$ receiving tenecteplase, and  

57/143 $(39.9\%)$ receiving alteplase reached excellent functional outcome (mRS score 0–1) after 3 months (OR 1.14, $95\%$ C $\left[0.70{-}1.85\right]\mathrm{~}$ , $\mathrm{p}=0.59,$ .  

No significant differences were identified among patients in the two treatment groups regarding frequency of symptomatic ICH during the first $48\mathrm{{h}}$ (OR 1.23, $95\%$ CI 0.50–3.00, $\mathrm{p}=0.65,$ . Death within 3 months was 18 $(14.3\%)$ in the tenecteplase group and 21 $(15.3\%)$ in the alteplase group $(\mathrm{p}=0.84)$ . At $24\mathrm{{h}}$ after thrombolysis, 46/115 $(40\%)$ receiving tenecteplase and 47/131 $(35.9\%)$ receiving alteplase had major clinical improvement $(\mathtt{p}{}=0.51)$ (Table 2).  

No interaction between age ${>}80$ and treatment was identified (OR 1.037, $95\%$ CI 0.902–1.91, $\mathrm{p}{=}0.612$ ).

# Per-protocol analyses  

The relative incidence of stroke mimics in the older group was $14.6\%$ compared to $18.1\%$ among those $<80$ years. When excluding patients with stroke mimics, 52/118 $(44.1\%)$ in the tenecteplase group versus 43/125 $(34.4\%)$ in the alteplase group $(\mathtt{p}{=}0.12)$ reached excellent functional outcome after 3 months (Table 3).  

# Discussion  

In this study, among patients $\ge80$ years with acute ischemic stroke, tenecteplase $0.4\mathrm{mg/kg}$ given within $4.5\mathrm{h}$ from stroke onset appeared to be at least as effective in reaching good clinical outcome as alteplase $0.9\mathrm{mg/kg}$ . The crude frequencies of favorable outcomes were even higher in the group receiving tenecteplase, however not significant. In a comprehensive systematic review, the benefits of alteplase compared with placebo were similar for patients $\geq80$ years as for patients $<80$ years.16 The results show that the effect and safety of tenecteplase seems to be comparable with alteplase in the elderly and indicates that the beneficial effect of thrombolysis shown at a high age can also be applied to the use of tenecteplase.  

Several studies have shown that the rates of symptomatic ICH due to alteplase are not significantly increased in patients aged $\geq80$ years.17–19 In our study, no significant difference in the incidence of symptomatic intracerebral hemorrhage among patients treated with tenecteplase and those receiving alteplase was found.  

We assessed for interaction of age ${>}80$ years with treatment, but no interaction was found. Despite the lack of interaction, it is still of interest to describe outcomes in ${>}80$ years old.  

As tenecteplase is more fibrin-specific and has duration of activity longer than alteplase, tenecteplase, given as a bolus, appears to be a more feasible thrombolytic agent. Campbell et al. have recently showed that intravenous tenecteplase at a dose of $0.25\mathrm{mg/kg}$ before thrombectomy was associated with an approximate doubling of the rate of arterial recanalization compared with alteplase after stroke due to LVO.20  

The present study shows that the effect and safety of tenecteplase seems to be comparable with alteplase in the elderly. Hence, high age should not be a reason to discourage from treatment with tenecteplase in acute ischemic stroke.  

The low number included may be a weakness. Only patients living independently were included, excluding older patients with cognitive or function impairments and severe comorbidities. The stroke severity of the patients included was therefore lower than expected with a median NIHSS score of 7. However, as far as we know, this is the first study on tenecteplase and acute ischemic stroke among patients $\ge80$ years, and we believe that the results, in spite of a probable selection bias in favor of minor stroke, will have high clinical relevance because the inclusion criteria reflected the Norwegian and European guidelines. In Norway, the mean age of patients with stroke is 76 years, making efficient treatment to the aging population most important in order to avoid reduced quality of life in the last phase of life.  

Tenecteplase, given at a dose of $0.4\mathrm{mg/kg}$ in this NOR-TEST sub-study about patients $\ge80$ years has the same efficacy and safety as alteplase. Therefore, high age should not be a reason to discourage from treatment with tenecteplase in acute ischemic stroke.
