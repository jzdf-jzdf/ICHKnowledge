# Effectiveness of Endovascular Therapy for Patients with Acute Ischemic Stroke: A MetaAnalysis of Randomized Controlled Trials  

Fuqiang Xi and Jijiao Xue  

- OBJECTIVE: This meta-analysis of published randomized controlled trials (RCTs) aimed to investigate the efficacy and safety of endovascular therapy (ET) for patients with acute ischemic stroke.  

- METHODS: The summary relative risks (RRs) with corresponding $95\%$ confidence intervals were calculated to measure the effectiveness of ET using a random-effects model.  

- RESULTS: Thirteen RCTs including 3408 patients were identified among 2851 initial electronic citations. Overall, the summary RR indicated that ET significantly increased the incidence of a modified Rankin Scale score of 0e2 (RR, 1.50; $P<0.001|$ , asymptomatic intracerebral hemorrhage (IH) (RR, 1.42; $P=0.001),$ subarachnoid hemorrhage (RR, 2.36; $P=0.001$ ), and major complications due to nonintracerebral bleeding (RR, 1.47; $P=0.014)$ . However, ET had no significant effects on the risk of 7-day death $\scriptstyle{\left|{\bar{P}}=\right.}$ 0.827); death after 30 days $\begin{array}{rl}{(P=}&{{}0.085);}\end{array}$ symptomatic IH $\left(P=0.288\right);$ type 1 $\vertP=0.413\rangle,$ type 2 $(P=0.300)_{\cdot}$ , and total parenchymal hematoma $\begin{array}{rl}{|P=0.160\rangle;}\end{array}$ intraventricular hemorrhage $\left(P=0.244\right);$ early neurologic deterioration $(P=$ 0.570); recurrent stroke $(P~=~0.830);$ and symptomatic edema $(P=0.485)$ .  

- CONCLUSIONS: The findings of this meta-analysis suggest the incidence of a modified Rankin Scale score of $_{0-2}$ in patients who received ET was significantly increased. Moreover, the harmful effects of ET might exist due to greater risk of asymptomatic IH, subarachnoid hemorrhage, and non-intracerebral bleeding, and these results should be verified by further large-scale long-term RCT.

# METHODS  

# Data Sources, Search Strategy, and Selection Criteria  

This study was performed and reported according to the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analysis Statement issued in 2009 (i.e., PRISMA checklist).19 Electronic searches for RCTs comparing the efficacy and safety of ET with medical therapy were conducted of PubMed, Embase, and the Cochrane Library from their inception to August 2018. The search terms included terms from Medical Subject Headings in PubMed and the Cochrane Library and terms from EMtree in Embase. The core terms including (“stroke” OR “cerebrovascular accident” OR “ischemic stroke” OR “transient ischemic attack” OR “cerebral ischemia” OR “cerebrovascular disorder”) AND (“intraarterial therapy” OR “intraarterial thrombolysis” OR “intraarterial” OR “endovascular treatment” OR “ET” OR “endovascular” OR “thrombectomy” OR “catheterbased treatment”) AND (“randomized controlled trials” AND “human” AND “English”). We manually searched the reference lists of the retrieved studies to identify any other eligible RCTs.  

Two authors independently conducted the literature search and selected the studies by reading the titles, abstracts, and full-text articles, and any disagreement was resolved by an additional author until consensus was reached. Studies were included if they met the following criteria: 1) patients: patients diagnosed with AIS and ${\ge}\mathrm{I}8$ years of age; 2) intervention: ET plus medical therapies; 3) control: medical therapy; 4) outcomes: all studies reported the incidence of an mRS score of $_{0-2}$ and the incidence of all-cause death, intracerebral hemorrhage, parenchymal hematoma, hemorrhage, early neurologic deterioration, major complication due to non-intracerebral bleeding, recurrent stroke, and symptomatic edema were also collected; and 5) study design: all of the included studies had to have an RCT design. Studies with an observational design were excluded due to various confounding factors.  

# Data Collection and Quality Assessment  

One author conducted the data collection according to a standard flowchart, while another author checked it. If any disagreement was detected, they discussed the issue until consensus was reached. The data collected included the study group’s name, publication year, country, sample size, mean age, percentage of male subjects, National Institutes of Health Stroke Scale (NIHSS) score, percentage of atrial fibrillation, history of hypertension, history of diabetes mellitus (DM), history of coronary artery disease, time from stroke onset to initiation of intravenous tissue plasminogen activator (t-PA), intervention, control, and investigated outcomes.  

The Jadad scale, a comprehensive method of assessing RCT quality in meta-analyses, was used to assess methodologic quality.20 The Jadad scale was based on randomization (1 or 0), concealment of the treatment allocation (1 or 0), blinding (1 or 0), completeness of follow-up (1 or 0), and the use of intentionto-treat analysis (1 or 0) for a total score of $_{0^{-}5}$ . The quality assessment was undertaken by 2 authors, and inconsistencies were resolved by an additional author referring to the original article.

# RESULTS  

# Literature Search  

The initial electronic searches produced $285\mathtt{I}$ articles; of them, $_{27}88$ were excluded due to irrelevant topics and duplication. The remaining 63 full articles were reviewed; of them, 13 were included in the final analysis. $7,8,\mathrm{{r}}4\substack{-\mathrm{{r}}8,29^{-}34}$ Fifty studies were excluded due to: study reporting the same patients $(\mathfrak{n}=37,$ ), observational design $({\mathfrak{n}}=8)$ ), and no appropriate control $(\mathfrak{n}=5,$ ). The literature search and study selection processes are presented in Figure 1. Our manual search of the reference lists of the aforementioned 13 RCTs did not yield any new eligible trials. Table 1 summarizes the general characteristics of the trials and patients.  

# Study Characteristics  

Thirteen RCTs recruiting a total of $3408$ patients with AIS were included in this meta-analysis. The included studies were published between 2013 and 2018, and $70\mathrm{-}656$ patients were included in each trial. Six RCTs were conducted in multiple countries, $\tau,8,\tau{}4,\tau8,29,33$ whereas the remaining 7 trials were conducted in a single country. $\mathrm{I}5^{-\mathrm{I}7}{,}3^{\mathrm{O}-}3^{2}{,}34$ The mean age of the included patients in each trial ranged from 65.5 to 72.6 years, and the percentage of male patients ranged from $45{\cdot}\mathbf{I}^{0}/\mathbf{0}$ to $58$ . $4\%$ , NIHSS score ranged from 13.0 to 19.0, and mean time from stroke onset to the initiation of medical therapy ranged from 113.8 to $648.0$ minutes. Furthermore, 11 trials used intravenous t-PA as conventional therapy, and the remaining 2 trials used standard medical therapy (without intravenous t-PA or alteplase) as conventional therapy. Eleven of the included trials had a  

Jadad score of $4$ , whereas the remaining 2 trials had a Jadad score of 3 (Supplementary Table 1).  

# Primary Outcome  

All the included studies reported the rate of an mRS score of $^{0-2}$ in the ET and medical therapy groups. Overall, we noted that patients who received ET demonstrated a significantly increased rate of an mRS score of $^{0-2}$ compared with medical therapy alone group (RR 1.50; $95\%$ CI ${\bfI}.23^{-}{\bfI}.82$ ; $\mathrm{~P~}<0.00\mathrm{I}$ ; Figure 2). The heterogeneity among the included trials was statistically significant (I-square, $73{\cdot}7\%$ ; $\mathrm{~P~}<0.00\mathrm{I}\$ ). The pooled result for an mRS score of $^{0-2}$ was robust by sequential exclusion of individual trials (Supplementary Figure 1). The results of univariate meta-regression analyses indicated that mean age $(\mathrm{P}=0.078)$ , occlusion location $\textstyle(\mathrm{P}=0.076,$ , DM $\textstyle({\mathrm{P}}=0.074,$ ), and control therapies $\mathrm{\check{P}}<0.00\mathrm{\check{I}}\$ might have biased the effectiveness of ET on the rate of an mRS score of $^{0-2}$ (Table 2). Subgroup analyses revealed significant differences between ET and medical therapy for the rate of an mRS score of $^{0-2}$ in most subsets, whereas no significant differences were seen when the mean patient age was older regardless of stroke territory, atrial fibrillation, hypertension, DM, and longer time to treatment (Table 2).  

# Secondary Outcomes  

The number of trials available for 7-day death and death after 30 days were 6 and 11 cohorts, respectively. There were no significant differences between ET and medical therapy for the risk of $7$ -day death (RR 1.03; $95\%$ CI $_{0.7}8\mathrm{-}\mathbf{I}.36$ ; $\mathrm{~P~}=\:0.827$ ; Supplementary Figure 2) and death after 30 days (RR 0.87; $95\%$ CI $0{\cdot}74^{-1.02}$ ; $\mathrm{~P~}=\:0.085$ ; Supplementary Figure 2). There was no evidence of heterogeneity among the included trials for $7$ -day death (Isquare, $0.0\%$ ; $\mathrm{P}=0.458$ ) and death after 30 days (I-square, $0.0\%$ ; $\mathrm{P}=0.644,$ ). The summary result for 7-day death was stable, while the result for death after 30 days was variable due to a marginal $95\%$ CI (Supplementary Figures 3 and 4).  

The number of trials available for asymptomatic intracerebral hemorrhage and symptomatic intracerebral hemorrhage were 3 and 12 cohorts, respectively. We noted that ET significantly increased the risk of asymptomatic intracerebral hemorrhage (RR 1.42; $95\%$ CI $\mathtt{I.I4-I.76}$ ; $\mathrm{~P~}=\mathrm{~}0.00\mathrm{I}$ ; Supplementary Figure 5), whereas ET had no significant effect on symptomatic intracerebral hemorrhage (RR 1.19; $95\%$ CI $0.87^{-}\mathrm{I}.62$ ; $\mathrm{~\textit~{~P~}~}=\mathrm{~\epsilon~}0.288$ ; Supplementary Figure 5). The sensitivity analyses indicated the pooled results for asymptomatic intracerebral hemorrhage and symptomatic intracerebral hemorrhage were robust and not altered by the exclusion of any specific trial (Supplementary Figures 6 and 7).

# Publication Bias  

Of 13 included trials, 8 studies reported no potential conflict of interest, whereas the remaining 5 trials did not report the conflict of interest. The funnel plot for the rate of an mRS score of $_{0-2}$ showed an asymmetry distribution (Figure 3). Although the Begg test showed no evidence of publication bias $\textstyle{\left({\mathrm{P}}\right.\mathrm{~=~}0.502},$ , the Egger test result indicated significant publication bias $\left({\mathsf{P}}\right.=$ 0.036). The conclusion was not altered by use of the trim and fill method to adjust publication bias.35  

# DISCUSSION  

This meta-analysis of RCTs evaluated the efficacy and safety of ET versus medical therapy for patients with AIS. This comprehensive large-scale quantitative meta-analysis included 3408 patients from 13 RCTs with a broad range of NIHSS scores. The summary results from this meta-analysis indicated that ET versus medical therapy was associated with an increased rate of an mRS score of $_{0-2}$ . However, patients who received ET were at an increased risk of asymptomatic intracerebral hemorrhage, subarachnoid hemorrhage, and major complications due to non-intracerebral bleeding. Moreover, there were no significant differences between ET and medical therapy in other safety outcomes, including 7-day death, death after 30 days, symptomatic intracerebral hemorrhage, type 1 parenchymal hematoma, type 2 parenchymal hematoma, total parenchymal hematoma, intraventricular hemorrhage, early neurologic deterioration, recurrent stroke, and symptomatic edema. Finally, the incidence of an mRS score of $^{0-2}$ might be affected by mean age, occlusion location, DM, and control therapies.  

The meta-analysis conducted by Sardar et al.13 found that ET had a superior benefit on the rate of an mRS score of $^{0-2}$ , whereas no significant differences were seen in all-cause mortality and symptomatic intracerebral hemorrhage. However, whether the effectiveness of ET on an mRS score of 0e2 differed according to study or patient characteristics was not investigated. Goyal et al.36 conducted a meta-analysis individual patient data from 5 RCTs and found ET was associated with lower risk of disability at 90 days, and this favoring result are mainly detected for patients aged 80 years or older, randomized more than 300 minutes after symptom onset, and patients not eligible for intravenous t-PA. The study conducted by Badhiwala et al.37 contained 8 RCTs and suggested patients with AIS received ET of mechanical thrombectomy produces improved functional outcomes and greater angiographic revascularization rate at 90 days than those who received intravenous t-PA. Moreover, Balami et al.38 conducted a meta-analysis of 8 RCTs and suggested ET was associated with an improved functional outcome, and a tendency toward decreased in mortality risk was observed. However, recently published RCTs should be included and updated to document the therapeutic effects of ET. Moreover, several other important indexes were not calculated, including parenchymal hematoma, hemorrhage, early neurologic deterioration, major complications due to non-intracerebral bleeding, recurrent stroke, and symptomatic edema. Therefore, we performed this meta-analysis of published RCTs to determine the efficacy and safety of ET versus medical therapy for patients with AIS. Moreover, whether the treatment effectiveness of ET versus medical therapy differ according to multicountry, sex, NIHSS score, Alberta Stroke Program Early Computed Tomography Score, stroke territory, location of occlusion, atrial fibrillation, hypertension, DM, time to randomization, time to treatment, control, and study quality were also conducted.  

The findings of this study suggest an added benefit of ET on the rate of an mRS score of $^{0-2}$ , consistent with the results of a previous study.13 However, several of the included studies reported inconsistent results. The IMS III trial found no significant difference between ET after intravenous t-PA and intravenous tPA alone on functional independence and safety outcomes.29 They pointed out that ET was associated with a high rate of revascularization and that it was a surrogate measure for functional outcomes between the two reperfusion therapies. Similar results were found in the SYNTHESIS Expansion trial30 and the MR RESCUE trial.31 The possible reason for this included time spent, incidence of revascularization, and endovascular devices used, which were associated with various functional outcomes. Moreover, despite reporting nonsignificant differences between ET and medical therapy for the rate of an mRS score of $^{0-2}$ , these trials also reported a nonsignificantly increased incidence of an mRS score of $_{0-2}$ . The reason for this could be due to the lower statistical power and broad $95^{\mathrm{{\circ}}}/{\mathrm{{oCI}}}$ .  

The summary results for the risk of safety outcomes indicated that patients who received ET were at a greater risk of asymptomatic intracerebral hemorrhage, subarachnoid hemorrhage, and major complications due to non-intracerebral bleeding. Further, the results of the sensitivity analysis indicated that the use of ET could reduce the risk of death after 30 days. No significant differences between these 2 strategies were seen in other safety outcomes. The reasons for this could be due to differences in the various characteristics of time to randomization and intervention, type of devices and successful reperfusion, and preselection imaging types across the included trials. Further, the event rates were lower, whereas the smaller number of included trials could have affected the study’s ability to detect the differences between ET and medical therapy.  

The results of the subgroup analyses were consistent with those of the overall analysis in most of the subsets. However, ET did not produce a benefit on the rate of an mRS score of $^{0-2}$ in the older patients. The reason for this could be poor restorability in elderly patients with AIS. This result was not consistent with previous meta-analysis due to the younger and older patients’ definition among included trials was variable in this study, whereas previous meta-analysis based on individual patient data.36 Furthermore, the rate of an mRS score of $_{0-2}$ were not associated with a statistically significant difference between ET and medical therapy regardless of stroke territory, atrial fibrillation, hypertension, DM, and time to treatment due to the smaller number of included trials reporting stratified results based these factors.  

The strengths of this meta-analysis are as follows: 1) this metaanalysis was based on RCTs, which could avoid potential bias concerns of observational study; 2) the summary analyses were based on a large number of patients, and these results were more robust than any individual trial; 3) subgroup analyses based on various study and patient characteristics were conducted that could provide the treatment effectiveness of ET in patients with specific characteristics; and 4) most safety outcomes were calculated to provide comprehensive quantitative results.  

The limitations of this meta-analysis should be mentioned. First, various patient characteristics could have affected the therapeutic effects of ET. Second, different cutoff values of mean age, NIHSS score, time to randomization, time to treatment, and the results of the stratified analyses might have introduced bias due to various cutoff values. Third, the types of endovascular devices and techniques differed across the included trials, which affected the rate of revascularization and the subsequent functional outcomes. Fourth, publication bias is inevitable due to this meta-analysis being based on published studies. Finally, a detailed analysis was not performed due to individual data not being available for the included studies.  

# CONCLUSIONS  

In conclusion, our results indicated that ET produced a greater benefit on the rate of an mRS score of $^{0-2}$ , whereas it could increase a patient’s risk of asymptomatic intracerebral hemorrhage, subarachnoid hemorrhage, and major complications due to nonintracerebral bleeding. Further large-scale RCTs should be conducted to explore the benefits of ET in patients with specific characteristics.
