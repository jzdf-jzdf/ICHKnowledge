# Endovascular thrombectomy with or without intravenous alteplase in acute stroke: a systematic review and meta‑analysis of randomized clinical trials  

Xuan Bai1 $\bullet$ Jianting Qiu1 $\cdot$ Yujie Wang1  

Received: 23 July 2022 / Revised: 28 September 2022 / Accepted: 29 September 2022 / Published online: 5 October 2022   
$\circledcirc$ The Author(s), under exclusive licence to Springer-Verlag GmbH Germany 2022  

# Abstract  

Background and purpose  This study investigated clinical outcomes after direct endovascular thrombectomy (EVT) compared to bridging therapy (EVT with prior intravenous alteplase) in acute stroke within $4.5\mathrm{~h~}$ after onset.  

Methods  PubMed and Embase were searched for eligible randomized controlled trials. The primary outcome was the rates of neurological functional independence defined as modified Rankin scale score 0–2 at 90 days, whose non-inferiority margin was set at $-15\%,-10\%,-6.5\%,-5\%$ , and − $1.3\%$ for its risk difference (RD).  

Results  We included six studies enrolling 2334 participants. The crude cumulative rates of functional independence were $49.0\%$ with direct EVT vs $50.9\%$ with bridging therapy, without significant difference (Odd ratio $[\mathrm{OR}]=0.93$ , $95\%$ confidence interval [CI] 0.79–1.09) between two groups, where the pooled RD was $-2\%$ $95\%\mathrm{CI}-6$ to $2\%$ ) whose lower $95\%$ CI bound fell within non-inferiority margins of $-15\%,-10\%$ , $-6.5\%$ , but not − $5\%$ and $-1.3\%$ . Between the two groups, no significant difference was found in excellent function rate $30.2\%$ vs $30.6\%$ , $\mathrm{OR}=0.99$ , $95\%$ CI 0.82–1.18) with RD of $0\%$ $(95\%{\mathrm{CI}}-3$ to $4\%$ ), mortality rate ( $16.0\%$ vs $15.0\%$ , $\mathrm{OR}=1.08$ , $95\%$ CI 0.86–1.35) with RD of $1\%$ $(95\%\mathrm{CI}-2$ to $4\%$ ), and symptomatic intracranial hemorrhage rate ( $4.3\%$ vs $5.0\%$ , $\mathrm{OR}=0.86\$ , $95\%$ CI 0.58–1.27) with RD of $0\%$ $(95\%\mathrm{CI}-2$ to $1\%$ ). Conclusions  No statistical difference was found in functional and safety outcomes between direct EVT and bridging therapy groups in acute stroke within $4.5\mathrm{h}$ after symptom onset. EVT alone was non-inferior to bridging therapy for several, but no the more stringent, non-inferiority margins.  

Keywords  Stroke $\cdot$ Endovascular thrombectomy $^{\ast}$ Intravenous thrombolysis $\cdot$ Neurological functional independence $^{\ast}$ Noninferiority  

# Background and purpose  

It has not been settled whether bridging therapy, administration of intravenous alteplase before endovascular thrombectomy (EVT), is superior to direct EVT. There are several randomized clinical trials (RCTs) on the topic [1–6]. But the results are inconsistent. EVT alone is not inferior to bridging therapy by alteplase administered within $4.5\mathrm{~h~}$ after symptom onset [1, 3], but other trials do not show non-inferiority of direct EVT compared with bridging therapy [2, 4–6]. Some meta-analysis of RCTs have been conducted, intending to provide more conclusive evidence. One of them detects no differences in functional outcomes between direct EVT and bridging therapy groups in patients with an acute large vessel occlusion who are eligible for EVT and intravenous thrombolysis [7], which suggests further RCTs are needed to ascertain the results as uncertainty for most endpoints remains enormous. As this study only included three studies from Asian [7]. This would limit generalizability of the results. Besides limited number of patient sample enrolled in previous meta-analysis [8–12], the non-inferiority are not assessed in some studies [7, 10–12]. With more recently published results of RCTs [5, 6], we performed a systematic review and meta-analysis of completed RCTs to investigate the non-inferiority of direct EVT to bridging therapy for stroke patients of large artery occlusion who are eligible for both treatments and present within $4.5\mathrm{h}$ from symptom onset.  

# Methods  

The present systematic review and meta-analysis was conducted and reported according to Cochrane handbook [13] and the Preferred Reporting Items of Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses (PRISMA) statement [14]. Literature search, study selection, data extraction and quality assessment were performed by two independent authors. Conflicts were resolved by discussion and a third author was consulted if discussion cannot settle the conflicts. Our review protocol has not been published or registered. The authors declare that the relative data within the article and its online supplementary material were available on reasonable request.  

# Data sources and search strategy  

PubMed and Embase were searched for relevant RCTs published from inception to September 13, 2022. The search strategy includes the combination of keywords: ("endovascular thrombectomy" OR "mechanical thrombectomy") AND ("intravenous thrombolysis" OR "tissue plasminogen activator" OR "alteplase" OR "tenecteplase") AND ("ischemic stroke" OR "cerebral ischemia"), with "Clinical Trial" and "Randomized Controlled Trial" as filters, and the details were shown in the Data Supplement.  

# Eligibility criteria and study selection  

We included original RCTs that were published in English and (1) enrolled acute stroke patients with large vessel occlusion who were eligible to receive alteplase within $4.5\mathrm{h}$ after symptom onset and were randomized to direct EVT and bridging therapy, and (2) compared endpoints of direct EVT to those of bridging therapy. Conference abstracts, observational studies and RCTs not randomizing patients according to administration of intravenous thrombolysis prior to EVT were excluded. After automatically removing duplication using citation manager EndNote X8, the titles and abstracts were screened and the full tests were selected according to eligibility criteria.  

# Data extraction and endpoints  

The characteristics of the included trials were extracted, including study country, the names of trials, the number of patients randomized, patient age and sex, admission NIHSS and ASPECT, sites of artery occlusion, the time from randomization to groin puncture or from door to groin puncture, definition of symptomatic intracranial hemorrhage (sICH), and confounding factors that were adjusted in the original studies. We also extracted the number of patients in two treatment approaches in accordance with the following endpoints. For each endpoint, the adjusted odds ratio (OR) with its $95\%$ confidence interval (CI) was extracted. The primary endpoint was functional independence in terms of $\mathrm{mRS}0{-}2$ at 90 days. The secondary endpoints included (1) excellent function in terms of mRS 0–1 at 90 days, (2) all-cause mortality at 90 days, (3) sICH according to criteria of consensus, (4) successful reperfusion, and (5) other systemic and procedure related adverse complications.  

# Quality assessment and risk of bias  

The risk of bias was assessed according to Cochrane Handbook [13]. The items evaluated included each trial’s randomization process, deviations from intended intervention, missing outcome data, measurement of the outcome, selection of the reported results and other bias. For every domain of bias assessment, a score of unclear, low, or high risk of bias was assigned.  

# Data synthesis and statistical analysis  

Unadjusted OR with its $95\%$ CI was used as a measure of the effect of direct EVT vs bridging therapy on endpoints, which was in accordance with the intention-to-treat principle. Adjusted OR was calculated from data reported in the original trials. Risk difference with its $95\%$ CI was employed to assess non-inferiority of primary endpoints of functional independence, where a framework of five non-inferiority margins were specified: $-15\%,-10\%,-6.5\%,-5\%$ , and − $1.3\%$ [8]. All the data were pooled using random effect model. Heterogeneity across studies was assessed with Cochrane $Q$ test and $I^{2}$ statistic. No significant heterogeneity was considered when $P$ value of Cochrane $Q$ test $(P_{\mathrm{Cochrane}Q})$ $\mathrm{was}>0.10$ or $I^{2}<50\%$ . Sensitivity analysis was performed by sequentially removing one trial at a time to evaluate the influence of each individual trial. Publication bias was examined by Egger test and Begg test. Statistical analyses were conducted using Stata 12.  

# Results  

Six multicenter, open-labeled RCTs [1–6] were included (Figure S1 in the Data Supplement) and 2334 participants were enrolled. The characteristics of the included studies and critical baseline data of patients were summarized in the Table 1. All the six studies set the time window as $4.5\mathrm{h}$ after the symptom onset. SKIP used $0.6\mathrm{mg/kg}$ alteplase [2], DIRECT SAFE used $0.9\mathrm{mg/kg}$ alteplase in 120 patients $(83\%)$ and tenecteplase in 25 patients $(17\%)$ [5], the other four trials used $0.9\mathrm{mg/kg}$ alteplase [1, 3, 4, 6]. The quality of included studies was assessed with details in Table S1 in the Data Supplement. All the included trials was of high risk due to their open-label design for the domain of "randomization process" [1–6]. A high risk of bias was assigned for the domain of "Deviations from intended intervention" in DIRECT-MT [1] due to the difference in the percentages of patients allocated to direct EVT (17/327, $5.2\%$ ) and bridging therapy (31/329, $9.4\%$ ) groups in whom EVT was not performed. A high risk of bias was assigned for the domain of "other bias" in DEVT [3] and DIRECT SAFE [5] due to early termination of the trials, where possible risk for overestimation in the reported effect sizes was posed. In the sensitivity analysis, the re-evaluated unadjusted ORs of endpoints were consistent after omitting each individual study (Fig. S2 in the Data Supplement). No publication bias was revealed on the trials for analysis of unadjusted ORs of endpoints (Table S2 in the Data Supplement).

The meta-analyzed efficacy endpoints were shown in Figs. 1 and 2. The crude cumulative rates of functional independence were $49.0\%$ with direct EVT vs $50.9\%$ with bridging therapy, without significant difference between two groups $(\mathrm{OR}=0.93\$ , $95\%$ CI $0.79\mathrm{-}1.09$ , $P=0.369$ ; $I^{2}{=}0\%$ , $P_{\mathrm{Cochrane}\ Q}=0.417)$ . The pooled risk difference of functional independence rate $\mathrm{was}-2\%$ $(95\%\mathrm{CI}-6$ to $2\%$ ) between direct EVT and bridging therapy. The lower $95\%$ CI bound of $-6\%$ fell within the non-inferiority margins of $-15\%,-10\%,-6.5\%$ , but crossed the more stringent − $5\%$ and the most stringent non-inferiority margin of $-1.3\%$ . The excellent function rate was similar in groups of direct EVT vs bridging therapy (crude cumulative rates $30.2\%$ vs $30.6\%$ , $\mathrm{OR}=0.99$ , $95\%$ CI 0.82–1.18, $P{=}0.883$ ; $I^{2}{=}0\%$ , $P_{\mathrm{Cochrane}Q}=0.658)$ . The pooled risk difference of excellent function rate was $0\%$ $95\%\mathrm{CI}-3$ to $4\%$ ) between direct EVT and bridging therapy. The probability of successful reperfusion was significantly lower in direct EVT group than that in bridging therapy group (crude cumulative rates $80.3\%$ vs $84.6\%$ , $\mathrm{OR}=0.74$ , $95\%$ CI 0.60–0.93, $P{=}0.008$ ; $I^{2}{=}0\%$ , PCochrane $_{Q}{=}0.574;$ , with pooled risk difference of successful reperfusion rate of $-4\%$ $95\%$ CI − 7 to $-1\%$ ) between two groups.

Other safety endpoints were also reported, which had no difference in two groups in each study. DIRECT-MT [1] reported other adjudicated serious adverse events (large or malignant MCA infarction, pneumonia, aspiration or other, allergic reaction to contrast material, and other infarction in new territory at 5–7 days) and procedural complications (any procedure complication, vessel dissection, contrast extravasation, embolization into a new territory, femoral access complications). DEVT [3] reported similar serious adverse events (large or malignant middle cerebral artery stroke, acute respiratory failure and acute heart failure, hemicraniectomy) and higher possibility of procedure-associated complication in bridging therapy group than those in direct EVT group, which included clot migration, arterial perforation, arterial dissection, and distal occlusion present at procedure end and puncture access complications. MR CLEAN–NO IV [4] reported similar occurrence of embolization to new territory, femoral-artery false aneurysm, groin hematoma, and infarction in new territory on follow-up CT or MRI. SWIFT DIRECT [6] reported severe and moderate systemic bleeding, any serious adverse event, procedural complications including groin hematoma and femoral artery pseudo-aneurysm. SKIP [2] and DIRECT SAFE [5] did not reported relative adverse events.

# Discussion  

Our meta-analysis showed no significant difference in the probability of functional outcomes, mortality, and sICH between direct EVT and bridging therapy in stroke patients with acute large vessel occlusion who were eligible for both treatment regimens in the time window of $4.5\mathrm{h}$ . Direct EVT was associated with a less likelihood of successful reperfusion than bridging therapy. There were similar occurrences of serious adverse events between two arms and possibly higher procedure-associated complication in bridging therapy group than those in direct EVT group. EVT alone is non-inferiority to bridging therapy for several, but not the more stringent, non-inferiority margins in primary outcome of functional independence.  

Many studies have investigated whether direct EVT was non-inferior to bridging therapy. Our study had some important strength. First, it was a meta-analysis of RCTs, a noninferiority type of study to investigate treatment efficacy and adverse events. A study that pooled patient-level data from five RCTs (EVT vs standard medical care) showed a similar adjusted treatment effect of EVT after large-vessel ischemic stroke in terms of mRS at 90 days in 1090 patients with bridging therapy and 188 with direct EVT, where the two groups of patients were not randomized [16]. Subsequent meta-analysis of observational studies demonstrated that bridging therapy patients had better functional outcomes, lower mortality, and equal odds of sICH compared with direct EVT patients [17, 18], whose results are inconsistent to ours. However, these studies have crucial limitations that the patients with direct EVT were ineligible for alteplase, mostly due to delayed admission and coagulopathy, which would cause imbalance between two groups. Second, this study included more trials and patients than previous metaanalysis of completed RCTs on the topic [7–12]. Third, in our study, not only statistical difference of efficacy and safety between direct EVT and bridging therapy were compared, but non-inferiority was also analyzed. Our pooled risk difference of functional independence rate was $-2\%$ ( $95\%$ $\mathrm{CI}-6$ to $2\%$ ) between direct EVT and bridging therapy. This lower limit of $95\%\mathrm{CI}-6\%$ fulfil the non-inferiority margin of − $10\%$ in DEVT [3] and DIRECT-SAFE [5], $-12\%$ in SWIFT DIRECT [6] and our specified non-inferiority margins of $-15\%,-10\%,-6.5\%$ , but not meet the more stringent specified non-inferiority margin of $-5\%$ , and the most stringent margins of − $1.3\%$ . The odd ratio of functional independence was 0.93 $(95\%\mathrm{CI}0.79–1.09,)$ in our analysis. The lower limit of $95\%\mathrm{CI}0.79$ fell within the non-inferiority margin of 0.74 in SKIP [2], but not meet the non-inferiority margin of 0.8 in DIRECT-MT [1] and MR CLEAN–NO IV [4]. There were two aspects of great significance in these results. On one hand, it might be reasonable to skip intravenous thrombolysis and proceed with a direct EVT in some AIS patients from large artery occlusion within $4.5\mathrm{~h~}$ after symptom onset who present directly to hospitals capable of performing EVT according to individualized evaluation. On the other hand, it should be cautious to recommend direct EVT over bridging therapy considering the relatively wide non-inferiority margin the present result fulfil, not meeting the most stringent margins. Analysis stratified to patient features, such as age, ethnic, large vessel occlusion sites might provide more information and evidence to this clinical conundrum.

In middle cerebral artery occlusive patients, it was demonstrated that downstream microvascular thrombosis by alteplase is an early phenomenon initiated before recanalization and stroke outcome was improved by alteplasedependent maintenance of downstream perfusion through a fibrinogen-dependent platelet aggregation reduction [19]. This might be an explanation for the higher probability of successful recanalization in bridging therapy group, which is also shown in post hoc analysis of DIRECT-MT [20]. Bridging therapy may be a risk factor for periprocedural thrombus fragmentation [21], and the fragments could migrate distally. Larger emboli could cause symptomatic cerebral occlusion, lead to incomplete recanalization, and be associated with lower NIHSS scores at discharge [21]. The increased chance of thrombus migration and resolution by alteplase was associated with better functional outcome [22, 23]. Meanwhile, it is still argued that intravenous alteplase in addition to EVT was associated with an increase in the rate of ICH in patients with large artery occlusion treated within $4.5\mathrm{~h~}$ [24]. This might be the reason of equal probability of functional outcome between two groups in spite of higher probability of successful recanalization in bridging therapy patients.

to alteplase administration prior to EVT, the open-label design potentially cause some bias. Two studies were allocated high risk of bias due to early termination for the domain of “other bias”, which might ameliorate the strength of evidence. Second, DIRECT-MT [1] and MR CLEAN–NO IV [4] included stroke patients of $\mathrm{NIHSS}\geq2$ , DEVT did not set criteria for NIHSS, which means that there were some minor stroke in the cohorts. Even though the incidence of early neurological deterioration of presumed ischemic origin was about $12\%$ in patients with acute minor stroke and large vessel occlusion [25], no consensus reached whether to directly transfer minor stroke patients who had large vessel occlusion and were treated with intravenous thrombolysis for additional thrombectomy. A recent registry study suggests similar effectiveness of intravenous thrombolysis alone compared to EVT with or without IVT in minor stroke patients [26]. Third, some features of the patients in the included trials were different, such as admission NIHSS and ASPECTS, large artery occlusion sites. Nevertheless, baseline infarct size may not modify the effect of alteplase prior to EVT [27] with regard to favorable functional outcomes and adverse events, and there was no significant treatment-by-occlusion site interaction [28]. Fourth, some factors might affect clinical outcomes and were not concerned, among which blood pressure and its variance is the most important, although it is reported that blood pressure variability is not associated with functional outcome or radiographic intracranial hemorrhage [29]. Fifth, the definitions and criteria of sICH and recanalization were not unified. Sixth, the thrombolytic agent used is all alteplase except that there are $17\%$ (25/145) enrolled patients used tenecteplase in DIRECT-SAFE [5]. Treatment with tenecteplase resulted in a superior rate of early reperfusion compared with alteplase [30]. And it is showed that tenecteplase before EVT is associated with a higher incidence of reperfusion and better functional outcome than alteplase among AIS patients within $4.5\mathrm{h}$ after symptom onset [31]. Tenecteplase bridging might yield better results than alteplase bridging. Finally, the time window for treatment was within $4.5\mathrm{h}$ after onset, which limits the generalization of the results to those patients of delayed presentation or unknown time of onset. As the included trials mainly include patients with anterior circulation stroke, the results could not be extended to the patients with posterior circulation strokes. Considering all these limitations, our results that there was no significant difference in functional outcome, mortality and sICH between direct EVT and bridging therapy groups within $4.5\mathrm{~h~}$ after onset should be explained combined with the results of noninferiority analysis.  

In conclusion, our study provided some critical information on the clinical conundrum of direct EVT versus a bridging therapy. Although no statistical difference was found in functional and safety outcomes between direct EVT and bridging therapy in ASI patients of large artery occlusion within $4.5\mathrm{~h~}$ after symptom onset, EVT alone is non-inferiority to bridging therapy for several, but not the more stringent, non-inferiority margins in primary outcome of functional independence.
