# Effect of fetal lung maturation on the efficacy of acetaminophen for premature infants with patent ductus arteriosus  

Chunqiang Bai, MMa, Fanyue Meng, BMb, Haiying Wu, MMc, Wenying Wu, MMa,\*  

# Abstract  

The objective of this study was to evaluate the effect of maturing fetal lung on clinical efficacy of acetaminophen in the treatment of premature infants with patent ductus arteriosus (PDA). A total of 441 premature infants admitted to our hospital from May 2020 to May 2021 were recruited, including 152 premature infants receiving fetal lung maturation (13 cases of PDA closure with drug use and 2 cases failed) and 289 cases without maturing fetal lung (17 cases of PDA closure and 8 cases failed). Finally, a total of 30 cases were enrolled in this clinical trial. All infants were divided into groups A and B according to whether fetal lung maturation was adopted before delivery. In group A, 13 infants received fetal lung maturation, and 17 in group B did not undergo fetal lung maturation. Infants in both groups were orally given with acetaminophen. After 3-day treatment, the second course of treatment was given immediately if PDA was not closed. The PDA closure rate and patency rate of PDA at the end of 2 treatment courses were statistically compared between 2 groups. The feeding intolerance, upper gastrointestinal bleeding, renal failure, necrotizing enterocolitis, bronchopulmonary dysplasia, periventricular-intraventricular hemorrhage, the age at total enteral nutrition and the length of hospital stay were also compared between 2 groups. After the $1^{\mathrm{st}}$ and $2^{\mathrm{nd}}$ treatment courses, the PDA closure rate in group A was $84.61\%$ , significantly higher than $52.94\%$ in group B $(P<.05)$ , whereas there was no significant difference in the PDA patency rate between 2 groups $(P>.05)$ . No significant differences were observed regarding the feeding intolerance, renal failure, necrotizing enterocolitis, periventricular-intraventricular hemorrhage, bronchopulmonary dysplasia, the length of hospital stay and the age at total enteral nutrition between 2 groups (all $P>.05)$ . In addition, the incidence of upper gastrointestinal bleeding in group A was $7.69\%$ , slightly lower than $5.88\%$ in group B $(P>.05)$ . Compared with premature infants untreated with fetal lung maturation interventions before delivery, premature infants who receive fetal lung maturation interventions combined with acetaminophen for PDA are likely to obtain a higher PDA closure rate and a lower incidence rate of the upper gastrointestinal bleeding.  

Abbreviations: BPD $=$ bronchopulmonary dysplasia, ${\mathsf{NEC}}=$ necrotizing enterocolitis, PDA $=$ patent ductus arteriosus, $\mathsf{PNH}=$ periventricular-intraventricular hemorrhage.  

Keywords: acetaminophen, echocardiography, fetal lung maturation, patent ductus arteriosus, premature infant  

# 1. Introduction  

Patent ductus arteriosus (PDA) is a common congenital heart disease, which primarily occurs in premature infants.[1] If PDA were not closed timely and effectively, continuous leftto-right shunt is likely to occur, which might lead to multiple serious complications, such as, renal insufficiency, osteodysplasia, necrotizing enterocolitis (NEC), intraventricular hemorrhage, periventricular leukomalacia, congestive heart failure, pulmonary hemorrhage and bronchopulmonary dysplasia (BPD), etc. All these complications can affect the quality of life and even worsen the survival rate of premature infants.[2] Therefore, timely and effective management of PDA plays a significant role in preventing the incidence of these life-threatening complications. In this article, PDA premature infants received relevant interventions for promoting fetal lung maturation followed by oral administration of acetaminophen. The therapeutic effect and adverse reactions were observed and statistically compared between premature PDA infants treated with and without fetal lung maturation, aiming to assess the role of fetal lung maturation in the treatment of PAD in premature infants.

# 2. Materials and methods  

This study was approved by The Ethics Committee of Affiliated Hospital of Chengde Medical University. Participants have provided their written informed consents to participate in this study.  

# 2.1. Inclusion and exclusion criteria  

Inclusion criteria were as follows: Gestational age $<37$ weeks; Birth weight $\geq2500\mathrm{g};$ ; Any of the following events detected by color Doppler echocardiography at 48 to 96 hours after birth: diastolic aortic regurgitation; the ratio of left atrial diameter to aortic diameter (AO ring) was larger than 1.3; the diameter of ductus arteriosus was larger than $1.5\mathrm{mm}$ . Exclusion criteria were as follows: Those with severe infection; those suffering from chromosome diseases and complex heart malformations were excluded from the present clinical trial.  

# 2.2. Therapeutic regimen  

All enrolled premature infants in both 2 groups were orally given with acetaminophen oral solution at a dose of $15\mathrm{mg/kg}$ once every 6 hours for consecutive 3 days (Hanmi Pharm. Co., Ltd., Beijing, China; Batch No. H20093620, $100\mathrm{mL}$ ). Within 24 hours after the end of the $1^{\mathrm{st}}$ treatment course, color Doppler echocardiography was performed. If PDA were confirmed, the $2^{\mathrm{nd}}$ treatment course was initiated immediately as the same procedure as the $1^{\mathrm{st}}$ treatment course. Color Doppler echocardiography was carried out within 24 hours after the completion of the $2^{\mathrm{nd}}$ treatment course to determine whether the PDA was closed or not.  

# 2.3. Fetal lung maturation regimen  

Three regimens were adopted as follows. For the first regimen, intramuscular injection of betamethasone at a dose of $12\mathrm{mg}$ was delivered twice with a time interval of 24 hours. For the second regimen, intramuscular injection of dexamethasone at a dose of $6\mathrm{mg}$ was given for 4 times with a time interval of 12 hours. The total doses of betamethasone and dexamethasone were equally $24\mathrm{mg}.$ , respectively. For the third regimen, intramuscular injection of dexamethasone was given at a dose of $10\mathrm{mg}$ once daily for consecutive 3 days and the total dose was $30\mathrm{mg}$ per course of treatment.  

# 2.4. Primary and secondary indexes  

Primary indexes consisted of the PDA closure rate and patency rate of PDA after the first and second courses of treatment. Secondary indexes included the incidence of feeding intolerance, the upper gastrointestinal hemorrhage, periventricular-intraventricular hemorrhage (PIVH), renal failure, NEC and BPD, the age at total enteral nutrition (infant formula amount was $120\mathrm{{mL}/kg{\cdot}day)}$ ) and the length of hospital stay. These indexes were recorded and statistically compared between 2 groups.  

# 2.5. Statistical analysis  

SPSS 25.0 statistical software was used for all data analyses (SPSS Inc., Chicago, IL). The measurement data with normal distribution were expressed as mean $\pm$ standard deviation (SD) and statistically compared between different groups by independent sample $t$ test. The counting data were expressed as n $\left(\%\right)$ and compared between different groups by Fisher exact test. A $P$ value of $\bar{<}.05$ was considered as statistical significance.  

# 3. Results  

# 3.1. Baseline data  

A total of 441 premature infants who were admitted to our hospital from May 2020 to May 2021 were recruited in this clinical trial. Among them, 152 premature infants received interventions for promoting fetal lung maturation (13 cases of PDA were closed with drug use, 2 cases failed) and 289 premature infants were not treated with promoting fetal lung maturation (17 cases of PDA were closed with medication therapy, 8 cases failed). Thirty premature infants were divided into group A $({\mathrm{n}}=13)$ ) and B $(\mathrm{n}=17)$ ). In group A, relevant interventions were delivered to promote fetal lung maturation before delibery. Among them, 8 infants were male and 5 female, the mean gestational age was $32.56\pm1.99)$ weeks on average, the mean birth weight was measured as $(1790.85\pm370.52)$ g. Those in group B (17 cases) did not receive fetal lung maturation before delivery. Among them, 10 premature infants were male and 7 female, the mean gestational age was $(32.49\pm1.92)$ ) weeks, and the mean birth weight was ${\bar{1}}789.47\pm369.58)$ g. All baseline data were matched between 2 groups with no statistical significance (all $P>.05;$ ).  

# 3.2. Comparison of PDA closure rate between 2 groups  

As shown in Table 1, the PDA closure rate after the first course of treatment was calculated as $61.54\%$ (8/13) in group A, which was significantly higher compared with $11.76\%$ (2/17) in group B ( $\bar{X^{2}}=6.125$ , $P=.013_{.}$ ). In addition, the PDA closure rates after the second course of treatment also significantly differed between group A and B $84.61\%$ vs $52.94\%$ ; $X^{2}=3.325$ , $P=.043)$ ). In group A, the PDA patency rate in group A was recorded as $15.38\%$ (2/13), which did not significantly differ from $23.53\%$ (4/17) in group B $\textstyleX^{2}=0.305$ , $\bar{P_{=}}.580$ ).  

# 3.3. Comparison of adverse reactions between 2 groups  

Regarding the incidence of adverse reactions, the incidence of BPD in group A was $15.38\%$ (2/13), and $5.88\%$ (1/17) in group B $X^{2}=0.0\dot{6}0$ , $P=.805)$ ). In group A, the incidence of PIVH was calculated as $7.69\%$ (1/13), and 0 in group B, with no statistical significance between 2 groups $(\bar{X}^{2}=\bar{0}.018$ , $P=.891\$ ).  

The incidence of renal insufficiency did not significantly differ between 2 groups ( $7.69\%$ vs 0; $X^{2}=0.000$ , $P=1.00{\dot{0}})$ . In group A, the incidence of NEC was $15.38\%$ (2/13), and $17.65\%$ (3/17) in group B $X^{2}=0.108$ , $P=.741$ ). No statistical significance was noted in the incidence of feeding intolerance between groups A and B $(23.08\%$ vs $23.53\%$ ; $X^{2}=0.165$ , $P=.684,$ ). In group A, the incidence of upper gastrointestinal hemorrhage was recorded as $7.69\%$ (1/13), which did not significantly differ from $5.88\%$ (1/17) in group B $X^{2}=4.903$ , $P=.046^{'}$ ), as illustrated in Table 2.  

# 3.4. Comparison of age at total enteral nutrition and length of hospital stay  

As demonstrated in Table  3, the mean age at total enteral nutrition in group A was recorded as $(25.\bar{7}7\pm14.17)$ d, and $25.86\pm22.{\dot{3}}7_{.}$ ) d in group B, with no statistical significance between 2 groups $t=0.001$ , $P=.999\$ ). In group A, the length of hospital stay was $^{\prime}45.08\pm14.94)$ d, which did not significantly differ from $39.00\pm24.51)$ in group B $\overset{\prime}{t}=0.78\bar{7}$ , $P=.438$ ).  

# 4. Discussion  

The ductus arteriosus (PA) is a muscular artery which connects 2 elastic arteries with different forces of resistance. PA is a normal fetal structure that only becomes pathologically abnormal if it remains patent after birth. Different clinical impacts can be observed as partial neonates may be asymptomatic or the infants may be symptomatic and present as early as the first days of life. Prematurity increases the incidence of persistent ductal patency, approximately $30\%$ of preterm infants. In premature neonates, PDA is associated with significant morbidity and mortality.[1,2] In previous study, premature infants presenting with intracranial hemorrhage were recruited. Logistic regression analysis shows that mechanical ventilation, NEC and PDA are the independent risk factors for intracranial hemorrhage of premature infants.[3,4] Another study[5] has demonstrated that premature infants with cerebellar hemorrhage have a higher incidence rate of PDA compared with their counterparts manifested as intraventricular hemorrhage.  

In recent years, the timing of PDA treatment remains controversial. presymptomatic treatment, preventive treatment and symptomatic treatment has been adopted by clinicians. In the present study, symptomatic treatment was adopted for 2 conditions including the hemodynamic changes that are clearly defined by the color Doppler ultrasound of the heart as well as the clinical symptoms caused by blood shunt through PD, which are consistent with the diagnostic criteria of PDA. Notably, premature infants with smaller gestational age and lower birth weight are inclined to a higher risk of PDA. In addition, the immature organs, such as heart and lung, are more likely to affect the hemodynamic changes. Therefore, premature infants were enrolled in the present clinical trial.  

A recent study[6] confirms that acetaminophen can promote the closure of PDA in neonates. Another report[7] emphasizes that acetaminophen can be used as an alternative drug for PDA closure. If ibuprofen is ineffective, or its contraindications are taken into account, then acetaminophen may be an alternative to PDA closure. Some scholars[8] successfully applied acetaminophen to treat 5 PDA infants who achieved unsatisfactory efficacy or developed contraindications after use of ibuprofen. As a result, the arterial catheter was closed. Subsequent studies[9] have confirmed that oral intake of acetaminophen after promoting fetal lung maturation yields higher clinical efficacy compared with their counterparts without fetal lung maturation. Previous studies[9] have found that acetaminophen is more efficacious than ibuprofen in the management of PDA closure, which can lower the incidence of upper gastrointestinal bleeding without sacrificing the risk of other adverse events, such as BPD and PIVH, etc. The findings in the present study are consistent with previous studies.[10–16] Oral intake of acetaminophen can increase the PDA closure in the first and second courses of treatment after promoting fetal lung maturation compared with their counterparts without promoting fetal lung maturation, and the PDA patency rate and safety are basically identical between 2 therapeutic procedures. Acetaminophen can directly act on the peroxidase site (prostaglandin synthase) and inhibit the synthesis of prostaglandins. Hence, it yields high efficacy in the treatment of PDA.  

Moreover, no statistical significance was observed in the incidence of upper gastrointestinal bleeding in premature infants between 2 groups. Specifically, premature infants in group A suffered from a relatively small amount of upper gastrointestinal bleeding, manifested with coffee-like substances in the gastric fluid, which basically exerted no impact on the continuation of drug treatment. However, in group B, premature infants experienced a large quantity of the upper gastrointestinal bleeding, which should be treated immediately. After at least 12-hour after stopping the bleeding, medication therapy can be resumed. However, statistical analysis cannot be performed due to the small sample size of bleeding. In addition, no significant difference can be observed in terms of the age at total enteral nutrition or the length of hospital stay between 2 groups, suggesting that promoting fetal lung maturation followed by oral intake of acetaminophen yield higher clinical efficacy in premature infants with PDA without increasing the risk of severe complications, such as upper gastrointestinal bleeding.

# 5. Conclusion  

Taken together, oral application of acetaminophen after promoting fetal lung maturation yields higher PDA closure rate in the treatment of PDA premature infants compared with their counterparts without fetal lung maturation interventions. In addition, 2 procedures has equivalent safety regarding the risk of feeding intolerance, upper gastrointestinal bleeding, renal failure, NEC, BPD, PIVH, the age at total enteral nutrition and the length of hospital stay. Consequently, oral intake of acetaminophen after fetal lung maturation is worthy of widespread application in clinical practice.
