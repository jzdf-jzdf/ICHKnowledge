# ABSTRACT  

Keywords: Mouse nerve growth factor Intracerebral hemorrhage Modified Rankin scale score Randomized clinical trial  

Background: ew drugs were confirmed to be effective in the treatments of neurological dysfunction caused by acute intracerebral hemorrhage (ICH). The present prospective clinical trial aims to evaluate the effect of mouse nerve growth factor (mNGF) on neurological function in patients with acute ICH.   
Methods: 60 patients with acute spontaneous ICH were randomized to receive mNGF (mNGF group) and citicoline (control group) for 4 weeks within $^{24-72\mathrm{h}}$ after onset, respectively. The primary outcome was difference in the neurological functional outcome at 3 months by the modified Rankin Scale score (mRS). The secondary outcomes were the changes in hematoma volume at 4 weeks and 3 months.   
Results: There were 55 patients receiving treatment (29 patients in the mNGF group, 26 patients in the control group). Among the patients, 46 patients finished the trial at 3 months; the odds of a shift towards death or dependence $\mathrm{(mRS~>~3)}$ at 3 months in the mNGF group were lower than that in the control group with adjustment for age, sex, NIHSS at admission, and hematoma volume at admission (adjusted OR, 0.185; $95\%\mathrm{CI}$ , $0.059\substack{-0.582}$ ; $P=0.0039_{.}$ ). The hematoma was gradually reduced in all 46 patients and absorbed after nonsurgical treatment at 3 months. There was no significant difference in hematoma volume between the two groups. No serious adverse event was found.  

Conclusions: The administration of mNGF and citicoline was well-tolerated in patients with acute ICH. mNGF was associated with improved neurological function and less disability in patients with ICH. Therefore, the quality of life of patients with ICH may be improved by mNGF.  

Trial registration: The trial is registered with the Chinese Clinical Trial Registry, number ChiCTR1800020258.  

# 1. Introduction  

Spontaneous intracerebral hemorrhage (ICH) remains the most deadly form of stroke throughout the world [1,2]. Most of the patients would suffer various degrees of neurological dysfunction, even though they survived; this would cause a huge burden to the society and families and reduce their quality of life seriously. Worldwide, the burden of hemorrhagic stroke significantly increased between 1990 and 2013, especially in developing countries [3]. Prevention and treatment of possibly modifiable factors such as hematoma enlargement, peri-hemorrhagic edema development, acute hydrocephalus, and elevated intracranial pressure (ICP) are focused by Acute ICH management to avoid secondary brain injury [4]. Overly pessimistic prognostication in ICH may deny meaningful recovery achieved by specialized neurocritical care. Several randomized and large-sized observational studies have suggested more evidence on how to treat acute ICH patients, especially on intensive blood pressure lowering. However, the one breakthrough intervention of drugs to improve clinical outcomes is still missing [4].  

Cytotoxic edema is a prominent pathological characteristic after the onset of ICH and causes mass death of nerve cells, serious damage to neuronal networks, and the impairment of sensation, movement, or cognition. How to optimally rescue the nerve cells and restore the surviving neuronal networks is the key to improve functional recovery because the dead nerve cells are not renewable. Nerve growth factor (NGF) is a neurotrophic supporting the survival and growth of neurons [5]. The NGF can be combined with the Trk-A receptor on effector neurons to induce the directional growth of nerve fibers in order to control the number and differentiation of survived neurons and promote the repair of damaged neurons. Possible treatment of NGF for neurological deficits following hypoxic-ischemic brain injuries [6,7] or traumatic brain injuries [8,9] has been suggested in several studies. Patients treated with NGF showed a significant improvement in motor and cognitive functions, which might be attributed to the reduction of neuron loss and reorganization of neuronal connection. The neuroprotective role of NGF has also been explored in many other diseases [10]. However, the challenge is that NGF is unable to cross the blood–brain barrier (BBB) when systemically administered [11]. The poor permeability of the BBB and the invasiveness of intraventricular administration are the major obstacles to the development of NGF-based clinical trials. Currently, pieces of evidence from multiple studies indicate that there is a long-term (3–4 weeks) BBB hyperpermeability following a stroke [12]. It is enlightened that BBB dysfunction after intracerebral hemorrhage may facilitate NGF to enter the central nervous system; thus, it could be employed to treat neurological impairments caused by ICH.  

Mouse NGF (mNGF) is extracted from male mouse submaxillary glands and has been approved for the treatment of nerve injury for more than ten years in China. Before mNGF's approval, the phase 1 trial was carried out under the supervision of Chinese drug regulatory authority and the data had been submitted for drug approval. In phase 1 trial, mNGF from 10 to $80\upmu{}\mathrm{g}$ was tested in 24 healthy volunteers. mNGF treatment in all five dose groups were well tolerated. According to this result, the dose of mNGF used in current study can be considered safe and well tolerated. Citicoline is a drug that can improve brain metabolism; it is routinely used to treat the sequelae of the nervous system caused by craniocerebral injuries or cerebrovascular accidents [13,14]. Therefore, the administration of citicoline is taken as the standard of care in this study; then, the efficacy of mNGF over this standard of care is investigated.  

# 2. Methods  

This is a multicenter, prospective, randomized, and controlled trial to determine the efficacy of mNGF on treating neurological dysfunction caused by intracerebral hemorrhage with the standard of care. All the data were collected from four different tertiary hospitals (Tianjin Medical University General Hospital, Tianjin Huanhu Hospital, 983 Hospital of the Joint Logistics Support Force of the Chinese People's Liberation Army, Wuqing Chinese Medicine Hospital Affiliated to Tianjin University of Traditional Chinese Medicine), all of which had the necessary medical experience and equipment and received more than $200\mathrm{ICH}$ patients one year. This study was approved by the Tianjin Medical University General Hospital Ethics Committee and registered with China clinical trials registry (ID: ChiCTR1800020258). Written informed consent was obtained from all patients.  

# 2.1. Study design  

Because this is the first study to investigate the effect of mNGF on ICH, we did not have any basic data to calculate the sample size. With the consideration of the economic factor, we planned to recruit 60 patients with acute intracerebral hemorrhage to meet the need of preliminary experiment, who would receive either citicoline or citicoline and mNGF within $24-72\mathrm{~h~}$ after onset. The criteria for the patients in this study were acute basal ganglia or thalamus hemorrhage confirmed by computed tomography (CT) with a hematoma volume of fewer than $40~\mathrm{ml}$ . Patients were eligible if they are 18 to 75 years old; they would be with a Glasgow Coma Scale (GCS) score of 9 or more and with the systolic blood pressure of $140~\mathrm{mm}~\mathrm{Hg}$ or more when symptom onset. The cortical ICH were excluded due to few patients had neurological function decline at 3 months, which was the primary outcome of this trial. Patients with an extensive hematoma over than $40\mathrm{ml}$ or other intracerebral hemorrhagic sites with a structural cerebral cause or with subarachnoid or intraventricular hemorrhage, undergo clot evacuation procedure, obvious hepatic or renal dysfunction, severe systemic diseases, disturbances of blood coagulation, and mental disorder or neural impairment induced by other existing neuropathy previously were excluded in this study. Other neurotrophic drugs should not be used over the study period.  

Qualified participants were randomly allocated to the mNGF or control group at a ratio of 1:1. The randomization process was performed by an interactive web response system (IWRS), which was powered by an independent statistician from Nanjing Medical University, China. This study is open label. The participants who were assigned to the mNGF group would receive an intramuscular injection of mNGF ${\bf\nabla}^{\prime}18upmu{\bfg}/{\bfd})$ and intravenous $\left(0.25~{\ g}/{\mathrm{d}}\right)$ citicoline treatment for 4 weeks. The standard of care was based on the guidelines for the management of spontaneous intracerebral hemorrhage from the American heart association/American stroke association [1]. Accordingly, in addition to mNGF and/or citicoline treatment, appropriate symptomatic treatments such as necessary dehydration treatment to reduce intracranial pressure, adjusting systolic blood pressure to lower than $140\mathrm{mm}\mathrm{Hg}.$ , and nutritional therapy would be provided to patients.  

# 2.2. Neuroimaging and neurological function assessments  

Demographic and clinical characteristics were recorded at the time of enrollment. The severity of the stroke was assessed with the use of the Glasgow Coma Scale (GCS) and National Institutes of Health Stroke Scale (NIHSS, on which scores range from 0 to 42; higher scores indicate more severe neurologic deficits) at baseline. The CT scan before the initiation of treatment was recorded as the baseline. The area of the hematoma was delineated by manual analysis; then, hematoma volume was estimated using the Tada formula according to the previous study [15].  

# 2.3. Outcomes  

The primary outcome was difference in the neurological functional outcome at 3 months by the modified Rankin Scale score (mRS). The secondary outcomes were the changes in hematoma volume at 4 weeks and 3 months.  

Adverse events were systematically reported up to 3 months after randomization. Follow-up included in-person clinical evaluation of head CT, primary and secondary evaluation, and adverse events at 4 weeks and 3 months. Safety outcomes were neurologic deterioration and laboratory examination including complete blood count, transaminase, and creatine kinase.  

# 2.4. Statistical analyses  

The data were analyzed independently of the study investigators. The sample size was set as 30 per group because this study is a principle of concept study. The trial data were analyzed according to the modified intention-to-treat principle and based on the full analysis set (FAS) and the per protocol set (PPS). The patients who took the drug at least once and underwent the primary evaluation at baseline were included in the FAS. The missing data at 3 months of treatment in the FAS were replaced according to the last observation carried forward (LOCF) principle. Patients who finished the 3 months follow-up were included in the PPS. The primary outcome was the shift of each category in the entire range of the mRS, which analyzed by ordinal logistic regression with adjustment for age, sex, NIHSS at admission, and hematoma volume at admission. Then we did sensitivity analysis by comparing the results of primary outcome in FAS and PPS, verifying whether the results would be influenced by the missing data. This can ensure the accuracy of the results of primary outcome.

# 3. Results  

Sixty patients were enrolled and randomized to mNGF group $(n~=~30)$ ) and control group $\mathbf{\Omega}(\mathbf{n}=30)$ ). After randomization, four patients in the control group and one patient in the mNGF group immediately withdrew from the trial. They did not take any drugs for the trial. 46 patients completed the studies at 3 months (Fig. 1).  

# 3.1. Patients' characteristics  

In Table 1, the following characteristics of the patients were summarized, including age, gender, and NIHSS score at admission. The mNGF group had 22 males and 7 females, with the average age of $55.6~\pm~10.2$ years; the control group had 19 males and 7 females, with the average age of $54.7~\pm~8.8$ years. No statistical difference in age and sex was found between the two groups. The proportion of patients who had concurrent disease was similar. Moreover, no significant difference in GCS scores and NIHSS scores at admission was found.

# 4. Discussion  

Nowadays, patients suffering from stroke have become a huge burden on society and family in the whole world [16]. Great guidance and value for clinical treatment have been provided by some important researches [4]; however, it is frustrating that there are still few drugs improving neurological dysfunction. In the present study, the effect of mNGF on neurological function recovery in patients with acute intracerebral hemorrhage was investigated. Three months after onset, the treatment with mNGF for 4 weeks significantly improved neurological functions. That suggested the promising effects of mNGF for patients with acute intracerebral hemorrhage.

Intracranial hemorrhage could induce secondary brain injury, which is the main reason affecting the curative effect of brain injury [17]. The clinical manifestations and treatment options of patients with ICH vary according to the site of bleeding and the amount of hematoma. Generally, conservative treatment is feasible in the case where the hematoma volume is less than $40~\mathrm{ml}$ ; the major location of microbleeds are in the basal ganglia region. Consequently, only the patients with mild-to-moderate acute ICH in the supratentorial basal ganglia region or thalamus with a hematoma volume of $20{-}40~\mathrm{ml}$ diagnosed by a head CT examination were included in this research.  

In terms of evaluation of curative effects, the functional rating scale in the different disease periods of cerebral stroke was selected as the main curative effect index for the evaluation of neural functional recovery; the brain hematoma absorption was regarded as the secondary curative effect observation index, which are reliability evaluation indexes for this disease state and treatment state. Combined with these observations, it was demonstrated that mNGF had an excellent effect in promoting neural functional recovery of patients with ICH; besides, the functional behavior of the patients was continuously improved after 8 weeks of mNGF treatment.  

NGF is the first member of neurotropic factors discovered; it promotes neural development, enhances neural plasticity, and accelerates nerve injury repair in the nervous system [18,19]. It was reported that NGF could achieve neuroprotective effect by inhibiting $\mathrm{Ca}^{2+}$ influx, glutamate toxicity [20], and apoptosis of corticospinal tract motor neurons within $48\mathrm{~h~}$ from the occurrence of stroke [21] due to the presence of non-apoptotic neurons around the hemorrhage area; it could also mediate inflammatory reactions within 7–14 days after stroke and promote macrophages to devour dying cells and glial scar, resulting in creating conditions for nerve regeneration [22]. It was suggested that the damage or degeneration of the nervous system would be repaired by mNGF [23]. These researches are consistent with our study results.  

Despite the lack of sufficient evidence, neurotrophic drugs are the routine prescription for stroke treatment in China. In this study, a few patients quit or shed due to the knowledge of the control group using citicoline only. The positive results of several previous small clinical studies have facilitated two recent large randomized multicenter trials involving the clinical effects of citicoline with traumatic brain injury [24] and acute stroke [25]; however, both of their outcomes were surprising negative. Considering that citicoline was undoubted used as a kind of neurotrophic drugs in China for many years, it was selected as a control rather than blank control.  

Over the past decades, mNGF has been widely used in neural repairs, with high safety in humans [18,26,27]. In this study, the red blood cell (RBC), white blood cell (WBC), hemoglobin (HGB), AST, ALT, BUN, creatinine, and urine protein of the participating patients were within the normal scope during the treatment process, indicating that NGF had little adverse effect on bone marrow and hepatic-renal function. The mild adverse events (such as pain at the injection site) were only reported; they were not required for symptomatic treatment, with spontaneous remission.  

# 5. Limitations  

The study has several limitations. First, the trial only enrolled Chinese patients in China. Whether mNGF had the same effect in other races/ethnicities or countries remains to be investigated. Second, the sample size of this trial was small. We acquired a preliminary result by this preliminary experiment, the more reliable results should be further investigated by the large multi-center randomized controlled trail. Third, the number of patients who were recruited from participating centers was different, potentially resulting in regional biases. Forth, we were lack the data of ICH score and symptom-onset to randomization time in this trial. These two factors may be the influence factors for outcome. We will add these two factors into the next trial.  

# 6. Conclusion  

The present study indicated that mNGF was associated with improved neurological function and less disability in patients with ICH; meanwhile, no serious adverse events occurred. Therefore, mNGF can be a potential choice for improving functional outcomes in patients with acute ICH. However, the long-term effect and safety of NGF need to be further investigated by studies with a larger sample size and longer follow-up period.
