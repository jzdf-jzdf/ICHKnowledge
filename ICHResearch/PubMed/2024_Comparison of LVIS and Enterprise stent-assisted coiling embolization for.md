# Comparison of LVIS and Enterprise stent‑assisted coiling embolization for ruptured intracranial aneurysms: a propensity score‑matched cohort study

# Abstract  

The role of a low-profile visualized intraluminal support stent (LVIS) and Enterprise in the treatment of unruptured intracranial aneurysms is well established. Although previous studies have investigated one single type of stent for the treatment of ruptured intracranial aneurysms (RIA), the safety and efficacy between the two types of stents has not been fully explored. Herein we conducted a study to compare the outcomes of the two stents for treatment of RIA. This is a prospective registry database of aneurysmal subarachnoid hemorrhage (aSAH) patients admitted to a single institution between 2018 and 2021. We collected patient baseline information, secondary complications, follow-up angiographic data, long-term prognostic outcomes, and conducted propensity score matching (PSM) analysis with 1:1 ratio and a multivariable logistic regression to compare the outcomes of the two types of stents. A total of 231 patients with RIAs were included in this study, with 108 treated using the LVIS device and 123 treated using the Enterprise device. Before PSM analysis, only the incidence of poor prognosis after 12 months was higher in the Enterprise group comparing to the LVIS group ( $20\%$ vs. $10\%$ , $\mathrm{P}{=}0.049.$ ). After PSM analysis, there was a higher occurrence of delayed cerebral ischemia (DCI) in the Enterprise group compared to the LVIS group (odds ratio [OR] 3.95, $95\%$ confidence interval [CI] [1.20–13.01], $\mathrm{P}{=}0.024,$ ). However, no significant difference in prognosis was observed after PSM adjustment. Furthermore, subgroup analysis revealed that patients with female $\mathrm{P}{=}0.019,$ , hypertension $(\mathrm{P}=0.048)$ ), and anterior circulation aneurysms $\mathrm{P}{=}0.019,$ ) receiving the Enterprise device had a higher risk of DCI. The overall efficacy of LVIS and Enterprise in the treatment of RIA is comparable, while the incidence of DCI in the LVIS group is lower than that in the Enterprise group after PSM analysis. Registration number: NCT05738083 (https://clinicaltrials.gov/).

# Introduction  

Aneurysmal subarachnoid hemorrhage (aSAH) is an acute cerebrovascular disease associated with significant morbidity and mortality. Early diagnosis and treatment of ruptured intracranial aneurysms (RIA) are crucial in reducing mortality and disability [1]. With advancements in endovascular therapy technology and interventional materials, the utilization of this treatment method for RIA has gained widespread acceptance, gradually emerging as an alternative to surgical clipping [2]. Furthermore, the development of intravascular stent manufacturing technology has led to the widespread use of stent-assisted-coiling embolization in RIA treatment due to its advantages such as a high complete occlusion rate and low recurrence rate [3].  

Stents can be categorized as braided stents and laser-cut stents based on their manufacturing methods [4]. In addition to the production techniques, braided and engraved stents differ in cell size, metal coverage, radial support, compliance, adherence, and blood flow guidance [5, 6]. Among these variations, the low-profile visualized intraluminal support (LVIS) stent is commonly used for treating intracranial aneurysm while Enterprise is frequently employed as a laser-cut stent. Recent studies have examined the effectiveness of LVIS and enterprise stents in treating intracranial aneurysms, with findings indicating that LVIS stents exhibit superior occlusion rates compared to enterprise stents. However, no significant differences were observed between the two in terms of complications or clinical outcomes [7–9]. Some studies have controversially shown that in unruptured IA, braided stents may have a higher risk of ischemic complication [6]. Additionally, there have been studies that validate the safety and efficacy of utilizing other types of stent-assisted coiling techniques for intracranial aneurysms [10, 11]. However, a comparative analysis of the occlusion rate, secondary complications, and long-term clinical outcomes between LVIS and Enterprise in the treatment of RIA is lacking.  

In this study, we aim to investigate the baseline characteristics of the RIA cohort in groups treated with LVIS and Enterprise stents, and subsequently compare the outcomes of these two types of stents on the occlusion rate, secondary complications, and long-term functional outcomes using propensity score matching (PSM) analysis.  

# Materials and methods  

# Study design and participant enrollment  

The data utilized in this study were obtained from the registry database (NCT05738083), which is a single-center prospective cohort study. All patients included in the study were diagnosed with aSAH through computed tomography (CT), CT angiography, or digital subtraction angiography by the Department of Neurosurgery at the Second Affiliated Hospital of Nanchang University between October 2018 and December 2021. Inclusion criteria consisted of: (1) spontaneous subarachnoid hemorrhage of RIA, (2) treatment time within $^{72\mathrm{~h~}}$ from onset, (3) complete medical records and radiographic images available, (4) receiving LVIS or Enterprise assisted coil embolization, and (5) recorded one-year postoperative follow-up. The exclusion criteria include the following: (1) Angiography indicating moyamoya disease, cerebrovascular malformation, etc., (2) Previous surgical treatment before admission, (3) Concomitant diseases such as cancer that affect overall survival, (4) Death in the acute stage of aSAH, and (5) Receiving other intracranial stent or flow diverterassisted coil embolization. The patient selection process is illustrated in Fig. 1 and 2.  

# Baseline characteristics collection  

The physician workstation system was utilized to collect baseline characteristics, encompassing (1) demographics such as age and sex; (2) medical history including hypertension, diabetes, and coronary heart disease; (3) anticoagulant use and adverse lifestyle factors such as smoking and alcohol consumption; (4) admission assessments comprising the World Federation of Neurosurgical Societies (WFNS) [12], Hunt and Hess scales (H–H), and modified Fisher scales (mFS) [13]; and finally, (5) aneurysm characteristics involving RIA length and width, neck size, location, surgical methods employed, as well as stent types used.  

# Endovascular procedure  

Stent-assisted coil embolization was performed by a team of five neuro-interventionalists (GZX, MHY, BX, LS, PH). Following general anesthesia, systemic heparinization was achieved through intravenous injection of 3000 IU heparin. Digital subtraction angiography (DSA) with 3D reconstruction were utilized to measure the neck and size of the aneurysm, which helped in determining the appropriate stent size based on the dimensions of the parent artery. The aneurysm was densely packed using either LVIS (MicroVention Terumo, USA) or Enterprise (Cordis, USA) stents with the half-stent release technique.

# Outcome definition  

The primary outcome of our study was the one-year followup modified Rankin scale (mRS) [14]. Secondary outcomes included complications such as rebleeding, hydrocephalus, delayed cerebral ischemia (DCI), and intraprocedural thrombosis; neurologic impairment at discharge assessed by the Glasgow Coma Scale (GCS); angiographic outcome evaluated using the Raymond-Roy occlusion classification (RROC); and one-year mortality.  

Rebleeding was defined as an increase in intracerebral bleeding confirmed by CT or acute clinical deterioration suspected to be caused by rebleeding [15]. Hydrocephalus occurrence was determined based on consecutive CT images showing: (1) Evans index greater than 0.3, which is the ratio of the maximum width of the frontal angle of the lateral ventricle to the inner diameter of the skull at the same level; and (2) enlargement of frontal and temporal horns of lateral ventricles, third and fourth ventricles with periventricular interstitial edema [16]. Vasospasm, symptomatic as DCI, occurs within 4–14 days after aSAH and is characterized by focal neurological decline without other causes such as aphasia, apraxia, hemianopsia, or neglect, confirmed by CT scans. Additionally, it involves a Glasgow coma score decrease of at least 2 points for at least $^{1\textrm{h}}$ , and new cerebral infarction on a CT scan within 4–30 days postaSAH, without other attributable causes [17]. According to the guidelines, DCI is briefly defined as imaging evidence of cerebral ischemia or new clinical deterioration that cannot be explained by any other cause than DCI in the middle and late stages of aSAH [18], which was described in detail in our previous study. Poor prognosis is defined as an mRS score of 3 to 6. The RROC (I: complete occlusion; II: neck remnant; III: residual sac) [19] was used to assess the status of Aneurysm occlusion. Any increase in RROC grade was considered recurrence.  

# Statistical analysis  

The Kolmogorov–Smirnov test was applied to all data to test the normal distribution. Continuous variables were presented as $\mathrm{Mean}\pm\mathrm{SD}$ and analyzed using either a t-test or Mann–Whitney U test, while categorical variables were expressed as frequencies and percentages and assessed with the $\mathbf{x}^{2}$ test or Fisher's exact test. A multivariate logistic regression model was employed to adjust for clinical characteristics between the LVIS group and Enterprise group in order to compare their effects on clinical outcomes. PSM analysis was utilized to eliminate confounding factors related to patient characteristics between those two type stents, with patients matched 1:1 using a nearest neighbor algorithm with a caliper of 0.2 [20].  

Statistical analyses were conducted using SPSS software (Version 26.0, IBM SPSS, New York, USA), R software (Version 4.3.0), and GraphPad Prism 8.3.0 (GraphPad Software, San Diego, California, USA). A significance level of $\mathrm{p}<0.05$ was adopted.  

# Results  

# Baseline characteristics  

In this study, a total of 231 patients with RIA were included, consisting of 108 patients treated with LVIS and 123 patients treated with Enterprise. All relevant patient characteristics are summarized in Table 1. There were no statistically significant differences between the two groups regarding gender, disease history (diabetes, coronary heart disease, smoking, drinking, anticoagulant usage), WNFS grade, Hunt-Hess grade, mFS, aneurysm size, wide neck morphology or presence of multiple aneurysms. However, compared to the LVIS group, the Enterprise group exhibited older age $(61.1\pm10.2\$ vs $57.6\pm10.8$ ; $\mathrm{p}{=}0.013,$ , higher prevalence of hypertension (64 out of 123 $[53\%]$ vs 41 out of 108 $[38\%]$ ; $\mathtt{p}{=}0.044)$ , greater proportion of small-sized aneurysms (104 out of 123 $[85\%]$ vs 79 out of108 $[73\%]$ ; $\mathrm{p}{=}0.038\$ and more anterior communicating artery aneurysms (35 out of $123[29\%]$ vs 15 out of 108 $[14\%]$ ; $\mathbf{p}{=}00.003.$ ).  

# Secondary complications and outcomes  

As presented in Table 2, the incidences of DCI, hydrocephalus, and rebleeding were observed in 15 patients $(14\%)$ , five patients $(5\%)$ , and four patients $(4\%)$ in the LVIS group, respectively; compared to 21 patients $(17\%)$ , 11 patients $(9\%)$ , and four patients $(3\%)$ in the Enterprise group. Immediate angiographic results demonstrated that Raymond class I was achieved in 103 cases $(95\%)$ and Raymond class II was achieved in five cases $(5\%)$ within the LVIS group; whereas within the Enterprise group, it was achieved in 117 cases $(95\%)$ and six cases $(5\%)$ . Only a total of 59 patients underwent one-year follow-up angiography assessment—with 57 achieving RROC I status and two achieving RROC II status within the LVIS group; while within the Enterprise group, there were a total of 48 patients—with 47 achieving RROC I status and one achieving RROC II status. Additionally, both groups had approximately 34 patients experiencing mild neurological impairment at discharge. No significant differences were found between the two groups regarding complications, clinical outcomes at discharge, one-year follow-up mRS scores or immediate/follow-up angiographic outcomes. However, the proportion of poor prognosis was found to be higher in patients treated with Enterprise stents $(\mathtt{p}{=}0.049)$ . The adjusted logistic regression model revealed that neither LVIS nor Enterprise stent types were independent risk factors for prognosis (adjusted $\mathrm{p}{=}0.168\mathrm{,}$ , with an adjusted risk reduction of 1.77 (adjusted odds ratio [OR] 1.77, $95\%$ confidence interval [CI] [0.79–3.89]), as shown in Table S1. Besides, Table S2 showed the multivariable logistic analysis for delayed cerebral ischemia prediction. Figure S1 presents the distribution of patients with mRS scores ranging from 1 to 6 across each group.  

# Propensity score matched analysis between LVIS and Enterprise groups  

A total of 55 patients were selected for each group through PSM analysis between the LVIS and Enterprise groups (Table 1). The two groups did not differ significantly in terms of age, gender, medical history (diabetes, coronary heart disease, smoking, drinking, anticoagulant use), WFNS grade, Hunt-Hess grade, mFS, aneurysm size and location or presence of wide neck or multiple aneurysms. However, after PSM was performed there was a significant difference in the incidence of DCI between the LVIS and Enterprise groups; with the risk of DCI being 3.95 times higher in the LVIS group compared to that in the Enterprise group $(\mathtt{p}{=}0.024$ ; OR: 3.95; $95\%$ CI [1.20–13.01]), as illustrated in Table 3. Furthermore, the Kaplan–Meier curves representing occurrence time of DCI of two groups are shown in Figure S2. Poor prognosis, rebleeding, hydrocephalus, the immediate angiographic outcome, and GCS scores at discharge (13–15) still did not differ significantly between these two groups. Moreover, subgroup analysis revealed that female patients with a history of hypertension were at an elevated risk for DCI following Enterprise stent-assisted coil embolization of ruptured anterior circulation aneurysms (Fig. 3, Table S3).  

# Discussion  

In this study, we utilized a prospective registry database to determine the baseline characteristics of different patients with RIAs treated with two types of stents. The Enterprise group had a higher proportion of female patients, a greater prevalence of hypertension, and a higher incidence of anterior communicating artery aneurysms compared to the LVIS group. After propensity score matching, when comparing the occurrence rate of secondary complications and longterm outcomes between the LVIS and Enterprise groups, we observed that the risk of DCI occurrence was higher in the Enterprise group than in the LVIS group. Furthermore, subgroup analysis revealed that female patients with a history of hypertension were at increased risk for DCI following stent-assisted coil embolization using the Enterprise device.

Since the emergence of intravascular stents in the 1990s [21], numerous studies have consistently demonstrated that stent-assisted coiling is a safe and effective approach for treating ruptured or unruptured intracranial aneurysms [22–26]. However, this study represents the first to establish the safety and efficacy of LVIS-assisted coiling and Enterprise-assisted coiling in managing RIA through PSM method. In this investigation, we conducted a comparative analysis of baseline characteristics and follow-up outcomes between the LVIS group and Enterprise group, revealing that over $80\%$ achieved good prognosis. Notably, the LVIS group exhibited a remarkable good prognosis rate exceeding $90\%$ , although this difference did not reach statistical significance. After PSM, there was no significant change in the proportion of patients with good prognosis. Similarly, Zhang et al. reported favorable clinical outcomes at follow-up for $88\%$ and $92\%$ of patients who underwent coiling with LVIS and laser cut stents (Enterprise, Solitaire, Neuroform), respectively, for the treatment of ruptured tiny aneurysms [27]. However, the impact of stent type on aneurysm outcome has been a subject of controversy, particularly regarding Enterprise and LVIS. Lee et al. demonstrated superior results with LVIS compared to Enterprise in unruptured aneurysms smaller than $10\mathrm{mm}$ [28].

Thrombosis or rebleeding frequently occurs during stentassisted coiling for ruptured aneurysm treatment, and the use of antiplatelet agents after stenting may increase the risk of rebleeding. In our study, four patients in one group and one patient in the other experienced rebleeding or thrombosis. However, after PSM, only one case of each occurred in both groups: LVIS had a lower incidence of thrombosis despite its wider metal coverage and smaller cell size $(7.4\%-10.1\%)$ [29–31], while Enterprise had a similar incidence to LVIS $(3.4\%-10.2\%)$ [32, 33].  

Hydrocephalus and DCI are common complications following aSAH, which can exacerbate the poor prognosis of aSAH patients. Changes in CSF dynamics, obstruction of arachnoid granules, and ventricular system by blood components may be the mechanisms underlying hydrocephalus [34]. The mechanism of DCI is still unclear but may be related to cerebral vasospasm, inflammation, and microthrombosis [35]. In this study, there was no difference in the incidence of hydrocephalus between the LVIS group and Enterprise group; however, after PSM $(\mathtt{p}{=}0.024$ , OR 3.95, $95\%\mathrm{Cl}1.20\small{-}13.01]\$ ), there was a significant difference in DCI between the two groups with an increased risk for Enterprise group (13 out of 54 patients or $24\%$ ) compared to LVIS group (4 out of 56 patients or $7\%$ ). Similar results were observed in previous studies: Heller's R found that eight out of fifty patients $(16\%)$ in Enterprise group had delayed ischemic events all related to incomplete stent apposition [30] while Wang et al., reported higher incidence rates for in-stent stenosis among Enterprise group (7 out of  

232 cases or $3\%$ ) than LVIS group (none), also attributed to incomplete stent apposition [29]. The high incidence of DCI of enterprise stent may also be related to its deployment in vascular segments with large differences in vessel diameter, which is prone to stent migration [36]. Most of the patients in our study had anterior communicating artery aneurysms and posterior communicating artery aneurysms. Similarly, Lee et al. also found that Enterprise had a stent migration rate of $4.5\%$ (LVIS $0\%$ ) [37].  

The results of our subgroup analysis further support the hypothesis that incomplete placement of Enterprise may contribute to the occurrence of DCI. Notably, the anterior circulation subgroup, characterized by a higher prevalence of naturally curved arteries, exhibited a significantly elevated risk of DCI $\mathbf{\bar{p}}{=}0.019,$ , likely due to the greater challenge in achieving complete adherence with the carved stent. Moreover, patients with long-term hypertension demonstrated an increased risk of DCI $\mathbf{\tilde{p}}{=}0.048,$ attributed to heightened difficulty in achieving full adherence to Enterprise caused by intimal thickening.  

The strength of this study lies in the utilization of prospective in-hospital registry data to employ a pseudo-randomization method for comparing the clinical efficacy of LVIS and Enterprise stents in the treatment of RIA. Despite similar occlusive efficacy and long-term clinical prognosis between the two stent types, there appears to be an increased likelihood of developing DCI within the LVIS group. Furthermore, we have incorporated potential relevant clinical features that currently impact the prognosis of patients with RIA while eliminating other confounding factors to assess the influence of stent type on these outcomes by PSM method.  

Several limitations should be acknowledged in this study. Firstly, being a single-center clinical trial, there is a potential for patient selection bias during enrollment. Future larger randomized controlled trials and additional variables are necessary to strengthen our conclusions. Secondly, differences in surgical techniques and practices among surgeons may introduce certain errors in the evaluation of stent efficacy. The optimal approach would involve all types of stents for RIA treatment being performed by a single interventional team. Additionally, the baseline characteristics did not include the measurement of RIA diameter, thereby resulting in a dearth of direct evidence to establish the correlation between DCI occurrence and in-stent stenosis.  

# Conclusion  

The overall efficacy of LVIS and Enterprise in the treatment of RIA is comparable; after PSM analysis, the incidence of DCI in the LVIS group is lower than that in the Enterprise group. Therefore, we recommend prioritizing the use of LVIS if both LVIS and Enterprise are suitable for treating RIA.
