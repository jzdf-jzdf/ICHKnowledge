# Is endovascular treatment alone as effective and safe as that with preceding intravenous thrombolysis for acute ischemic stroke? A meta-analysis of randomized controlled trials

INTRODUCTION: This meta-analysis aimed to evaluate the safety and efficacy of direct endovascular therapy (EVT) and bridging therapy (EVT with preceding intravenous thrombolysis i.e. IVT), in acute anterior circulation, large vessel occlusion stroke.   
eViDeNce acQuisiTioN: following the PrisMa guidelines, a systematic literature review of the english language literature was conducted using PubMed, Cochrane CENTRAL, SCOPUS and ClinicalTrials.gov. Outcomes of interest were measured by the modified Rankin Scale (mRS), and included: no disability (mRS0), no significant disability despite some symptoms (mRS1), slight disability (mRS2), moderate disability (mRS3), moderately severe disability (mRS4), severe disability (mRS5), mortality (mRS6). Additionally, we inspected patients having excellent outcome, functional independence outcome, and poor outcome, along with successful reperfusion and intracranial hemorrhage. We calculated pooled risk ratios (RRs) and their corresponding $95\%$ confidence intervals (CI).   
EVIDENCE SYNTHESIS: A total of seven RCTs involving 2,392 patients were finally included. The chances of achieving successful reperfusion were significantly more with IVT+EVT as compared to EVT alone (RR: 0.97; $95\%$ CI: 0.94, 1.00; $\mathrm{P}{=}0.03$ ) $(\mathrm{I}^{2}{=}0\%)$ . There was no significant difference in the number of patients having outcomes ranging from mRS0 to mRS6, excellent outcome, functional independence, poor outcome or incidence of intracranial hemorrhage, who underwent either EVT alone or $\mathrm{IVT+EVT}$ .   
CONCLUSIONS: Additional trials are needed to determine if the absence of significant differences is due to insufficient sample size or if the combination therapy is truly not beneficial.

everal national guidelines recommend intravenous thrombolysis (IVT) followed by endovascular therapy (EVT) for patients with large vessel occlusion (LVO) in the anterior circulation, within 4.5 hours of symptom onset.1-5 A comprehensive algorithm regarding the guidelines for treatment of acute stroke, incorporating imaging and patient selection criteria for EVT, has been depicted by Bhatia K, et al. in their review.6 Numerous studies have reported better outcomes in patients treated with the  

IVT+EVT combination.7-10 This bridging therapy with IVT has been shown to facilitate prior initiation of treatment and successful reperfusion, which may obviate the need for endovascular procedures.11 Moreover, it can enhance recanalization by fibrin degradation, reduce downstream emboli, and improve collateral circulation.12, 13  

Conversely, this combination of IVT+EVT has numerous drawbacks as well, such as an increased risk of cerebral or systemic hemorrhage, thrombus fragmentation,

worsening distal perfusion, delayed EVT onset, and limitations in the use of antithrombotic and heparin therapy within 24 hours.14-16 As a result, several observational studies have portrayed IVT+EVT as providing no additional benefit.17-19 In addition, the efficacy of IVT alone is seen limited in acute ischemic stroke (AIS) patients with LVO (AIS-LVO), with only $6-30\%$ patients achieving successful recanalization.20 The comparatively higher rates for the same as well as the improved, overall survival rates in those undergoing EVT make the latter emerge as a more favorable treatment option.21-26 These findings have generated significant interest in the field of stroke research.  

While experts and clinical practice guidelines agree on the merits of utilization of the IVT+EVT combination versus EVT alone, doubts exist regarding whether this superiority is significant enough to advocate the use of bridging therapy for patients with AIS-LVO. In an effort to settle this debate, several randomized controlled trials (RCTs) have been conducted to ascertain the effectiveness and safety of one method over the other. The findings of four such trials were incorporated in a prior meta-analysis that was published in 2022,27 but its conclusions revealed no significant difference between the two arms. However, a succeeding meta-analysis comprising RCTs and observational studies yielded significant benefits of combination therapy, regarding mortality, successful recanalization and favorable functional outcomes.28 In contrast, recent RCTs depicting non-inferiority of EVT alone as compared to bridging therapy were published from that point forward, putting clinicians in a dilemma.  

Hence, this meta-analysis was conducted with an aim to provide updated information and report any additions to the information known previously regarding safety and efficacy.  

# Evidence acquisition  

# Data sources and search strategy  

The preferred reporting items for systematic reviews and meta-analyses (PRISMA) guidelines29 were followed for this systematic review and meta-analysis. A systematic search was conducted from inception till January, 2023 using the following databases: PubMed, Cochrane CENTRAL, and SCOPUS. In addition, we also searched ClinicalTrials.gov for any possible published or unpublished trials. The following keywords and mesh terms were used: “stroke,” “intravenous alteplase,” “thrombolysis,” “endovascular treatment,” “mechanical thrombectomy,” and “endovascular therapy.” The comprehensive search strategy is available in Supplementary Digital Material 1, Supplementary Table I. Three authors (A.A, F.S and S.A.S) independently conducted the literature searches, and resolved any disparity with discussion. The reference lists of the retrieved studies and previous meta-analyses were also searched for any relevant article.  

# Study selection and eligibility criteria  

All the retrieved articles were exported to EndNote Reference Library (version X9; Clarivate Analytics, Philadelphia, Pennsylvania), where duplicates were identified and removed. Three authors (A.A, F.S and S.A.S) independently screened and shortlisted the remaining studies based on their relevance to the inclusion criteria. After screening the titles and abstracts of the articles, the whole paper was read. Any disagreement was resolved with discussion. We included the studies in our analysis if they met the following, predetermined eligibility criteria: 1) they were RCTs; 2) they included patients with AIS due to anterior circulation LVO (AIS-LVO) with 4.5 hours as the beginning time window; and 3) they compared the efficacy and safety of EVT alone versus IVT+EVT. Reviews, case reports, conference papers, abstracts, animal experiments or non-English articles were excluded.  

# Data extraction and assessment of study quality  

The following data were extracted from the shortlisted articles: 1) study characteristics, such as first author, date of publication, sample size, location of study, inclusion criteria, and follow-up duration; 2) patient’s baseline characteristics (age, BMI, comorbidities and site of large vessel occlusion); and 3) outcomes related to the safety and efficacy, such as no disability (mRS 0), no significant disability despite some symptoms (mRS 1), slight disability (mRS 2), moderate disability (mRS 3), moderately severe disability (mRS 4), severe disability (mRS 5), mortality (mRS 6), successful reperfusion, intracranial hemorrhage, excellent outcome (mRS 0-1), functional independence (mRS 0-2), and poor outcome (mRS 3-5). The quality of the included RCTs were independently assessed by three reviewers (A.A, F.S, and S.A.S), using the Cochrane risk of bias tool for RCTs.30 Random sequence generation, allocation of treatment concealed from participants and investigators, participants randomized to treatment, blinding of outcome assessment, inadequate results, selective reporting, and other biases are the seven domains of risk of bias. For each study, all the domains were rated as “low risk,” “high risk,” or “unclear risk.”  

# Statistical analysis  

All the statistical analyses were conducted using RevMan (version 5.3; Copenhagen: Nordic Cochrane Centre, The Cochrane Collaboration, 2014). We used Risk Ratio (RR) with $95\%$ confidence interval (CI) as a meaningful effect measure to calculate the dichotomous data. The randomeffects model was used for analysis, and Higgins I2 static was used to evaluate heterogeneity. An I2 of $<50\%$ , 50- $75\%$ , and $>75\%$ was considered as low, moderate, and high heterogeneity, respectively. $30\mathrm{~A~P~}$ value of $<0.05$ was considered statistically significant. In the case of heterogeneity $>50\%$ , a sensitivity analysis was performed by the leave-one-out analysis method to identify the study causing heterogeneity.  

# Evidence synthesis  

# Study selection  

We initially identified 8141 articles, of which 3,028 duplicates were excluded. In the remaining 5113 articles, 4920 were excluded after screening titles and abstracts. We read 193 full articles and excluded 186. The final list of articles after complete screening included; DIRECT MT (Direct Intra Arterial Thrombectomy to Revascularize Acute Ischemic Stroke Patients With LVO Efficiently in Chinese Tertiary Hospitals),31 DEVT (Direct Endovascular Treatment Versus Standard Bridging Therapy for Patients With Acute Stroke With LVO in the Anterior Circulation),32 SKIP (Direct Mechanical Thrombectomy in Acute LVO Stroke),33 and MR CLEAN-NO IV (Multi-center Randomized Clinical Trial of Endovascular Treatment for Acute Ischemic Stroke in the Netherlands).34 These were the RCTs already taken into account in the previous meta-analysis. The three new RCTs that were taken into consideration were: DIRECT SAFE,35 SWIFT DIRECT Trial36 and [Thrombectomy Alone versus Bridging Therapy in Acute Ischemic Stroke: Preliminary Results of an Experimental Trial].37 The detailed screening process is presented in Figure 1.  

# Baseline characteristics and quality assessment  

There were a total of 2392 participants in these seven RCTs, out of which 1193 $(50\%)$ received EVT alone, while 1199 $(50\%)$ underwent IVT+EVT. The patients’ median age ranged from 64 to 76 years old. The summary of the baseline characteristics is provided in Supplementary Digital Material 2, Supplementary Table II.31-37 All seven studies present in our meta-analysis were of moderate quality, as shown in Figure 2.31-37

Results of meta-analysis  

# No disability after procedure (mRS0)  

All the studies in our meta-analysis reported data on patients having no disability after the procedure (mRS0). Pooled analysis revealed that there was no significant difference in the number of patients with no disability after the procedure, regardless of the therapy they received (RR: 0.86; $95\%$ CI: 0.70, 1.06; $\mathrm{P}{=}0.15$ ; Figure 3A)31-37 $(\mathrm{I}^{2}{=}0\%)$ .  

No significant disability despite some symptoms after procedure (mRS1)  

All included studies presented data on patients with no significant disability despite having some symptoms after the procedure (mRS1). Our meta-analysis showed no significant difference in such patients, regardless of the intervention they received (RR: 1.07; $95\%$ CI: 0.90, 1.28; $\mathrm{P}{=}0.43$ ; Figure 3B)31-37 $(\mathrm{I}^{2}{=}0\%)$ ).  

# Slight disability after procedure (mRS2)  

All seven studies included data on patients having slight disability after the procedure (mRS2). Combined analysis revealed that there was no significant difference in the count of patients with slight disability after the procedure who underwent either EVT alone or IVT+EVT (RR: 0.94; $95\%$ CI: 0.80, 1.10; $\mathrm{P}{=}0.45$ ; Figure 3C)31-37 $(\mathrm{I}^{2}{=}0\%)$ ).  

# Moderate disability after procedure (mRS3)  

Data on patients having moderate disability after the procedure (mRS3) was reported by all seven studies. Integrated analysis revealed that EVT alone or IVT+EVT had no significant difference in the number of patients with moderate disability after the procedure (RR: 1.13; $95\%$ CI: 0.93, 1.38; $\mathrm{P}{=}0.21$ ; Figure 3D)31-37 $(\mathrm{I}^{2=}0\%)$ .  

# Moderately severe disability after procedure (mRS4)  

Metrics related to patients having moderately severe disability after undergoing the procedure were reported by all seven studies. Analyzing the combined data revealed that the number of patients with moderately severe disability after undergoing EVT alone or IVT+EVT was not significantly different (RR: 0.97; $95\%$ CI: 0.75, 1.26; $\mathrm{P}{=}0.81$ ; Figure 3E)31-37 $(\mathrm{I}^{2}{=}0\%)$ ).  

# Severe disability after procedure (mRS5)  

Patients who incurred severe disability after the procedure were presented as outcomes in all seven studies. Metaanalysis of all articles showed that despite undergoing either EVT alone or $\mathrm{IVT+EVT}$ there was no significant difference in the number of patients with severe disability after the procedure (RR: $0.9795\%$ CI: 0.76, 1.24; $\mathrm{P}{=}0.82$ ; Figure 3F)31-37 $(\mathrm{I}{}^{2}{=}0\%)$ .  

# Mortality (mRS6)  

Patient mortality was recorded by all seven studies. Our meta-analysis concluded that there was no significant difference in patient mortality regardless of the procedure they underwent (RR: 1.06; $95\%$ CI: 0.88, 1.28; $\mathrm{P}{=}0.56$ ; Figure 3G)31-37 $(\mathrm{I}{=}0\%)$ .  

# Excellent outcome, functional independence outcome, and poor outcome  

All seven RCTs included in this meta-analysis reported the data of excellent outcome (mRS0-1), functional independence outcome (mRS0-2), and poor outcome (mRS3-5), respectively, in the EVT alone and $\mathrm{IVT+EVT}$ groups. The pooled RR were (RR: 0.96; $95\%$ CI: 0.86, 1.08; $\mathrm{P}{=}0.54$ ; Figure 4A)31-37 $(\mathrm{I}^{2}{=}0\%)$ for excellent outcome, (RR: 0.96; $95\%$ CI: 0.89, 1.03; $\mathrm{P}{=}0.27$ ; Figure 4B)31-37 $(\mathrm{I}^{2}{=}0\%)$ for the functional independence outcome and (RR: 1.04; $95\%$ CI: 0.96, 1.12; $\mathrm{P}{=}0.36$ ; Figure $4\mathrm{C})^{31-37}$ $(\mathrm{I}^{2}{=}0\%)$ ) for the poor outcome.  

# Successful reperfusion  

Successful reperfusion of the vessels was evaluated by all included studies. Evaluation of the integrated data revealed that successful reperfusion was significantly more likely to be achieved in patients who had undergone $\mathrm{IVT+EVT}$ as compared to patients who were given EVT alone (RR: 0.97; $95\%$ CI: 0.94, 1.00; $\mathrm{P}{=}0.03$ ; Figure 5A)31-37 $(\mathrm{I}^{2}{=}0\%)$ .  

# Intracranial hemorrhage  

Incidence of intracranial hemorrhage was presented in all included articles. Analysis of data from all articles showed that there was no significant difference in events of intracranial hemorrhage in patients undergoing EVT alone or $\mathrm{IVT+EVT}$ (RR: 0.78; $95\%$ CI: 0.48, 1.24; $\mathrm{P}{=}0.29$ ; Figure 5B)31-37 $(\mathrm{I}^{2}{=}91\%)$ ). Sensitivity analysis was performed due to high heterogeneity which brought it down to an acceptable level but the difference remained insignificant (RR: 0.92; $95\%$ CI: 0.84, 1.14; $\mathrm{P}{=}0.44$ ; Figure 5C)31-37 $12\%$ .

However, a significant difference was found in successful reperfusion in favor of combination therapy – a correlation un-established in the previous meta-analysis of RCTs.27  

Importantly, The SWIFT-DIRECT trial conducted by Fischer U et al.36 represents a pivotal study in our metaanalysis, as it was the only trial to demonstrate a significant reperfusion rate in patients who underwent combination therapy, as opposed to EVT alone. Despite pre-interventional reperfusion associated with alteplase occurring infrequently, patients assigned to the IVT+EVT combination had higher final post-interventional reperfusion rates.36 Fischer U et al. attributed this finding, along with the overall high rates of good outcome, to their conservative selection of ideal thrombectomy candidates, frequent use of flow-arrest devices, and the high standard of care provided by the participating centers.36 The gravity of this effect appears to be clinically relevant, as successful reperfusion is one of the most crucial determinants of functional outcome38 – evidenced by the fact that fewer than $10\%$ patients without successful reperfusion reached functional independence in the same trial.36  

The seven RCTs evaluated in this meta-analysis rendered different conclusions regarding the outcome of functional independence, between the two patient arms.31-37 DIRECT MT,31 DEVT,32 SKIP33 and the experimental trial by Ann NH, et al.37 all proved non-inferiority of EVT alone versus the IVT+EVT combination, whereas DIRECT-SAFE35 and SWIFT DIRECT36 failed to demonstrate non-inferiority. CLEAN-NO $\mathrm{IV}^{34}$ showed EVT alone was neither superior nor noninferior to bridging therapy. Several plausible reasons may explain these inconsistencies, as elaborated below.  

Foremost, the way in which the endovascular treatment was conducted was different among the studies. Mitchell PJ et al.35 utilized the Trevo device as the primary intervention while either of the second-generation stent retrievers, suction tubes, or a combination of the two was used in the study conducted by An NH et al.37 In DIRECT MT,31 $96.2\%$ of the bridging therapy group utilized stent retrievers as the basic option, the SWIFT-DIRECT trial used Solitaire stent retrieval revascularization devices and in CLEAN-NO IV,34 aspiration was the first approach in $20.9\%$ of the bridging therapy group. Grech R, et al.39 concluded in their meta-analysis and systematic review that stent retrievers are safer and have higher rates of recanalization and functional independence and should thus be used in RCTs. Aside from that, the use of intravenous alteplase prior to thrombectomy may have direct effects on suction and stent retrievers’ first approaches, as elaborated by the COMPASS trial’s post-hoc analysis.40

In addition to that, there was also a difference in selection criteria amongst the trials. For instance, the inclusion criteria in DEVT32 and SKIP33 trials included patients with ICA or M1 and excluded patients with M2 occlusions, which are known to have a significant response rate to IVT. This was in comparison to the studies conducted by An NH et al.,37 Fischer U et al.,36 DIRECT-MT,31 and MR CLEAN-NO IV,34 which included patients with LVO of the internal carotid artery (ICA), the first segment of the middle cerebral artery (M1), or second segment of the middle cerebral artery (M2). Added to the inclusion of patients with ICA, M1 and M2, Mitchell PJ et al.,35 included basilar arteries, tandem extracranial and intracranial atherosclerotic disease. These differences in inclusion criteria may impact the generalizability of the results and suggest the need for further research to determine the optimal treatment strategy for patients with AIS-LVO.  

Further, the previous meta-analysis only collected RCTs majorly from China, Japan and one from Europe. DIRECT-MT,31 DEVT,32 SKIP33 and An NH et al.37 were conducted in Asian individuals, who are reported to have a significantly higher prevalence of intracranial atherosclerotic disease.27 This condition is known to be less responsive to IVT,41 and therefore, the results of these trials may not be generalizable to populations outside of Asia. Mitchell PJ et al.,35 on the other hand, included patients from various regions, including China, Vietnam, New Zealand, and Australia, and thus their results may be more representative of a broader population.  

Moreover, there were variations in workflow measures observed among the trials included in our meta-analysis.

The administration of alteplase in acute stroke up to 4 hours at a dose of $0.9\mathrm{mg}/\mathrm{kg}/3.4.5$ hour has been approved since $199542$ and was used in all trials except for the SKIP trial33 which was conducted in Japan, where the approved dose is $0.6\mathrm{mg/kg}$ . In the MR CLEAN-NO IV Trial,34 both the times from onset to groin puncture and alteplase administration were shorter. To add, in cases of failed thrombolysis in the EVT group, IV thrombolysis was used as a rescue therapy in the trial.  

Finally, it is important to consider the potential influence of selection bias on the varying results observed in the RCTs and other observational studies. Zhang J, et al.27 noted that many studies fail to clearly differentiate between eligible and ineligible participants for IVT, with those studies that account for this bias reportedly yielding less favorable outcomes. These confounding biases may have a significant impact on the divergent outcomes reported in RCTs. Therefore, further evidence from RCTs is needed to reconcile this discrepancy, as non-RCTs are often subject to selection bias and lack the benefits of randomization.  

Summarily, whilst most studies did not conclude a significant superiority in either of the groups, it is imperative to carry out more studies as there is some evidence that the rates of symptomatic intracerebral hemorrhage (sICH) increase post-thrombolysis.43 Several factors have been identified as being associated with a higher risk of sICH, including age, gender, comorbidities such as diabetes, blood pressure, stroke severity, and early ischemic changes on CT like hypodensity, loss of gray-white matter distinction and cerebral edema.43 A robust assessment will warrant for these factors to be adjusted for, in future studies. Saposnik G, et al., found that patients with late improvement post- acute stroke within 24 hours had worse outcomes at 3 months in those with elevated glucose levels, time to thrombolysis and cortical involvement.44 Additionally, a meta-analysis assessing the difference between standard therapy and intra-arterial thrombolysis concluded that although there is a slight benefit of intra-arterial thrombolysis before the standard therapy, symptomatic intracranial hemorrhage risk was four times more likely $\mathrm{RR}{=}3{\cdot}90$ ; $95\%$ CI=1·41-10·76; $\mathrm{P}{=}0{\cdot}006$ ).45  

Limitations of the study  

There are several limitations to our study. Firstly, this meta-analysis utilized publicly available data, and as such, there is a possibility that the results may differ when applied to individual patients. The trials included in this study were conducted in China, Japan, European countries, New Zealand or Australia, and hence the findings may not be generalizable to other nations or locations. In addition, the studies included in this meta-analysis failed to account for certain confounders such as prior disease status, which may have influenced the results. Other limitations were the heterogeneity in selection criteria among the trials, as well as the approach used in them to identify non-inferiority margins which put them at a risk of being too broad to draw definitive conclusions.27 Future studies with standardized criteria could provide a more definitive conclusion on the non-inferiority of EVT compared to IVT. Notably, while our study examined thrombolysis in general, the literature mostly focuses on the use of alteplase, which is the standard choice. Finally, EVT was initiated before the completion of alteplase infusion in majority of the patients, consequent to which our findings can also not be applied to “drip-and-ship” patients - those who received alteplase in one hospital before being moved to a hospital with EVT.  

# Conclusions  

Our study did not establish non-inferiority between EVT alone or IVT followed by EVT, for the treatment of AISLVO. However, a significant difference was noted between the reperfusion rates of the two therapies. As the treatment option remains debatable, our study pooled seven RCTs and further enumerated the results of previous meta-analysis. There was no significant difference seen between the functionality, mortality or intracranial hemorrhage rates observed in patients post treatment. Nevertheless, more studies on larger scale, with unbiased assessment techniques and including larger populations will help understand the results of the therapies in an extensive way and may aid clinicians in reaching a revolutionary conclusion for the treatment options for stroke, involving the anterior circulation.
