# Hesperidin reduces adverse symptomatic intracerebral hemorrhage by promoting TGF-β1 for treating ischemic stroke using tissue plasminogen activator  

Zhanzhong Qin1 $\cdot$ Linlin Chen1 $\cdot$ Min Liu1 & Haihua Tan2 & Lei Zheng1  

Received: 14 May 2019 / Accepted: 22 August 2019 / Published online: 2 September 2019   
$\copyright$ Fondazione Società Italiana di Neurologia 2019  



# Abstract  

Treatment with recombinant tissue plasminogen activator (rt-PA) is the most effective therapeutic option against brain ischemic stroke at the present time. However, elevated incidence of symptomatic intracerebral hemorrhage (SIH) greatly hinders ideal treatment outcome of rt-PA. We sought to assess the impacts of hesperidin on SIH following rt-PA therapies. Patients with ischemic stroke were assigned into two groups in a random fashion, to receive either rt-PA $^+$ placebo $(\mathrm{Pc})$ or rt-PA $^+$ hesperidin. Treatment outcome was evaluated $^{241}$ after the initial reperfusion using the transcranial Doppler ultrasonography (TCD) and the NIH Stroke Scale (NIHSS). Further, serum concentrations of transforming growth factor (TGF)- $\cdot\beta1$ , matrix metalloproteinase (MMP)-2, and MMP-9 were examined. Following the initial administration, stroke patients continued to receive either daily $\mathrm{Pc}$ or daily hesperidin, and the treatment outcome after 7 days was examined using the TCD, NIHSS, Glasgow Outcome Scale (GOS), and the Modified Rankin Scale (MRS). Combined treatment of rt-PA with hesperidin yielded significant improvement of outcomes, as revealed by better TCD and NIHSS scores as well as decreased SIH incidences, which could be attributable to elevation of TGF- $\cdot\beta1$ and reduction in serum levels of both MMP-2 and MMP-9 caused by hesperidin. Follow-up hesperidin treatment for 7 consecutive days also markedly enhanced the recovery of stroke patients, as indicated by TCD, MRS, GOS, and NIHSS. Findings of the present study strongly suggested potential clinical application of hesperidin supplement in rt-PA therapies to reduce SIH and thereby improve the treatment outcomes of rt-PA in patients with ischemic stroke.

# Introduction  

One of the most common causes of mobility and mortality globally is brain ischemic stroke [1]. Ischemic brain damages frequently lead to serious neurological dysfunctions, without any established direct treatments. To date, reperfusion with thrombolytic recombinant tissue plasminogen activator (rt  

PA) remains the most effective therapeutic option of treatment against acute brain ischemic stroke, as evidenced in the investigation carried out by the National Institute of Neurological Disorders and Stroke [2]. However, rt-PA reperfusion needs to be applied within $^{3\mathrm{~h~}}$ following the stroke onset to be safe and effective [3]. Hemorrhagic transformation is one of the adverse effects of rt-PA therapy, which sometimes leads to elevated incidence rate of symptomatic intracerebral hemorrhage (SIH), as well as rapid serious neurological damages as consequences. Therefore, the clinical safety and efficacy of rt-PA is severely limited [4].  

Tissue plasminogen activator (t-PA), a serine protease, degrades fibrin clots as a thrombolytic agent [5]. It has been shown in animal models of brain ischemic stroke that excess or delayed rt-PA treatment causes hyperperfusion, leading to extracellular matrix (ECM) degradation via upregulation of matrix metalloproteinases (MMPs), namely MMP-2 and MMP-9, further disrupting the blood-brain barrier (BBB) [6–9]. Hence, suppression of MMP upregulation induced by rt-PA treatment, as well as subsequent ECM degradation and BBB disruption, might help to prevent SIH and thereby promote ideal therapeutic outcomes. Transforming growth factor (TGF)- $\cdot\beta1$ , a pleiotropic cytokine, reportedly promotes ECM reconstruction and production [10], and inhibits MMP expression (MMP-9 in particular) [11, 12]. Further, upregulation of TGF- $\cdot\beta1$ preserved BBB integrity [13]. One recent study using a rodent model of stroke showed that TGF- $\cdot\beta1$ prevented the BBB disruption and, particularly interesting to us, inhibited the hemorrhagic transformation following thrombolysis [14].  

A growing body of clinical investigations has demonstrated the health benefits of flavonoids. Hesperidin, the major flavanone glycoside in citrus fruits [15], was demonstrated to exert beneficial actions in treating various forms of diseases. For example, it was found to exhibit protective effect against ischemic reperfusion cerebral injury in rats [16]. Importantly, hesperidin increased secretion of TGF- $\beta1$ [17], and we were thus prompted to examine its impacts on the adverse effects of rt-PA treatment. We speculate that supplementation of hesperidin in rt-PA treatment could attenuate SIH, thereby promoting the recovery of neurological functions and improving the overall treatment outcomes of rt-PA reperfusion.  



# Materials and methods  



# Enrollment of patients  

From May 2015 through April 2018, a total of 385 patients, who had an ischemic stroke within 3 h with a clearly defined onset, were initially enrolled in the current study. Requirements for enrollment included a deficit measurable on the NIH Stroke Scale (NIHSS) and a brain computed tomographic (CT) scan at baseline showing no signs of previous intracranial hemorrhage and without large arterial occlusion (LAO). All patients were evaluated regarding their medical histories, and those with conditions listed below were excluded from further study $(n=44)$ ): a seizure at the onset of stroke; diastolic blood pressure $>110\mathrm{mmHg}$ or systolic blood pressure $>185\mathrm{mmHg}$ ; symptoms indicating subarachnoid hemorrhage; prior intracranial hemorrhage; urinary tract hemorrhage or gastrointestinal hemorrhage within 3 weeks; arterial puncture at a non-compressible site within 1 week; minor or rapidly improving symptoms; major surgeries within 2 weeks; prothrombin time over $15\mathrm{~s~}$ ; consumption of anticoagulants or heparin within 2 days prior to the stroke onset and had a prolonged partialthromboplastin time; counts of platelet $<100,000/\mathrm{mm}^{3}$ ; or glucose concentrations $<2.7\mathrm{mM}$ or $>22.2\mathrm{mM}$ . Patients who had received aggressive treatments to control the blood pressure were also excluded. All patients provided signed written consent and agreed with our data utilization before enrollment. The study was approved by the investigational review board of The Liaocheng Third People’s Hospital of Shandong Province.  



# Randomization and treatment  

Three hundred foty-one eligible patients were assigned to two treatment groups, using a permuted block randomization method stratified to their baseline NIHSS scores. All patients regardless of group assignment were administered rt-PA (Activase, Genetech) in the dosage of $0.9\mathrm{mg/kg}$ body weight $90~\mathrm{mg}$ maximum), of which $10\%$ was given as an initial bolus and the remaining $90\%$ was administered through infusion at a constant rate over $^\textrm{\scriptsize1h}$ . Among the 341 eligible patients, 170 received cellulose (as placebo) while the other 171 received hesperidin at the dosage of $4\mathrm{mg/kg}$ body weight ( $400~\mathrm{mg}$ maximum) at the same time of rt-PA reperfusion, of which $10\%$ was given as the initial bolus and the remaining $90\%$ was administered over $^\textrm{\scriptsize1h}$ . Following up to the combined treatment on the 1st day, patients who were assigned to receive the initial $\mathrm{rt-PA~+}$ placebo continued to receive daily placebo, while patients who were initially given $\mathrm{rt-PA~+}$ hesperidin continued to receive daily hesperidin at the dosage of $4~\mathrm{mg/kg}$ body weight, both through oral administration. Doses of hesperidin were chosen following previous published clinical trials, where daily consumption of no more than $600~\mathrm{mg}$ hesperidin was found to be safe for human [18, 19]. Placebo and hesperidin were code-labeled to make their contents blind to both the patients and investigators. Investigators who were blind to group assignment performed assessments at $^{24\mathrm{~h~}}$ , 7 days, and 2 months after the treatment.  



# Definition of endpoints  

Primary endpoints included the following: transcranial Doppler ultrasonography, scored with a 0–6 scale, was utilized to monitor the blood flow in the middle cerebral artery (MCA) [20–25], with MCA blood flow of $<140~\mathrm{cm/s}$ scored 0; MCA blood flow of $140{-}159~\mathrm{cm/s}$ scored 1; $160{-}179~\mathrm{cm/s}$ scored 2; $180{-}199~\mathrm{cm/s}$ scored 3; $200-$ $239\mathrm{cm/s}$ scored 4; $;240{-}299\mathrm{cm}/\mathrm{s}$ scored 5; and MCA blood flow of greater than $300~\mathrm{cm/s}$ scored 6. The NIHSS is a 42- point scale that measures neurologic deficits in 11 categories [26]. Normal functions without signs of neurologic deficit are scored 0. It is regarded as an improvement $^{24\textrm{h}}$ post treatment if there is an at least 4-point reduction in NIHSS score or all symptoms are resolved. The Glasgow Outcome Scale (GOS) is a widely accepted measure of functional recovery, with score 1 indicating a good recovery; score 2, moderate disability; 3, severe disability; 4, survival yet in a vegetative state; and score 5, death [27]. Modified Rankin Scale (MRS) is a simplified evaluation of overall functions, with 0 indicating the complete absence of symptoms and 5 indicating severe disability [28]. Scores of 0 or 1 on the MRS and NIHSS, as well as 1 on the GOS, were regarded as indicative of an ideal outcome.  

Secondary endpoints were defined as serum concentrations of TGF- $\beta1$ , MMP-2, and MMP-9. Samples of blood were harvested from the patients prior to the start of rt-PA therapy as the baseline and at $^{24\mathrm{~h~}}$ post treatment as the endpoint. Samples of blood were immediately centrifuged to avoid protein degradation, and samples of the plasma component were stored at $\mathrm{~-~}80~^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$ within $^{1\textrm{h}}$ of collection. Serum concentrations of TGF- $\cdot\beta1$ , MMP-2, and MMP-9 were assessed using human TGF- $\cdot\beta1$ , MMP-2, and MMP-9 ELISA kits (Abcam, Cambridge, MA, USA) per the provided protocols.

# Statistical analysis  

The current study adopted the intention-to-treat analysis. Chisquare test or Student’s $t$ test was used to determine the statistical significance of the difference between the rt-PA $^+$ placebo and rt-PA $^+$ hesperidin groups. $P$ values less than 0.05 were considered as indicative of statistical significance. Sample size was estimated using our preliminary data by Cohen’s d method [29]. The means of parameters from both groups were divided by standard deviation to calculate the standardized effect size, the largest of which was then adopted by Cohen’s d power table to determine minimum group size with sufficient statistical significance and power.

# Results  



# Baseline characteristics  

From May 2015 through April 2018, 341 eligible patients were divided into two groups in a random fashion, with 170 patients in the rt-PA $^+$ placebo group and 171 patients in the rt$\mathrm{PA}+$ hesperidin group. During the study period, 14 patients from the rt-PA $^+$ hesperidin group and 19 patients from the rt$\mathrm{PA}+$ placebo group, respectively, were lost to follow-up after initial treatment. Data from all eligible patients were analyzed in the current study (Fig. 1).  

Baseline parameters of patients in the two groups were presented in Table 1, and no statistical difference in the NIHSS scores at baseline between the two groups was observed (Fig. 2a). Further, we evaluated medical histories of all enrolled patients and again observed no significant difference between the two treatment groups (listed in Table 2).

# Hesperidin improved TCD and NIHSS scores from baseline within 24 h of treatment  

Outcomes at $24\mathrm{~h~}$ after the initial co-treatment, as assessed using transcranial Doppler ultrasonography (TCD) (measuring the MCA blood flow) and NIHSS scores, were summarized in Fig. 2. TCD scores demonstrated the improved MCA blood flow in a higher percentage of patients from the rt-PA $^+$ hesperidin co-treatment group, in comparison with the placebo group (Fig. 2b). Further, our data also revealed more patients with NIHSS scores of 0–10 in the rt-PA $^+$ hesperidin cotreatment group as compared with the placebo group (Fig. 2c).  

Additionally, safety endpoints, including incidence rates of symptomatic hemorrhage and coagulopathy within $^{24\mathrm{~h~}}$ of treatment, were summarized in Table 3. Nine out of 170 patients from the placebo group had SIH within 1 day of treatment (as shown in Table 3), of which 6 patients died (Fig. 2, $4\%$ death in rt- $\mathrm{-PA+Pc}$ group). On the other hand, there were only 2 fatal incidences of SIH out of the 170 patients from the $\mathrm{rt-PA~+}$ hesperidin group (as shown in Table 3). Incidence rates of coagulopathy were low in both treatment groups and without significant difference (Table 3).  



# Serum levels of MMP-2 and MMP-9 were reduced by hesperidin  

SIH caused by rt-PA reperfusion was believed to be a consequence of MMP hyper-activation, in particular MMP-2 and MMP-9, which would subsequently disrupt the BBB [6–9]. Hesperidin reportedly promoted the secretion of TGF- $\cdot\beta1$ [17], which has been shown to suppress MMP expression in several reports [11, 12]. Therefore, we assessed the serum concentrations of MMP-2 and MMP-9, as well as TGF- $\cdot\beta1$ , in all patients from both groups. TGF- $\cdot\beta1$ , MMP-2, and MMP9 levels were examined prior to the start of rt-PA treatment as the baseline, as well as at $^{24\mathrm{h}}$ after the rt-PA treatment as the endpoint. As illustrated in Fig. 3, co-treatment of hesperidin and rt-PA dramatically upregulated the plasma level of TGF- $\cdot\beta1$ in comparison with the placebo group. Agreeing with prior reports, in patients who received $\mathrm{rt-PA}+\mathrm{Pc}$ treatment, plasma concentrations of both MMP-2 and MMP-9 at the endpoint were significantly elevated compared with that of the baseline. Importantly, co-treatment with hesperidin greatly blunted the elevation of the serum levels of both MMPs.  



# Hesperidin improved both short- and long-term outcomes of the initial rt-PA therapy  

Following the initial co-treatment, patients assigned to receive $\mathrm{rt-PA}+$ placebo continued to be given placebo, while patients assigned to receive rt-PA $^+$ hesperidin continued to be given hesperidin on a daily basis. We monitored the short- and longterm recovery at 7 days and 2 months, respectively, of all patients. Treatment outcomes at 7 days were shown in Fig. 4, as quantified using TCD (Fig. 4a), NIHSS (Fig. 4b), MRS (Fig. 4c), and GOS (Fig. 4d). Results from all outcome measures showed a favorable recovery in the hesperidin treatment group. Significantly more patients in the hesperidin group showed resolution of all symptoms or minimal deficit at 7 days after the initial co-treatment and the following hesperidin therapy, in comparison with the placebo group. Long-term recovery was evaluated using similar scales after 2 months (Fig. 5a– d), and results generated by all scales consistently revealed better recovery in the hesperidin group.

# Discussion  

Despite the fact that rt-PA is currently the most effective therapeutic option for treating acute brain ischemic stroke, its clinical safety remains short of satisfactory. Reperfusion of rt-PA needs to be applied within $^\textrm{\scriptsize3h}$ after stroke onset [5], greatly hindering the clinical usage of rt-PA treatment. Moreover, high incidence rate of SIH post treatment, in certain cases fatal, brings about another significant clinical risk. In an effort to reduce these adverse effects following rt-PA therapy, it was found that stent retriever thrombectomy, when conducted within $^\textrm{\scriptsize8h}$ of stroke onset, was able to ameliorate poststroke disability and enhance functional recovery [30]. However, as a minimal yet invasive procedure, thrombectomy has its own complications and risks, especially in the elderly or those with chronic health problems, including infection, anesthetic side effects, bleeding, and occasional clot reforming requiring additional procedures.  

In the present clinical investigation, we co-administered hesperidin with rt-PA reperfusion and found such cotreatment dramatically enhanced the recovery of patients with acute brain ischemic stroke. At 1 day following the treatment, outcome of patients who received rt-PA showed a marked improvement, as indicated by the decreased NIHSS scores showing significantly lower disability levels, in comparison with patients in the rt-PA $^+$ placebo group. Moreover, hesperidin co-treatment greatly decreased incidence of SIH. Hence, our findings demonstrated the clinical potential of hesperidin to be utilized as a supplement in the rt-PA treatment, for improved treatment outcomes and amelioration of adverse effects, even with long-term positive effects with continuous administration.

Hesperidin has been shown to stimulate TGF- $\cdot\beta1$ secretion [17], and TGF- $\beta1$ reportedly promoted the production and reconstruction of ECM [10] and protected the integrity of BBB [13], likely via the inhibition of MMP expression [11, 12]. We thus speculate that the observed beneficial effects of hesperidin on treatment outcome were probably mediated through TGF- $\cdot\beta1$ and MMPs.  

The role of MMP-2 and MMP-9 in the clinical therapies against stroke has long been acknowledged. Both MMPs contribute to the degradation of components in the basement membrane including laminin and collagen IV and thereby cause BBB disruption [31, 32], leading to greater risk of cerebral hemorrhage [33]. Stroke patients with elevated MMP-9 level often suffer parenchymal hemorrhages after rt-PA treatment [34]. In addition, exogenous t-PA that upregulates MMP-9 can further alleviate stroke symptoms [35], in agreement with our finding that rt-PA treatment alone induced significant elevation of the serum concentrations of both MMPs. Importantly, TGF- $\cdot\beta1$ serum level was substantially elevated in patients who received co-treatment of $\mathrm{rt-PA~+}$ hesperidin and which in turn suppressed serum levels of both MMPs. Our findings indicated that hesperidin could function to upregulate TGF- $\cdot\beta1$ and thereby inhibit MMPs in human patients.  

Therefore, our speculation is that the inhibition of MMP-2 and MMP-9 induced by hesperidin, through upregulation of  

TGF- $\cdot\beta1$ , likely leads to the attenuation of adverse effects after rt-PA therapy, SIH in particular. In summary, the present trial for the first time provided evidence of the positive actions of hesperidin on ameliorating adverse responses of thrombolytic rt-PA therapy. Co-treatment with hesperidin yielded significant improvement in the treatment outcomes within 1 day of rt-PA and dramatically decreases the incidence rate of SIH following rt-PA. Such positive effect of hesperidin is highly attributable to its property of promoting TGF- $\cdot\beta1$ secretion, which in turn inhibited MMP-2 and MMP-9. Follow-up administrations of hesperidin continuously following the initial reperfusion of rt-PA also elicited improvement in the outcomes at 1 week post-stroke therapy. Our findings suggested the usage of hesperidin as a supplement during rt-PA reperfusion to better treat against brain ischemic stroke. Limitations of the study should nevertheless be pointed out, including the following: (1) due to urgency for initial treatment, MCA flow velocity was not assessed in the acute phase of the stroke, raising questions of its indication in our study setting and (2) scales assessed in the current study missed high-grade stenosis with very low flow velocities. Future study should be designed with these considerations to verify our current conclusions.
