# Early enteral nutrition intervention promotes multiple functional recovery in patients with traumatic intracerebral hemorrhage: A prospective randomized controlled study  

Jingjing Wang 1, Ke Yu 1, Yuping Zeng  

Department of Neurology, West China Hospital, Sichuan University, Sichuan Province, China  



# ARTICLE  INFO  



# ABSTRACT  

Keywords:   
Traumatic intracerebral hemorrhage   
Early enteral nutrition   
Neurological function   
Endocrine system   
Intestinal flora   
Nutritional indicator Objective: To delve into how early enteral nutrition intervention exert its part in promoting multiple functional recovery in patients with traumatic intracerebral hemorrhage (TICH).   
Method: This prospective randomized controlled study recruited 152 traumatic intracerebral hemorrhage pa­ tients in our hospital. The observation group $({\ttn}=77)$ received early enteral nutrition intervention $(\leq48\mathrm{~h~})$ , while the control group $(\mathtt{n}=75)$ were given delayed enteral nutrition $(>48\mathrm{~h~})$ . Further comparison was per­ formed on the recovery of various physiologic functions between the two groups.   
Results: After treatment, GCS score and GOS score in the observation group were significantly higher than those in the control group $(\mathbf{P}<0.05)$ , and mRS Score was significantly lower than that in the control group $(\mathbf{P}<0.05)$ . Moreover, the observation group demonstrated a considerable post-treatment elevation in adrenocorticotropic hormone (ACTH) and cortisol (Cor) levels, which were notably higher than the control group’s levels (both $\mathbf{P_{\lambda<}}$ 0.05). The changes in Bifidobacteria, Enterococcus, and Escherichia coli values pre and post-treatment were more remarkable in the observation group (all $\mathrm{~P~}<0.05)$ . Kaplan-Meier survival curve analysis indicated a substantial difference in survival curves between patients who provided with early enteral nutrition and those where it later $(\mathtt{P}<0.05)$ .  

Conclusion: The early application of enteral nutrition can promote neurological function recovery, improve the disorder of intestinal flora and the patient’s nutritional status, reduce the increase of injury factors under stress, and lower the mortality risk among patients suffering from TICH.  



# 1. Background  

Traumatic intracerebral hemorrhage (TICH) frequently leads to disability and death in patients with traumatic brain injury [1,2]. After TICH, patients may present strong stress response, with large secretion of adrenocortical hormones (cortisol, Cor), and significant increase in the concentration of glucagon (GC) to promote the rise of blood glucose (Glu), thereby affecting internal environment stability in the body. Meanwhile, patients with hypertensive intracerebral hemorrhage demonstrated remarkable increase in serum Cor and Glu levels that might affect their prognosis, indicating that changes in Cor and Glu function significantly in the occurrence and progression of intracerebral hemorrhage [3]. During stress in TICH patients, endocrine changes may have a negative impact on intestinal flora, leading to intestinal barrier dysfunction and high risk of malnutrition [4]. Significantly, improving intestinal flora disorder and nutritional status may be valuable for the prognosis of TICH to some extent [5]. Sufficient nutritional and energy supplementation exert a fundamental part in promoting the activity of the endocrine system and the synthesis of substances during stress in post-traumatic patients at the early stage [6]. Some encouraging studies showed that early enteral nutrition intervention within $48\mathrm{~h~}$ after injury could achieve a better curative effect than delayed enteral nutrition [7]. However, it is still unclear whether early enteral nutrition intervention can promote multiple functional recovery in patients with traumatic cerebral hemorrhage. Accordingly, our hospital has applied early enteral nutrition intervention for TICH patients since 2016. Corre­ sponding report is described as follows.  



# 2. Materials and methods  



# 2.1. General information  

This study was designed as a prospective randomized controlled study, with the flowchart shown in Fig. 1. Prior to randomization, all patients had provided written informed consent forms and could decide voluntarily to choose whether to continue the trial at any time. The recruited subjects of this study were inpatients of this hospital. Inclusion criteria: $\textcircled{1}$ patients diagnosed with TICH through CT and fulfilling the requirements of the "Chinese Guidelines for the Diagnosis and Treatment of Cerebral Hemorrhage (2019) [8]"; $\textcircled{2}$ patients older than 18 years diag­ nosed with TICH; $\textcircled{3}$ patients with the Glasgow Coma Scale (GCS) score of $<13$ points; and $\textcircled{4}$ patients who had been observed for $>2$ weeks. Exclusion criteria: $\textcircled{1}$ patients with medical history of respiratory or gastrointestinal diseases; $\textcircled{2}$ patients with history of endocrine diseases that affected nutritional metabolism; $\textcircled{3}$ patients who underwent crani­ otomy during hospitalization, which increasing the risk of malnutrition and pneumonia; $\textcircled{4}$ patients who required mechanical ventilation before being diagnosed with pneumonia; and $\textcircled{5}$ patients who died within 2 weeks after admission. To reduce selection bias, the patients who were enrolled were allocated randomly into two groups — the treatment group and the control group—— in a 1:1 ratio, employing a Random Number Generator in Microsoft Excel. The Ethics Committee of this hospital granted approval for this study (2017KY-122).  



# 3. Study design  

All patients completed relevant auxiliary examinations after admis­ sion. On the basis of general supportive treatments such as hemostasis, analgesia, nutritional therapy and psychotherapy, patients in the observation group $\left(\mathtt{n}=77\right)$ received early enteral nutrition intervention $(\leq48\mathrm{~h~})$ , while those in the control group $(\mathtt{n}=75^{\circ})$ were treated with delayed enteral nutrition $\left(>48\mathrm{h}\right)$ . The basal energy expenditure (BEE) was calculated using the correction formula recommended by WHO 2010, with an additional $30\%$ added on this basis for TICH patients. The energy intake was set at 20–35 kcal/ $(\mathrm{kg}\mathrm{~d})$ , with the protein mass $>1~{\bfg}/({\bfkg.d})$ ; carbohydrates accounting for $60{-}65\%$ of the total energy, fat intake not exceeding $30~\%$ of the total energy, dietary fiber intake maintained at $30{-}35~\mathrm{g/d}$ , and water intake of $>1500\mathrm{ml/d}$ . No special antibiotics or other drugs affecting microbiom or conciuossness are used. During the period of treatment, patients were closely monitored and promptly provided with symptomatic treatment in case of any adverse reactions. The feeding protocol used was that enteral nutrition was initiated at $20\mathrm{ml/h}$ , with gastric residuals checked every $4\textrm{h}$ . If the residual volume was $<200~\mathrm{ml}$ , the feeding speed should be increased by $20\mathrm{ml/h}$ until reaching the target speed; otherwise, enteral nutrition would be maintained or a jejunal nutrition tube would be placed. When realizing the feeding goal, gastric residuals should be checked every $^{12\mathrm{h}}$ . Various physiologic functions of the enrolled patients were accessed both before and after treatment (2 weeks after surgery). The primary endpoint of follow-up in our study was the last follow-up or the time of death. Patients were followed by WeChat, phone, outpatient follow-up.

# 4. Evaluation criteria  

Primary outcomes: $\textcircled{1}\mathbf{G}\mathbf{C}\mathbf{S}$ score (before treatment and 2 weeks after surgery), modified Rankin Scale (mRS) score (before treatment and 3 months after surgery), and Glasgow Outcome Score (GOS) (3 months after surgery) were used to evaluate the degree of neurological impair­ ment of patients [9]: The highest score of GCS was 15, indicating clear consciousness; the score below 8 presents a severe disorder of con­ sciousness; the lowest score was 3. mRS Scores ranged from 0 to 5, with 0 being completely asymptomatic, 1 being asymptomatic but with no obvious dysfunction, 2 being mildly disabled, 3 being moderate disabled, 4 being moderate to severe disabled, and 5 being severe disabled. GOS score was 5 for good postoperative recovery, 4 for mild postoperative disability, 3 for severe postoperative disability, 2 for mild postoperative disability, and 1 for death. $\textcircled{2}$ Comparison of the difference in intestinal flora before surgery and 2 weeks after surgery in both pa­ tient groups: An amount of $0.2g$ of fecal samples was collected in the early morning and place in a $0.9\%$ sterile NaCl solution. anaerobic and aerobic cultivation were performed after dilution with a 10-fold continuous dilution. The Colony-Forming Units $\left(\mathrm{CFU}/{g}\right)$ was taken and the logarithm was taken.  

Secondary outcomes: $\textcircled{1}$ Endocrine system indicators((Before surgery and 2 weeks after surgery) included adrenocorticotropic hormone (ACTH), Cor, GC and Glu; $\textcircled{2}$ Comparison of the difference in nutritional indicators before surgery and 2 weeks after surgery in both patient groups: Peripheral venous blood ( $^{2\mathrm{ml}}$ each) was collected and centri­ fuged to take the supernatant for the measurement of hemoglobin, al­ bumin and prealbumin via Hitachi 7600 automatic biochemical analyzer. $\textcircled{3}$ The incidence of intracranial rebleeding, acute hydroceph­ alus, and chronic hydrocephalus, as well as mortality were also recorded in the two groups. Diagnostic criteria for hydrocephalus [10]: Evans index (maximum width between the frontal horns of the lateral ventri­ cles divided by the maximum transverse inner diameter of the skull on the same plane) $\scriptscriptstyle>0.3$ (Fig. 2), or enlargement of the frontal horns of lateral ventricles, temporal horn and third ventricle of cerebrum with brain edema around lateral ventricles. Generally, acute hydrocephalus is defined when it is discovered within 2 weeks after surgery, and chronic hydrocephalus is discovered after 2 weeks.

# 6. Results  



# 6.1. Comparison of baseline data between the two groups of patients  

From June 2016 to June 2021, our hospital admitted 207 TICH pa­ tients. However, 51 patients were disqualified in accordance with the exclusion criteria. In the end, our study enrolled 156 eligible patients, comprising a treatment group of 78 patients and a control group of 78 patients, of which 1 patient and 3 patients voluntarily opted out of the study, respectively. Comparative analyses of age, gender, smoking rate, hypertension or diabetes rate, Acute Physiology and Chronic Health Evaluation(APACHE) II score, GCS score, modified Rankin Scale (mRS) score, bleeding volume, length of hospital stay and other baseline data revealed no statistically significant differences between the two groups prior to treatment (all $P>0.05$ ; Table 1), suggesting comparability between groups.  



# 6.2. Comparison of the recovery of various physiologic functions between the two groups of patients  

As depicted in Table 2, before treatment, there was no significant difference in neurological function scores between the two groups $(\mathbf{P}>0.05)$ . After treatment, GCS scores and GOS scores in the obser­ vation group were significantly higher than those in the control group $(\mathbf{P}<0.05)$ , and mRS Scores were significantly lower than those in the control group $(\mathbf{P}<0.05)$ . Furthermore, no great difference was detected in the pre-treatment indicators of the endocrine system between the two groups (all $P>0.05$ ). The observation group displayed a remarkable boost in ACTH and Cor levels (both $P<0.05$ ), both of which were obviously higher in the observation group (both $\mathrm{P}<0.05\$ in compari­ son with the control group. Meanwhile, GC in the control group decreased notably $(P<0.05)$ , while the observation group demon­ strated a notable decline in both GC and Glucose levels (both $P<0.05\$ , both of which were considerably lower in the observation group than the control group (both $P<0.05\$ ). Additionally, when compared with the control group, the difference values of Bifidobacteria, Enterococcus and Escherichia coli pre and post-treatment in the observation group were notably greater (all $P<0.05$ ) in contrast to the control group. Moreover, the difference values in hemoglobin, prealbumin, and albumin in the observation group was remarkably greater (all $P<0.05$ ). In addition, contrasted with the control group, the incidence rates of intracranial rebleeding, chronic hydrocephalus and corresponding mortality rates in the observation group were considerably lower (all $P<0.05$ ); while no great difference was noted in the incidence of acute hydrocephalus $(P>0.05)$ .

# 7. Discussion  

As a prevalent injury encountered in clinical settings, craniocerebral trauma has a relatively high disability and mortality rate, causing great difficulty in clinical treatment. Neurological impairment after cranio­ cerebral trauma involves multiple stages and steps, among which TICH is an important cause in the process. Intracranial hematoma of TICH induced by craniocerebral trauma, on the one hand, may directly compress the surrounding brain tissue, increase intracranial pressure, and induce mechanical damage to the brain tissue; on the other hand, it may exacerbate damage to cerebral tissues as it can produce toxic byproducts such as reactive oxygen species, inflammatory factors, and excitatory amino acids [11,12]. With the continuous development of nutriology and the gradual change in the concept of disease treatment, it has gradually become a hot spot concerning early nutritional interven­ tion for critically ill patients clinically. According to previous research, timely and scientific nutritional intervention in the early stages of TICH patients can significantly improve their survival rate, and alleviate various functional impairments such as energy deficiency, hypoxia, and metabolic disorders of the brain cells caused by insufficient nutrient substrates in vivo [13]. Meanwhile, in addition to providing nutrition and maintaining the positive nitrogen balance of the body, early nutri­ tional intervention can also lay the foundation for normal energy metabolism of brain cells, rapid repair of damaged brain cells, improvement of the overall immunologic function, and acceleration of the recovery of patients with TICH [14]. Moreover, nutritional inter­ vention exhibits an intimate association with the recovery of neuro­ logical function in TICH patients. Notably, both medication and rehabilitation training require a solid foundation, and the patient’s own nutritional status is exactly the foundation of all treatments. Improve­ ment in the efficacy of medication and rehabilitation training depends largely on the strong foundation of patients. Active early nutritional intervention, medication and rehabilitation training can exert good synergistic effects that may contribute to great improvement in neuro­ logical function recovery of patients in the later stage [15].

The hypothalamus-pituitary-adrenal cortex axis is an important endocrine axis involved in stress response, which is activated and re­ leases a large amount of adrenocortical hormones under the stimulation of external stressors. Cerebral hemorrhage is a strong stress event that activates the stress response. Significant increase in the content of serum Cor has been reported in patients with hypertensive and spontaneous intracerebral hemorrhage [16]. In general, the secretion of Cor may contribute to maintaining internal environment homeostasis. However, there may be aggravation in the condition of illness and negative impact on the prognosis when the increase in Cor is insufficient to meet in vivo needs for maintaining internal environment homeostasis. As confirmed by many scholars, insufficient secretion of Cor is a prognostic risk factor in patients with severe trauma [17]. Patients with TICH may be in an absolute stress state after the onset, characterized by an increased release of GC within the body, causing a rapid surge in blood Glu con­ centration, and stimulating the islet B cells to secrete insulin simulta­ neously. TICH patients may experience a significant increase in blood Glu and insulin levels during fasting. It has been reported previously that besides elevated fasting insulin levels, TICH patients also had insulin resistance [18], which may further exacerbate energy deficiency and hypoxia in brain cells. Meanwhile, excessive increase in blood Glu concentration may also cause complete disorder of lipid metabolism, destroy the vascular endothelial barrier, and lead to the formation of atherosclerosis and thrombosis, eventually elevating the risk of intra­ cranial rebleeding.  

No great difference in the comparison of various indicators between the two groups before treatment in current research. While after treat­ ment, ACTH and Cor in the observation group were notably boosted in comparison with those before treatment, both of which were obviously boosted in the observation group, in contrast to the control group; GC in the control group declined remarkably, while both GC and Glu in the observation group decreased obviously, both of which were notably lower in the observation group when contrasted to the control group. In terms of the possible reasons, early nutritional intervention in the observation group can provide absolute nutrition and energy assurance for the body, as well as materials that benefit ACTH and Cor synthesis, which may ensure a sufficient supply of stressors and substances under stress. Moreover, early nutritional intervention can provide the body with sufficient energy substances, significantly reduce self-mobilization, and obviously decrease blood GC and Glu, ensuring the supply of energy and oxygen to brain cells.  

The occurrence of intracranial rebleeding in the control group in this study was greatly enhanced than that in the observation group $(17.3~\%$ vs. $3.9\%$ ). It can be explained that early nutritional intervention in the observation group can maintain dynamic energy balance of the body to realize a relatively stable state of all indicators. By contrast, patients in the control group were detected with high blood Glu concentration, lipid metabolism disorder, vascular arteriosclerosis, and thrombosis, result­ ing in an obviously higher risk of intracranial blood vessel rupture and rebleeding again. In terms of mortality, it was $14.7~\%$ and $2.6~\%$ in the control group and the observation group, respectively, with remarkably boosted rate in the former group when contrasted to the latter group. Meanwhile, based on Kaplan-Meier survival curve analysis, the cumu­ lative survival rate in patients with delayed enteral nutrition $\left(>48\mathrm{h}\right)$ from the control group was lower than that in those with early enteral nutrition $(\leq48\mathrm{~h})$ from the observation group. Considering the high incidence of intracranial rebleeding in the control group, the fatality risk would increase exponentially in TICH patients with intracranial rebleeding. The main reason is that brain cells would be injured again after the first serious damage without recovery, leading to further cell apoptosis and thus extremely high mortality rate of the affected patients. Relevant report demonstrated that the mortality rate could reach as high as $90\%$ in TICH patients after intracranial rebleeding [19]. In addition, the occurrence of chronic hydrocephalus was notably reduced in the observation group in contrast to the control group. Furthermore, in­ dividuals with chronic hydrocephalus exhibited significantly elevated levels of white blood cells, neutrophils, NLR, CRP and LDH than those in patients with non-chronic hydrocephalus. The reason may be that early enteral nutrition can inhibit the inflammatory response of the cerebral and intestinal axis, reduce intestinal permeability, reduce colony transmission, inhibit systemic inflammation, and thus reduce the inci­ dence of hydrocephalus [20,21]. Studies have shown that early enteral nutrition reduces the expression of inflammatory factors IL-1b and TNF-a in blood, thus reducing the occurrence of hydrocephalus [22].  

Concerning the recovery of neurological function, no great difference was detected in NIHSS scores between the two groups before treatment. However, after treatment, both groups experienced a notable decrease in NIHSS scores compared to their pre-treatment scores. Notably, the observation group demonstrated significantly lower scores than the control group. In our opinion, early nutritional intervention of the observation group can provide sufficient energy substances in the early stage, while maintaining normal energy metabolism of brain cells, minimizing the damage caused by TICH to brain cells, and repairing damaged brain cells, which may greatly reduce the risk of brain cell apoptosis and thus significantly accelerate the recovery of neurological function.  

In individuals with TICH, postoperative catabolism acceleration, infection, irritability ulcer and other causes may cause high risk of malnutrition and hence lead to disturbance of intestinal flora, which may further aggravate the existing infection. It highlights the necessity of early nutritional intervention in promoting postoperative recovery and reducing infection. Normal intestinal flora includes the microbiota colonized in a certain digestive tract area of the host for a certain period of time. Under normal circumstances, the intestinal flora is in dynamic balance to boost intestinal immunity, which are important components of the biological barrier to prevent intestinal infections [23]. While after surgery for TI, there would be disordered intestinal flora, decreased levels of dominant flora such as Bifidobacteria, while increased pro­ portion of Enterococcus and Escherichia coli, resulting in the disap­ pearance of normal dynamic balance [24]. In our study, TICH patients with early enteral nutrition intervention demonstrated significantly higher proportion of Bifidobacteria, but less Enterococcus and Escher­ ichia coli than those in the control group. It suggests that early enteral nutrition can enhance the internal status of intestinal flora, promote the growth and proliferation of probiotics in the intestine in an efficient manner. Besides, the difference values in nutritional indicators (e.g., hemoglobin, prealbumin, and albumin) before and after treatment were greater in the observation group in contrast to the control group, sup­ porting that early enteral nutrition intervention possessed the ability to enhance the nutritional status of patients.  

However, there were several limitations which need to be addressed in the future. First of all, this was a prospective randomized controlled study performed at a single center. The number of patients involved in this study was relatively small due to the rigorous inclusion and exclu­ sion criteria. Secondly, a high-quality methodology is essential for high validity and reliability of randomized trials. The limitation of this study is obvious as the clinicians are unlikely to be blinded in this trial. To fill this gap, a randomized, controlled, multicenter clinical trial is needed.  



# 8. Conclusion  

Compared with delayed enteral nutrition, early application of enteral nutrition can promote neurological function recovery, improve disorder of intestinal flora, provide better nutritional effectiveness, reduce injury factors under stress and lower mortality risk among patients suffering from TICH. It is hoped that the results will provide new evidence about early enteral nutrition support for TICH patients.
