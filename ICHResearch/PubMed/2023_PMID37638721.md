BACKGROUND AND OBJECTIVES: Chronic subdural hematoma (CSDH) sometimes recurs after surgical treatment and requires reoperation. In Japan, Japanese herbal medicines (Kampo), such as Goreisan and Saireito, have been used as adjunctive therapies to prevent the recurrence of CSDH. However, no prospective randomized study has proven the efficacy of Kampo medicine in all patients. To investigate whether Goreisan and Saireito reduce the postoperative recurrence of CSDH in a prospective randomized study.  

METHODS: Between April 2017 and July 2019, a total of 118 patients who underwent initial burr hole surgery for CSDH were randomly assigned to the following 3 groups: (1) Goreisan for 3 months (Group G), (2) Saireito for 3 months (Group S), and (3) no medication (Group N). The primary end point was symptomatic recurrence within 3 months postoperatively, and the secondary end point was complications associated with the administration of Kampo medicine.  

RESULTS: Among 118 patients, 114 (Group N, $\mathfrak{n}=39$ ; Group G, $\mathsf{n}=37$ ; and Group S, $\mathsf{n}=38$ ) were included in our analysis. In this study, byakujutsu (containing Atractylodes rhizome) Goreisan and Saireito were used, unlike other prospective randomized studies in which sojutsu (containing Atractylodes lancea rhizome) Goreisan was used. The overall recurrence rate was $11.4\%$ (13/114: 10 for Group N, 2 for Group G, and 1 for Group S). The recurrence rate of Group G was significantly lower than that of Group N ( $5.4\%$ vs $25.6\%$ ; $P=.043)$ . The recurrence rate of Group S was also significantly lower than that of Group N $2.6\%$ vs $25.6\%$ ; $P=.02$ ). No patients developed complications associated with the administration of Kampo medicine.  

CONCLUSION: This is the first study to show that Kampo medicine reduced the recurrence rate of CSDH in an overall population. This study demonstrated that byakujutsu Goreisan and Saireito may have favorable effects, unlike other studies, because byakujutsu has stronger anti-inflammatory activity than sojutsu.

Kampo medicine, the traditional herbal medicine of Japan, has a history of more than 1500 years.16 Among a number of Kampo medicines, Goreisan and Saireito have been used empirically as adjunctive therapies to prevent the recurrence of CSDH.14,15 Goreisan has a hydragogue effect and is used for treating systemic edema, nephrotic syndrome, and other associated symptoms.17 Saireito is a combination of Goreisan and Shosaikoto and has both the hydragogue effect of Goreisan and the anti-inflammatory action of Shosaikoto.18 Some case series and retrospective studies have shown that Goreisan and Saireito reduced the postoperative recurrence of CSDH.14,15,19,20 Recently, 3 prospective and randomized studies have been conducted to investigate whether Goreisan reduces the recurrence rate of CSDH.21-23 These studies showed no beneficial effect in overall patients.21-23 On the other hand, there have been no prospective randomized studies to investigate whether Saireito reduces the recurrence rate of CSDH.  

Kampo formulations for prescription in Japan contain different components of Atractylodes species. The formulations distributed from some pharmaceutical companies contain byakujutsu (Atractylodes rhizoma), and formulations from other companies contain sojutsu (Atractylodes lancea rhizome), although the names of the Kampo formulations and the indications for treatment are the same.24 In fact, there are 2 types of Goreisan and Saireito based on whether they contain byakujutsu or sojutsu: byakujutsu Goreisan and Saireito and sojutsu Goreisan and Saireito. Because the plant origins of byakujutsu and sojutsu differ, it has been considered that their pharmacological activities differ.24 Some experimental studies have shown that byakujutsu has stronger anti-inflammatory action than sojutsu while having an equivalent hydragogue effect.25-27 Previously reported prospective randomized studies used sojutsu Goreisan.21-23 Therefore, we hypothesized that byakujutsu Goreisan may reduce the recurrence rate in overall patients. Moreover, we hypothesized that Saireito treatment may be associated with a greater reduction in the recurrence rate in comparison with Goreisan. We performed a prospective randomized study to investigate whether byakujutsu Goreisan and Saireito reduce the postoperative recurrence of CSDH.  



# METHODS  



# Study Design  

This investigator-indicated, single-center prospective, randomized controlled trial was conducted between April 2017 and July 2019. This study was performed in accordance with ethical principles of the Declaration of Helsinki and in compliance with ethical guidelines for clinical research. We conducted this study to investigate whether Kampo medicines, such as Goreisan and Saireito, reduce the postoperative recurrence of CSDH. The primary end point was the recurrence of CSDH within 3 months postoperatively, and the secondary end point was complications of Kampo medicine and the functional outcome (as assessed using the modified Rankin Scale [mRS]) at the final medical examination. The sample size was determined as follows. We previously reported that the recurrence rate of CSDH at our institution was $13.3\%$ .5 The previously reported recurrence rate was approximately $5\%-33\%$ .1-7 We found an estimated recurrence rate of $30\%$ in the control group and  

$2\%$ in the group receiving Kampo medicines. The estimated sample size for the 2-tailed $t$ test was determined to be 33 in each group when Type I and Type II errors were considered at significance levels of 0.05 and 0.20, respectively. The study was approved by the institutional review board of our hospital. Written informed consent was obtained from all enrolled patients. This study protocol was registered and assessed by a third-party panel.  



# Definition of Recurrent CSDH  

We defined the recurrence of CSDH as ipsilateral hematoma that increased on follow-up computed tomography (CT) with only objective deteriorated neurological deficits (eg, cognitive impairment, speech disturbance, hemiparesis, and gait disturbance) assessed by the attending neurosurgeon and that required reoperation within 3 months after the last operation. Subjective symptoms alone (eg, persistent headache and personality change) were not considered to be recurrent CSDH.  



# Study Population  

Patients of ${>}20$ years of age who underwent initial surgery for symptomatic unilateral CSDH with objective neurological deficits were recruited. The exclusion criteria were as follows: (1) bilateral CSDH; (2) severe dementia; (3) $\mathrm{mRS}>3$ ; (4) treatment with drugs that are reported to have a preventative effect against the recurrence of CSDH (eg, corticosteroids, carbazochrome sodium sulfonate hydrate, etizolam, statin, and tranexamic acid); and (5) treatment with Kampo medicine. In this study, bilateral CSDH was excluded because it seemed to be difficult to define recurrence when it occurred on only one side. Moreover, if the hematoma subtype (based on CT findings) differed on each side, it was also difficult to decide the hematoma subtype. Treatment with Kampo medicine was also excluded because inappropriate combinations of Kampo medicines can reduce their pharmacological actions because of drug interactions.  



# Burr Hole Surgery and Postoperative Management  

We performed burr hole irrigation with closed-system drainage under local anesthesia. Saline was used as the irrigation solution. In all cases, early ambulation was prescribed. The drain was removed within 2 days after placement. In the absence of complications, patients were generally discharged within 10 days. In all cases, antiplatelet or anticoagulant therapy was discontinued before surgery. These agents were usually restarted 1 week after the last operation. Drugs that were reported to have a preventative effect against the recurrence of CSDH and other Kampo medicines were not newly administered.  



# Hematoma Subtypes and Hematoma Volume Based on CT Findings  

We performed CT at the initial diagnosis of all cases. We classified hematomas into 4 subtypes (homogeneous type, mixed type, niveau formation type, and acute on chronic type) based on the CT findings.5,7 The homogeneous type was defined as a hematoma exhibiting homogeneous density regardless of whether it showed low, iso, or high density (Figure 1A1-1A3). The mixed type was defined as a hematoma exhibiting mixed density (Figure 1B). The niveau formation type was defined as hematoma with distinct separation (Figure 1C). The acute on chronic type was defined as previously reported7 (Figure 1D). The hematoma volumes were calculated by the XYZ/2 method.28

# Primary Evolution Items  

Underlying diseases that have been reported as risk factors for recurrence (eg, hypertension, diabetes mellitus, malignant neoplasm, cerebral vascular disease, liver disease, renal disease, coagulopathy, and heart disease), use of anticoagulants or antiplatelet drugs, etiology (traumatic or nontraumatic), and mRS before admission were self-reported by the patient/guardian and obtained from a questionnaire. The mRS at the final examination was assessed by the attending neurosurgeon. CT findings and hematoma volume were obtained from radiographic studies and assessed by the attending neurosurgeon.  



# Kampo Medicines: Goreisan and Saireito Byakujutsu Goraisan  

Goreisan is composed of 5 herbal components: Alisma tuber, Poria sclerotium, Polyporus sclerotium, A. rhizoma (byakujutsu), and cinnamon bark. It is used to adjust the body’s water balance, mainly through the inhibition of aquaporin (AQP) 4 channels and other AQP subtypes.17,29  



# Byakujutsu Saireito  

Saireito is a combination of 2 herbal medications, Shosaikoto and Goreisan. Shosaikoto is composed of 7 herbal components: Bupleurum root, Pinellia tuber, Scutellaria root, Ginseng, Jujube, Glycyrrhiza, and ginger. Shosaikoto is used for patients with a moderately strong constitution to relieve various acute febrile diseases.30 Previous investigations have reported that Shosaikoto has potential application as an anti-inflammatory drug by inhibiting proinflammatory cytokines and promoting endogenous enhancement of steroid secretion.18,31 Therefore, Saireito has both antiinflammatory and water utilization benefits.  



# Randomization  

After burr hole surgery, patients were randomized into 1 of the following 3 groups with reference to a random number table: (1) Goreisan for 3 months (Group G), (2) Saireito for 3 months (Group S), and (3) no medication (Group N). In Group G, patients received 3 months of treatment with oral Goreisan extract granules ( $(6\mathrm{g},2$ times a day [Kracie Pharm, Ltd.]), starting within 3 days after surgery. Group S received 3 months of treatment with oral Saireito extract granules $(8.1\mathrm{g},2\$ times a day [Kracie Pharm, Ltd.]), starting within 3 days after surgery.  



# Follow-Up Strategy  

After being discharged from the hospital, patients received regular follow-up CT scans in our ambulatory practice each month for 3 months after the operation. Follow-up was ceased when patient was considered cured (defined by the disappearance of hematoma on follow-up CT).  



# Statistical Analysis  

The results were analyzed using the Bartlett test, $\chi^{2}$ test of independence, Fisher exact test, and Welch test to compare the clinical characteristics and postoperative recurrence rate. As a post hoc test, the Holm test was used to compare the postoperative recurrence rate among the 3 groups. All values are expressed as the mean and standard deviation. $P$ values of $<.05$ were considered to indicate statistical significance. A multivariate logistic regression analysis was also performed to identify factors independently related to the recurrence of CSDH. Variables with $P$ values of $<.1$ in a univariate analysis were selected for the multivariate analysis. All of these statistical analyses were performed using Statcel version 4 (OMS Publishing).  



# RESULTS  



# Patient Population  

We recruited participants from April 2017 to July 2019. A flow diagram of patient enrollment and randomization is presented in Figure 2. A total of 171 patients were admitted to our institution between April 2017 and July 2019. Among these cases, 118 met the inclusion criteria and gave their consent to participate in this study. These 118 cases were randomly divided into 3 groups. Two patients in Group N, 1 in Group G, and 1 in Group S were excluded because they were lost to follow-up. Consequently, 39 patients in Group N, 37 in Group G, and 38 in Group S were analyzed. Patient demographics, hematoma subtypes, and preoperative hematoma volume on CT are shown in Table 1. No significant differences were observed among the 3 groups.  



# Effects of Kampo Medicine  

Table 2 shows the primary and secondary outcomes. The overall recurrence rate was $11.4\%$ (13/114). Each Kampo medicine reduced the recurrence rate in comparison with the control group. Kampo medicine might significantly reduce the postoperative recurrence of CSDH $\mathit{\Omega}^{\prime}P=.002\rangle$ . No patients developed complications in association with the administration of Kampo medicine. No significant differences in functional outcomes (as assessed by the mRS) were detected among the 3 groups. Figure 3 shows the results of a post hoc test using the Holm test. The recurrence rate of Group G was significantly lower than that of Group N $(5.4\%$ vs $25.6\%$ ; $P=.043^{\cdot}$ . The recurrence rate of Group S was also significantly lower than that of Group N $(2.6\%$ vs $25.6\%$ ; $P=.02,$ ). Although the recurrence rate of Group S was the lowest, the difference between Groups S and G was not statistically significant ( $2.6\%$ vs $5.4\%$ ; $P=.61$ ).  



# Comparison of the Clinical Characteristics of Patients With Recurrent CSDH  

We compared age, recurrence period, and recurrence rate according to the hematoma subtypes among the 3 groups of patients with recurrent CSDH. Dot plots showed no significant difference in age or recurrence period among the 3 groups (Figure 4A and 4B). We investigated whether the recurrence rate differed among the hematoma subtypes in patients taking Kampo medicine. Table 3 shows a comparison of the recurrence rate in each hematoma subtype between Group N and patients taking Kampo medicine (Groups $\mathrm{G}+\mathrm{S}$ ). Kampo medicine may have reduced the recurrence rate in all types of hematoma, especially the acute on chronic type, which showed a statistically significant reduction $(P=.008)$ .

# DISCUSSION  

The recurrence of CSDH requires reoperation and causes prolonged hospital stays and higher morbidity and mortality.32,33 Our results also showed that recurrence was an independent risk factor for deteriorated functional outcomes. Hence, effective and safe medications to prevent the recurrence of CSDH are essential. In Japan, some case series have shown that Kampo medicines such as Goreisan and Saireito diminished CSDH without surgical treatment in patients who were unable to undergo surgical treatment.15,34 Since then, Goreisan and Saireito have been used empirically in the prevention of postoperative recurrence of CSDH rather than other anti-inflammatory medications, such as steroids or statins, because Kampo medicines have few side effects and are more accepted by the Japanese population. Although some retrospective studies showed that Goreisan and Saireito reduced the rate of postoperative recurrence, no prospective and randomized studies have demonstrated that Kampo medicine reduced the recurrence rate in overall patients. This is the first study to show that Kampo medicine reduced the recurrence rate in overall patients.  



# Pharmacologic Action of Kampo Medicine in CSDH  

Recently, AQPs have been considered one of the factors affecting CSDH growth.14,15,21 AQPs are water channel proteins that exist in cell membranes and play important roles in maintaining homeostasis, as well as cell migration and proliferation.35,36

Among the 13 subtypes of AQPs, AQP1 and AQP4 have been reported to be expressed in the outer membrane of CSDHs.15,21,37 AQP1 might affect angiogenesis of the outer membrane and be the source of CSDH enlargement.21,37 AQP4 is expressed in the outer membrane of CSDHs, especially where inflammatory cell invasion is frequently observed.15,21 AQP4 might cause fluid to flow into the subdual space and contribute to the occurrence and enlargement of hematoma s.15,21 In fact, some experimental studies have shown that Goreisan and Saireito have a suppressive effect on the expression and function of AQP1, AQP4, and other channels.17,29,38,39 Thus, it is considered that their suppression of AQP1 and AQP4 might contribute to reducing the incidence of recurrent CSDH.  



# Anti-Inflammatory Action May Be the Key Point in the Recurrence of CSDH  

The inflammatory process is considered important in the pathogenesis of recurrent CSDH because inflammatory cells or inflammatory-associated substances induce angiogenesis and/or growth of the outer membrane of CSDH.5,40-45 The degree of inflammatory cell invasion is essentially correlated with the expression of AQP4 in CSDH.15 The persistence of unrestricted inflammatory processes within the hematoma capsule is considered to trigger and exacerbate CSDH.5,40-45 Moreover, the recurrence of CSDH tends to occur in the setting of strong inflammation.5,40-45 Therefore, the regulation of inflammation may also be essential for reducing the recurrence rate in patients with CSDH.5 In fact, it has been reported that the administration of statins and steroids reduces the postoperative recurrence rate through anti-inflammatory effects.9,11,46,47 Because byakujutsu Goreisan has a stronger anti-inflammatory effect than sojutsu Goreisan, 25-27 this study may have shown a favorable effect, which was not observed in other prospective randomized studies. Moreover, Saireito has a stronger anti-inflammatory benefit than Goreisan15,18 because it inhibits proinflammatory cytokines and promotes the endogenous enhancement of steroid secretion through the secretion of hypothalamic corticotropin releasing factor.15,48,49 Hence, we suspected that Saireito might be associated with a greater reduction in the recurrence rate in comparison to Goreisan. In line with our expectations, this study showed that the Saireito group had the lowest recurrence rate.

# Safety of Kampo Medicine  

Kampo medicine has few side effects because it involves the administration of herbal medicines. However, there is a possibility of side effects, including general fatigue, allergic reaction, hepatic dysfunction, and interstitial pneumonia. We investigated the complications of Kampo medicine as the secondary end point of this study and found no complications. Furthermore, no complications were reported in previous studies. Thus, Kampo medicine might be safer than other anti-inflammatory drugs, such as steroids or statins.  



# Acute on Chronic Type May Be Specific for CSDH  

We have previously reported that the acute on chronic type is significantly associated with CSDH with brain herniation signs on arrival because the abrupt expansion of hematoma tends to occur and causes the rapid progression of consciousness disturbance.7 In this study, we found that the acute on chronic type was the only independent risk factor for recurrence and that Kampo medicine treatment was most effective for this subtype of CSDH. The acute on chronic type may be associated with a higher inflammatory action in comparison with other hematoma subtypes.  



# Study Limitations  

This study was associated with some limitations. First, the study population was smaller in comparison with other prospective randomized studies. Second, we excluded cases of bilateral hematoma. These factors may have influenced the results. Third, there was neither a placebo arm nor a comparator arm to analyze the effects of conventional anti-inflammatory medications such as steroids and atorvastatin. These may be useful to analyze the comparative effect. Hence, this study may be considered a pilot study. Further investigation including a larger sample, a placebo arm, and a comparator arm of conventional antiinflammatory medications may be needed to validate our results.

# CONCLUSION  

This prospective randomized study showed that Kampo medicine significantly reduced the postoperative recurrence rate in the overall population of patients with CSDH. Goreisan and Saireito, which contain $A.$ . rhizoma (byakujutsu), may be effective because of their strong anti-inflammatory action.
