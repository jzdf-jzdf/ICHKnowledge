# ABSTRACT  

Objective To evaluate the non-inferiority of endovascular treatment (EVT) alone versus intravenous thrombolysis (IVT) followed by EVT and to assess its heterogeneity across prespecified subgroups.  

Methods  We pooled data from two trials (SKIP in Japan; DEVT in China). Individual patient data were pooled to assess outcomes and heterogeneity of treatment effect. The primary outcome was functional independence (modified Rankin Scale score 0–2) at 90 days. Safety outcomes included symptomatic intracranial hemorrhage (slCH) and 90-day mortality.  

Results  We included 438 patients (217 EVT alone; 221 combined $NT+EVT,$ . The meta-analysis failed to demonstrate the non-inferiority of EVT alone over combined $N\mathsf{T}{+}\mathsf{E}\mathsf{V}\mathsf{T}$ in achieving 90-day functional independence $(56.7\%$ vs $51.6\%$ ; adjusted common odds ratio (cOR)=1.27,95%CI0.84 to 1.92,Poninfeioty . Effect sizes favoring EVT alone were present with stroke onset to puncture time longer than 180 min $:\mathsf{COR}=2.28,$ 95% CI 1.18 to 4.38, pinteraction ≤180 vs >180 min and intracranial internal carotid artery ICA occlusions (for ICA ${\mathsf{cOR}}{=}3.04\$ , $95\%$ CI 1.10 to 8.43, $\uprho_{\mathrm{interacion}}$ ICA vs $\mathsf{\Pi}_{\mathrm{MCA}}\mathsf{=}0.08\)$ . The rates of sICH $(6.5\%$ vs $9.0\%$ ${\ttcOR}=0.77$ , $95\%\subset$ 0.37 to 1.61) and 90-day mortality ( $12.9\%$ vs $13.6\%$ ; ${\mathsf{cOR}}{=}1.05$ , $95\%\mathsf{C}|0.58$ to 1.89) were comparable. Conclusions  The cumulative data of these two recent Asian trials failed to unequivocally demonstrate the non-inferiority of EVT alone over combined $N{\mathsf{T}}{\mathsf{+EVT}}$ However, our study suggests a potential role for more individualized decision-­making. Specifically, Asian patients with stroke onset to EVT longer than 180 min, as well as those with intracranial ICA occlusions and those with atrial fibrillation might have better outcomes with EVT alone than with combined $N{\mathsf{T}}{\mathsf{+EV}}$ .

# INTRODUCTION  

Endovascular treatment (EVT) has become the standard of care for properly selected patients presenting with anterior circulation large vessel occlusion strokes (LVOS) within up to 24 hours from symptoms onset.1–8 Current guidelines state that patients eligible for intravenous thrombolysis (IVT) should receive IV thrombolytics even if mechanical thrombectomy is being considered.9 However, while IVT prior to EVT has many theoretical benefits it also has potential disadvantages, including additional risks and costs.10  

In this context, six randomized clinical trials (three in Asia, two in Europe, and one in Australasia) were recently completed to evaluate the non-inferiority of EVT alone (primary/direct thrombectomy) versus intravenous alteplase followed by EVT (combined/bridging therapy) in patients with anterior circulation LVOS who presented to a thrombectomy-capable center and were eligible for IVT within 4.5 hours from stroke onset.11–16 Despite their individual merits and meaningful conclusions, each of these trials had some limitations, mostly related to their relatively limited sample sizes and generous non-inferiority margins. The current individual patient-level pooled analysis aims to pool data from two of the Asian trials to better evaluate the non-inferiority of primary thrombectomy versus intravenous alteplase followed by EVT (bridging or combined therapy) and assess any significant heterogeneity of treatment effect across prespecified subgroups.

# METHODS Study inclusion and procedures  

We designed the Systemic THrombolysis Randomization IN Endovascular Stroke Therapy (SHRINE) Collaboration to pool patient-level data from two clinical trials (SKIP in Japan and DEVT in China)12 13 performed between January 2017 and May 2020. In those trials, patients with acute ischemic stroke caused by LVOS involving the intracranial internal carotid artery (ICA) or the proximal middle cerebral artery (MCA-­M1 segment) who were eligible for IVT with alteplase within 4.5 hours of stroke onset were randomly assigned to receive either EVT alone (primary EVT) or standard care with combined $\mathrm{IVT+EVT}$ (control), with a primary outcome of functional independence on the modified Rankin Scale (mRS) score 0–2 at 90 days.  

The two trial populations were relatively homogeneous but had a few significant differences. The SKIP trial included patients aged 18–85 years with baseline National Institutes of Health Stroke Scale (NIHSS) score $\geq6$ (range 0 (no symptoms) to 42 (most severe neurologic deficits)) who were functionally independent prior to stroke (mRS score 0–2; range 0 (no symptoms) to 6 (death)) and had small to moderate infarct burden on baseline imaging (eg, Alberta Stroke Program Early CT Score (ASPECTS $\geq6$ on non-contrast CT or $\geq5$ on diffusion-weighted imaging; range 0 to 10, with higher scores indicating fewer early ischemic changes)). The DEVT trial did not have an upper age limit and included patients regardless of their baseline NIHSS score or ASPECTS but excluded those with pre-stroke mRS score $\geq2$ . Treatment assignments were also very similar with the exception that the SKIP trial used a reduced dose of alteplase $\left(0.6\mathrm{mg}\right)$ per $\mathrm{kg}$ body weight, according to Japanese guidelines) while DEVT used the standard (0.9 mg per kg body weight) dose. The study statisticians from each trial collected the patient-level data by direct access to the study databases. An independent statistician (TPM) collated all data from the two individual trials and cross-checked them against their previous publications.  

# Outcomes  

The prespecified primary outcome in this pooled analysis was the proportion of patients with functional independence (mRS score 0–2) at 90 days. Prespecified secondary outcomes included the mRS score distribution (ordinal shift analysis) at 90 days; the proportion of patients with excellent (mRS score 0–1) and ambulatory $(\mathrm{mRS~}0{-}3)$ outcomes at 90 days; the rates of successful reperfusion at the end of the endovascular procedure (defined as modified Treatment in Cerebral Infarction (mTICI) scale score of 2b or 3, corresponding to reperfusion of at least $50\%$ of the affected vascular territory)17; the stroke severity at 24 hours after stroke onset as measured with the NIHSS; the change in NIHSS score from baseline to 24 hours; and the proportion of patients with major early neurological recovery at 3–7 days or discharge (defined as a reduction in NIHSS score from baseline of at least 10 points or reaching 0–1).  

Safety outcomes were the proportion of patients with symptomatic intracranial hemorrhage (as defined by the Safe Implementation of Thrombolysis in Stroke–Monitoring Study (SITS-­MOST), local or remote parenchymal hemorrhage type 2 on the post-treatment imaging scan, combined with a neurological deterioration of 4 points or more on the NIHSS from baseline, or from the lowest NIHSS value between baseline and 24 hours, or leading to death),18 neuroradiological parenchymal hematoma type 1 (hematoma occupying $<30\%$ of the infarcted territory with mild mass effect) or type 2 (hematoma occupying $>30\%$ of the infarcted territory with substantial mass effect) within $36{-}48$ hours, and mortality within 90 days.

# Statistical analysis  

Individual patient data were collated by an independent statistician (TPM) to assess the primary efficacy outcome of 90-day functional independence (mRS score 0–2) as well as the secondary efficacy outcomes, including the mRS score distribution (ordinal shift analysis) at 90 days and safety outcomes including symptomatic intracranial hemorrhage (sICH) and 90-day mortality in the pooled population, and to study the heterogeneity of this treatment effect across prespecified subgroups. This analysis employed a one-stage regression approach for individualpatient data meta-analyses in which common odds ratios (cORs), $95\%$ CIs, and $\mathrm{P}$ values are computed using multilevel regression models.19 In order to appropriately account for inter-tral variability, intercepts were included as a trial-level random effect; treatment assignment and baseline covariates were included as fixed effects. Covariates included age, sex, baseline stroke severity (NIHSS score), baseline ASPECTS, site of occlusion, glucose, and time from stroke onset to arterial puncture.  

The primary analysis aimed to answer the following research question: ‘When considering the chances of functional independence at 90 days, is endovascular mechanical thrombectomy alone non-inferior to the current standard of care (intravenous alteplase followed by mechanical thrombectomy) in eligible patients presenting with acute ischemic strokes due to proximal anterior circulation occlusions?'. To satisfy the non-inferiority hypothesis, the lower boundary of the two-sided $95\%$ CI for the cOR of the primary outcome (90-day mRS score 0-2) with the direct EVT group as compared with the bridging group was prespecified at $\geq0.85$ . For analyses of the full $\mathrm{mRS}$ , we report unadjusted and adjusted treatment effects using cOR to indicate the odds that the intervention would lead to an improvement of $\geq1$ points on the mRS in a shift analysis. Missing data for relevant variables included: 20 $(4.6\%)$ for NIHSS score at 24 hours, 23 $(5.3\%)$ for NIHSS score at discharge/7 days, 2 $(0.46\%)$ for ICH, 4 $(0.91\%)$ for mTICI grade, and 4 $(0.91\%)$ for occlusion site. Ten complete datasets were imputed using fully conditional specification.20  

We examined the heterogeneity of treatment effect by prespecified clinically relevant variables on the primary outcome (mRS score 0–2 at 90 days) and the two main secondary outcomes (mRS score distribution at 90 days and 90-day mortality) using a multiplicative interaction term (treatment×prespecified variable) and mixed methods modeling. Prespecified variables included age, sex, baseline stroke severity on NIHSS, baseline ASPECTS, site of occlusion, glucose levels, time from stroke onset to arterial puncture, atrial fibrillation, diabetes mellitus, smoking, concomitant ipsilateral carotid artery stenosis or occlusion (tandem lesion), and stroke mechanism (cardioembolic, intracranial atherosclerotic disease, other). Stratum-specific treatment effects along with the P value for the interaction term were reported graphically using forest plots. Statistical analyses were conducted using SPSS (version 26; Armonk, New York, USA). All P values were two-sided and statistical significance was defined as a $\mathrm{P}$ value of $<0.05$ . For secondary analyses, there was no prespecified plan for adjustment for multiplicity, and no definite inferences can be drawn from these data.

# RESULTS  

By pooling data from the DEVT and SKIP trials, we acquired data for 438 subjects, including 221 assigned to combined $\mathrm{EVT+IVT}$ therapy (control population) and 217 assigned to EVT alone (intervention population). The median (IQR) for the age, baseline NIHSS score, baseline ASPECTS, and time from stroke onset to arterial puncture in the overall population was 72 (64–78) years, 16 (12.5–21), 8 (6–9), and 182 (134–230) minutes, respectively. Two hundred and sixty $(59.4\%)$ patients were male. Occlusions of the intracranial ICA and MCA were present in 112 $(25.6\%)$ and 326 $(74.4\%)$ patients, respectively. Baseline characteristics were generally well-balanced between the populations (table 1), but there was a trend towards longer time from stroke onset to arterial puncture in the combined therapy group (188 (146–232) vs 170 (128–228) min, $\mathrm{P}{=}0.06)$ .

The proportion of patients achieving functional independence (mRS score 0–2) at 90 days was $56.7\%$ in the EVT alone and $51.6\%$ in the combined therapy group (unadjusted $\mathrm{OR}{=}1.23\$ , $95\%\mathrm{CI}0.84\$ to 1.79); adjusted $\mathrm{cOR}{=}1.27$ , $95\%\mathrm{CI}0.84\$ to 1.92) (table 2; online supplemental figure S1). As the lower boundary of the confidence interval (0.84) was slightly lower than the prespecified non-inferiority margin of 0.85, we could not establish non-inferiority for the primary outcome $(\mathrm{P_{non-inferiority}}=0.06)$ (online supplemental figure S2). Functional outcomes were overall similar across the two groups (90-day mRS shift: adjusted $\mathrm{cOR}=1.01$ , $95\%\mathrm{CI}0.72$ to 1.40). There were no differences in the rates of excellent outcomes (90-day mRS score 0-1: $39.2\%$ vs $37.6\%$ ; adjusted $\mathrm{OR}{=}1.03$ , $95\%\mathrm{CI}0.67$ to 1.58), ambulatory outcomes (90-day mRS score 0-3: $70\%$ vs $67\%$ ; adjusted $\mathrm{OR}{=}1{.}11$ , $95\%\mathrm{CI}0.70\$ to 1.77), early neurological response $(56.7\%$ vs $52.0\%$ ; adjusted $\mathrm{OR}{=}1.14$ , $95\%\mathrm{CI}0.77$ to 1.70), or successful reperfusion $\mathrm{(mTICI~}2\mathrm{b}-3\colon88.0\%$ vs $89.6\%$ ; adjusted $\mathrm{OR}{=}0.83$ , $95\%\mathrm{CI}0.45$ to 1.52) across the EVT alone and combined groups.  

The rates of sICH $(6.5\%$ vs $9.0\%$ ; adjusted $\mathrm{{OR=0.77}}$ , $95\%\mathrm{CI}0.37$ to 1.61), any parenchymal hematoma $(8.8\%$ vs $13.1\%$ ; adjusted $\mathrm{OR}{=}0.62$ , $95\%\mathrm{CI}0.33$ to 1.17), and 90-day mortality $(12.9\%$ vs $13.6\%$ ; adjusted $\mathrm{OR}{=}1.05$ , $95\%\mathrm{CI}0.58\$ to 1.89) were also similar for the EVT alone and combined groups. However, the proportions of any ICH ( $26.3\%$ vs $39.4\%$ ; adjusted $\mathrm{OR}{=}0.54$ , $95\%\mathrm{CI}0.35$ to 0.82) and arterial puncture site complications $(0.5\%$ vs $4.1\%$ ; adjusted $\mathrm{OR}{=}0.11$ , $95\%\mathrm{CI}$ 0.01 to 0.86) were significantly lower in patients treated with EVT alone (table 2). Subgroup analysis for any ICH and parenchymal hematoma were also consistent with the overall findings (online supplemental figure S3,S4).  

For subgroup analysis of functional independence at 90 days, effect sizes favoring EVT alone were present with stroke onset to puncture time longer than $180\mathrm{min}$ (cOR 2.28, $95\%$ CI 1.18 to 4.38); Pinteraction ≤180 vs $_{>180\mathrm{min}}=0.02\$ ) and intracranial ICA occlusions (cOR for ICA, 3.04, 95% CI 1.10 to 8.43; $\mathrm{P_{interaction}}$ ICA vs $_{\mathrm{{\scriptscriptstyleMCA}}}=0.08)$ (figure  1A). We also observed heterogeneity of treatment effect across time from stroke onset to puncture $\leq180\mathrm{vs}>180\mathrm{min}$ for the overall mRS distribution shift at 90 days (figure 1B). In addition, there was significant heterogeneity favoring EVT alone in patients with atrial fibrillation both for sICH $(\mathrm{P_{interaction}}=0.02)\$ and for 90-day mortality $(\mathrm{P_{interaction}}=0.03)$ (figure 2A,B).  

# DISCUSSION  

In this individual-patient level pooled analysis encompassing 438 patients treated in the Japanese SKIP trial and the Chinese DEVT trial, randomization within 4.5 hours of stroke onset to EVT alone resulted in similar overall outcomes to the standard of care of combined IVT followed by EVT treatment. However, the study failed to demonstrate the non-inferiority of EVT alone over combined therapy. Notably, effect sizes favoring EVT alone were present in patients with stroke onset to puncture times longer than $180\mathrm{min}$ , intracranial ICA occlusions, and atrial fibrillation. These findings support the consideration of a tailored approach to the decision-­making regarding intravenous thrombolysis with alteplase in patients presenting directly to a thrombectomy-capable center.  

The fact that the lower boundary of the CI for the OR of the primary outcome closely approximated the prespecified noninferiority margin (0.84 vs 0.85; $\mathrm{P_{non-inferiority}}=0.06)$ is intriguing and raises the question as to whether a large sample size might have yielded a different conclusion. The chosen threshold of $0.85$ was lower than that used for the functional outcomes in SKIP $(0.74)^{12}$ and DIRECT-­MT/MR CLEAN no IV trials (both at 0.80).11 14 This threshold was also stricter than that used in previous non-inferiority trials in the stroke thrombectomy space, including the COMPASS and Penumbra Separator 3-D trials.21 22

Nonetheless, it is important to acknowledge that we have used a post hoc threshold since the results of the SKIP and DEVT trials were already known at the time of the pooled analysis. Furthermore, there is a consensus that these are overly generous margins. Indeed, stroke expert survey studies have identified that the minimal clinically important differences for functional independence across two treatments should be set at much lower values, ranging between $1\%$ and $5\%$ .23  

Two study-level meta-analyses including 1633 patients from the published DIRECT-­MT, SKIP, DEVT, and MR CLEAN NO-IV trials supported the non-inferiority of EVT alone over the combined treatment (crude cumulative rates of 90-day mRS score 0–2: $46.0\%$ vs $45.5\%$ ; absolute risk difference: $1\%$ ( $95\%$ CI $-4\%$ to $5\%$ ) while suggesting that EVT alone reduced the odds of successful reperfusion and any ICH.24 25 Notwithstanding, opposite effect directions were reported in the recent publications of the SWIFT-DIRECT and DIRECT-SAFE trials.1 16  

An expert committee of the European Stroke Organization (ESO)-European Society for Minimally Invasive Neurological Therapy (ESMINT) conducted a study-level random-effects meta-analysis of the six randomized controlled trials encompassing 2331 patients. Compared with patients randomized to combined therapy, the pooled unadjusted OR for good outcome in patients randomized to EVT alone was 0.93 ( $95\%$ CI 0.79 to 1.10, $\mathrm{P}{=}0.38$ ; $\scriptstyle{\mathrm{I}^{2}=0\%}.$ ). Non-inferiority was not met based on the prespecified criterion set by the committee. There were no significant differences in reduced disability (90-day mRS score shift adjusted $_{\mathrm{cOR}=0.92}$ , $95\%\mathrm{CI}0.80\$ to 1.07, $\mathrm{P}{=}0.27$ ; $\scriptstyle{\mathrm{I}^{2}=0\%}^{\mathrm{~}}$ 0, excellent outcome (90-day mRS 0-1 unadjusted $\mathrm{OR}{=}0.99$ , $95\%\mathrm{CI}$ (0.82 to 1.18), $\mathrm{P}{=}0.88$ ; $\scriptstyle{\mathrm{I}^{2}=0\%}^{\mathrm{}}$ ), $\mathrm{sICH}$ (unadjusted $\mathrm{{OR=0.77}}$ , $95\%\mathrm{CI}0.52\$ to 1.13, $\mathrm{P}{=}0.18$ ; $\scriptstyle{\mathrm{I}^{2}=0\%}$ ), or all-cause 90-day mortality (unadjusted $\mathrm{OR}{=}1.06$ , $95\%\mathrm{CI}$ 0.84 to 1.35, $\mathrm{P}{=}0.60$ ; $\mathrm{I}^{2}{=}0\%\stackrel{.}{=}$ ) with EVT alone versus combined therapy. Both successful reperfusion (unadjusted $\mathrm{{OR}}{=}0.72$ ,  

$95\%\mathrm{CI}0.57$ to 0.92, $\mathrm{P}{=}0.01$ ; $\scriptstyle{\mathrm{I}^{2}=0\%}^{\mathrm{}}$ ) and any ICH (unadjusted $\mathrm{OR}{=}0.80$ , $95\%\mathrm{CI}0.66$ to 0.96, $\mathrm{P}{=}0.02$ ; $\mathrm{I}^{\dot{2}}{=}10\%$ were less frequent with EVT alone.26  

It is possible that the lack of increased risk of sICH with bridging versus EVT alone in the aforementioned meta-analysis was related to its relatively small sample size given the overall low frequency of sICH as an event. A cohort study comprising 15 832 patients treated with EVT within 6 hours of time last known well across 555 hospitals participating in the US Get With The Guidelines-Stroke Registry compared the outcomes of those receiving $\mathrm{'n}{=}5284$ , $33.4\%^{\cdot}$ ) versus not receiving intravenous alteplase. Patients treated with alteplase were younger, had an earlier time of hospital arrival, and were less likely to have atrial fibrillation, hypertension, and diabetes. IVT was associated with lower in-hospital mortality ( $11.1\%$ vs $13.9\%$ ; $\mathrm{aOR}{=}0.83$ , $95\%\mathrm{CI}0.77$ to $0.89$ ,; $\mathrm{P}{<}0.001$ ), higher chances of functional independence at discharge (mRS score 0–2: $28.5\%$ vs $20.7\%$ , $\mathrm{aOR}=1.36$ , $95\%$ CI 1.28 to 1.45; $\mathrm{P}{<}0.001$ ), and better reperfusion (mTICI 2b–3: $90.9\%$ vs $88.0\%$ ; $\mathrm{aOR}{=}1.39$ , $95\%\mathrm{CI}1.28\$ to 1.50, $\mathrm{P}{<}0.001$ ) but also with higher risk of sICH $(6.5\%$ vs $5.3\%$ ; $\mathrm{OR}{=}1.28$ , $95\%\mathrm{CI}1.16$ to 1.42, $\mathrm{P}{<}0.001$ ) after adjusting for other covariates.27 Our analysis had a much smaller sample size and failed to show differences in the rates of sICH but did demonstrate significant differences in the rates of both any ICH and arterial puncture site complications, favoring EVT alone.  

The overall data suggest that there seems to be a benefit to the bridging approach with intravenous alteplase in relation to EVT alone. Nevertheless, this benefit appears to be relatively low and needs to be balanced against the additional costs and risks associated with intravenous alteplase. Thus, a more individualized approach should be considered in the future.10 In this context, the results of our subgroup analysis are particularly noteworthy. The time dependence of the effect of IVT for both reperfusion and clinical outcomes is well recognized.28 29  

Therefore, it is not surprising that we found an effective modification for bridging therapy according to time from stroke onset to puncture shorter versus longer than $180\mathrm{min}$ . Likewise, the reperfusion rates with IVT are highly influenced by occlusion site. Our data suggest that EVT alone might be the preferable approach for patients with intracranial ICA occlusions. Indeed, previous studies have demonstrated that reperfusion occurs only in about $5{-}10\%$ of the patients treated with intravenous alteplase.30 31 Finally, our analysis found effect modification by atrial fibrillation with higher chances of both sICH and 90-day mortality with combined therapy as compared with EVT alone. The results are in alignment with previous studies demonstrating worse outcomes in patients receiving IVT with atrial fibrillation than in those without atrial fibrillation .32–34 The IRIS (Improving Reperfusion strategies in Ischemic Stroke) consortium plans a patient-level pooled analysis of all six randomized clinical trials on the use of intravenous alteplase prior to EVT. This pooled analysis will comprise 2041 patients and will hopefully help to identify the different patient subgroups that might benefit from one strategy versus the other.  

# Limitations  

Before we dive into the specific limitations of this analysis, it is critical to understand that the results of any studies comparing EVT alone with the combined approach are limited to very specific settings. These results do not apply to any primary stroke centers, to thrombectomy-capable centers with suboptimal EVT workflows, or to situations when neuroendovascular treatment is not immediately available. Under these conditions, intravenous thrombolysis should be unequivocally provided to all eligible patients. Our study has all the limitations inherent to any post hoc analysis. Subgroup analyses are potentially subject to type I error due to the multiplicity of testing. The employment of techniques to adjust for multiplicity would probably have made the results of the subgroup analysis non-significant. The demographic applicability of our results may be limited to the Asian population. As compared with Western patients, Asian patients tend to present with more severe ischemic strokes, have more sICH and overall complications after alteplase, and have worse discharge functional outcomes and higher in-hospital mortality.35 From a pathophysiological standpoint, Asian subjects have a significantly higher incidence of intracranial atherosclerotic disease, and the presence of underlying stenosis may be associated with a lower likelihood of response to intravenous thrombolysis.36 Finally, recent randomized controlled trials suggest that intravenous tenecteplase is a reasonable alternative to alteplase while offering some advantages including greater ease of use and lower costs.37 38 Comparative studies using tenecteplase are therefore needed.  

# CONCLUSIONS  

Although the cumulative data of these two trials do not confirm the non-inferiority of EVT alone versus combined therapy, the clinical benefit of bridging in Asian patients seems to be relatively modest, and its risk–benefit profile probably varies across different population subgroups. Our study suggests a potential role for more individualized decision-­making. Specifically, Asian patients with stroke onset to EVT longer than $180\mathrm{min}$ , as well as those with intracranial ICA occlusions and those with atrial fibrillation, may have better outcomes with EVT alone than combined therapy with intravenous alteplase followed by EVT.
