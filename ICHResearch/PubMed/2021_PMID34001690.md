# Dual Versus Triple Therapy in Patients with Acute Coronary Syndrome and an Anticoagulation Indication: A Systematic Review with Meta-Analysis and Trial-Sequential Analysis  

Rahul Gupta MD,\* Aaqib H. Malik MD, MPH,\* Rajiv Gupta MS, Purva Ranchal MD,\* Srikanth Yandrapalli MD $\cdot\ddag$ Brijesh Patel DO,s William H. Frishman MD, $\ast\neq$ Wilbert S. Aronow MD, FACC, FAHA,\* Jalaj Garg MD, FACC, FESC  

Abstract: Choosing an antithrombotic regime in patients with acute coronary syndrome (ACS) and a concomitant indication for anticoagulation is a challenge commonly encountered by clinicians. Our aim in this article is to evaluate the safety and efficacy of triple antithrombotic therapy (TT, anticoagulant plus dual antiplatelet) versus dual antithrombotic therapy [dual therapy (DT), anticoagulant plus single antiplatelet] in patients with ACs. We included all randomized trials comparing the outcomes of single versus dual antiplatelet therapy in patients with ACS on anticoagulants. The primary outcome was major adverse cardiac events (MACE). Other outcomes studied were all-cause mortality, cardiovascular mortality, myocardial infarction (MI), stroke, stent thrombosis (ST), and major bleeding. The Mantel-Haenszel risk ratio (RR) random-effects model was used to summarize data. Six studies, with a total of 11,437 patients, met our selection criteria. With a follow-up duration of 9-14 months, there was no significant difference between DT and TT in terms of MACE [RR 0.96; $95\%$ confidence interval (Cl), 0.79-1.17], all-cause mortality (RR 1.00; $95\%$ CI, 0.77-1.29), cardiovascular mortality (RR 1.03; 95% CI, 0.79-1.34), MI (RR 1.14; $95\%$ CI, 0.90-1.45), stroke (RR 0.83; $95\%$ CI, 0.56-1.23), and ST (RR 1.32; $95\%$ CI, 0.87-2.01). Compared with TT, DT was associated with significant reductions in major bleeding $4.1\%$ versus $6.5\%$ (RR 0.61; $95\%$ CI, 0.45-0.81; number needed to treat $=42$ ), clinically significant bleeding $10.5\%$ versus $16.4\%$ (RR 0.62; $95\%$ CI, 0.48-0.80) and intracranial hemorrhage $0.4\%$ versus $0.8\%$ (RR 0.43; $95\%$ CI, 0.24-0.77). In patients on anticoagulant therapy, the strategy of single antiplatelet therapy (DT) confers a benefit of less major bleeding with no difference in MACE, all-cause mortality, cardiovascular mortality, MI, stroke, and ST.  

Key Words: anticoagulation, acute coronary syndrome, percutaneous coronary intervention, dual therapy, triple therapy, meta-analysis

significant proportion of patients undergoing percutaneous coronary intervention (PCI) for acute coronary syndrome (ACS) have other comorbidities, including atrial fibrillation (AF), venous thromboembolism (VTE), and/or mechanical heart valves. These patients present a unique dilemma as they require an anticoagulant in addition to antiplatelet therapy for the prevention of thromboembolic events. Observational data have shown that rates of major bleeding in patients on triple therapy (TT) after a PCI are $2.2\%$ by 30 days, with an increased risk at the end of 1 year $(12\%)$ . Current guidelines endorse short-term TT with an oral anticoagulant and dual antiplatelet agents (DAPT) in high-risk patients.12 Dual therapy (DT), in the form of an antiplatelet agent with an oral anticoagulant, has shown improved safety compared to TT.? This improved safety, in terms of lower hemorrhagic complications with DT, is expected; however, the efficacy, in terms of reducing ischemic complications, has been tested extensively in numerous trials recently.46 In light of the latest data, we conducted a systematic review and a meta-analysis of all the randomized controlled trials (RCTs) to assess the efficacy and safety of DT compared to TT in patients with ACS with an indication for anticoagulation. Additionally, we performed a trial sequential analysis (TSA) to assess the reliability of the conclusions of our meta-analysis.  

# METHODS  

# Study Selection and Outcomes  

PubMed, Embase, and Cochrane library were searched from the inception through November 07, 2019. The keywords of percutaneous coronary intervention OR PCI OR acute coronary syndrome OR ACS AND vitamin K antagonist (VKA) OR VKA OR warfarin OR dabigatran OR rivaroxaban OR apixaban OR edoxaban OR novel oral anticoagul\* OR new oral anticoagul (NOAC)\* OR DOAC (direct-acting anticoagul)\* were used to identify relevant RCTs with the limits for human and RCTs activated. All references of the retrieved articles were reviewed for further identification of potentially relevant studies. The search methodology was not limited to a specific population or a clinical presentation and is depicted in detail in Fig. 1. After reduplication, the studies’ titles and abstracts were screened for relevancy. Full manuscripts of potentially relevant studies were then reviewed by two independent authors (R.G. and A.M.). RCTs were considered for inclusion if they consisted of patients who underwent PCI and required oral anticoagulation for any indication. All studies with retrospective design, abstracts, case reports, conference presentations, editorials, reviews, and expert opinions were excluded from our analysis.  

Our primary outcome of interest was “trial defined major adverse cardiac events” (MACE). Other outcomes of interest were theInternational Society on Thrombosis and Haemostasis major bleeding or clinically significant bleeding, ischemic stroke, stent thrombosis (ST), myocardial infarction (MI), all-cause death, and cardiovascular death. Throughout this process, we followed the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses guidelines for all stages of the design and implementation. An unapproved dose of rivaroxaban $(2.5\mathrm{mg}$ twice daily) was not considered for our analysis. Additionally, we concentrated on only VKA-based TT; VKA-based DT, and NOAC-based TT were excluded since the data were only available in one of the trials. The subgroup analyses were performed by dividing the groups into patients who underwent anticoagulation with warfarin and those who had DOACs, along with a division of studies based on the indication of anticoagulation. Finally, for this analysis, data for both dosages of dabigatran ( $110\mathrm{mg}$ and $150\mathrm{mg}$ were combined. Study quality was assessed via the Jadad scoring system for RCTs.7

# Statistical Analysis  

The Review Manager (RevMan) computer software (version 5.3); Copenhagen: The Nordic Cochrane Centre, The Cochrane Collaboration, 2014; and R version 3.5.1 (R Development Core Team, 2010) was used to perform the meta-analysis. We used event rates for categorical variables and utilized a random-effects model to account for the expected clinical and methodological heterogeneity among trials. Results are presented as a risk ratio (RR) with $95\%$ confidence intervals (CI). The consistency and heterogeneity were assessed by calculating $\mathrm{I}^{2}$ , and publication bias was assessed by creating a funnel plot to evaluate for the existence of small-study effects.  

TSA is a meta-analysis technique that combines features from cumulative meta-analysis with sample size calculation and interim analysis.\* It creates monitoring boundaries to identify if the definite benefit, harm, or futility (lack of difference between the study arms) can be determined.By combining statistical techniques, the boundaries are adjusted to the minimal clinically significant difference, the power of meta-analysis, data heterogeneity, and repetitive testing. In summary, TSA informs if current results and amount of information (number of patients, events) are enough for definitive conclusions. In the TSA method (proposed by Wetterslev et al), every trial is added in chronological order and the meta-analysis is performed at every step. It creates a cumulative meta-analysis (represented by the Z curve) and boundaries for futility, benefit, and harm; if the curve crosses one of the boundaries, or reaches the optimal sample size line, a definite conclusion can be assumed. We performed these analyses, defining the power as $80\%$ ,type I error as $5\%$ ,and the expected relative risk reduction between DT and TT group as $20\%$ ,as is the standard practice.  

# RESULTS  

The initial search yielded 408 articles. After electronic and manual deduplication on title review, 304 studies remained. A further abstract review led to the exclusion of the least relevant articles, and 12 articles were selected for the final full-text review. After full-text review, there were 6 RCTs with 11,437 patients for which quantitative analysis was possible (Fig. 1).3-12 Four of these trials were performed in patients with AF. Table 1 shows the baseline characteristics of patients in the included trials, respectively. The mean age of our study population was 70.7 years with $75\%$ male patients. The follow-up duration ranged from 9 to 14 months.  

# Ischemic Events  

There was no statistically significant difference between DT and TT in terms of trial defined MACE (RR 0.96; $95\%$ CI, $0.79\mathrm{-}1.17$ $P=0.71$ $\mathrm{I}^{2}=0\%$ ) (Fig. 2), all-cause mortality (RR 1.00; $95\%$ CI, 0.77-1.29; $P=0.98$ $\mathrm{I}^{2}=0\%$ ) (Fig. 3), cardiovascular mortality (RR 1.03; $95\%$ CI, $0.79\substack{-1.34}$ $P=0.81$ $\mathrm{I}^{2}=0\%$ (Figure 1, Supplemental Digital Content 1, http://links.lww.com/ CIR/A19), MI (RR 1.14; $95\%$ CI, 0.90-1.45; $P=0.27$ $\mathrm{I}^{2}=0\%$ (Figure 2, Supplemental Digital Content 1, http://links.lww.com/ CIR/A19), stroke (RR 0.83; $95\%$ CI, 0.56-1.23; $\overset{\cdot}{P}=0.35$ $\mathrm{I}^{2}=0\%$ (Figure 3, Supplemental Digital Content 1, http://links.lww.com/ CIR/A19), and ST (RR 1.32; $95\%$ CI, $0.87{-}2.01$ $P=0.19$ $\mathrm{I}^{2}=0\%$ (Figure 4, Supplemental Digital Content 1, http://links.lww.com/ CIR/A19).  

The TSA, based on a relative risk reduction of $20\%$ reached the futility zone only for MACE (Fig. 2) and all-cause mortality (Fig. 3); concluding that no further studies were required as evidence was conclusive with no additional benefit for theuseof TT.  

# Safety Outcomes  

All 6 trials reported outcomes of major bleeding, clinically significant bleeding and intracranial hemorrhage. Compared with TT, the use of DT was associated with a significant reduction in major bleeding $4.1\%$ versus $6.5\%$ [RR 0.61; $95\%$ CI, 0.45-0.81; $P<0.01$ $\mathrm{I}^{2}=61\%$ ; number needed to treat $\left(\mathrm{NNT}\right)=42]$ (Fig. 4), clinically significant bleeding $10.5\%$ versus $16.4\%$ (RR 0.62; $95\%$ CI, 0.48-0.80; $P<0.01$ ， $\mathrm{I}^{2}=82\%$ $\mathrm{NNT}=17$ (Fig. 5), and intracranial hemorrhage $0.4\%$ versus $0.8\%$ (RR 0.43; $95\%$ CI, 0.24-0.77; $P<0.01$ $\mathrm{I}^{2}=0\%$ $\mathrm{NNT}=201$ ) (Figure 5, Supplemental Digital Content 1, http://links. lww.com/CIR/A19).  

The TSA, based on a relative risk reduction of $20\%$ reached the trial sequential monitoring boundary only for major bleeding (Fig. 4) and clinically significant bleeding (Fig. 5); concluding that no further studies were required as evidence was conclusive.  

Subgroup analyses based on individual DOACs and a history of AF were consistent with the results of the entire meta-analysis. The use of a DOAC in patients with AF on DT was associated with a reduction in major bleeding, clinically significant bleeding, and intracranial hemorrhage. Similarly, for efficacy outcomes, there was a lack of any additional benefit, with trends towards an increase in ST (RR 1.55; $95\%$ CI, 0.99-2.41; $P=0.06$ $1^{2}\%$ (Figure 4, Supplemental Digital Content 1, http://links.lww.com/ CIR/A19).  

# DISCUSSION  

Our analysis has several important findings. First, in patients requiring concomitant anticoagulant therapy, DT was associated with no significant increase in MACE, all-cause mortality, MI, stroke, or ST. Second, DT use was associated with a significant reduction of major bleeding, clinically significant bleeding, and intracranial hemorrhage. Third, the TSA demonstrated that no further studies were required as the evidence concluded that there is no additional benefit for the use of TT for MACE, all-cause mortality, major bleeding, and clinically significant bleeding.

A significant number of patients who present with MI have a concomitant need for anticoagulation. The overall incidence of new AF post-MI has ranged from $6\%$ to $19\%$ 13,14 This is in addition to patients with a prior diagnosis of AF that ranges from $1.5\%$ to $3\%$ .15 Similarly, nearly $1\%$ of patients with VTE have an MI within the first year of their diagnosis, with an increased risk to about $5\%$ Over 20 years.16 The incidence of MI post-mechanical prosthetic heart valves is low as most of these patients undergo revascularization before the valve procedure.17 In all the patients mentioned above, the presence of an additional indication requiring anticoagulation makes them at an increased risk of adverse clinical outcomes.18,19 As is shown in our analysis, the lower risk of bleeding is expected and the evidence is overwhelming.20.21 With the newer generation drug-eluting stents, there has been a hope that the ischemic profile of these patients is more amenable to DT, as the risk of ST is expected to be lower. The TSA performed in this meta-analysis convincingly demonstrated that the evidence for the use of DT over TT in these patients seemed optimal and further trials assessing the superiority of DT from ischemic standpoint would be futile. None of the trials included in this analysis were powered to assess individual outcomes separately. Therefore, this meta-analysis has additional value as it evaluates the individual ischemic outcomes.

It is important to note that the TT is frequently associated with the interruption of DAPT due to the associated increased bleeding events.22.23 This may lead to an increase in ischemic complications along with ischemia related to anemia resulting from hemorrhagic episodes. With the use of DOACs, that are proven to reduce bleeding complications compared to warfarin in a variety of settings, it is prudent to expect that ischemic complications may be lowered when added toDAPT.24,25

Some significant limitations of our analysis should be considered. We used the trial-level data; therefore, we were unable to do sensitivity analysis based on the baseline comorbidities, clinical presentation, or procedural characteristics such as type, position, or length of coronary stenting. Also, we included What is the Optimal Antiplatelet and Anticoagulant Therapy in Patients with Oral Anticoagulation and Coronary Stenting (WOEST） and Intracoronary  

Stenting and Antithrombotic Regimen - testing of a 6-week versus a 6-month Clopidogrel Treatment Regimen in Patients with Concomitant Aspirin and Oral Anticoagulant Therapy Following DrugEluting Stenting (ISAR-TRIPLE) trials that included patients with any indication of oral anticoagulation. Furthermore, the results of these trials did not alter the overall trend of the outcomes. Also, the antiplatelet agent used in ISAR-TRIPLE was aspirin, and to counter that, we performed the sensitivity bias analysis as shown in the supplement (Figure 6, Supplemental Digital Content 1, http:// links.lww.com/CIR/A19). Finally, ISAR-TRIPLE was designed to assess 6 weeks versus 6 months therapy of clopidogrel when added to aspirin andOAC.

In conclusion, our meta-analysis confirms and supports the use of DT in patients undergoing PCI with an indication for oral anticoagulation. TSA demonstrated that no further studies were required as the evidence was conclusive that DT was associated with lower bleeding risk with similar ischemic outcomes compared to TT.
