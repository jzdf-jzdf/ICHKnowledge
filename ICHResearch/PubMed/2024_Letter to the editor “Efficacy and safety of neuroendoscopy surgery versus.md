# Letter to the editor: “Efficacy and safety of neuroendoscopy surgery versus craniotomy for supratentorial intracerebral haemorrhage: an updated meta-analysis of randomized controlled trials”

Dear Editor,  

I appreciate your interest in the article titled "Efficacy and safety of neuroendoscopy surgery versus craniotomy for supratentorial intracerebral haemorrhage: an updated metaanalysis of randomized controlled trials", published in Neurosurgical Review. This research paper is crucial because it provides the most comprehensive and recent comparison of neuroendoscopy (NE) versus craniotomy for treating intracerebral haemorrhages (ICH). In addition to improving functional outcomes, NE reduces residual hematoma and infection rates. Clinical decision-makers can benefit from the findings, suggesting that NE could be an effective alternative in certain circumstances. Additionally, the study emphasizes the need for further high-quality research by addressing gaps in the current literature.  

A significant aspect of this study is that it addresses the critical issue of hypertensive intracerebral haemorrhage (ICH), which accounts for a substantial proportion of strokes and carries a high morbidity and mortality rate. This study compares neuroendoscopy (NE) with traditional craniotomy to determine which surgical techniques are more effective in achieving better patient results. This minimally invasive technique offers a safer alternative to craniotomy with quicker recovery times, reduced residual hematoma volumes, and lower infection rates compared to craniotomy [1].  

This study is essential for improving surgical management practices for ICH, especially given that NE reduced mortality rates only slightly. This research highlights the need for high-quality, large, randomized controlled trials to validate NE’s efficacy and safety, potentially leading to a paradigm shift in the surgical treatment of ICH. The purpose of this paper is to contribute to the ongoing efforts to decrease the devastating effects of hypertensive ICH on patients by providing a pathway for more tailored and less invasive surgeries [1].  

This research paper compares neuroendoscopy (NE) to craniotomy for surgically evacuating hypertensive intracerebral hemorrhage (ICH), the most comprehensive and updated meta-analysis. This study synthesizes data from several randomized controlled trials (RCTs) to demonstrate that NE offers significant benefits, including improved functional outcomes, decreased disability, and lower residual hematoma volumes. There is evidence that NE, a minimally invasive technique, might be a safer and more effective replacement for traditional craniotomies, possibly resulting in improved recovery and lower infection rates. These insights can benefit future clinical practices and surgical decision-making regarding ICH management [1].  

A few limitations accompany this research paper, although it provides valuable insights into the comparative efficacy and safety of Neuroendoscopy (NE) and craniotomy for hypertensive intracerebral haemorrhage (ICH). Meta-analysis incorporates studies with small sample sizes, which can affect robustness and generalizability. The combination of different functional outcome measurements across studies warrants caution when interpreting the results since this may introduce some variability. A significant heterogeneity observed in some endpoints, especially the hematoma evacuation rate, raises questions about the consistency of the study. A possible selection bias may have been introduced when non-English language studies were excluded, causing the overall conclusions to be influenced by pertinent research that might otherwise have been included [2].  

As demonstrated by this research paper, neuroendoscopy (NE) is an important surgical option for evacuating hypertensive intracerebral haemorrhage (ICH), especially in terms of functional outcomes. Despite these limitations, small sample sizes and heterogeneity in some analyses suggest that additional large-scale, high-quality, randomized controlled trials are needed. A future research program should explore NE’s efficacy in patients with larger hematomas and intraventricular haemorrhage and refine surgical techniques for reducing infection rates. Additionally, examining the long-term outcomes and cost-effectiveness of NE compared to craniotomy could contribute to a better understanding of its role in ICH management and influence clinical guidelines and treatment procedures [2].  

This systematic review and meta-analysis, encompassing seven RCTs and 879 patients, demonstrates that Neuroendoscopy has superior outcomes to craniotomy when treating hypertensive intracerebral haemorrhage (ICH). In addition to improving functional outcomes, NE reduced functional disability, increased hematoma evacuations, and decreased residual hematoma volumes. While NE did not show a reduction in mortality rates compared to craniotomy, it showed potential for reducing infection rates. A large, randomized study is necessary to verify these findings and fully establish NE’s efficacy and safety profile compared with traditional craniotomy. However, the analysis highlighted considerable heterogeneity and underscored the need for more extensive, high-quality studies.
