# Randomized Trial of Perfusion-Based Circulatory Management in Infants of Very Low Birth Weight  

Akio Ishiguro, MD, PhD, Ayumi Sasaki, MD, Yukiko Motojima, MD, Shuntaro Oka, MD, Ryo Nishiguchi, MD, Ryouta Nakagawa, MD, Eri Nishimura, MD, Ayaka Iwatani, MD, Yuka Iwasaki, MD, Naoyuki Miyahara, MD, Kana Saito, MD, Mitsuhiro Haga, MD, Kanako Ito, MD, PhD, Masayo Kanai, MD, PhD, Mohamed Hamed Hussein, MD, PhD, and Kazuhiko Kabe, MD  

Objective To establish the superiority of blood flow (BF)-based circulatory management over conventional blood pressure (BP)-based management strategies used for preventing intraventricular hemorrhage (IVH) in infants of very low birth weight (VLBW).  

Study design We conducted a nonblinded, single-centered randomized trial with the aim to prevent IVH by managing BF. Infants with VLBW were assigned randomly to a BF-based group or BP-based (BP group) circulatory management group. The incidence of IVH was the outcome of interest. The IVH also data were compared among healthy patients and patients responsive and unresponsive to the intervention.  

Results A total of 219 and 220 infants with VLBW were assigned to the BF and BP groups, respectively. The IVH incidence rate was lower in the BF group, but the difference was not statistically significant (BF group, $6.8\%$ vs BP group, $10.9\%$ ; $P=.14,$ ). In $21\%$ of patients of the BP group and $20\%$ of the BF group, the intervention failed. In BF group, the IVH incidence rate was significantly greater in infants with unsuccessful intervention when compared with healthy individuals $(6\%$ vs $23\%$ , $P=.001$ ). Multivariate logistic regression analysis revealed a correlation between low blood flow and IVH (aOR 3.24; $95\%$ CI 1.49-7.08, $P=.003)$ ) but not between low BP and IVH $(P=.73)$ . Conclusions The BF management protocol did not significantly decrease the incidence of IVH. However, after further optimization, we speculate the treatment strategy holds promise in decreasing the incidence of IVH. Trial registration UMIN-CTR: UMIN000013296. (J Pediatr 2022;243:27-32).  

nfants with very low birth weight (VLBW) often suffer from cardiovascular failure, which is correlated to poor neurodevelopmental outcomes. To improve the prognosis, circulatory management has been emphasized in neonatal intensive care. In the 1990s, several reports indicated the correlation between hypotension and poor neonatal outcomes, such as mortality and short- and long-term neurologic disabilities,1-4 and the evidence encouraged the application of blood pressure (BP)-based therapeutic strategies in neonatal intensive care units (NICUs).5,6 However, recent evidence indicates that circulatory management, which primarily focuses on elevating the BP in infants with VLBW, does not improve outcomes.7-9  

In 2009, Dempsey et al proposed circulatory management with “permissive hypotension” in VLBW neonatal population.10 The authors showed superior neonatal outcomes in infants with hypotension with evidence of improved tissue perfusion comparable with that of infants with normal BP. In contrast, hypotension with poor tissue perfusion was associated with worse outcomes. The findings indicated the importance of evaluating tissue perfusion for disease management in infants with VLBW and that circulatory management based on perfusion monitoring could be promising. Indeed, several recent studies have demonstrated correlations between poor outcomes and low blood flow (BF) or hypoperfusion in both vital and nonvital organs.11-14  

We have been focusing on peripheral perfusion using newly developed skin BF-monitoring methods. Our previous study demonstrated its correlation with systemic $\mathrm{BF}^{15}$ as well as the correlation between low peripheral perfusion and the incidence of IVH.16 Based on our findings, in this randomized controlled trial (RCT), we hypothesized that maintaining good perfusion as inferred from a decrease in the skin BF would lead to a lower incidence rate of IVH.

# Methods  

We conducted a pragmatic, unblinded, single-center, randomized parallel-group trial to prevent intracranial complications by managing BF in Saitama Medical Center, Saitama Medical University, Japan. The aim of this study was to establish the superiority of BF-based circulatory management strategy over conventional BP-based circulatory management. The study was preregistered in the University Hospital Medical Information Network Clinical Trial Registry in Japan (trial registration number UMIN000013296). This research protocol complied with the provisions of the Declaration of Helsinki. This study was approved by the Ethics Committee of Saitama Medical Center, Japan.  

The incidence rate of IVH in infants with VLBW in our NICU was $13\%$ before the initiation of the study. We expected the incidence rate of IVH in the intervention group to be lowered to $6\%$ . A total of 440 participants (220 in each study group) were enrolled in the trial imparting $80\%$ power to detect the effect of BF management in a 2-sided alpha level of 0.05.  

Infants of VLBW born in our institution and admitted to the NICU were eligible for enrollment if informed consent from the parents was obtained within 6 hours after birth. Infants were excluded if any of the following was present: congenital heart disease, chromosomal and/or major anomalies, gestational age less than 22 weeks, and Apgar scores of 0 at 1 minute, thus requiring epinephrine administration and/or chest compression. An initial ultrasound scan of the head was performed for all infants on admission and before randomization. Infants who developed IVH before randomization also were excluded.  

The minimization method using central registration system via web was used for participant randomization (Internet Data and Information Center for Medical Research), whereby patients were stratified by birth body weight $\check{\Xi}1000\mathrm{g}$ and $1000{\cdot}1499\mathrm{g}$ ) and sex (male and female). Attending physicians enrolled eligible infants online within 6 hours after birth. The randomization was masked at the time of randomization. The central registration system assigned infants into 2 groups: a BF-based group (BF group or intervention group) and a BP-based group (BP group or comparison group) on a one-to-one basis. All measurements and data collection were performed in the onsite NICU facility.  

Skin BF was measured for all infants enrolled in the study. A noninvasive continuous monitoring technique was used to measure skin BF as described in our previous study.17 In brief, the system consisted of a laser Doppler flowmeter (CDF-2000; Nexis), a probe, and a computer. Probes were attached to the dorsal surface of the right or left foot approximately $3\mathrm{mm}$ from the base of the fingers, avoiding any visible vessels. The probe comprised a laser output and detection system. The laser beams from the probe penetrated the skin to a depth of approximately $3{-}4~\mathrm{mm}$ , and hit the red blood cells in the capillary vessels, venules, and arterioles within a specified hemispherical region. The beams reflected off the input beams were received by the detector. The data were collected and stored in a computer. The BF data values were extracted at 10-second intervals and averaged for every hour between 6 and 72 hours.  

BP was measured in all the infants enrolled in the study. In infants with arterial lines, measures of mean arterial BP were recorded automatically in their electronic health charts. In infants without arterial lines, the BP was measured by nurses at least every 3 hours using oscillometric methods. Invasive BP measurement was initiated when attending physicians considered it necessary due to hemodynamic instability. Data for every 6 up to 72 hours were collected for analysis.  

An ultrasonography scan of the head was performed before randomization, at postnatal day 1, 2, 3, and at any day between 7 and 10 after birth using SONOS 5500 (Philips Medical Systems) to confirm the presence and severity of IVH. Ultrasonography also was performed in a timely fashion whenever attending physicians suspected the development of IVH or deemed a follow-up of IVH to be necessary.  

In the BF group, circulatory agents were administered to keep the skin BF above the cutoff values, if necessary. The cutoff value of skin BF was predetermined to be $14~\mathrm{mL}$ min within 24 hours after birth and $17\mathrm{mL/min}$ for subsequent time until 72 hours. In our previous study using the aforementioned cutoff values, we have shown the correlation between BF and incidence of IVH.16  

In the BP group, circulatory agents were administered to keep the mean arterial BP above the number of gestational weeks. In both groups, the initiation and the selection of circulatory agents were determined based on the pathophysiology assumed from the echocardiography. In summary, echocardiography was performed whenever the skin BF (in the BF group) or BP (in the BP group) was lower than the preset level in addition to the routine daily echocardiography. The ejection fraction, mean velocity of circumferential fiber shortening, end-systolic wall stress, diameter of ductus arteriosus, etc, were recorded during the process of echocardiography. Physicians diagnosed and stratified the pathologic conditions of cardiovascular failure as distributive, cardiogenic, or hypovolemic dysfunction and administered the appropriate circulatory agents (Figure 1; available at www. jpeds.com).  

IVH was diagnosed by serial ultrasonography scans of the head performed by the attending physicians and was classified into grades 1-4 according to the Papile classification. Grade 3 and 4 IVH were defined as severe IVH. The primary outcome was the incidence rate of all grades of IVH. We also evaluated limiting to severe IVH diagnosed between 7 and 10 days after birth.  

The secondary outcomes evaluated in the study included rate of neonatal death within 7 days after birth; periventricular leukomalacia diagnosis based on magnetic resonance imaging of the head at around 40 weeks of corrected age and diagnosis of necrotizing enterocolitis or retinopathy of prematurity in infants born premature who were in need of intervention. Secondary outcomes also included successful application of intervention in infants of VLBW. Interventions were judged as successful when each targeted measure was achieved within 3 hours after the interventions.  

In both groups, we noted a high number of patients in whom the interventions failed, which could affect the accuracy of evaluating the effect of interventions between the groups. Therefore, after the results of the trial were available, we subclassified the patient data into successful interventions, unsuccessful interventions, and others and added an exploratory analysis that compared the incidence rate of IVH among the infants in each group. An intervention was classified as unsuccessful when the value of the measure in each group was under the target level continuously for 3 hours.  

# Statistical Analyses  

Background characteristics and outcomes were compared between the groups by the Mann–Whitney $U$ test or the $\chi^{2}$ test. All statistical values have been reported as median (range), and a 2-tailed $P$ -value of $<.05$ was considered statistically significant unless otherwise indicated. The $\chi^{2}$ test and Fisher exact test with the Bonferroni correction were applied for multiple comparisons. Multivariate regression analysis was performed to identify the risk factors for IVH. Gestational age, birth body weight, sex, circulatory support, low BF, and low BP were selected for the analyses. SPSS Statistics version 21 (IBM Corp) was used for all statistical analyses.  

# Results  

In total, 698 infants with VLBW were admitted to our NICU between May 1, 2014, and January 31, 2021; 443 eligible infants were assigned randomly to the BF and BP groups. Three participants randomized to the BF group were excluded, as the participants had been assigned to the groups even though they did not meet the inclusion criteria. The data of 1 infant in the BF group were lost because of data-storage problems and hence was not added to the analysis. The data of 219 infants in the BF group and 220 infants in the BP group were included in the final analysis (Figure 2).  

Demographic characteristics of each study group are summarized in Table I. The rate of premature rupture of membranes and histologic chorioamnionitis (Branc ${\ge}2$ ) was significantly greater in the BF group; otherwise, the groups were similar.  

The primary outcome data are shown in Table II. The incidence rate of IVH was lower in the BF group, but no statistical difference between the groups was observed (BF group, $6.8\%$ vs BP group, $10.9\%$ ; $P=.14, $ . Similar trends were observed when comparing the study groups based on their birth weight, $_{<1000~\mathrm{g}}$ (BF group, $10\%$ vs BP group, $18\%$ ; $P=.07\$ ) and $1000{-}1499\textrm{g}$ (BF group, $3\%$ vs BP group, $2\%$ ; $P=1.00$ ). The incidence of severe IVH was lower in the BF group but did not reach significance (BF group, $0.9\%$ vs BP group, $2.3\%$ ; $P=.45$ ). Changes in BF and BP over the first 72 hours after birth in both groups are shown in Figure 3 (available at www.jpeds.com).  

The intervention failed in $21\%$ of the BP group and $20\%$ of the BF group. In the BF group, the incidence rate of IVH was not significantly different between infants with normal BF and those with successful intervention to normalize BF $(3\%$ vs $8\%$ , respectively; $P=.22$ ). However, when infants with unsuccessful intervention were compared with those with normal BF, we observed differences in rates of IVH ( $3\%$ vs $23\%$ , respectively: $P=.001\$ , Bonferroni correction $P$ -value criteria $<.016$ ) (Table III).  

In the BP group, the incidence rate of IVH was significantly greater in infants with successful intervention compared with infants with normal BP $23\%$ vs $6\%$ , respectively; $P<.001$ , Bonferroni correction $P$ -value criteria $<.016$ ), but no statistically significant difference was observed between infants with normal BP and infants with unsuccessful intervention $6\%$ vs $9\%$ ; $P=.44$ ).  

A total of $37.0\%$ and $42.7\%$ of infants in the BF and BP groups, respectively, received circulatory agents. In the BF group, the dobutamine use was greater than in BP group $(32.9\%$ vs $24.1\%$ , $P=.045^{\circ}.$ ) but no other differences were observed in use of circulatory agents (Table II).  

Multivariate logistic regression analysis revealed that IVH was significantly correlated with low BF (aOR 3.24; $95\%$ CI 1.49-7.08, $P=.003\$ ) and gestational age (aOR 0.60; $95\%$ CI 0.45-0.79, $P<.001$ ) but not with low BP $\left(P=.73\right)$ ), birth body weight $\left(P=.97\$ ), sex $\begin{array}{r}{(P=.97\end{array}.$ ), and circulatory support $\left(P=.88\right)$ . The analysis also revealed dobutamine use was not correlated with IVH $(P=.25)$ ) but low BF $(P=.01\$ ) and gestational age $\mathit{\Omega}(P=.001)\$ ) were correlated with IVH. Incidence rates of other neonatal complications revealed no significant difference between the groups (Table II). No serious side effects were reported during the study period.  

# Discussion  

In this study, we observed no significant difference between the incidence of IVH in infants who were managed based on skin BF-based data and those who were managed based on their BP dynamics. Both groups had a substantial number of infants with unsuccessful interventions. To determine the effect of the circulatory management, we added this post hoc analysis to compare the incidence rates of IVH among infants with successful and unsuccessful interventions and healthy infants. We found that the BF circulatory management decreased the incidence of IVH when the skin BF was maintained above the reference value or intervention itself managed to increase above the reference value immediately. Moreover, multivariate logistic regression analysis revealed a correlation between low BF and IVH but not between low BP and IVH. These findings suggest that our BFmanagement strategy holds promise in decreasing the incidence of IVH.  

On the contrary, in the BP group, the incidence rate of IVH in infants with successful intervention was greater than infants with normal BP. Recent studies have indicated that circulation management aiming to increase BP cannot improve the short- or long-term neonatal outcomes.18,19 Rather, the administration of vasopressors has worsened the neonatal outcomes in recent studies.20,21 Our findings are similar to those of previous studies and indicate that medical interventions focused on elevating BP in neonatal patients using vasoactive agents need to be applied with due diligence.

Few studies have attempted to determine the efficacy of circulatory management by using systemic BF and tissue perfusion-based measuures.22,23 Dempsey et al proposed the concept of permissive hypotension in neonatal population, whereby hypotension modulation using medical intervention is not necessary if good tissue perfusion is observed.10 A web-based questionnaire from 216 respondents in 38 countries revealed that $80\%$ of them would consider using the concept in extremely low gestational age newborns.23 However, the existing evaluation methods used to determine the perfusion status before applying circulatory management are not straightforward. In the study from Dempsey et al, the perfusion was comprehensively evaluated by attending physicians using conventional measures such as acidosis, skin color, heart rate, capillary refill, and urine output.10 These conventional measures often lack objectivity and cannot measure continuously; moreover, there has been no report to show adequate reliability of circulatory measures. To accurately apply tissue perfusion measures to circulatory management, they need to be monitored objectively and continuously in real time.  

In the last 1 or 2 decades, several studies have reported tissue perfusion measures whereby physiological changes were monitored using newly developed methods in infants born preterm ,17,24 which involved monitoring the response to circulatory agents,25 correlating cardiac function and systemic BF,16,24,26 and correlating neonatal outcomes.16,26,27 B ased on the pre-existing data, future studies assessing the efficacy of perfusion-based management in neonatal practice and prognosis are pertinent. An ongoing study (SafeBoosC III trial) has been evaluating the efficacy of brain tissue oxygenation for respiratory management using nearinfrared spectroscopy.28 Our study adds important information as a relatively large RCT assessing the efficacy of perfusion-based circulatory management using newly developed perfusion monitoring methods.

In our study, the major limitation was the large number of cases with unsuccessful interventions. However, perfusionbased management may have positive clinical outcomes if introduced successfully. There is opportunity to improve the circulatory practice to decrease the number of unsuccessful interventions. The most probable reason contributing to the failure of the intervention was delay in its initiation. In our study, attending physicians initiated the administration of circulatory agents after recognizing physiological measures were below the threshold level in most cases. This approach might have caused the infant’s perfusion to be in a state of hypoperfusion for a relatively long period before the intervention was begun. Initiation of the intervention early on before the skin BF dropped below the threshold level could possibly decrease the number of cases with unsuccessful intervention and decrease the overall incidence rate of IVH.  

Other limitations of this study include an unblinded study design, because BP monitoring is an essential measure in NICU-based care that cannot be interrupted or measured in a blinded method. Skin BF also was measured in all infants. Our protocol could therefore have led to treatment against low BP in the BF group and low BF in the BP group. Furthermore, in some infants, the attending physician considered that immediate circulatory support was needed before randomization. In most cases, the attending physicians took immediate parents’ consent and proceed to randomization but in few cases, circulatory support was initiated before randomization. This might have been a potential confounder in this trial. We also noted that rates of premature rupture of membranes and histologic chorioamnionitis were significantly greater in the BF group, both have been previously reported to contribute to an increased risk of IVH.29,30 Finally, several laser Doppler flow meters are available for clinical use. Blood flow data are apparatus-dependent, and it is advisable to refrain from generalization of the results.

In conclusion, BF and BP circulatory management practices were compared in infants of VLBW. Low BF was correlated with IVH incidence rate, although active BF management did not significantly lower the incidence rate of IVH. However, in patients with good perfusion, a lower incidence rate of IVH was observed. Skin BF monitoring may prove to be useful in circulation management and BF management can potentially lower the incidence rate of IVH because it provides data for more accurate management of systemic and peripheral BF. The efficacy of the method described in this study requires further validation through multicentered RCTs. n
