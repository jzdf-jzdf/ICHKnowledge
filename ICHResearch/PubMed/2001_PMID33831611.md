# Neurological Outcome, Mental Fatigue, and Occurrence of Aneurysms ${>}15$ Years After Aneurysmal Subarachnoid Hemorrhage  

Jennifer Samuelsson1,2, Hugo Jakobsson1, Alexandros Rentzos3,4, Asgeir S. Jakola1,2, Daniel Nilsson1,2 - OBJECTIVE: Long-term data on neurological and radiological outcome after aneurysmal subarachnoid hemorrhage (aSAH) are scarce. The aim of this study was to report neurological and radiological outcome ${\tt>}15$ years after aSAH.  

- METHODS: Patients with aSAH who were randomly assigned to endovascular treatment (EVT) or microsurgical treatment (MST) during 1997e2001 were included. Main end points were neurological outcome assessed by modified Rankin Scale, fatigue assessed by mental fatigue scale, and radiological outcome assessed by magnetic resonance angiography. Results for mental fatigue scale were compared with a control group.  

- RESULTS: After 15e21 years, 46 $(62.2\%)$ of the 74 survivors replied to a questionnaire. Of these patients, 18 received MST, and 28 received EVT. Modified Rankin Scale score of 0e2 was found in $100\%$ of patients in the EVT group and $88.8\%$ of patients in the MST group. Moderate or severe mental fatigue was found in 7/28 patients $(25\%)$ in the EVT group and 7/18 patients $(38.8\%)$ in the MST group $(P<0.05),$ whereas moderate or severe mental fatigue was observed in 3/34 patients $(8.9\%)$ in the control group. Magnetic resonance angiography was performed in 29 patients. In the EVT group, new neck remnants were found in 2/16 patients $(12.5\%),$ and de novo aneurysm was found in 2/16 patients $(12.5\%)$ . In the MST group, de novo aneurysm was found in 1/13 patients $(7.7\%)$ .  

- CONCLUSIONS: Neurological outcome at long-term follow-up after aSAH was good; however, mental fatigue was overrepresented in patients compared with healthy control subjects regardless of treatment modality. Residual or de novo aneurysm was found in $17\%$ of patients warranting radiological long-term follow-up.  



# INTRODUCTION  

n aneurysmal subarachnoid hemorrhage (aSAH) is associated with a mortality rate of up to $40\%$ at 1 month.1 The neurological outcome after aSAH is dependent on the damage caused by the initial bleeding, the occurrence of disease-related complications (vasospasm, hydrocephalus, and rebleeding), and complications from the treatment of the aneurysm. Taking into consideration that many survivors after aSAH have an expected remaining lifetime of at least 25 years, we need more data on the durability of the treatment and the long-term neurological outcome after aSAH. Moreover, fatigue is commonly reported after aSAH and can be a major obstacle for the patient in resuming his or her previous life, including several years after the bleeding.2,3 Only 1 previous study has reported on the prevalence of mental fatigue ${>}\mathbf{I}5$ years after aSAH.  

Our primary aim with this study was to review the long-term neurological outcome in patients from Western Sweden in the randomized International Subarachnoid Aneurysm Trial (ISAT) with at least 15 years of follow-up. Secondary aims were occurrence of mental fatigue, rate of aneurysm remnant, rate of de novo aneurysm formation, rebleeding, and need for retreatment.

# MATERIALS AND METHODS  

This study was approved by the Ethics Board of the University of Gothenburg, The Sahlgrenska Academy, Gothenburg, Sweden. Patients included in the ISAT at the Department of Neurosurgery at Sahlgrenska University Hospital as of April 1997 until April 2001 were reviewed. The present study was designed as a long-term follow-up of the 98 patients who were included and randomly assigned in the ISAT and followed for 5 years according to the ISAT guidelines. Details of patient inclusion are shown in the flowchart in Figure 1. The mean follow-up in the present study was 15.7 years (range, 15e21 years).  

A questionnaire was sent out by mail to all surviving 74 patients from the initial ISAT cohort to determine the score on the modified Rankin scale (mRS).5 We also collected 5-year mRS scores for nonresponders from the ISAT protocol to control for selection bias. Mental fatigue was assessed by the mental fatigue scale (MFS).6  

Baseline data included age at aSAH, sex, Fisher grade, localization of aneurysm (anterior or posterior circulation), aneurysm size, aneurysm neck size, vasospasm (angiographic), and treatment method (microsurgical treatment [MST] or endovascular treatment [EVT]). Whether patients had been smoking and in that case for the amount of time and if they had hypertension in the need of treatment and in that case for how long were also recorded.  

Clinical data at the time of treatment were collected from the patients’ previous medical records (Table 1). Neurological status at the time of admission was evaluated using either the Glasgow Coma Scale or the Reaction Level Scale.7 Neurological outcome was assessed by the mRS at 5 years for all patients and at ${\tt>}\mathbf{I}5 $ years after aSAH for patients responding to the questionnaire. Good neurological outcome was defined as mRS score of $_{0-2}$ , and a poor neurological outcome as mRS score of $_{3^{-}5}$ .

Radiological data (computed tomography, digital subtraction angiography) at the time of treatment, at follow-up, and in case of retreatment were reviewed by an interventional neuroradiologist (A.R.). Magnetic resonance imaging was performed during longterm follow-up. Three-dimensional time-of-flight angiography and contrast-enhanced angiography were added to a basic magnetic resonance imaging protocol including T1-and $\mathrm{T}2$ -weighted sequences, diffusion-weighted imaging, fluid-attenuated inversion recovery sequences, and susceptibility-weighted imaging. All images were reviewed by neuroradiologists.  

The patients who underwent at least 1 retreatment or had a rebleed confirmed via medical imaging (computed tomography) during the 15-year period after aSAH were recorded. For deceased patients, causes of death were collected at a group level from the National Cause of Death Registry.  



# Mental Fatigue Scale  

The MFS was sent by mail to all surviving patients to assess the occurrence and severity of mental fatigue. The MFS was also sent out to 34 age-matched control subjects without a history of stroke or any neurosurgical procedure. The range of MFS scores is $_{0^{-}32}$ with breakdown as follows: a score of ${\bfO}{-}\bf{IO}$ is defined as no mental fatigue; $\mathbf{I0.}5^{-}\mathbf{I4.5}$ , risk of developing mild or mental fatigue; $\bar{1}5^{-20}$ , moderate mental fatigue; ${>}20$ , severe mental fatigue.6  



# Statistical Analysis  

We used IBM SPSS Statistics Version 25.0 (IBM Corporation, Armonk, New York, USA) for data analysis. Variables such as mean age, localization and size of aneurysm, sex, treatment modality, hypertensive treatment, and smoking status are presented with descriptive statistics. When comparing groups, we used Fisher exact test for $2\times2$ tables and Pearson $\chi^{2}$ test for $2\times$ n tables. For continuous data, we used independent samples t test or Mann-Whitney U test as appropriate based on data distribution. Level of significance was set as ${\pmb{\mathscr{L}}}=0.05$ . The number of patients included in this study prevented a multivariate analysis.

# DISCUSSION  

This population-based study of patients previously included in the ISAT and randomly assigned to EVT or MST shows that the proportion of patients with good outcome after ${\tt>}\mathbf{I}5 $ years is high regardless of treatment modality. In the ISAT long-term study, the United Kingdom cohort was followed for 10e18.5 years, reporting mRS score $^{0-2}$ in $82\%$ of patients with EVT and $78\%$ of patients with MST with better outcomes for patients with EVT compared with MST.8 In the 10-year follow-up of the Barrow Ruptured Aneurysm Trial (BRAT), mRS score $^{0-2}$ was found in $83\%$ of patients with MST and $79\%$ in patients with EVT after 10 years. There were no significant differences between treatment modalities.  

This study confirms data from the above 2 major studies, showing that the absolute majority of patients have a good neurological outcome many years after aSAH. Despite the majority of patients being graded as being in a good neurological condition, $25\%$ of patients in the EVT group and $38\%$ in the MST group experienced moderate or severe mental fatigue according to the MFS. In contrast, in healthy control subjects, only $\mathbf{I}4\cdot7\%$ experienced mental fatigue. In 4 patients with aSAH (2 from the EVT group and 2 from the MST group) graded with severe mental fatigue (MFS scores ${>}20$ ), mRS score was $_{0-2}$ , so even though they had a good neurological outcome, mental fatigue was severely affecting their everyday life.  

Previous studies have shown that a substantial number of patients with aSAH have difficulties resuming their previous life related to cognitive deficits, even though they are neurologically intact.9 Many different aspects of cognition may be affected after aSAH, including emotional control, memory, social interaction, and concentration. However, in the only available qualitative study of aSAH survivors, self-reported mental fatigue and memory problems are the most common affecting daily life.10  

Mental fatigue is an important outcome to discuss, as there is a possibility that early intervention with neurorehabilitation may improve or prevent some of these symptoms. Self-assessment with the MFS is a simple but important screening tool to identify patients in need of follow-up for mental fatigue.  

This study found a new aneurysm or neck remnant in 5 of 29 $\left(\mathrm{{\bfr}}7^{0}/0\right)$ of patients ${>}\mathbf{I}5$ years after aSAH. Three of the patients in the EVT group had a second endovascular procedure, and 1 patient in the MST group had a de novo aneurysm and underwent a microsurgical clip ligation.

Long-term radiological follow-up with digital subtraction angiography or magnetic resonance angiography has shown that neck remnants or aneurysm recurrence occur in $\scriptstyle{12.4^{0}}/{0}-{28^{0}}/{0}$ of patients after EVT.9,11-14 Aneurysm recurrence at long-term follow-up after MST is rare; 2 large studies have shown rates of about $\mathrm{\Omega_{I}}\%$ .9,15 In the present study, recurrence after MST could not be studied, as artifacts from the clip made this impossible. This is a limitation, but for this study we needed a radiological method with a very low risk profile, so angiography, which carries a small risk of periprocedural stroke, was not considered an option. On one hand, based on the previously mentioned studies and our radiological results, we can conclude that MST entails a more definitive treatment, and EVT carries a higher risk of recanalization, which in turn demands more treatments. On the other hand, today EVT has made it possible to treat older patients or patients who otherwise would be treated conservatively. A study using computed tomography angiography 8.9 years after MST for aSAH in 610 patients found a new aneurysm in $\tau6\%$ of patients, with a majority at a site distant from the clipped aneurysm.16  

In a meta-analysis of the risk of de novo aneurysm formation in patients who previously had an aneurysm diagnosed comprising data from almost 15,000 patients found de novo aneurysms to occur in only $2\%$ of patients, most of them late, after ${>}8$ years, and with a mean time to rupture of 10 years.17 Their main conclusion was that screening is likely of low yield if performed too frequently and that patients could be screened in time intervals of at least $5\mathrm{-}\ttIO$ years.  

A similar meta-analysis with long-term follow-up of patients who underwent MST concluded that there is a cumulative risk of $9.6\%{-22\%}$ for aneurysm recurrence or de novo formation up to 20 years after treatment.18 Secondary prevention controlling hypertension and cessation of smoking is probably the most important intervention to reduce the risk of developing a new aneurysm. Nevertheless, long-term radiological follow-up $\mathbf{I}0{-}\mathbf{I}5$ years after treatment of aSAH should be considered, especially in younger patients with multiple aneurysms or risk factors such as hypertension and/or smoking.  

The small size of the patient population is a limitation in this study, and we had $37.8\%$ nonresponders. However, we collected $5^{-}$ year data from nonresponders, showing that they had a similar outcome as responders with close to $75\%$ of patients reporting a good outcome at 5 years, and it is unlikely that there have been any major changes in their mRS score between 5 and 15 years. It should be noted that this study demonstrates the long-term results of only simple endovascular coiling, as newer, methods such as balloon- or stent-assisted procedures, flow diverter stents, and intrasaccular flow modification devices, had not yet been developed at the time of treatment.  



# CONCLUSIONS  

Neurological outcome at long-term follow-up after aSAH was good; however, mental fatigue was overrepresented in patients compared with healthy control subjects regardless of treatment modality. Residual or de novo aneurysm was found in $\mathbf{I}7\%$ of patients. Patients treated for aSAH should be offered long-term follow-up after at least 10 years for mental fatigue and radiological follow-up to identify aneurysm remnants and de novo aneurysms.
