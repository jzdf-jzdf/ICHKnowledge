BACKGROUND: Although targeted temperature management (TTM) may mitigate brain injury for severe subarachnoid hemorrhage (SAH), rebound fever correlates with poor outcomes.  

OBJECTIVE: To study the effect of endovascular TTM after rewarming from initial surface cooling during a high-risk period for delayed cerebral ischemia.  

METHODS: We studied patients with World Federation of Neurological Surgeons grade V SAH before and after the introduction of endovascular TTM. Both groups (36 patients each) were treated with TTM at $34^{\circ}\mathsf{C}$ with conventional surface cooling immediately after SAH diagnosis, together with emergency aneurysm repair. When rewarmed to $36^{\circ}C_{},$ around 7 days later, the study group underwent TTM at 36 to $38~^{\circ}\mathsf{C}$ for 7 days with an endovascular cooling system. The control group was treated with antipyretics.  

RESULTS: Sex, age, Glasgow Coma Scale score, modified Fisher computed tomography classification, aneurysm location, and treatment methods were not different between the study and control groups. Differences were detected in the incidence of fever $>38^{\circ}C$ (13 vs 26 patients, $P=.0021$ ), duration of fever $>38~^{\circ}C$ (4.1 vs 18.8 hours, $P=.0021$ ), incidence of vasospasm-related cerebral infarction ( $17\%$ vs $42\%$ , $P=.037)$ ), and the likelihood of excellent outcomes (0 and 1 on a modified Rankin Scale) at 6 months $(42\%$ vs $17\%$ , $P=.037)$ . In endovascular TTM, shivering occurred more frequently in patients with better outcomes, requiring aggressive treatment to avoid fever.  

CONCLUSION: Endovascular TTM at 36 to $38^{\circ}\mathsf{C}$ after surface cooling was feasible and safely performed in patients with severe SAH. Combined TTM for 2 weeks was associated with a lower incidence of vasospasm-related infarction and may improve outcomes.

Advanced fever control with surface or endovascular temperaturemodulating devices has been shown to contribute to improved outcomes after SAH, including decreased levels of mortality and disability.5 We have been using very early targeted temperature management (TTM) at $34~^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$ in patients with World Federation of Neurological Surgeons (WFNS) grade V SAH to mitigate the progress of early brain injury (EBI).6,7 However, after completion of TTM with surface cooling, patients tend to develop rebound fever, which could aggravate cerebral vasospasm and deteriorate outcomes. We sought to study the feasibility and potential benefits of normothermic TTM with an endovascular cooling system during the high-risk period for developing delayed cerebral ischemia (DCI).  

# METHODS  

# Study Design  

This was a nonrandomized interventional study using historical controls. The study was conducted at a tertiary critical care center with approval from the relevant institutional review board (2011-01). We analyzed 36 consecutive patients with WFNS grade V SAH in each group with (June 2014-August 2021) and without (February 2010-May 2014) the endovascular cooling system. All patients underwent TTM at $34~^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$ with immediate initial surface cooling. A prospective observational database of patients with SAH was used for the analysis, and patients were followed up at 6 months for an outcome assessment.  

# Patients and Initial Management  

All potentially salvageable patients with WFNS grade V SAH were included. The exclusion criteria were as follows: age older than 75 years, preexisting medical conditions (e.g., end-stage cancer or severe dementia), a devastating hematoma in the dominant hemisphere, obvious episodes of prolonged hypoxia or hypotension, and no recovery of brainstem reflexes even after resuscitation. The same inclusion and exclusion criteria were applied to both groups. The stages of initial resuscitation and induction of TTM are shown in Table, Supplemental Digital Content 1, http:// links.lww.com/NEU/D315. Written informed consent was obtained from the next of kin.  

# Data Collection  

All patients underwent a prospective collection of baseline clinical, laboratory, and demographic characteristics. The temperature data were obtained from chart reviews or records stored in the Thermogard system. Laboratory data, neurological and radiological findings, and adverse events were reviewed during the hospital stay.  

# Surface Cooling  

Cooling was initiated immediately after the decision to treat the aneurysm. For rapid cooling, nasogastric lavage with iced saline and intravenous administration of cold saline were used under buprenorphine, midazolam, and rocuronium administration. Body core temperature was monitored in the urinary bladder using a Foley catheter. During cooling, the ruptured aneurysm was obliterated as soon as possible, primarily by surgical clipping, together with external decompression, and cisternal or ventricular drainage. Thereafter, TTM at $34~^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$ was maintained at least 48 hours with a conventional cooling blanket (Medi  

Therm II, Gaymar Industries, Inc). In cases of severe brain swelling or elevated intracranial pressure, surface cooling was continued beyond 48 hours. Patients were rewarmed at a rate of ${\le}1\mathrm{{\nabla}}^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$ per day if the intracranial pressure was within the normal range. This early clinical experience has been reported previously.6,7  

# Endovascular TTM  

After being rewarmed to $36~^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$ for around 7 days after SAH onset, patients underwent cerebral digital subtraction angiography to assess cerebral vasospasm and aneurysm obliteration. An endovascular catheter with 2 cooling balloons (Cool Line Catheter, Asahi Kasei ZOLL Medical Co., Ltd) was inserted through the left internal jugular vein and placed in the superior vena cava under fluoroscopic guidance. Thereafter, the surface cooling blanket was removed, and continuous administration of sedatives and muscle relaxants was terminated. Patients in the study group underwent TTM at 36 to $38~^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$ for an additional 7 days using the Thermogard XP Temperature Management System (Asahi Kasei ZOLL Medical Co., Ltd). The target temperature was gradually set to a higher level of up to $37.5~^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$ in accordance with the patients’ bladder temperature. The fever management protocol is shown schematically in Figure 1.  

# Control  

Consecutive patients $\left(\mathrm{n}=36\right)$ ) before the introduction of endovascular TTM served as historical controls. After removing of the cooling blanket, these patients were treated with antipyretics. Body surface cooling was performed, as required.  

# Shivering Management  

Shivering was assessed using the bedside shivering assessment scale8 and was treated following the antishivering protocol.9 Acetaminophen, dexmedetomidine, and propofol were administered using a step-by-step method according to the degree of shivering.  

# Outcome Assessment  

Outcome at 6 months was assessed by physicians at the rehabilitation hospitals using the Glasgow Outcome Scale (GOS) and modified Rankin Scale (mRS). Good recovery and moderate disability on the GOS and scores of 0 to 3 on the mRS were designated as favorable outcomes. In addition, a score of 0 or 1 on the mRS was considered an excellent outcome.  

# Statistical Analysis  

Baseline characteristics are summarized as number $(\%)$ for categorical variables and were compared using the $\chi^{2}$ test or Fisher exact test as appropriate. Continuous variables are summarized as mean and SD values and were compared using the Student $t$ test. All tests were two-sided, with $P<.05$ indicating a statistical significance. All analyses were performed using JMP, version 14.0.0 (SAS Institute Inc).

# Baseline Characteristics  

The characteristics of both groups of patients are presented in Table 1. There were no differences in age, sex, Glasgow Coma Scale and neurological findings, aneurysm location, modified Fisher computed tomography classification, laboratory data indicating SAH severity (levels of blood glucose, serum potassium, D-dimer, and glucose/potassium ratio), and treatment methods.  

# Clinical Course and Adverse Events  

Table 2 presents the clinical course and any adverse events. There was no difference in the initial temperature and time to reach $<35.5~^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$ and $<34.0~^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$ . The temperature reached $<35.5~^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$ within 2 hours and $<34.0~^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$ at ${\sim}5 $ hours in both groups. The endovascular group underwent TTM for a total of 14.7 days, ie, 6.8 days longer for TTM with the device compared with that in the control group $(P<.0001)$ .  

During endovascular TTM or 7 days after initial surface cooling, ${\sim}8$ to 14 days after the onset of SAH, both the incidence and duration of fever ${>}38^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$ were significantly greater in the control group than in the endovascular TTM group (26 $[72.2\%]$ vs 13 $\left[36.1\%\right]$ , $P=.002$ , and 18.8 vs 4.1 hours, $P=.002$ , respectively).  

Interventional radiology (IVR) was performed for angiographical vasospasm in 13 patients $(36.1\%)$ in the control group and $4$ patients $(11.1\%)$ in the endovascular TTM group $(P=$ .025). Vasospasm-related cerebral infarction developed more frequently in the control group than in the endovascular TTM group (14 $[38.9\%]$ and 2 $[5.6\%]$ patients $\left[P=.001\right]$ , respectively) within the initial 2 weeks; it was eventually detected in 15 $(41.7\%)$ and 6 $(16.7\%)$ $\left(P=.037\right)$ patients, on average, at 11.1 and 14.0 days after onset $\left(P=.16\right)$ , respectively.  

During endovascular TTM, 24 patients had awakened while 8 could take food orally. Neurological examination results were available in these patients, one of whom presented with DCI and underwent an IVR procedure.  

Endovascular TTM was not associated with increased complications, and it significantly reduced the occurrence of pneumonia $\begin{array}{rlr}{(P}&{{}=}&{.046)}\end{array}$ . The frequency of shunt surgery for hydrocephalus did not differ significantly according to the different temperature control methods, but was marginally lower in patients with IVR than in patients who underwent surgical clipping $(15.5\%$ vs $50.0\%$ , $P=.063)$ .  

There was no difference between the 2 groups in the incidence of adverse events during initial surface cooling. During endovascular cooling or the corresponding period in the control group, there were 11 $(30.6\%)$ and 2 $(5.6\%)$ cases of cerebral infarction $(P=.0012)$ , and 8 $(22.2\%)$ and 2 $(5.6\%)$ cases of pneumonia $\scriptstyle(P=$ .085) in the control and endovascular groups, respectively (Table, Supplemental Digital Content 2, http://links.lww.com/NEU/ D316).  

There was no significant difference regarding favorable outcomes for GOS ( $58.3\%$ vs $38.9\%$ , $P=.099\$ ); however, the mRS assessment revealed a significant increase in excellent outcomes (4 $1.7\%$ vs $16.7\%$ , $P=.037.$ ) and a marginal increase in favorable outcomes $(61.1\%$ vs $36.1\%$ , $P=.059,$ ) (Figure 3) in the endovascular TTM group compared with those in the control group.

However, temperature elevation and cerebral infarction were observed in 4 patients after the completion of endovascular TTM. Shivering management was essential during endovascular TTM to suppress temperature elevation. Our results demonstrate the potential benefits and limitations of combined surface and endovascular TTM for 2 weeks after SAH onset.  

Recently, EBI—immediate injury to the brain within 72 hours of the ictus secondary to SAH—has drawn attention as the primary cause of mortality in patients with SAH.10 Intracranial circulatory arrest occurs at the moment of aneurysm rupture, leading to profound ischemic and reperfusion injury. Together with extravasated blood and elevated intracranial pressure, EBI progresses through complex pathophysiological mechanisms. Furthermore, it is suggested that the etiology of delayed vasospasm may be linked to that of EBI. Animal experiments have suggested the potential benefits of early hypothermia treatment.11,12 To date, no treatment for EBI has been clinically established.  

Although there is controversy regarding the optimal target temperature, the efficacy of TTM in postcardiac arrest patients has been demonstrated.13-16 We have been using very early induction of TTM at $34~^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$ to mitigate the progress of EBI in patients with WFNS grade V SAH. Our initial experience showed that this approach was feasible and could be safely performed.6,7  

To date, there have been several reports on TTM for SAH. TTM at 33 to $35~^{\circ}C$ has been indicated for good-grade SAH during operation,17 poor-grade SAH (Hunt and Hess grade IV and V) and refractory intracranial hypertension, cerebral vasospasm,18 intracranial hypertension and DCI,19,20 vasospasm and DCI,21 and poor-grade SAH (Japan Coma Scale score of 100 or higher, or Hunt and Hess grade IV and V) after surgery ,22,23 with some of these studies reporting potential benefits.19-21,23 Our approach differs from those in previous reports in that we initiated cooling only for patients with WFNS grade V in the hyperacute phase before aneurysm treatment to mitigate, if possible, progressive EBI.  

In postcardiac arrest patients after TTM, rebound fever is common and associated with poor neurological outcomes among survivors.24,25 In SAH, rebound fever during the high-risk period of DCI may exacerbate cerebral vasospasm. Fatal cerebral vasospasm after rewarming has been reported in a patient with a head injury.26  

Pneumonia is a common adverse event in patients undergoing TTM. We found a lower incidence of pneumonia in the endovascular TTM group. Pneumonia often develops after rewarming. In our study, appropriate antimicrobial therapy administered based on infection surveillance during endovascular TTM might be a possible explanation for the lower incidence of pneumonia.  

The safety and potential benefits of TTM using endovascular systems have been reported (Table 3).19,27-34 Endovascular TTM is highly efficacious and enables a rapid and stable fever control but is associated with a higher cost. In Japan, endovascular cooling has been approved for use in SAH for 7 days of normothermia. We used endovascular TTM at 36 to $38~^{\circ}C$ following the indication criteria after completion of surface cooling. Endovascular TTM has the advantage of allowing temperature control while monitoring neurological symptoms in awake patients. Although it may seem paradoxical, resistance to cooling was associated with better outcomes in this study. This could be explained by relatively less damage to the thermoregulatory centers. Endovascular cooling was effective, but shivering management was essential to avoid high fever in awake patients. Temperature elevation and DCI after endovascular TTM have emerged as new issues to be resolved; however, it is not clear whether prolonged temperature control is effective or simply postpones disease progression.

# Limitations  

This study has some limitations. First, the patients were assessed in the very early phase after ictus. Some patients may have recovered after further observation. However, all patients arrived in a comatose state. Loss of consciousness at symptom onset is an important manifestation of EBI after SAH and a predictor of death or poor functional outcomes.35 In addition, levels of blood glucose, serum potassium, and D-dimer36 and the glucose potassium ratio37 suggest that these patients were critically ill. Second, this was a nonrandomized interventional study using a historical control, not a prospective randomized control study. Third, outcome assessors were not blinded. Potential bias in the assessment could not be excluded. Fourth, quality improvement over the years might have affected the outcomes. Because this study was conducted over a long duration of ${\mathord{\left/{\vphantom{\mathord{\left/{\vphantom{\left.10}}\right.\kern-\nulldelimiterspace}}}}\right.\kern-\nulldelimiterspace}>10}$ years, time might serve as an explanatory variable.  

# CONCLUSION  

Endovascular TTM at 36 to $38~^{\circ}C$ after surface cooling was feasible and safely performed in patients with severe SAH. Combined TTM for 2 weeks was associated with a lower incidence of vasospasm-related infarction and may improve outcomes. A more prolonged TTM may be warranted as some patients developed fever and DCI after 2 weeks.
