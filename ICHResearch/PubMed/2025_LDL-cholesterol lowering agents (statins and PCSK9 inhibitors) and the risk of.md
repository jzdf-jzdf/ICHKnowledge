# LDL-cholesterol lowering agents (statins and PCSK9 inhibitors) and the risk of intracerebral hemorrhage: A network meta-analysis

Background and Purpose: Statin therapy reduces the risk of ischemic stroke; however, certain studies have observed an increased incidence of intracerebral hemorrhage (ICH). Moreover, proprotein convertase subtilisin/ kexin type 9(PCSK-9) inhibitors have emerged as a powerful class of lipid-lowering medications, potentially with a lower propensity for causing hemorrhagic events. To investigate this matter further, we conducted a network meta-analysis of randomized controlled trials (RCTs) involving statins and PCSK-9 inhibitors that reported oc­ currences of ICH.  

Methods: We performed a literature search of Medline, Web of Science, and The Cochrane Library from database inception until August 2023. All randomized controlled trials of statin therapy and PCSK-9 inhibitors that re­ ported ICH or hemorrhagic stroke were included. The primary outcome variable was ICH. The risk of bias of each included study was assessed by using the Cochrane Handbook for Systematic Reviews of Interventions. We performed network meta-analysis to compare and rank statin and PCSK-9 inhibitors therapies. This study is registered (2023110026. inplasy.com).  

Results: A total of 26251 citations were identified by the search, and 38 potentially eligible articles were included. In total, data from 271411 individuals were analyzed. The data showed that there was not a significant increased risk of intracerebral hemorrhage for all statins and PCSK-9 inhibitors compared with placebo. atorvastatin and rosuvastatin were associated with a lower risk of death than placebo (ORs ranging between 0.79 and 0.82). For risk of intracerebral hemorrhage and mortality. there was not a significant increased risk among all drugs.  

Conclusions: LDL-Cholesterol lowering agents (statins and PCSK-9 inhibitors) was not associated with a signifi­ cant increased risk of ICH. Our network meta-analysis provides strong evidence for the safety of statins and PCSK-9 inhibitors, but more studies are needed to further validate this conclusion.  

# Introduction  

Hypercholesterolemia are linked to a heightened risk of experiencing an ischemic stroke.1,2 Statins, which are hydroxymethyl glutaryl-CoA (HMG-CoA) reductase inhibitors, has been established as a funda­ mental approach in both primary and secondary prevention of cardio­ vascular disease (CVD).3 Moreover, some animal experiments and basic studies have demonstrated that statins possess anti-inflammatory properties, support the stability of vascular endothelium, enhance the expression of nitric oxide synthase, and promote the generation of new neurons and the formation of synapses, thereby achieving neuroprotective effects.4 Although clinical trials have shown substantial decreases in the occurrence of ischemic strokes, a large population cohort study and subsequent meta-analyses have revealed no decrease in stroke mortality with statin therapy.5,6  

Although numerous studies have demonstrated that the benefits of statin therapy outweigh the drawbacks, significant attention has been directed towards the risk of bleeding in patients undergoing statin treatment. The Stroke Prevention by Aggressive Reduction in Choles­ terol Levels (SPARCL) study, which investigated the efficacy of highdose atorvastatin for secondary stroke prevention, revealed an inde­ pendent association between treatment with atorvastatin and an elevated risk of hemorrhagic stroke by the post hoc analysis.7 However, a subsequent meta-analysis conducted by the Cholesterol Treatment Trialists (CTT) Collaboration, which encompassed 20 clinical trials investigating statin therapy, revealed a statistically nonsignificant inclination towards an elevated risk of hemorrhagic stroke.8 However, the results of these studies are contradictory. In addition, previous studies have not explored the effect of different statins on hemorrhagic stroke risk. PCSK9 inhibitors are the most potent lipid-lowering agents, surpassing statins in efficacy while demonstrating a significantly lower association with identified off-target antithrombotic effects. These in­ hibitors work by degrading LDL (low-density lipoprotein) receptors on the liver surface, which increases receptor turnover and enhances the clearance of circulating LDL levels. Furthermore, similar to statins, PCSK9 inhibitors have undergone extensive evaluation in multiple large, randomized trials, providing a robust dataset to characterize both hemorrhagic and ischemic stroke events. Consequently, we conducted a formal meta-analytic review to compare and contrast the rates of hem­ orrhagic stroke in randomized clinical trials of statins and PCSK9 inhibitors.  

The current body of medical evidence still lacks sufficient support to determine whether statins effectively reduce the incidence of intrace­ rebral hemorrhage. The guidelines for cerebrovascular diseases lack clear recommendations, resulting in conflicts between conventional secondary prevention and drug treatment for patients with prior ischemic cardiovascular. This study conducted a network meta-analysis of 38 literature sources to provide clinical evidence for the treatment of intracerebral hemorrhage.  

# Methods  

The study protocol was registered (2023110026. inplasy.com). The completed review has been prepared in consultation with the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-analyses (PRISMA) Extension Statement for Network Meta-analysis  

Search strategy and selection criteria  

We thoroughly searched the EMBASE, PubMed, Web of Science, and the Cochrane Library databases s from database inception until August 2023, and we also reviewed relevant meta-analyses. We restricted our search to papers published in English in all four databases. To find more qualifying studies, we manually examined references in relevant publi­ cations. We used the search terms “Atorvastatin” OR “Fluvastatin” OR “Lovastatin” or “Rosuvastatin” or “Pravastatin” or “Simvastatin” or “pitavastatin” or “statins”. We also included the terms “PCSK9 in­ hibitors” or “Alirocumab” or “bococizumab” or “evolocumab”. We included randomized controlled trials (RCTs) of statins.  

The articles could be incorporated into the present meta-analysis if the literature matched the following predetermined criteria: 1) Inter­ vention and comparison: treated with statins or PCSK9 inhibitors; 2) Outcome measures: intracerebral hemorrhage/hemorrhagic stroke rates reported. 3) Study design: a full-text English-written RCT that has been officially published.  

All investigators independently selected the studies, reviewed the main reports and supplementary materials, extracted the relevant in­ formation from the included trials, and assessed the risk of bias. Dis­ agreements on inclusion during full-text review were resolved by consensus.  

# Risk of bias assessment and certainty of evidence  

We assessed the studies’ risk of bias in accordance to the Cochrane Handbook for Systematic Reviews of Interventions.  

Data analysis  

For studies published more than once (ie, duplicates), we included only the report with the most informative and complete data. We esti­ mated summary odds ratios (ORs) for dichotomous outcomes using pairwise and network meta-analysis. Prior to the performance of the NMAs, we appraised the clinical and methodologic characteristics of the included studies to judge the appropriateness of the transitivity assumption and conducted random-effects (RE) pairwise meta-analyses of the available data to inspect for statistical heterogeneity of treatment effects using I2 values.  

We performed all NMAs using ADDIS version 16.6 and JAGS pack­ ages in R version 4.2.0, evaluated under a Bayesian framework with Markov Chain Monte Carlo simulation. the binomial likelihood was used for dichotomous outcomes.10 In network meta-analysis, we assumed that the amount of heterogeneity was the same for all treatment com­ parisons. To assess the amount of heterogeneity, we compared the posterior distribution of the estimated heterogeneity variance with its predictive distribution. To rank the treatments for each outcome, we used the surface under the cumulative ranking curve (SUCRA) and the ranking probabilities.11 The transitivity assumption underlying network meta-analysis was evaluated by comparing the distribution of clinical and methodological variables. We introduced node-splitting methods for checking the consistency of direct and indirect evidence.12 The convergence of models was evaluated by potential scale reduction factor (PSRF). Model fit was assessed by comparing the posterior total residual deviance with the number of unconstrained data points. The selection between models was based on the deviance information criteria (DIC).13 Results from RE models are presented for all analyses (eTable 1). We used comparison-adjusted funnel plots to investigate whether results in imprecise trials differ from those in more precise trials.  

# Result  

26,251 citations were identified by the search and 38 potentially eligible articles were retrieved in full text (Fig. 1).3,14-49 In total, we analyzed data from 271,411 individuals: 11,012 received Alirocumab, 20,152 received Atorvastatin, 13,662 received Bococizumab, 1050 received Fluvastatin, 3876 received Lovastatin, 28,116 received Pra­ vastatin, 21,450 received Rosuvastatin, 13,717 received Simvasta­ tin–Ezetimibe, 26,493 received Simvastatin and clopidogrel, 13,784 received Evolocumab. The mean age was 62.8 years (SD 9.6) for pa­ tients. 156,298 $(57.0\%)$ of 2,74,345 of the sample population were men. The appendix (eTable 2) summarized the characteristics of included studies.  

Most studies received a low risk of bias. The appendix (eFigure 1) showed that four studies received a high risk of bias for absence of allocation concealment and blindin g.35,36,50 Regarding publication bias, the outcome of intracerebral hemorrhage in the study were not exactly symmetrical (eFigure 2), suggesting that the publication bias may have existed.  

The appendix (eFigure 3) provided the results of heterogeneity test. There is moderate heterogeneity for intracerebral hemorrhage. For mortality, there is obvious heterogeneity in the comparison for alir­ ocumab vs placebo $(\mathrm{I}2{=}87.8~\%)$ , rosuvastatin vs placebo $(\mathrm{I}2{=}93.7~\%)$ .  

The appendix (eTable3-5) provided the details of consistency eval­ uation. PSRF (potential scale reduction factor) was used to assess the convergence of the results. All the results have good convergence $(\mathrm{PSRF}{<}1.1\$ , eTable 6).  

Fig. 2 shows the network of eligible comparisons for intracerebral hemorrhage, and mortality. All drugs had placebo-controlled trial. The appendix (eFigure 4) provides detailed pairwise meta-analyses results.  

Fig. 3 shows the network meta-analysis’ results for the all outcomes. In terms of intracerebral hemorrhage, there was not a significant increased risk of intracerebral hemorrhage for all statins compared with placebo. In terms of mortality, atorvastatin and rosuvastatin were associated with a lower risk of death than placebo (ORs ranging between 0.79 and 0.82).

# Subgroup analysis  

According to diverse patient population groups, subgroup analysis was carried out. For patients with coronary heart disease, Alirocumab, Atorvastatin, Lovastatin, Rosuvastatin, Pravastatin, Simvasta­ tin–Ezetimibe, Simvastatin were included and 14 studies were analysed14,20,25,27,31,32,34,36-38,41,42,46,48 (eFigure7). There was no sig­ nificant increase in the risk of intracerebral hemorrhage or death for any of the drugs compared to placebo. Atorvastatin and lovastatin were associated with lower mortality rates than other drugs; however, the evidence for lovastatin is less reliable as it was based on only one study, indicating the need for further validation.

# Discussion  

The prognosis of ICH remains poor, as there is currently no specific treatment available. Primary etiologies of ICH include hypertension and cerebral amyloid vascular disease (CAA), while secondary risk factors encompass brain tumor, aneurysm, arteriovenous malformation, coag­ ulation abnormalities, and brain trauma. Moreover, some studies have found that statins increase the risk of ICH. They have extensive appli­ cation in the primary and secondary prevention of cerebrovascular diseases associated with atherosclerosis as well as treatment of chronic subdural hematoma. However, the findings of epidemiological studies have indicated an elevated incidence of hemorrhagic stroke and increased mortality related to ICH in populations characterized by low levels of cholesterol.54,55 It has been observed that decreased levels of cholesterol in the bloodstream tend to facilitate the occurrence of arte­ rial muscle necrosis and microaneurysm formation.56 Moreover, several studies reported that statins increase blood-brain barrier permeability, and inhibit platelet aggregation, thrombosis, and thrombin-linked re­ action after acute ICH, resulting in further enlargement of cerebral hematoma and poor prognosis. But, in other studies, statins had neu­ roprotective effects.57 Statins have the ability to maintain the integrity of vascular endothelial cells, regulate the immune system and inhibit the inflammatory process.58 However, the results of these studies are con­ tradictory. Therefore, in this study, we conducted a network meta-analysis of statin, including “Atorvastatin” OR “Fluvastatin” OR “Lovastatin” or “Rosuvastatin” or “Pravastatin” or “Simvastatin” or “pitavastatin” or “statins” or “PCSK9 inhibitors” or “Alirocumab” or “bococizumab” or “evolocumab”, and patient outcomes after ICH to further guide clinical treatment decisions.

The SPARCL trial investigating the use of high-dose statin for sec­ ondary stroke prevention revealed a higher incidence of ICH in patients receiving active treatment compared to those on placebo.3 Similarly, there was a 2-fold increase in hemorrhagic stroke in among patients with prior ischemic stroke treated with statins.59 However, several previous meta-analyses of statin therapy have found no increased risk of hem­ orrhagic stroke.4,8,60,61 The meta-analysis of 11 trials revealed an insignificant incidence of hemorrhagic stroke associated with statin therapy. In patients with ischemic stroke, any statin therapy was not associated with an increased risk of ICH.61 Studying intensive LDL reduction with statins, the CTT collaboration performed a meta-analysis of 26 randomized controlled trials (RCTs) and reported a nonsignificant trend toward an increased risk of ICH. A more recent meta-analysis including 23 RCTs found no evidence of increased risk of ICH.60  

We performed a comprehensive network meta-analysis of previous RCTs of association between treatment with stains and the risk of hemorrhagic stroke. We included 38 RCTs with a total of 274345 sub­ jects. In our analysis, active therapy was associated with a nonsignificant increase in the risk of ICH. Subgroup analysis further indicated that, among different patient categories, statins and PCSK9 inhibitors did not increase the risk of cerebral hemorrhage. This suggests that any poten­ tial increase in ICH risk could be attributable to other non-LDL effects of statin therapy. This is in agreement with a large meta-analysis of observational cohort studies that found no correlation with fatal stroke and baseline total cholesterol in almost 900000 subjects.6  

We conducted a subgroup analysis on the ICH risks associated with the use of statins and PCSK9 inhibitors for primary and secondary pre­ vention among patients with coronary heart disease or healthy peoples without coronary heart disease. Our results indicate that there is no difference in the ICH risk associated with statin and PCSK9 inhibitors use for primary and secondary prevention of heart disease among patients or healthy peoples. However, in the study of cerebral infarction, due to the limited number of studies included, further subgroup analysis could not be conducted. We also conducted a subgroup analysis on the ICH risk associated with the use of statins and PCSK9 inhibitors for primary and secondary prevention. Our results indicate that there is no difference in the ICH risk associated with statins and PCSK9 inhibitors. Moreover, prior research has shown that lipophilic statins were associated with higher rates of ICH than hydrophilic statins. In our study, neither lipo­ philic statins nor non-lipophilic statins increase the risk of ICH in patients.  

Our literature search is comprehensive and includes all published data to date. Our analysis included not only all the currently recom­ mended stains but also PCSK9 inhibitors. First, to improve the quality of evidence and methodological rigor, we included only RCTs, which ensured homogeneity and similarity in experimental design and imple­ mentation. In addition, before analysis, all included studies were eval­ uated for quality, risk of bias, and heterogeneity. We employ a variety of methods, including node-splitting methods, to test the consistency of our findings to ensure the reliability of our results. The actual analysis illustrated that our results are in good agreement. Moreover, we ranked the results based on SUCRA, probability graphs were used to analyze the probability of each drug in each ranking to better explain the ranking results.  

There are several limitations to this analysis. Firstly, patient-level data was not accessible to us and there may exist unaccounted vari­ ables pertaining to patients that could have influenced the rates of hemorrhagic stroke. Secondly, despite conducting the most compre­ hensive analysis to date on the association between statin therapy and risk of ICH in randomized trials, it is plausible that there may exist additional trials, particularly those published in languages other than English, which were not included. Although we included all pertinent trials, regardless of size, no publication bias or heterogeneity was found in our analysis.  

LDL-Cholesterol lowering agents (statins and PCSK-9 inhibitors) was not associated with a significant increased risk of ICH. Our network meta-analysis provides strong evidence for the safety of statins and PCSK-9 inhibitors, but more studies are needed to further validate this conclusion.
