# ORIGINAL RESEARCH  



# Dual Antiplatelet Therapy and Outcomes in Acute Mild to Moderate Stroke With Versus Without Large-Artery Atherosclerosis Post Hoc Analysis of ATAMIS  

Yu Cui $\circledcirc$ , PhD; Quan-Ying Liu, MM; Hui-Sheng Chen $\circledcirc$ , MD, PhD  

BACKGROUND: We conducted a post hoc analysis of the ATAMIS (Antiplatelet Therapy in Acute Mild to Moderate Ischemic Stroke) trial to investigate whether the priority of clopidogrel plus aspirin to aspirin alone was consistent between patients with and without stroke pathogenesis of large-artery atherosclerosis (LAA)  

METHoDS AND RESULTS: Patients with stroke classification randomized to a clopidogrel-plus-aspirin group and aspirin-alone group in a modified intention-to-treat analysis set of ATAMIS were classified into LAA and non-LAA subtypes. The primary outcome was early neurologic deterioration at 7 days, defined as a ${>}2.$ -point increase in National Institutes of Health Stroke Scale score compared with baseline, and safety outcomes were bleeding events and intracranial hemorrhage. We compared treatment effects in each stroke subtype and investigated the interaction. Among 2910 patients, 225 were assigned into the LAA subtype (119 in the clopidogrel-plus-aspirin group and 106 in the aspirin-alone group) and 2685 into the non-LAA subtype (1380 in the clopidogrel-plus-aspirin group and 1305 in the aspirin-alone group). Median age was 66 years, and $35\%$ were women. A lower proportion of early neurologic deterioration was found to be associated with dual antiplatelet therapy in the LAA subtype (adjusted risk difference, $-10.4\%$ $[95\%$ CI, $-16.2\%$ to $-4.7\%]$ ; $P{=}0.001$ ) but not in the non-LAA subtype (adjusted risk difference, $-1.4\%$ $[95\%$ CI, $-2.6\%$ to $0.1\%]$ ; $P{=}0.06$ ). No significant interaction was found $(P{=}0.11^{\cdot}$ ).  

CONCLusioNs: Compared with the non-LAA subtype, patients with stroke of the LAA subtype may get more benefit from dual antiplatelet therapy with clopidogrel plus aspirin with respect to early neurologic deterioration at 7 days.

# What Is New?  

In acute mild to moderate ischemic stroke within 48 hours of symptom onset, compared with patients not diagnosed by large-artery atherosclerosis stroke, those with stroke of large-artery atherosclerosis may safely get more benefit from dual antiplatelet therapy with clopidogrel plus aspirin with respect to prevention of the occurrence of early neurologic deterioration at 7 days.  



# What Are the Clinical Implications?  

These findings may support the use of dual antiplatelet therapy with clopidogrel plus aspirin in acute mild to moderate ischemic stroke with large-artery atherosclerosis pathogenesis.

However, monotherapy with aspirin alone is still recommended by guidelines for patients with acute mild to moderate ischemic stroke1 who are at high risk of early neurologic deterioration (END), which is associated with poor prognosis.5  

The ATAMIS (Antiplatelet Therapy in Acute Mild to Moderate Ischemic Stroke) trial was the first randomized clinical trial with large sample size to demonstrate that DAPT with clopidogrel and aspirin significantly reduced the risk of END at 7 days compared with aspirin alone in patients with acute mild to moderate ischemic stroke within 48 hours after stroke onset.6 Recently, the INSPIRES (Intensive Statin and Antiplatelet Therapy for Acute High-Risk Intracranial or Extracranial Atherosclerosis) trial further identified that the time window for DAPT could extend to 72 hours after stroke onset in minor stroke or high-risk transient ischemic attack with presumed atherosclerotic cause,7 which indicated that DAPT was more effective than monotherapy for patients with stroke caused by largeartery atherosclerosis (LAA).8 Furthermore, similar results were previously reported in the secondary stroke prevention for patients with intracranial atherosclerotic stenosis.9,10 In this context, we hypothesized that DAPT mainly showed priority to monotherapy in acute mild to moderate ischemic stroke caused by LAA in ATAMIS.  

Thus, in this post hoc analysis of the ATAMIS trial, we aimed to investigate whether the efficacy and safety of DAPT versus monotherapy were consistent among patients with and without LAA, and the interaction between stroke subtypes and treatment effect of different antiplatelet therapies.  



# METHODS  

The data that support the findings of this study are available from the corresponding author upon reasonable request.  



# Study Design, Participants, and Procedure  

Details have been published on the design and protocol of the ATAMIS trial,11 which was a multicenter, open-label, blinded-end point, randomized clinical trial enrolling 3000 patients to assess the effect of clopidogrel plus aspirin in acute mild to moderate ischemic stroke. Inclusion criteria included age ≥18 years, modified Rankin Scale (mRS) scores before stroke $\leq1$ , diagnosed with acute ischemic stroke, presented within 48 hours after stroke onset, and baseline National Institutes of Health Stroke Scale (NIHSS) scores ranged from 4 to 10. Exclusion criteria included patients eligible for intravenous thrombolysis or endovascular therapy who had a clear indication for anticoagulation or a history of intracerebral hemorrhage.  

In this post hoc analysis, patients in the modified intention-to-treat analysis set with definite stroke subtype, according to TOAST (Trial of Org 10 172 in Acute Stroke Treatment) criteria,12 were included and divided into LAA and non-LAA subtypes. In each subtype, patients were subdivided into a clopidogrel-plus-aspirin group and an aspirin-alone group on the basis of the randomized assignment in the trial. All study procedures were reviewed and approved by the Ethics Committee of General Hospital of Northern Theater  

Command (Approval Number: k [2016] 6–1), and written informed consents were obtained from the patients or their legally authorized representative. The study was registered with ClinicalTrials.gov (NCT02869009). This post hoc analysis reports on the basis of the Strengthening the Reporting of Observational Studies in Epidemiology guidelines.  



# Outcomes  

The primary outcome was occurrence of END at 7 days ${\mathrm{>}}2 $ -point increase in NlHSScompared with baseline [excluding cerebral hemorrhage]).13 The secondary outcomes included excellent functional outcome at 90 days (mRS score of 0–1), a shift in the distribution of the mRS score at 90 days, change in NIHSS score compared with baseline at 14 days, time from randomization to occurrence of new stroke14 within 90 days, and time from randomization to the occurrence of other vascular events or all-cause death within 90 days. The safety outcomes included occurrence of bleeding events and intracranial hemorrhage due to possibly direct association with antiplatelet therapy. The assessment of the NIHSS score at admission and follow-up were done by the same assessor. Follow-up at 90 days was assessed in person or by telephone through blinded measurements by trained and certified assessors in each center.  



# Statistical Analysis  

Considering the potential imbalance of baseline characteristics, which may introduce bias, primary analyses were adjusted in the study. We summarized continuous variables as median (interquartile range) and categorical variables as frequencies (percentages) and compared them using the Mann–Whitney or $\chi^{2}$ test. We estimated the absolute number of events and risk difference, odds ratio, geometric mean ratio, or hazard ratio with $95\%$ CIs for treatment effect.  

Generalized linear models and Cox regression analysis were performed to evaluate the treatment effect. The primary adjusted model included any unbalanced baseline characteristics $\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\Delta}}P$ value ${<}0.1$ ) and prespecified factors (age, sex, history of diabetes, history of hypertension, onset-to-treatment time and baseline NlHSS score) in the ATAMIS trial, and the secondary adjusted model only included any unbalanced baseline characteristics. Adjusted interactions analyses between treatment effects and stroke subtypes were conducted by including unbalanced characteristics between stroke subtypes. Missing data in the adjusted analyses were imputed through simple imputation.  

We conducted 4 sensitivity analyses to identify the robustness of results from primary analysis. First, a subgroup analysis of primary outcome was conducted according to 6 prespecified factors (age $[<65$ years or ${\ttz}65$ years], sex [female or male], history of diabetes [yes or no], history of hypertension [yes or no], onsetto-treatment time $[<24$ hours or ${}^{224}$ hours] and NIHSS score at randomization $[<7$ or ≥7]). Second, a perprotocol analysis restricted to patients who received the completed intervention as specified in the protocol was performed for the primary outcome.11 Third, propensity score matching analysis, which included prespecified covariates, assigned antiplatelet therapies, and any imbalanced baseline variables between stroke subtypes with a ratio 1:1, a caliper of 0.01, and a nearest-neighbor matching strategy, was performed to generate a new cohort with balanced sample size between stroke subtypes to control the sample size bias. Fourth, the primary outcome was compared between patients with identified stroke pathogenesis of LAA and small-artery occlusion.  

All analyses presented were exploratory, and all $P$ values were nominal. Two-sided $P$ values $_{<0.05}$ were considered significant. All statistical analyses were performed using SPSS software version 26.0 (IBM, Armonk, NY) and R software version 4.1.0 (R Foundation for Statistical Computing, Vienna, Austria).  



# RESULTS  



# Study Participants  

A flow diagram of the current study is shown in Figure 1. Of 2910 patients included (median age, 66 years; 1018 $[35\%]$ women), 225 were assigned to the LAA subtype (clopidogrel plus aspirin, 119; aspirin alone,106) and 2685 were assigned to the non-LAA subtype (clopidogrel plus aspirin, 1380; aspirin alone,1305). Table 1 shows the baseline characteristics of patients and the occurrence of END at 7 days between stroke subtypes. Several baseline characteristics showed a difference between stroke subtypes, such as current smoking, history of stroke, history of transient ischemic attack, NIHSS score at admission, and location of responsible vessel. The occurrence of END at 7 days in patients with the LAA subtype was higher than that in those with the non-LAA subtype ( $11.6\%$ versus $5.2\%$ . Baseline characteristics were well balanced between treatments in the LAA subtype, while there was an imbalance in location of responsible vessel between treatments in the non-LAA subtype.  



# Outcomes  

Table 2 shows the efficacy and safety outcomes in the current study. Compared with aspirin alone, the primary analyses showed significantly decreased risk in the END at 7 days of clopidogrel plus aspirin in the LAA subtype $(6.7\%$ versus $17.0\%$ ; adjusted risk difference, $-10.4\%$ $[95\%$ CI, $-16.2\%$ to $-4.7\%]$ ; $P{=}0.001$ ), but not in the non-LAA subtype ( $4.6\%$ versus $5.9\%$ ; adjusted risk difference, $-1.4\%$ $[95\%$ CI, $-2.6\%$ to $0.1\%]$ ; $P{=}0.06)$ .

The primary outcome from the propensity score matching analysis set and from the per-protocol analysis set was consistent with that in the patients from the modified intention-to-treat analysis set. # DISCUSSION  

In this post hoc analysis of the ATAMIS trial, we found the risk of END at 7 days was higher in patients with LAA stroke than those with non-LAA stroke and significantly decreased in patients with LAA stroke after receiving clopidogrel-plus-aspirin treatment compared withaspirinalonebut not inthosewithnon-LAAstroke. Furthermore, we found that patients with LAA stroke benefited from DAPT within 48 hours of symptom onset but patients with non-LAA stroke only within 24 hours.  

END is an independent predictor of poor prognosis after acute ischemic stroke,15,16 which is common and remains a challenge to overcome in the clinical practice. Previous studies demonstrated that patients with LAA stroke were at risk of developing END.17–19 In the current study, the risk of END at 7 days in the patients with LAA stroke was higher than that in those with non-LAA stroke regardless of antiplatelet strategy received ( $11.6\%$ versus $5.2\%$ , which was similar to that in the previous studies.17–19 Given the higher proportion of END in the patients with LAA stroke in our study and better efficacy of DAPT in preventing recurrent stroke in this population in the previous studies,20,21 we further investigated whether patients with LAA stroke may benefit more from DAPT compared with monotherapy. We found that patients with LAA stroke showed lower risk of END than those with non-LAA stroke after receiving DAPT. The END occurring in the LAA stroke may be attributed to the reduced blood supply within the responsible stenosis vessel to the ischemic region, which will result in the progression of cerebral infarction. Another potential reason may be the high risk of responsible vessel occlusion. Thus, DAPT with strong antithrombotic effect might show better efficacy than mono-antiplatelet in this stroke subtype with regard to improving blood supply or preventing vessel occlusion. The current results were also similar to our previous findings that stronger antithrombotic strategy with DAPT and short-term argatroban may effectively prevent END at 7 days in a similar population.22 On the other hand, previous studies demonstrated that DAPT with clopidogrel plus aspirin was more effective than aspirin alone in reducing microemboli in patients with intracranial atherosclerotic stenosis.23,24 Thus, the current findings that patients with LAA may benefit more from DAPT may be attributed partially to the mechanism of endothelial stabilization and reduction of platelet-rich embolism formation. Collectively, this study suggests that patients with LAA stroke may experience higher risk of END at 7 days, and DAPT with clopidogrel plus aspirin may be suitable for this population regarding prevention of END.

In the current analysis, we found the benefit of DAPT in patients with LAA stroke within 48 hours of symptom onset, which was similar to the results from the currently published INSPIRES trial that indicated combined clopidogrel–aspirin therapy initiated within 72 hours after stroke onset led to a lower risk of new stroke at 90 days than aspirin therapy alone in patients with mild ischemic stroke or high-risk transient ischemic attack of presumed atherosclerotic cause.7 Thus, the time window for DAPT preventing END in patients with LAA stroke was within 48 hours of stroke onset, which needs to be confirmed in the future. However, in the subgroup analysis, we did not identify a significant difference between antiplatelet treatments among patients with LAA in either 0 to 24 hours or 24 to 48 hours, which may be attributed to the small sample size after classification, although an obvious difference compared with patients with non-LAA. Furthermore, significant interaction between DAPT and primary outcome in the LAA subtype was observed within 24 to 48 hours but not within 0 to 24 hours, which was unexpected. This phenomenon may be attributed to the smaller sample size after stratification by the time from onset to antiplatelet treatment.  

Our study has several limitations. First, this analysis would be affected by the imbalanced sample size between stroke subtypes and relatively lower proportion of LAA subtype. Although we have conducted the propensity score matching analysis to test the robustness of findings, the efficacy of DAPT in this population warrants further investigation in trials with an adequate and balanced sample size. Second, given that the determination of presumed stroke subtype of patients greatly depends on vessel examination, many patients without vessel examination were classified into stroke with undetermined pathogenesis in the ATAMIS trial. Therefore, the results may not exactly represent the efficacy of DAPT in patients with non-LAA stroke, which also may affect the significance test for interaction between pathogenesis and efficacy of antiplatelet treatments. Third, as symptomatic intracranial atherosclerotic stenosis is a major cause of stroke recurrence, this post hoc analysis could not have estimated the effect of DAPT on preventing recurrent stroke due to the relatively low occurrence of new stroke or other vascular events in the ATAMIS trial. Finally, given that the subgroup analysis was not powered and had no prespecified correction for multiple comparisons, findings of the subgroup analysis and subsequent conclusions need to be interpreted with great caution. Thus, these findings warrant confirmation, particularly in a non-Chinese population.
