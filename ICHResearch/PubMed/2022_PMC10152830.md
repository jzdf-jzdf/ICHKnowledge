# Endovascular Thrombectomy With or Without Intravenous Thrombolysis: A Meta-Analysis of Randomized Controlled Trials  

Muhammad F Ishfaq 1,2,✉, Sana Gulraiz 1, Wei Huang 1, 2, Iryna Lobanov a 1,2, Renee H Martin 3, Brandi R French 2, Farhan Siddiq 4, Erdem Gurkas 1, Emrah Aytac 1, Camilo R Gomez 2, Adnan I Qureshi 1,2  

Author information Article notes Copyright and License information PMCID: PMC10152830  PMID: 35450475  

# Abstract  

# Background  

We performed this meta-analysis of randomized clinical trials to compare the outcomes in patients treated with endovascular thrombectomy who receive prior intravenous thrombolysis with those who do not receive such treatment. Recently, one randomized trial reported outcomes to address this issue, so timely update of meta-analysis is needed to determine the value of administering intravenous thrombolysis before endovascular thrombectomy.  

# Materials and methods  

Four randomized clinical trials are included in our meta-analysis. We calculated pooled odds ratios and $95\%$ CIs using random-effects models. The primary efficacy endpoint was a favorable outcome defined by a modified Rankin Scale score of 0 (no symptoms), 1 (no significant disability), or 2 (slight disability) at 90 days post-randomization. Secondary endpoints analyzed were any intracerebral hemorrhage, symptomatic intracerebral hemorrhage, and mortality.  

Results  

Of the 1633 patients randomized, the proportion of patients who achieved a favorable outcome was similar between endovascular thrombectomy alone and combined approach with intravenous thrombolysis and endovascular thrombectomy (1631 patients analyzed; odds ratio 1.02; CI 0.84–1.25; $\mathrm{p}=0.83$ ). Risk of any intracerebral hemorrhage was significantly lower among those randomized to endovascular thrombectomy alone (1633 patients analyzed; odds ratio 0.75; CI 0.57–0.99; $\mathrm{p}=\mathrm{0}.04\$ ). Rates of symptomatic intracerebral hemorrhage $\mathrm{\left(p=0.36\right)}$ and mortality $\left(\mathrm{p}=0.62\right)$ were not significantly different between the two groups.  

# Conclusions  

Compared with endovascular thrombectomy preceded by intravenous thrombolysis, endovascular thrombectomy resulted in similar rates of favorable outcome with a lower rate of intracerebral hemorrhage. A large phase 3 trial is required to conclusively demonstrate equivalency of both approaches to guide future practice.  

Keywords: Intervention, stroke, thrombectomy, thrombolysis  

# Introduction  

The benefit from endovascular thrombectomy, with or without prior intravenous thrombolysis, over intravenous thrombolysis alone in reducing death or disability in patients with acute ischemic stroke due to large vessel occlusion has been identified in several randomized controlled trials. 1 However, the value of prior intravenous thrombolysis in patients with acute ischemic stroke who are candidates for endovascular treatment is unclear. 2 In theory, intravenous thrombolysis may increase the chance of recanalization and reduce distal fragmentation during endovascular thrombectomy. 3 However, previous studies have not been able to identify any of the above mentioned benefits.4–7 Intravenous thrombolysis may increase the risk of post-procedure intracerebral hemorrhage and increase the cost incurred. 4 Due to the above-mentioned considerations, several studies have supported the need for a risk benefit assessment of administering intravenous thrombolysis prior to endovascular treatment.1,8,9 We performed this meta-analysis to combine the results of all existing trials to provide a comprehensive assessment of the benefit and risk associated with endovascular thrombectomy alone when compared with combined intravenous thrombolysis and endovascular thrombectomy in patients with acute ischemic stroke. Moreover, a randomized controlled trial is recently published on the similar topic, and to the best of our knowledge, outcome of this trial has not been reported in any meta-analysis before, therefore it is timely to update the metanalysis to address the utility of adding intravenous thrombolysis before endovascular treatment in acute ischemic stroke.  

Materials and methods  

Study design  

We performed a meta-analysis of relevant randomized controlled trials and stratified analyses by important differences in trial characteristics. We followed the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses guidelines. We performed a computerized literature search of the Medline and Cochrane databases on March 25, 2021, with the following search terms: “endovascular treatment alone”, “endovascular treatment plus intravenous thrombolysis”, “thrombectomy devices”, “acute ischemic stroke”, “thrombectomy alone”, “proximal intracranial occlusion in the anterior circulation”, “randomized control trial”, “intra-arterial revascularization”, “retrievable stents”, “modified Rankin scale”, “intracerebral hemorrhage”, “symptomatic intracerebral hemorrhage”, “mortality”, “recanalization”, “serious adverse events”, “large vessel occlusion”, “thrombectomy plus intravenous thrombolysis”, “intravenous thrombolysis”, “endovascular thrombectomy with the Solitaire FR stent retriever”, “recombinant pro-urokinase” and “intravenous and intra-arterial recombinant tissue plasminogen activator (rtPA)”. No other search restrictions were applied.  

We included trials if they enrolled patients with acute ischemic stroke (within $4.5\mathrm{~h~}$ of symptom onset) for endovascular thrombectomy and randomly assigned patients to endovascular thrombectomy alone or combined intravenous thrombolysis and endovascular thrombectomy in patients with acute ischemic stroke. Trials that included ${<}10$ patients, and those that did not report clinical outcomes according to grades of modified Rankin scale post-randomization were excluded. The protocol is registered in the International Prospective Register of Systematic Reviews (PROSPERO) with identification number 266571.  

# Outcomes  

The primary efficacy endpoint was the proportion of randomized patients who achieved a modified Rankin Scale score of 0 (no symptoms), 1 (no significant disability), or 2 (slight disability) at 90 days post-randomization. 10 Secondary efficacy endpoints were the proportion of randomized patients who achieved successful reperfusion, modified Rankin Scale score of 0 or 1, and mortality at 90 days post-randomization. Post-treatment any type of intracerebral hemorrhage, and symptomatic intracerebral hemorrhage were the safety endpoints analyzed. Information on these endpoints was abstracted by M.F.I. and S.G. independently on different datasheets which was compared for any kind of discrepancy or errors before entering into a structured final dataset.  

# Statistical analysis  

We calculated odds ratios and $95\%$ CIs by using Comprehensive Meta-Analysis 2·2·048 (Biostat, Englewood, New Jersey) for each of the trials. We compared the calculated odds ratios with the odds ratios or hazard ratios reported in the original article when available to ensure congruence. If the specified endpoints were not reported in a trial, that trial was excluded only from the pooled analyses of the specific endpoints that were not reported. We calculated pooled odds ratios by using a randomeffects model by using the method of DerSimonian and Laird. 11 Heterogeneity was assessed by using the Cochran Q statistic, and when there was heterogeneity, we assessed the magnitude of  

heterogeneity with the $\mathrm{I}^{2}$ measure (the percentage of total variability due to true between-study heterogeneity).  

In sensitivity analyses, we restricted the analyses to trials who received regular dose of intravenous thrombolysis i.e $0.9\mathrm{mg/kg}$ and we analyzed for heterogeneity on the basis of masking within the trial. We analyzed the results only for trials that assessed the primary outcome at 90 days postrandomization by using blinded ascertainment. We assessed publication bias by visual inspection of funnel plots and by calculation of the $\mathrm{P}$ value (2-sided) for the Egger intercept. We did not make corrections for multiple hypotheses testing because of the exploratory nature of the analyses. All tests were 2-sided, with $\mathrm{P}<.05$ deemed as significant. 12  

# Results  

We initially identified 55 research papers during our literature search evaluating endovascular thrombectomy alone versus combined intravenous thrombolysis and endovascular thrombectomy (Figure 1). Three papers were excluded because they were post-hoc analyses of randomized controlled trials,8–10 and forty-eight papers were excluded because they were either small case series or matched score analysis without randomization of patients into two groups of endovascular thrombectomy alone or combined intravenous thrombolysis and endovascular thrombectomy. The remaining 4 trials met the inclusion criteria and were included in the meta-analysis, resulting in 1633 patients.11–14 SKIP trial (Effect of mechanical thrombectomy without vs. with intravenous thrombolysis on functional outcome among patients with acute ischemic stroke: The SKIP randomized clinical trial administered $0.6\mathrm{mg/kg}$ dose which is the recommended dose in Japan, 11 DEVT trial (Effect of endovascular treatment alone vs. intravenous alteplase plus endovascular treatment on functional independence in patients with acute ischemic stroke: The DEVT randomized clinical trial) administered full dose of $0.9\mathrm{mg/kg}$ in all eligible patients, 12 DIRECT-MT (Endovascular Thrombectomy with or without Intravenous Alteplase in Acute Stroke: DIRECT MT) 30 patients $(9.1\%)$ ) did not receive either the full dose $\mathrm{(n=20}$ ) or any dose $\mathrm{(n}=10\$ ) of intravenous thrombolysis, and 37 patients $(11.2\%)$ did not undergo thrombectomy, 13 and MR CLEAN NO IV was the only trial done on majority of non-Asian ethnicity cohort. The characteristics of included studies are provided in the Table 1. Large vessel occlusion patients who underwent endovascular thrombectomy received any of the following procedures at discretion of the treatment team: thrombectomy with stent retrievers, thromboaspiration, intra-arterial thrombolysis, balloon angioplasty, stenting, or a combination of these approaches.

Among 1633 patients randomized, 747 $(45.7\%)$ achieved a modified Rankin scale score of 0, 1, or 2 at 90 days post-randomization. Distribution of modified Rankin scale at 90 days in each group is given in Supplemental Figure 1 . The proportion of randomized patients with acute ischemic stroke who achieved a modified Rankin scale score of 0, 1, or 2 at 90 days post-randomization was not significantly changed among patients randomized to endovascular thrombectomy alone $(376[46\%]$ of 816 patients) compared with those randomized to combined intravenous thrombolysis and endovascular thrombectomy (371 $[45.5\%]$ of 815 patients) (1631 patients analyzed; odds ratio 1.02; CI 0.84–1.25; $\mathrm{p}=0.83$ ) as demonstrated in Figure 2. There was no heterogeneity among the trials (Cochran Q statistic, 1.67; 3 df; $P=.64$ ; $\mathrm{I}^{2}=0\%\dot{}$ ). The proportion of randomized patients with acute ischemic stroke who achieved a modified Rankin scale score of 0 or 1 at 90 days post randomization was not statistically different among those randomized to endovascular thrombectomy alone (209 $[25.6\%]$ of 816 patients) compared with combined intravenous thrombolysis and endovascular thrombectomy (198 $[24.2\%]$ of 815 patients) (1631 patients analyzed; odds ratio 1.08; CI 0.86–1.36; $\mathrm{p}=0.49$ ) (Supplemental Figure 2a ). There was no heterogeneity among the trials (Cochran Q statistic, 1.38; 3 df; $P=.71;1^{2}=0\%)$ ). There was no difference in mortality at 90 days post randomization in patients randomized to endovascular thrombectomy alone when compared to combined intravenous thrombolysis and endovascular thrombectomy (1633 patients analyzed; odds ratio, 1.07; $95\%$ CI, 0.82–1.39; $P=.62$ ; Supplemental Figure 2b ).

In the first sensitivity analysis, the proportion of randomized patients with acute ischemic stroke who achieved a modified Rankin Scale score of 0, 1, or 2 at 90 days post-randomization was also not significantly different among those randomized to endovascular thrombectomy alone (316 $[44.2\%]$ of 715 patients) compared with combined intravenous thrombolysis and endovascular thrombectomy (312 $[43.8\%]$ of 712 patients) (odds ratio 1.01; CI 0.82–1.25; $\mathrm{p}=0.90\$ ) after exclusion of the trial 11 that administered $0.6\mathrm{mg/kg}$ of intravenous tissue plasminogen activator. In the second sensitivity analysis, the proportion of randomized patients with acute ischemic stroke who achieved a modified Rankin scale score of 0 or 1 at 90 days post randomization was also not significantly different among those randomized to endovascular thrombectomy alone (odds ratio 1.14; CI 0.89–1.47; $\mathrm{p}=0.30$ ) after exclusion of trial 11 that administered lower dose of intravenous tissue plasminogen activator i.e. 0.6 mg/kg.  

Odds of any intracerebral hemorrhage was significantly lower among patients randomized to endovascular thrombectomy alone (280 $[34.3\%]$ of 817 patients) compared with those randomized to combined intravenous thrombolysis and endovascular thrombectomy (326 $[40\%]$ of 816 patients) (1633 patients analyzed; odds ratio 0.75; CI 0.57–0.99; $\mathrm{p}=0.04$ ) as demonstrated in Figure $\underline{{{3}}}(\underline{{{a}}})$ . There was no significant heterogeneity among the trials (Cochran Q statistic, 5.10; 3 df; $P=.16;\mathrm{I}^{2}=41\%$ ). The specific definition of symptomatic intracerebral hemorrhage varied among trials (Supplemental table 1 ), but 99 patients had a symptomatic intracerebral hemorrhage. In pooled analyses, there was a statistically non-significant trend of lower rate of symptomatic intracerebral hemorrhage in favor of patients randomized to endovascular thrombectomy alone compared with those randomized to combined intravenous thrombolysis and endovascular thrombectomy (1631 patients analyzed; odds ratio, 0.82; $95\%$ CI, 0.55–1.24; $P=.36$ ) (Figure $\underline{{3}}(\underline{{\mathrm{b}}})$ ). There was no heterogeneity among the trials concerning survival (Cochran Q statistic, 1.96; 3 df; $P=.58$ ; $\mathrm{I}^{2}=0\%$ ) or symptomatic intracerebral hemorrhage (Cochran Q statistic, 1.16; 3 df; $P=.76;1^{2}=0\%$ ). There was no difference in successful reperfusion in patients randomized to endovascular thrombectomy alone when compared to combined intravenous thrombolysis and endovascular thrombectomy (odds ratio, 0.79; $95\%$ CI, 0.61– 1.01; $P=.06\$ ). The criteria of successful reperfusion in different trials is mentioned in Supplemental table 2 . There was no evidence of publication bias having a significant effect on the results (Egger regression intercept $P$ value [2-tailed] $=0.211$ . In our meta-analysis, we demonstrated the non-inferiority of endovascular thrombectomy alone when compared with combined intravenous thrombolysis and endovascular thrombectomy in patients with acute ischemic stroke with large vessel occlusion. The analysis included data from more recent trials in contrast to previous reviews and post-hoc analyses.8–10,15 Such a design allowed incorporation of technological advancements and larger sample sizes within the analysis. Due to small number of patients in each trial, a meta-analysis is important to increase the precision of estimate of various endpoints. MR CLEAN NO IV 14 was designed to also test for superiority of EVT alone, unlike the previous 3 trials.11–13 Trial specific non-inferiority threshold of endovascular thrombectomy alone compared with combined intravenous thrombolysis and endovascular thrombectomy was met in DEVT and DIRECT-MT;12,13 MR CLEAN-NO IV failed to show either superiority or non-inferiority of endovascular thrombectomy alone to combined intravenous thrombolysis and endovascular thrombectomy, 14 and the SKIP trial could not demonstrate significance for non-inferiority. 11  

There was a significantly lower rate of intracerebral hemorrhage and a trend towards lower rate of symptomatic intracerebral hemorrhage among patients randomized to endovascular thrombectomy alone. Intravenous thrombolysis can increase the risk of intracerebral and systemic bleeding complications compared with a placebo in patients with acute ischemic stroke. 16 Endovascular thrombectomy alone does not increase the rate of symptomatic intracerebral hemorrhage compared with medical management as shown by the HERMES study (Highly Effective Reperfusion Evaluated in Multiple Endovascular Stroke Trials). 1 The higher risk of intracerebral hemorrhage is probably related to direct effect of intravenous thrombolysis resulting in coagulopathy and blood-barrier disruption. 17 There was variation in the dose of intravenous tissue plasminogen activator (Table 1) and definition of symptomatic intracerebral hemorrhage between trials (Supplemental table 1 ).  

Some issues should be considered before interpretation of the results of this meta-analysis. The rate of 3-month rate of favorable functional outcome was lower among patients randomized in the DIRECT MT $(36.5\%)$ ) compared with SKIP $(58.3\%)$ ), DEVT $(50.4\%)$ , and MR CLEAN-NO IV $(50\%)$ . The differences were probably attributed to higher rates of collaterals in DEVT $(68.4\%)$ than DIRECT $\mathrm{MT}(24\%)$ . using the 5-point American Society of Interventional and Therapeutic Neuroradiology/Society of Interventional Radiology (ASITN/SIR) collateral flow grading scale, There were lower rates of internal carotid artery occlusion in DEVT $(15\%)$ compared with SKIP $(37.7\%)$ , DIRECT $\mathrm{MT}(35\%)$ and MR CLEAN-NO IV $(38.2\%)$ ). The studies included in our meta-analysis have included mostly Asian patients with exception of MR CLEAN-NO IV cohort, so we cannot generalize the finding. In addition to differences in response to intravenous thrombolysis, 18 there are other differences in patient triage paradigms, 19 which may limit the generalization to other regions. We also attempted to provide explanatory data by performing stratified analyses by sensitivity analyses. We acknowledge that another option would have been to just perform a narrative review, but we chose to perform a meta-analysis because these studies represented treatments in which the value of the average effect will be of interest We used trial-level data because patient-level data were not available. Patient-level data are unlikely to change the overall findings but may provide insight into confounding effects of patient and procedure-related variables. The possibility of publication bias cannot be completely excluded due to the borderline value for no significance (Egger test, $P=.211\$ ). The analysis does not provide any data on the effect of endovascular thrombectomy on quality of life, cognitive deficits, and 1-year death and disability.  

Generalizability of these randomized controlled trials to real-world practice requires careful consideration, three11–13 out of four randomized trials were carried out in Asian populations, which have a higher prevalence of intracranial stenosis. Intravenous thrombolysis did not appear to have delayed the initiation of thrombectomy in all included trials. However, the median time of hospital arrival to start of intravenous thrombolysis was 31 min in MR CLEAN NO IV 14 which is about half the time of that reported in two other trials $\underline{{\phantom{-}}}\underline{{1}}\underline{{2}},\underline{{1}}\underline{{3}}$ that may put intravenous thrombolysis group at a disadvantage in these two trials. SKIP trial reported randomization to intravenous thrombolysis time with mean (standard deviation) of 14 (10). 11 MR CLEAN NO IV, DIRECT MT, and DEVT trials reported data on successful recanalization on first angiographic run who underwent endovascular thrombectomy versus combined approach with intravenous thrombolysis and endovascular thrombectomy; $7(2.8\%)$ versus $9(3.7\%)$ , $8(2.4\%)$ versus 23 $(7\%)$ , $2(1.8\%)$ versus $3(2.6\%)$ , respectively $\cdot^{12-14}$ Clot migration in endovascular thrombectomy versus combined approach with intravenous thrombolysis and endovascular thrombectomy group was also reported in DIRECT MT, DEVT, and SKIP trials; $6(1.8\%)$ versus $17(5.1\%),20(17.7\%)$ versus $28(23.9\%)$ , 25 $(25\%)$ versus $28(27\%)$ .11–13 Data was not provided for any and ship patients enrolled in any of these included 4 randomized trials.  

The current ASA/AHA guidelines recommend intravenous thrombolysis within $4.5\mathrm{~h~}$ of symptoms onset even if endovascular thrombectomy is being considered. The current data is not considered strong enough to change the guidelines with elimination of intravenous tissue plasminogen activator in those who are candidates for endovascular treatment. 2 There are several more trials are ongoing to compare bridging therapy with tenecteplase which looks promising based on recent studies on safety and efficacy of tenecteplase in acute ischemic stroke with large vessel occlusion. 20 There are also trials that are either ongoing or whose results have not yet been published after peer review, such as; SWIFT DIRECT (Solitaire™ With the Intention For Thrombectomy Plus Intravenous t-PA vs. DIRECT Solitaire™ Stent-retriever Thrombectomy in Acute Anterior Circulation Stroke), and DIRECT SAFE (A Randomized Controlled Trial of DIRECT Endovascular Clot Retrieval vs. Standard Bridging Thrombolysis With Endovascular Clot Retrieval Within 4.5 Hours of Stroke Onset) because information relevant to meta-analysis was not available.  

# Conclusion  

Our study reported similar rates of favorable outcome with a lower rate of intracerebral hemorrhage in patients who underwent endovascular thrombectomy alone when compared to endovascular thrombectomy preceded by intravenous thrombolysis. A large phase 3 trial is required to conclusively demonstrate equivalency of both approaches to guide future practice. Based on our results, such a trial should demonstrate non-inferiority of the endovascular thrombectomy alone  

when compared with combined intravenous thrombolysis and endovascular thrombectomy in patients with acute ischemic stroke with large vessel occlusion.
