# Chronic subdural hematomas: single versus double burr holes  

This letter refers to the controversial issue of performing one versus two burr holes in the treatment of chronic subdural hematoma (CSDH). The retrospective study of Taussky et al.,1 in which the use of one versus two burr holes for CSDH treatment, was analyzed in respect to recurrence rates and complications, stated that the treatment of CSDH with one burr hole only is associated with a significantly higher postoperative recurrence rate, longer hospitalization length and higher wound infection rate. Katsigiannis instead in his retrospective clinical series founded no prognostic differences between one or two burr-hole but rather a worse outcome in patients treated with a mini-craniotomy.2 Whether one or double burr holes for unilateral CSDH is the optimal surgical strategy is still controversial3 and there is a paucity of reports comparing double (DBHC) and single burr hole craniotomy (SBHC) in the literature, and particularly a complete absence of prospective clinical trials studies.4 We present a prospective case series study that shows results discordant from those recorded by Taussky et al. To the best of our knowledge this is the first prospective study comparing results after SBHC and DBHC.  

We present the first prospective study comparing results after SBHC and DBHC.

Between March 2013 and January 2017, a total of 240 consecutive patients with unilateral CSDH, midline shift and/or neurological impairment due the subdural hematoma were admitted in our clinic and recruited into the study. Of 282 cases assessed for eligibility we excluded 22 patients in whom subdural hematoma was bilateral and 20 patients who declined to participate in the study.  

Patients were evaluated assessing their age, gender, neurological status based on the Markwalder’s Grade Scale (MGS) and preoperative CT $(95\%)$ or MRI $(5\%)$ .  

The first 120 consecutive patients were included in group I and treated by SBHC, while the following consecutive 120 patients were allocated in group II and underwent the DBHC. Thirty patients were lost at follow-up (in group I, 10 have become untraceable and seven refused to continue the study; for group II, eight have become untraceable and five refused to continue the study), therefore 210 were analyzed (103 for group I and 107 for group II).  

CTs were performed within 3 days of surgery, then patients were invited to attend a CTs after 1 month. Further CTs were obtained if the patients showed unexpected neurological deterioration. The mean clinical follow-up was about 30 months (range 6-42 months).  

Patients with a new neurological status impairment and CTs positive for recollection of CSDH were classified as recurrence and underwent a second operation. The trial was prepared according to the CONSORT guideline (Figure 1).  

Surgical procedures were performed under local anesthesia and sedation. The patient was positioned supine with head turned $30^{\circ}$ on contralateral side. In DBHC series the two burr holes were drilled one frontal and one parietal. In SBHC group the single burr hole was over the maximum width of the hematoma, with the exact location determined from the CT scan.

The subdural collection was washed out with saline solution using a nelaton catheter until the subdural fluid run clear.  

A soft drain is inserted into the subdural space through the burr hole, the drain is tunneled away from the scalp incision and connected to a gravity drainage system. In DBHC procedure drainage was inserted in parietal burr hole. The drain was then carefully removed after 48 hours.  

The $t$ -test was applied to compare the differences among the measured data. Categorical variables were compared using $\chi^{2}$ test or Fisher’s Exact test. $\mathrm{P}\mathrm{<}0.05$ was statistically significant.  

The two groups were similar regarding age, sex, imaging characteristics, neurological status and risk factors $(\mathrm{P}{>}0.05)$ . For group I (SBHC), the mean age was 66.5 years (range 45-91 years), male-to-female ratio was 56:47, 34 patients had hemostasis impairment due to anticoagulant/antiplatelet therapy $(27.2\%)$ , six $(5.8\%)$ alcohol abuse, and 11 patients were reported to be affected by diabetes mellitus. Based on the MGS, at the admission the mean value was 1.52. The mean preoperative hematoma thickness was $18\mathrm{mm}$ .  

Group II included 107 patients (59 male, 48 female), the mean age was 67.2 years (range 46-91 years), 27 patients $(25.2\%)$ were on anticoagulant/antiplatelet agents, seven $(6.5\%)$ alcoholic and 10 $(9.3\%)$ diabetic patients. The mean MGS rate was 1.54 and the mean preoperative thickness was $18\mathrm{mm}$ (Table I).  

The recurrence rate after surgery was $14.5\%$ (15 patients) for group I and $14.9\%$ (16 patients) for group II. Mean thickness of residual subdural fluid collection was about $5\mathrm{mm}$ for both groups $(\mathrm{P}{>}0.579)$ . Also, the two groups did not differ for mortality rate ( $1.9\%$ group I, $2.8\%$ group II) and for mean hospitalization time too $(\mathrm{P}{>}0.412)$ .  

Postoperative neurological status was similar between group I and $\mathrm{II}$ according to the MGS (group $\mathrm{I}{=}0.40$ , group $\mathrm{II}{=}0.42\$ . Mean operative time was significantly shorter $(\mathrm{P}{=}0.004)$ in group I of SBHC $27.6\mathrm{min}$ ) than in group II of DBHC $(52.4\mathrm{min}$ ).

In our series the two groups were statistically homogenous in terms of demographic, clinical and radiological characteristics, such as surgical procedure, performed with irrigation and drain, except for number of burr holes.  

Results regarding recurrence rate, mortality and length of stay in hospital were similar in the two groups and suggested both SBHC and DBHC as safe and effective surgical procedure for the treatment of CSDH. However, as already suggested by other authors,5 we recommend the use of SBHC because this technique allows for less infection rate and smaller postoperative volume of pneumocrania, and finally it takes shorter operation time.
