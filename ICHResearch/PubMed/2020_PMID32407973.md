# Early Exercise after Intracerebral Hemorrhage Inhibits Inflammation and Promotes Neuroprotection in the Sensorimotor Cortex in Rats  

Keigo Tamakoshi, a,b\* Keishi Hayao a,b and Hideaki Takahashi a,b a Department of Physical Therapy, Niigata University of Health and Welfare, Japan b Institute for Human Movement and Medical Sciences, Niigata University of Health and Welfare, Japan  

Abstract—The present study examined the effect of early exercise on brain damage and recovery of motor function following intracerebral hemorrhage (ICH) in rats. Subjects were randomly assigned to no training after ICH (ICH), no training after sham surgery (SHAM), early treadmill exercise after ICH $({\mathsf{ICH}}+{\mathsf{ET}})$ , and late treadmill exercise after ICH $(\mathsf{ICH}+\mathsf{LT})$ groups. The $\mathsf{ICH}+$ ET and $\mathsf{ICH}+\mathsf{LT}$ groups were trained for seven consecutive days starting on day 2 and day 9 after surgery, respectively. At post surgery day 16, the brain was surgically excised, and lesion volume, cortical thickness, neuronal number, dendritic length, and dendritic complexity were analyzed. Expression levels of IL-1b, TGF-b1, and IGF-1 mRNAs in ipsilateral sensorimotor cortex were measured by RT-PCR. The recovery of motor function in the $\mathsf{ICH}+$ ET group was the most accelerated. Cortical thickness and neuronal number were significantly higher in the $\mathsf{ICH}+\mathsf{ET}$ group than the ICH and $\mathsf{ICH}+\mathsf{LT}$ groups. The length and complexity of dendrites were also significantly greater in the $\mathsf{ICH}+\mathsf{ET}$ group compared with the ICH and $\mathsf{ICH}+\mathsf{LT}$ groups. Expression of IL-1b mRNA was significantly lower in the $\mathsf{ICH}+\mathsf{ET}$ group than that in the ICH group. Collectively, these results suggest that early treadmill exercise after ICH promotes recovery of sensorimotor function by preventing neuronal death and ensuing cortical atrophy and by preserving dendritic structure compared with late treadmill exercise and no exercise. Early exercise may prevent neurodegeneration and functional loss by inhibiting neuroinflammation. $\copyright$ 2020 IBRO. Published by Elsevier Ltd. All rights reserved.  

Key words: intracerebral hemorrhage, early exercise, motor functional recovery, sensorimotor cortex, neurodegeneration, neuroinflammation.  



# INTRODUCTION  

Intracerebral hemorrhage (ICH) causes critical damage to the brain accompanied by impairment of motor function in the long term. Many studies have showed that early rehabilitation training improves sensorimotor dysfunction after ICH. The effects of training regimens such as constraint-induced movement therapy (CIMT), running on a treadmill, and motor skills training are known from research on animals (Takamatsu et al., 2010; Tamakoshi et al., 2014; Ishida et al., 2015). In our previous study, we found that in rats, running on a treadmill improved the recovery of motor function and altered dendritic morphology in the striatum and sensorimotor cortex after ICH (Takamatsu et al., 2010, 2016). We further found that improved motor function after running on a treadmill after ICH may be associated with the prevention of dendritic regression caused by upregulation of TrkB expression (Takamatsu et al., 2016).  

Rehabilitation training initiated during the early phase after a stroke effectively recovers motor function, and evidence indicates that early exercise after ischemic stroke improves cerebral blood flow in the ischemic region, reduces infarct volume, and promotes functional outcomes (Yang et al., 2003; Nielsen et al., 2013; Zhang et al., 2013a,b). Other studies have reported that poststroke brains display heightened sensitivity to rehabilitation experience immediately after the stroke, which subsequently declines (Biernaskie et al., 2004). Increased levels of growth-promoting genes and proteins are produced immediately after the onset of stroke (Murphy and Corbett, 2009). The expression of growth-promoting genes and proteins involving M2 type microglia. M2 microglia expresses nerve growth factor IGF-1 and antiinflammatory factor TGF- $\cdot\upbeta$ , and is involved in neuroprotection. However, immediately after the onset of stroke, resting microglia change to ramified microglia with M1 type function and express pro-inflammatory factors such as IL- $\cdot1\upbeta$ . Thus, M2 type microglia activation time may be appropriate rehabilitation intervention time.  

Rehabilitation training after a stroke is important for promoting the recovery of motor function. In hemorrhagic stroke in rats, early treadmill training improves motor function compared with late treadmill training (Lee et al., 2005; Park et al., 2010). However, the detailed mechanism underlying this remains unknown because insufficient research is available to compare the effects of early and late running on a treadmill after ICH.  

Therefore, we focused on the effects of early and late running on a treadmill on damage to the striatum after ICH. We found that early running on a treadmill had no effect on damage to the striatum. In contrast, we discovered that running on a treadmill after ICH positively affected neurons and plasticity-related proteins expressed in the sensorimotor cortex (Takamatsu et al., 2016). Therefore, we investigated the temporal effects of exercise on cortical thickness, the number of neural cells, and dendritic plasticity in the sensorimotor cortex as well as damage to the striatum of rats subjected to ICH. In addition, we examined IL-1b, TGF-b1, and IGF1, which are factors expressed by microglia that induce neuroprotection and apoptosis in early stroke. Early rehabilitation may involve neuronal cell death and neuroplasticity by changing these factors.  



# EXPERIMENTAL PROCEDURES  



# Rats  

Adult male Wistar rats $(200-250~\mathfrak{g}$ , $n=91$ ) were housed together in clear Plexiglas enclosures at $23^{\circ}C\pm1^{\circ}C$ under a $12\mathsf{h}/12\mathsf{h}$ light/dark cycle. Food and water were freely available. Animal care and surgical procedures were performed according to the animal care guidelines of the Niigata University Committee on Health and Welfare. All efforts were made to minimize suffering and the number of animals used. The rats were randomly assigned to the groups as follows: sham operated rats $(n=28$ , SHAM), rats that did not exercise after ICH $'n=32$ , ICH), rats that exercised early after ICH $(n=31$ , $\mathsf{ICH}+\mathsf{ET},$ ), and rats that exercised late after ICH ( $n=22$ , $\mathsf{ICH}+\mathsf{LT})$ .  



# ICH surgery  

ICH was induced in the left stratum as previously described (Rosenberg et al., 1990; Tamakoshi et al., 2014). Rats were anesthetized with sodium pentobarbital $(45\mathrm{mg}/\mathrm{kg}$ , i.p.) and placed in a stereotaxic frame. A small hole was drilled $3.0{\cdot}\mathrm{mm}$ laterally to the midline and $0.2\cdot\mathrm{{mm}}$ anterior to the bregma, and $1.2\upmu\mathrm{I}$ saline containing $0.24\cup$ of bacterial collagenase (Type IV; SigmaAldrich, St Louis, MO, USA) was infused through a needle $4.0{\cdot}mm$ below the surface of the skull.  



# Exercise protocol  

The exercise protocol used in this study was slightly modified from the method used in previous studies (Tamakoshi et al., 2018). Before surgery, rats were trained to run on a motorized treadmill daily for $15\mathsf{min}$ at $6{-}11\mathsf{m/min}$ . The $\mathsf{ICH}+\mathsf{ET}$ group ran the treadmill for 7 days from postoperative day 2 and then remained inactive for another 7 days. The $\mathsf{ICH}+\mathsf{LT}$ group remained inactive for 7 days from postoperative day 2 and then ran on the treadmill for another 7 days. Treadmill training was 60 min/day, $9\mathsf{m/min}$ , on the first day and subsequently at $11\mathsf{m/min}$ .  



# Behavioral assessments  

Forelimb placing test. Vibrissae-evoked forelimb placing test was performed on all rats as previously described (Woodlee et al., 2005). Briefly, the animal (ICH: $n=24$ , $\mathsf{ICH}+\mathsf{ET}$ $n=23$ , $\mathsf{ICH}+\mathsf{LT}$ : $n=22$ ) is held by the torso and its vibrissae is brushed against the edge of a tabletop to elicit a forelimb placing response by the limb on the same side. Data are presented as the percentage of successful placing responses elicited in 10 trials. The dorsal hand-evoked forelimb placing test was performed as previously described (Ishida et al., 2011). Rats were held in the air with the forelimb suspended and advanced to the table. The replacing responses of the rats on the table were counted when the dorsum of the paw touched the edge of the table. Data are presented as the percentage of successful placing responses of 10 trials. All rats were tested on days 1, 8, and 15 after ICH.  

Horizontal ladder test. The horizontal ladder test was performed using an apparatus comprising side walls constructed of transparent panels and metal rungs as previously described (Metz and Whishaw, 2002). Rats (SHAM: $n=15$ , ICH: $n=16$ , $\mathsf{ICH}+\mathsf{ET}$ : $n=13$ , ICH $^+$ LT: $n=12$ ) walked across a ladder that was irregularly shaped with varying distances between rungs from 1 to 3 cm at each crossing. Rats were trained daily three times for 3 days from 2–4 days before surgery and then tested daily three times before surgery. Rats were tested on postoperative days 1, 8, and 15. The numbers of steps and correct placements (the midportion of the palm of the forelimb was placed on the rung), partial placements (the wrist or the digits of the forelimb were placed on the rung), and errors (the forelimb fell between the rungs) of the contralateral forelimb at each crossing were counted. The rates of correct, partial, and erroneous placements for these steps and their average values were calculated and represent the average of three trials. The rats evaluated for this behavior were used for brain damage analysis with Nissl staining and dendrite analysis with Golgi staining.  

Rotarod test. Before ICH, rats (SHAM: $n=8$ ICH: $n=8$ $\mathsf{ICH}+\mathsf{ET}$ : $n=6$ $\mathsf{ICH}+\mathsf{LT}$ : $n=5$ ) were conditioned to the accelerating rotarod. Each rat received a training session on the rotarod set at a constant speed of 4 rpm and was tested until they remained on the rotating spindle for $60\mathtt{s}$ . Each rat then was given five trials on the accelerating rotarod, during which the spindle increased from 4 to 40 rpm over 5 min (Chen et al., 2001). This data was averaged for five trials. Rats were tested on postoperative days 1, 8, and 15. The rats evaluated for this behavior were used for dendrite analysis with Golgi staining.  



# Brain damage  

The rats (SHAM: $n=7$ , ICH: $n=8$ , $\mathsf{ICH}+\mathsf{ET}$ : $n=7$ , $\mathsf{ICH}+\mathsf{LT}$ : $n=7$ ) were deeply anesthetized using sodium pentobarbital and then transcardially perfused with $0.9\%$ saline followed by $0.1\mathsf{M}$ phosphate buffer (PB, pH 7.5) containing $4\%$ paraformaldehyde. The brains were excised and fixed in the same fixative. Coronal sections $(40-\upmu\mathrm{m}$ thick) of specimens were incubated in cytoprotective $30\%$ sucrose and then cut every $400~\upmu\mathrm{m}$ from $+1.9$ to $-0.1$ using a cryomicrotome. Six sections were stained with Nissl, and the lesion volume, ventricle volume, cortical thickness (distance from cingulum to the surface of the cortex) were calculated using Image J software (MacLellan et al., 2008; Tamakoshi et al., 2014). These analysis items were measured in each of the six slices, and the percentages for the opposite side were calculated and averaged. The thickness, cell number, and cell density were analyzed in layer V of each section. The average values are presented, and each value is expressed as the ratio of injured to uninjured hemispheres (Fig. 4A–C)  



# Dendritic analysis  

Rats (SHAM: $n=8$ , ICH: $n=8$ , $\mathsf{ICH}+\mathsf{ET}$ : $n=6$ , ICH $+\textsf{LT}$ : $n=5$ ) were randomly selected after postoperative 16 days, deeply anesthetized using sodium pentobarbital $(45\mathrm{mg}/\mathrm{kg}$ , i.p.), and transcardially perfused with $0.9\%$ saline. The brain was immediately excised and placed in a mixture of mercuric chloride, potassium dichromate, and potassium chromate solution (FD Rapid Golgi Stain Kit; FD NeuroTechnologies, Inc., USA). Golgi staining procedures followed those recommended by the manufacturer. Brain sections prepared using a cryomicrotome were affixed to slides, dehydrated, cleared with xylene, and cover slipped in Permount (Merck, Darmstadt, Germany). We observed neurons in layer $\vee$ of the sensorimotor area using an optical microscope and photographed 3–5 cells. We used the Sholl Analysis plugin for NIH ImageJ software (Takamatsu et al., 2010, 2016). This technique involves superimposing a grid with concentric rings distributed at $10{\cdot}{\upmu}\mathrm{m}$ intervals, centered on the soma of a cell. The number of process intersections per ring was computed to evaluate branching complexity. The length of intersections per neuron was determined. Dendritic complexity and length data were averaged from 3 to 5 cells in each rat (Fig. 6A–C).  



# mRNA expression  

On postoperative days 8 (SHAM: $n=6$ , ICH: $n=8$ , ICH $^+$ ET: $n=8$ ) and 15 (SHAM: $n=7$ , ICH: $n=8$ , ICH $^+$ ET: $n=10$ , $\mathsf{ICH}+\mathsf{LT}$ : $n=10$ ), the sensorimotor cortex was excised, and qRT-PCR was performed. Briefly, total RNA was extracted from the bilateral sensorimotor cortex and striatum of each rat using the TRIzol (Life Technologies Japan Ltd, Tokyo, Japan). RNA concentration and purity were measured using a Quantus Fluorometer (Promega KK, Tokyo, Japan).  

Total RNA was used for one-step qRT-PCR with a One Step SYBR PrimeScript PLUS RTPCR Kit (Takara Bio, Shiga, Japan) and an ABI7300 Real-Time PCR System (Applied Biosystems, Foster City, CA, USA) according to the manufacturers’ instructions. The levels of the mRNA encoding $\upbeta$ -actin served as internal controls. Primer sequences were as follows: $\upbeta$ -actin, forward; $5^{\prime}$ - GGAGATTACTGCCCTGGCTCCTA- $\cdot3^{\prime}$ , reverse; $5^{\prime}$ -GAC TCATCGTACTCCTGCTTGCTG- $\cdot3^{\prime}$ ; IL- $\cdot1\upbeta$ , forward; $5^{\prime}{\circ}\mathsf{C}$ CCTGAACTCAACTGTGAAATAGCA- $\cdot3^{\prime}$ , reverse; $5^{\prime}$ -CC CAAGTCAAGGGCTTGGAA- $\cdot3^{\prime}$ ; TGF- $\upbeta1$ , forward; $5^{\prime}$ -CA TTGCTGTCCCGTGCAGA- $\cdot3^{\prime}$ , reverse; $5^{\prime}$ -AGGTAACGC CAGGAATTGTTGCTA- $\cdot3^{\prime}$ ; IGF-1, forward; $5^{\prime}$ -GCACTCT GCTTGCTCACCTTTA- $\bullet^{\prime}$ , reverse; $5^{\prime}$ -TCCGAATGCTG GAGCCATA- $\cdot3^{\prime}$ ; The mRNA level of each gene was normalized to that of $\upbeta$ -actin mRNA. The relative amounts of each product were calculated using the comparative Ct method (Tamakoshi et al., 2016, 2017).  



# Statistical analysis  

Results are expressed as the mean $\pm$ standard error of the mean (SEM). Statistical analyses were performed using the Statistical Package for the Social Sciences ver. 18.0 (Chicago, IL, USA) or Statcel4 (OMS, Saitama, Japan). The Shapiro–Wilk test was used to examine the normality of all results. One-way ANOVA and Tukey’s post hoc test was used for analyzing the significance of differences in the data between each group. Data that did not show normality in the Shapiro– Wilk test were analyzed using nonparametric tests (Kruskal–Wallis test, followed by the Steel–Dwass multiple comparison test). The criterion for significance was $P<0.05$ (See Fig. 1).

# Brain damage  

There were no differences in the lesion and ventricular volumes between all groups after postoperative 16 days (lesion volume: $\mathsf{H}(2)=1.1$ , $P>0.05$ , $\eta{\sfp}2=0.07$ , ventricular volume: $F(2,19)=0.18,$ $P>0.05$ $\eta\mathsf{p}^{2}=0.02)$ (Fig. 5A, B). The cortical thicknesses of ipsilateral to the lesions significantly decreased in the ICH group compared with those of the SHAM groups; moreover, it significantly increased in the $\mathsf{ICH}+\mathsf{ET}$ group compared with those of the ICH and $\mathsf{ICH}+\mathsf{LT}$ groups. Furthermore, the thicknesses of Layer $\vee$ ipsilateral to the lesions significantly decreased in the ICH group compared with those of the SHAM groups. The thicknesses of Layer $\vee$ ipsilateral to the lesions of the $\mathsf{ICH}+\mathsf{ET}$ group significantly increased compared with those of the ICH and $\mathsf{ICH}+\mathsf{LT}$ groups; however, it continued to decrease in the $\mathsf{ICH}+\mathsf{LT}$ group compared with those of the SHAM group (H(3) $=24.1$ , $P<0.0001$ , $\eta\mathsf{p}^{2}=0.75)$ (Fig. 5D). The numbers of pyramidal neurons in the ipsilateral layer V significantly decreased in the ICH group compared with those of the SHAM groups. The numbers of pyramidal neurons in the ipsilateral layer $\vee$ of the ICH $+\mathsf{ET}$ group significantly increased compared with those of the ICH and $\mathsf{ICH}+\mathsf{LT}$ groups. It continued to decrease in the $\mathsf{ICH}+\mathsf{LT}$ group compared with those of the SHAM group $(F(3,\quad25)=10.2,\quad$ $P<0.0001$ , $\eta\mathsf{p}^{2}=0.55)$ (Fig. 5E). There were no significant differences between neuronal densities between all groups in layer $\vee$ of the bilateral cortex (Fig. 5F).  

Early exercise after cerebral hemorrhage does not reduce tissue damage caused by hematoma, but it promotes neuroprotection in the motor cortex.

# DISCUSSION  

Here we investigated the effects on rats of early and late rehabilitation after ICH on sensorimotor function and neural protection in the sensorimotor cortex. We found that early rehabilitation had no significant effect on brain damage to the striatum and enlargement of the ventricle. Therefore, we analyzed cortical thickness, neuron number, dendritic morphology, and the expression of genes associated with inflammation and neural plasticity of the sensorimotor cortex after ICH. Our results reveal that early exercise after ICH inhibited atrophy of the cortex after inhibition of neural cell death and dendritic regression in the sensorimotor cortex compared with late exercise or no exercise. Further, we found that $||\cdot1|\r_{\mathrm{\beta}}$ mRNA levels of rats that engaged in early exercise were decreased in this region after ICH. To the best of our knowledge, this study is the first to show the effects of early exercise on inhibiting neural cell death and dendritic regression associated with downregulation of IL-1b mRNA levels in the sensorimotor cortex of rats subjected to ICH.  

We previously found that rehabilitation training such as running on a treadmill, motor skills, and CIMT have no effect on damage to the striatum after rats are subjected to ICH (Takamatsu et al., 2010, 2014; Ishida et al., 2011). Consistent with these findings, the treadmill-running protocol used in this study does not reduce damage to the striatum and the expansion of the ventricular after  

ICH. Other exercise conditions may have an effect on brain damage to the striatum (DeBow et al., 2003; Lee et al., 2005). Future studies are therefore required to identify effective exercise regimens that mitigate brain damage after ICH.  

In collagenase-injected or blood-induced models of ICH, cortical thickness reduced on the ipsilateral side compared with that on the contralateral side (MacLellan et al., 2008). Similarly, this alteration, which occurred in the present study, was caused by the reduction of cell number and the regression of dendrites in the sensorimotor cortex. In an iron-injection model of ICH, edema occurs in the ipsilateral cortex as well as the striatum $^{24\mathrm{h}}$ after surgery (Caliaperumal et al., 2012). Further, we found that the transcription of genes of rats that encode proteins that are involved in the inflammatory response increases in the sensorimotor cortex immediately after ICH (data not shown). These factors may induce neural cell death and the regression of dendrites in the sensorimotor cortex.  

Here we show that rats that exercised early after ICH there was inhibition of the decrease in cortical thickness (i.e., atrophy), particularly in layer V. Moreover, the decrease in the population of pyramidal neurons in layer V was inhibited by early exercise as well. Treadmill exercise decreases lesion size and suppresses the enhancement of caspase-3 expression after intrastriatal hemorrhage (Lee et al., 2003). Zhang et al. (2013a,b) used an ischemic model to show that early exercise can reduce apoptosis that occurs in focal cerebral ischemia by the inhibition of caspase-3 and upregulation of Bcl-2. Although early exercise contributes to the recovery of motor function, it (started within $^{24\textrm{h}}$ of ICH) has little effect after a stroke (group ATC, 2015). Studies of an animal mode system demonstrate that increased expression of proapoptotic proteins such as BAX and caspase-3 occurs when exercise is initiated $^{6\mathfrak{h}}$ after a stroke (Li et al., 2017a,b). Furthermore, very early exercise elevates the levels of cell stress and increases the expression of proinflammatory cytokines that are associated with injury caused by cerebral ischemia (Li et al., 2017a,b). Thus, there is a specific time after ICH when rehabilitation protects against neural death.

We show here that early exercise inhibits atrophy of the cortical thickness through the reduction of dendritic regression as well as the inhibition of neural cell death. Moreover, the reduction of dendritic regression associated with early exercise affects the contralateral cortex to a greater extent than the ipsilateral cortex. Further, robust rehabilitation training enhances dendritic growth in the uninjured hemisphere after focal ischemic injury to rats (Biernaskie and Corbett, 2001). Consistent with these studies are findings that acrobatic tasks improve motor function of rats with experimentally induced unilateral damage to the contralateral sensorimotor cortex (Jones et al., 1999), and the neuron-specific expression of brain-derived neurotrophic factor is high in the contralateral cortex after focal ischemia in rats (Madinier et al., 2013). These findings suggest that secondary damage to the ipsilateral motor cortex after an intrastriatal hemorrhage may induce neural plasticity in the contralateral motor cortex.

Evidence indicates that neural protection conferred by early exercise after ICH is induced through inhibition of the expression of IL- $\cdot1\upbeta$ mRNA. After the play important roles in the repair and regeneration of the central nervous system (Hanisch and Kettenmann, 2007). For example, microglia with an ameboid morphology are immediately activated by brain damage and mediate the release of cytokines and chemokines associated with neural inflammation and neurological damage, which can significantly inhibit the repair of the brain, including neurogenesis (Xiong et al., 2016). Activated microglia (M1-type) are found in the perihematomal region immediately after a hemorrhagic stroke (Zhao et al., 2007). Further, acute and strong microglial reactivity 6 h after ICH occurs in the sensorimotor cortex (Neves et al., 2017). Thus, microglial reactivity in the sensorimotor cortex may influence motor functional recovery. The neuroinflammation induced by activated microglia inhibits neurogenesis after a stroke. Overexpression of $||\cdot1|\rrangle$ contributes to chronic neuroinflammation, which adversely affects hippocampal neurogenesis in adults (Wu et al., 2013). After cerebral ischemia in rats, exercise downregulates the overexpression of Tolllike receptor 2 (TLR2), Toll-like receptor 4 (TLR4), myeloid differentiation factor 88, and nuclear factor-kappaB (Ma et al., 2013). These proteins induce the activation proapoptotic signal transduction pathways and upregulate the expression of proinflammatory factors (Tang et al., 2007; Brea et al., 2011). The TLR2/TLR4 heterodimer mediates inflammatory injury induced by hematomas during ICH (Wang et al., 2014). Early exercise after ICH may downregulate the expression of TLR, which inhibits IL-1b. IL-1b expression may differ when exercise conditions are varied according to type, frequency, intensity, and duration. Further studies are therefore required to identify the effects of each exercise condition on IL-1b expression.  

Here we found that there were no significant differences in the expression of TGF- $\upbeta1$ and IGF-1 mRNAs between early and late exercise. TGF- $\upbeta1$ mRNA is expressed in the ischemic cortex from days 2 to 15 after permanent occlusion of middle cerebral artery (Wang et al., 1995). In a mouse model of ischemic stroke, TGF- $\upbeta1$ reduces infarct volume, improves functional recovery, and enhances neurogenesis (Ma et al., 2008). IGF-1 protects the central nervous system from injury (Schäbitz et al., 2001; Zhu et al., 2009). These factors and vascular endothelial growth factor, which are proangiogenic and anti-inflammatory, are produced by M2-type microglia (Ponomarev et al., 2013). We hypothesize that early exercise activates M2-type microglia related to tissue repair, which is promoted by these factors after ICH. Evidence indicates that exercise after ischemic stroke influences the recovery of neurological function by increasing the population of neural progenitor cells via activation of the IGF-1/Akt signaling pathway (Zheng et al., 2014). However, activation of microglia after exercise varies according to age, sex, and the affected region of the brain (Kohman et al., 2013). These factors, which are induced by M2-type microglia, play important roles in promoting the recovery of motor function after a stroke. Our present findings indicate the potential importance of administering the appropriate rehabilitation therapy to mitigate the detrimental consequences of activation of M2-type microglia in the sensorimotor cortex.  

In conclusion, compared with late exercise, early exercise after rats are subjected to ICH promotes the recovery of motor function and inhibits the expression of proinflammatory factors that inhibit neural cell death and dendritic regression in the sensorimotor cortex. Although early exercise after ischemic stroke decreases the volume of the lesion, it may have no benefit for ameliorating the neuronal damage caused by a hemorrhagic stroke. Alternatively, neural protection in the sensorimotor cortex plays an importance role in promoting the recovery of motor functional after ICH. During rehabilitation therapy for hemorrhagic patients, the sensorimotor cortex, which is not directly influenced by hematoma, may serve as a therapeutic target to increase the efficacy of the recovery of motor function.
