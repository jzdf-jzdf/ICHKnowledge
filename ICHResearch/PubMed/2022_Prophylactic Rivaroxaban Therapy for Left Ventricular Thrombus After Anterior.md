# Prophylactic Rivaroxaban Therapy for Left Ventricular Thrombus After Anterior ST-Segment Elevation Myocardial Infarction  

Zhongfan Zhang, MD,a,\* Daoyuan Si, MD, PHD,a,\* Qian Zhang, MD,a Lina Jin, MD, PHD,b Haikuo Zheng, MD, PHD,a Ming Qu, MM,c Miao Yu, MM,a Zhenya Jiang, MM,a Delin Li, MM,a Souping Li, MM,a Ping Yang, MD, PHD,a Yuquan He, MD, PHD,a Wenqi Zhang, MD, PHDa  

# ABSTRACT  

OBJECTIVES The aim of this study was to investigate the effects of rivaroxaban on left ventricle thromboprophylaxis in patients with anterior ST-segment elevation myocardial infarction (STEMI).  

BACKGROUND Anterior STEMI is associated with an increased risk of left ventricular thrombus (LVT) formation. The contemporary role of prophylactic rivaroxaban therapy remains unclear.  

METHODS We randomly assigned 279 patients with anterior STEMI who had undergone primary percutaneous coronary intervention to receive, in a 1:1 ratio, low-dose rivaroxaban $2.5\mathrm{mg}$ twice daily for 30 days) and dual antiplatelet therapy (DAPT) or only DAPT. The primary efficacy outcome was the LVT formation within 30 days. Net clinical adverse events were assessed at 30 days and 180 days, including all-cause mortality, LVT, systemic embolism, rehospitalization for cardiovascular events, and bleeding.  

RESULTS The addition of low-dose rivaroxaban to DAPT reduced LVT formation within 30 days compared with only DAPT $(0.7\%$ vs $8.6\%$ ; HR: 0.08; $95\%$ CI: 0.01-0.62; $P=0.015$ ; $P<0.001$ for superiority). Net clinical adverse events were lower within 30 days in the rivaroxaban group versus those in the only DAPT group and remained relatively low throughout the follow-up period. There were no significant differences in bleeding events between the 2 groups in 30 days and 180 days. However, 1 case of intracranial hemorrhage (major bleeding) occurred in the rivaroxaban group within 30 days.  

CONCLUSIONS Our results supported that the short-duration addition of low-dose rivaroxaban to DAPT could prevent LVT formation in patients with anterior STEMI following primary percutaneous coronary intervention. A larger multipleinstitution study is necessary to determine the generalizability. (J Am Coll Cardiol Intv 2022;15:861–872) $\circleddash$ 2022 by the American College of Cardiology Foundation.

Previously, conventional triple anticoagulation (combination vitamin K antagonists [VKAs] and dual antiplatelet therapy [DAPT]) was recommended to prevent LVT in patients at high risk of STEMI, although it was not supported by high-quality evidence.5,6 With the recognition of the prognostic relevance of major bleeding from such a therapeutic regimen,7-10 interest in this area was waning. Prophylactic VKA anticoagulant therapy combined with DAPT, in the latest practice, is not recommended in patients with potential risk factors for LVT formation.11 For left ventricle thromboprophylaxis in this subgroup of patients at high risk, clinicians continue to face uncertainty in decision making. However, the advent of direct oral anticoagulants (DOACs), which attenuate fibrin formation by selective inhibition of factor Xa or thrombin, has renewed the interest in inhibition strategies that combine DAPT with an anticoagulant drug in this area.

As one of the most widely used DOACs, rivaroxaban has achieved positive results in atrial fibrillation thromboprophylaxis, which may potentially provide a new strategy for the prevention of LVT. Given the absence of randomized data evaluating the contemporary role of rivaroxaban prophylactic anticoagulation on LVT formation in patients with anterior STEMI, we designed a randomized clinical trial (RCT) to investigate the effect of rivaroxaban and DAPT on LVT formation in patients with anterior STEMI.  

# METHODS  

TRIAL DESIGN AND POPULATION. This was an investigator-initiated, single-center, open-label, superiority, randomized controlled trial, which was conducted in a national chest pain demonstration center located in China-Japan Union Hospital of Jilin University, Changchun, China. This trial was approved by the Ethical Review Board of China-Japan Union Hospital of Jilin University (approval no. 2019071611) and was registered in the Chinese Clinical Trial Registry (ChiCTR1900026249).  

Patients were eligible for this trial if they met the criteria for anterior STEMI and underwent primary percutaneous coronary intervention (PCI) without left ventricular thrombosis in baseline echocardiography. Anterior STEMI was defined as the following: 1) ischemic chest discomfort or other symptoms suggestive of ischemia for at least 30 minutes; and 2) at least 1-mm (0.1-mV) ST-segment elevation in ${\ge}2$ contiguous precordial leads on a standard 12-lead electrocardiogram. Exclusion criteria in this trial included: 1) intracranial, gastrointestinal, or

RANDOMIZATION AND MASKING. We randomly assigned patients (1:1) to either the group of therapy with rivaroxaban $\left(2.5~\mathrm{mg}\right)$ twice daily) and DAPT or only DAPT as needed. Patients were randomized within 48 hours after hospital admission. Randomization was generated by the statistician using a computer-generated sequence. The allocation sequence was concealed by the electronic case report form system until after randomization. Site investigators enrolled patients. Patients and investigators were not masked to group assignment; the statistician was masked for analysis of the primary outcome variable.

PROCEDURES. DAPT was the background therapy, including low-dose aspirin ${\mathrm{100~mg}}$ daily) therapy and a $\mathrm{P}2\mathrm{Y}_{12}$ inhibitor (clopidogrel $75\mathrm{mg}$ daily or ticagrelor $90~\mathrm{mg}$ twice daily). Because the most recent guidelines do not have relevant recommendations in prophylactic triple anticoagulation and duration in this area,11 we designed a 30-day duration for prophylactic anticoagulation according to the occurrence law of LVT (most within 30 days) and the current consensusbased recommendations regarding the length of triple therapy in other specific clinical settings in STEMI (Supplemental Appendix). Rivaroxaban $(2.5\mathrm{mg}$ twice daily) and DAPT were used in patients in the rivaroxaban group for 30 days or until it had to be discontinued because of the occurrence of an adverse event within 30 days. Patients in this trial also continued to receive all other prescribed medication. All patients were followed up for 90 to 180 days to assess the short-term prognosis with short-duration use (30 days) of rivaroxaban; during this period, patients would receive repeat imaging examinations and clinical follow-up. LVT was routinely evaluated by echocardiography, which was consistent with current guidelines. Given the accuracy of echocardiography, the results were evaluated by 2 cardiologists. If the diagnosis was suspicious, we confirmed it with cardiac magnetic resonance or contrast echocardiography (Supplemental Figures 1 to 3). Cardiac magnetic resonance was the last diagnostic tool when other imaging procedures were nonconfirmatory.12 All adverse or serious adverse events were recorded at the subsequent follow-up visits. Meanwhile, patients received interventions in case of uncontrolled events, including serious embolism and major bleeding, resulting in safety concerns, as judged by the investigator.

STATISTICAL ANALYSIS. The trial hypothesis was that rivaroxaban plus DAPT would be superior to only DAPT in preventing LVT. Intention-to-treat (ITT) analysis was the standard method for assessing superiority in the randomized trial.14 Thus, all randomized patients formed the ITT population, which was the population for the primary analyses. The key supporting analysis also was conducted on a perprotocol (PP) population, defined as all randomized patients who received the allocated treatment and completed follow-up.  

The incidence of LVT after anterior STEMI in previous studies was $4\%$ to $26\%$ , and the incidence in our center was $14.5\%$ (Supplemental Appendix). Based on previous observational studies and data from our center, we anticipated LVT formation in $4\%$ of the rivaroxaban group compared with $14.5\%$ of the DAPT group. Allowing for a $15\%$ dropout rate and a further $5\%$ loss for medical reasons, a sample size of 280 patients was required for $80\%$ power using a 2-sided test with $a=0.05$ . Additional power analyses were not performed for secondary outcomes.  

The characteristics were described by absolute and relative frequencies for categoric data and the median (IQR) or mean $\pm\mathsf{SD}$ for continuous data. Time-toevent data were presented as Kaplan-Meier estimates, and differences between the 2 groups were assessed by the log-rank test. Given the number of events available, univariate Cox proportional hazards regression analyses were used to identify the effect of prophylactic anticoagulation with rivaroxaban on the outcomes.15 Proportional hazard assumptions were checked by visual inspection and a weighted residuals test. No interim analysis and subgroup analysis were planned and performed. Data analyses were performed using SPSS version 24.0 (IBM Corp) and GraphPad Prism 7.00 software (GraphPad). All $P$ values had a 2-sided significance level of $5\%$ .

# RESULTS  

PATIENTS. Overall, 279 patients underwent randomization (Figure 2); 139 patients were assigned to rivaroxaban and DAPT therapy, and 140 were assigned to only DAPT therapy. The key clinical characteristics of all randomized patients are shown in Table 1 and are well balanced across the 2 groups. The median age was 58.0 years, and $22.1\%$ were women. The randomized patients had substantial comorbidity rates; $52.3\%$ had smoked, $48.0\%$ had hyperlipidemia, $42.7\%$ had multiple coronary artery disease, $35.8\%$ had hypertension, and $18.9\%$ had diabetes. Six patients in the rivaroxaban group and 3 patients in the DAPT-only group discontinued randomized treatment before 30 days (5 self-discontinued, 3 withdrew consent, and 1 stopped because of major bleeding). In total, 32 patients were lost to follow-up after the admission visit in all randomized patients, 14 withdrew consent during the intervention, and 5 discontinued the drug on their own. Therefore, the study population consisted of 279 patients in the ITT analyses and 228 in the PP analyses. The clinical characteristics of the PP population are provided in Table 2.  

PRIMARY OUTCOME. In the ITT population, the time-dependent cumulative event LVT rate within 30 days is represented in Figure 3A, showing a low incidence of LVT within 30 days in the rivaroxaban group versus the DAPT-only group (HR [log-rank test]: 0.080; $95\%$ CI: $0.03\substack{-0.24}$ ; log-rank $P=0.002$ ). LVT within 30 days occurred in 1 patient $(0.7\%)$ in the rivaroxaban group and 12 patients $(8.6\%)$ in the DAPT-only group (HR: 0.08; $95\%$ CI: $0.01\substack{-0.62}$ ; $P=0.015^{'}$ ) (Table 3). The estimated treatment difference was $-7.9\%$ $95\%$ CI: $-0.13$ to $-0.03$ ; $P<0.001$ for superiority), which met the criteria for the superiority of rivaroxaban and DAPT relative to only DAPT (Central Illustration). In the PP population, the time-dependent cumulative event rate of primary events is represented in Figure 3B, showing a decrease of primary events in the rivaroxaban group versus the DAPT-only group (HR [log-rank test]: 0.082; $95\%$ CI: 0.03-0.24; log-rank $P=0.002;$ . Primary events occurred in 1 patient $(0.9\%)$ ) in the rivaroxaban group and 12 patients $(10.3\%)$ in the DAPT-only group (HR: 0.08; $95\%$ CI: $0.01\substack{-0.63}$ ; $P=0.0.016^{'}$ ) (Table 3).  

SECONDARY OUTCOMES. The secondary outcome rates in the ITT population are presented in Table 3. Within 30 days, NACEs occurred in 9 patients in the rivaroxaban group and 23 patients in the DAPT-only group $(6.5\%$ and $16.4\%$ , respectively; HR: 0.37; $95\%$ CI: 0.17-0.80; $P=0.011_{.}^{'}$ ). The cumulative NACE rates are shown in Figure 3C. Two patients $\left(1.4\%\right)$ died from any cause in the rivaroxaban group and 3 patients $(2.1\%)$ in the DAPT-only group (HR: 0.667; $95\%$ CI: 0.11-3.99; $P=0.657;$ ). Meanwhile, rehospitalization for cardiovascular events occurred in 2 patients $\left(1.4\%\right)$ in the rivaroxaban group and 1 patient $(0.7\%)$ in the DAPT-only group. SE occurred less frequently in patients in the rivaroxaban group than in those in the DAPT-only group but with no significant differences $(0.7\%$ and $2.9\%$ , respectively; HR: 0.51; $95\%$ CI: $0.09\substack{-2.69}$ ; $P=0.415;$ ). The supporting analyses in the PP population were consistent with the main analyses (Figure 3D, Table 3).

SAFETY OUTCOMES. The bleeding rate was similar between the rivaroxaban and DAPT-only groups (Table 3). The median follow-up time for both the ITT and PP populations was 180 days (IQR for both groups: 180-180). In the ITT population, LVT occurred throughout the follow-up period in 2 patients $(1.4\%)$ in the rivaroxaban group and 14 patients $(10.0\%)$ in the DAPT-only group (HR: 0.14; $95\%$ CI: 0.03-0.60;  

$P=0.008;$ (Figure 4A, Table 4). It is noteworthy that the rivaroxaban group compared with the DAPT-only group exhibited a certain reduction in SE within the followup period, although there was no significant difference.

# DISCUSSION  

In the absence of data from RCTs on oral anticoagulation (OAC) in left ventricle thromboprophylaxis, this randomized trial was performed. We found that among patients with anterior STEMI following primary PCI, the addition of low-dose rivaroxaban $(2.5~\mathrm{mg}$ twice daily for 30 days) to DAPT reduced LVT formation within 30 days compared with only DAPT. The reductions in NACE outcomes also emerged within 30 days in the rivaroxaban group versus those in the DAPT-only group. Even after discontinuing rivaroxaban, relatively fewer NACEs were observed in the rivaroxaban group during the follow-up period. These findings provided additional insight into factor Xa inhibition rivaroxaban on left ventricle thromboprophylaxis in patients following a STEMI.  

Identifying safe and effective strategies to prevent LVT remains worthwhile because of the poor outcomes in patients with STEMI who developed LVT. Previous guidelines recommended triple therapy with VKA and DAPT in patients at high risk of LVT formation (anterior STEMI with associated wall motion abnormalities) to improve clinical outcomes (triple therapy duration $=3$ months).5,6 Evidence that formed the basis of previous guidelines came from a relatively earlier historical time. The current recognition of the prognostic relevance of major bleeding from the triple therapy with VKA questions the suggestion of prophylactic anticoagulation in patients with wall motion abnormalities following an acute anterior STEMI.9,10 The European Society of Cardiology 2017 STEMI guidelines do not provide any recommendation on strategies to prevent LVT formation.11 Rivaroxaban, as an OAC that directly and selectively inhibits factor Xa, has a different mechanism from VKA and plays a central role in thrombosis. The drug provides effective anticoagulation in various conditions with additional clinical benefits compared with warfarin.16-18 In atrial fibrillation thromboprophylaxis, rivaroxaban has been shown to have at least equivalent efficacy as warfarin while exposing patients to relatively lower risks of clinically significant bleeding.16 In stable coronary artery disease, the addition of rivaroxaban to aspirin lowered major vascular events, and deaths were reduced by $23\%$ , although there was no significant increase in intracranial bleeding or other critical organ bleeding. Given the positive results of rivaroxaban in various clinical settings, it is reasonable to extrapolate its efficacy to left ventricle thromboprophylaxis.

The net clinical benefit was measured by the composite of NACEs in this trial. The composite, which included all-cause mortality, LVT, SE, rehospitalization for cardiovascular events, and bleeding, favored the addition of rivaroxaban to DAPT in patients with anterior STEMI in the short duration. We found that there were lower rates of NACEs within 30 days in the rivaroxaban group, and they remained relatively low throughout the follow-up period compared with the DAPT-only group. Similar results were also obtained in the ATLAS ACS 2-TIMI 51 (AntiXa Therapy to Lower Cardiovascular Events in Addition to Standard Therapy in Subjects with Acute Coronary Syndrome-Thrombolysis In Myocardial Infarction 51) study, which in subgroup analysis reported that rivaroxaban plus DAPT reduced cardiovascular events in patients with a recent STEMI, and this benefit emerged early (30 days) and persisted during continued treatment with background antiplatelet therapies.20 These benefits have not been observed in previous warfarin-related studies. To some extent, the positive findings of this trial and the ATLAS ACS 2-TIMI 51 study both underscored the efficacy of greater degrees of antithrombotic therapy with rivaroxaban beyond DAPT following a STEMI. Nonetheless, cautious interpretation of these findings on NACEs is required given the modest sample size in this study. It is also noteworthy that the rivaroxaban group also observed a relatively lower incidence of SE than the DAPT-only group but was not statistically significant, which may be related to the small number of events in our study. According to previous studies on rivaroxaban, it is reasonable to assume that rivaroxaban plus DAPT has a positive effect on SE in patients with anterior STEMI, which needs to be confirmed in larger pragmatic RCTs.

In patients who require DAPT and OACs, balancing the benefit versus the bleeding risks remains a challenge. The addition of rivaroxaban to DAPT would be expected to increase bleeding compared with DAPT alone, which was observed in previous RCTs.20,21 Current recommendations advocate triple antithrombotic therapy should be as short as possible or even avoided based on the individual’s ischemic and bleeding risk profile.22 In the present study, a 30-day duration for prophylactic rivaroxaban anticoagulation was performed. A similar bleeding rate within 30 days was observed in both groups. However, 1 case of intracranial hemorrhage (major bleeding) occurred in the rivaroxaban group; this patient also had an intracranial vascular malformation. Moreover, in this study, ticagrelor was used in background antiplatelet therapies. The regimen of rivaroxaban $2.5\mathrm{mg}$ twice daily, aspirin, and ticagrelor has not been tested thoroughly. Limited data from the PIONEER AF-PCI (An Open-label, Randomized, Controlled, Multicenter Study Exploring Two Treatment Strategies of Rivaroxaban and a Dose-Adjusted Oral Vitamin K Antagonist Treatment Strategy in Subjects With Atrial Fibrillation Who Undergo Percutaneous Coronary Intervention) study reported that 33 patients received this regimen, and a relatively higher risk of bleeding compared with clopidogrel was observed.21 In this trial, in the rivaroxaban group, bleeding occurred in 3 $(3.5\%)$ ) of the patients who received ticagrelor (including 1 with major bleeding) and in 1 $\left(1.8\%\right)$ of the patients using clopidogrel. Based on the results from this trial and the PIONEER AF-PCI trial, we tend to think that higher potency P2Y12 inhibitors in the regimen of $2.5\mathrm{mg}$ rivaroxaban and DAPT should be used cautiously. However, the small number of events in this trial and the PIONEER AF-PCI trial limits the ability to draw definitive conclusions in this respect. In aggregate, the significant reduction in LVT and NACEs observed with the addition of low-dose rivaroxaban to DAPT in this trial was reassuring, which indicated that the bleeding risks observed did not offset the clinical benefit. Meanwhile, the potential effects of a more potent antiplatelet regimen on LVT prevention still need to be studied.23 In our study, the different $\mathrm{P}2\mathrm{Y}_{12}$ inhibitor–based DAPT was balanced well between the 2 groups and did not bias the observation of the results.  

STUDY LIMITATIONS. First, considering the low incidence of LVT after STEMI in the modern time, we selected patients with anterior STEMI, a population with a relatively high incidence of LVT, as the study population, but it was not limited to patients with anterior STEMI and anterior apical akinesis or dyskinesis. Although positive results were observed in this trial, more rigorous screening of patients would be beneficial. Second, a relatively high dropout rate was presented in the trial because of the impact of the coronavirus disease pandemic. Although the stability of the results was assessed in the ITT and PP analyses, a lower dropout rate might further reduce the attrition bias. Third, we designed a 30-day duration for prophylactic anticoagulation according to the occurrence law of LVT and the current recommendations regarding triple therapy in other clinical settings in STEMI. Based on the NACE results (most occurred within 90 days) observed in this trial, perhaps the appropriate extension of anticoagulation duration will be more beneficial under close follow-up. Fourth, with a cohort size of only 279 patients, this study was underpowered to detect the relative differences in the individual components of NACEs. Finally, our study was conducted at a large single institution. A larger multiple-institution trial with a more diverse population would be needed to assess the generalizability of our findings.  

# CONCLUSIONS  

Our results support the short-duration addition of low-dose rivaroxaban $\mathrm{\phantom{-}}2.5\mathrm{mg}$ twice daily) to DAPT to prevent LVT formation in patients with anterior  

STEMI who had undergone coronary interventions. Larger studies are necessary to determine generalizability.

# PERSPECTIVES  

WHAT IS KNOWN? Due to poor outcomes after left ventricular thrombosis, estimating the risk of thrombus formation and embolization as well as the utility of prophylactic treatment remains critical.  

WHAT IS NEW? In patients with anterior STEMI following primary PCI, the short-duration addition of low-dose rivaroxaban ( $2.5\mathrm{mg}$ twice daily) to DAPT prevented LVT formation and reduced NACEs compared with only DAPT.  

WHAT IS NEXT? Further studies are required to elucidate the mechanisms by which rivaroxaban favorably influences LVT formation in patients with anterior STEMI who had undergone interventional treatments. The duration of anticoagulation also needs to be further determined.
