# A clinical study on the association of clinical outcome and acute systolic blood pressure in cerebral hemorrhage patients  

Shifeng Kan1\*, Ran Sun1\*, Song Chai1, Ruijuan Zhou1, Haixin Yuan2, and Bo Yu1  

1Department of Rehabilitation, Shanghai General Hospital, Shanghai Jiaotong University, and 2Department of Rehabilitation, Shanghai Fifth Rehabilitation Hospital, Shanghai, China  

Key words blood pressure – cerebral hemorrhage – intensive treatment – hematoma enlargement  

Abstract. Objective: To investigate the outcome of the rapid lowering of elevated blood pressure in patients with intracerebral hemorrhage and to understand its association with clinical outcome. Materials and methods: Between July 2014 and June 2018, a total of 1,500 patients diagnosed with cerebral hemorrhage were randomized and assessed for their neurological symptoms and diagnosed with CT scan. 1,500 $(42\%)$ patients received intensive treatment, while 1,645 $(58\%)$ patients were assigned the guidelinerecommended therapy. The systolic blood pressure of these patients was measured every half hour during the first day of admission. The intensive-treatment group was further categorized into five different subgroups in $10\mathrm{-mmHg}$ intervals. On the other hand, the clinical outcome, as represented by the volume of hematoma, adverse events, modified Rankin scale etc., was measured and analyzed. Results: The volume of hematoma varied with a p-value of 0.014 among the investigated groups. There was no direct correlation among the five groups based on the systolic blood pressure groups and modified Rankin scale 4 – 6. The $140-150$ $\mathrm{mmHg}$ group observed an elevated risk compared to the $120-130~\mathrm{mmHg}$ group in the modified Rankin scale ( $(\mathrm{OR}=1.59\$ ; $95\%$ CI $(0.98\textrm{--}2.61)$ ). The hematoma enlargement increased significantly with a $\mathrm{p}$ -value of 0.012. There was no direct association or statistical significance between the occurrence of the clinical outcome and the multivariate relationship between the five groups based on the multivariates $(\mathrm{p}=0.513)$ . Conclusion: Systolic blood pressure ranging between 120  and $130\mathrm{mmHg}$ serves as an optimal goal for acute intracerebral hemorrhage by reducing the hematoma enlargement. It is also evident that the lowering of high mean systolic blood pressure after blood pressurelowering therapy usually leads to cardiorenal injury.

# What is known about the subject  

Elevated blood pressure is common among ICH patients in acute condition and results in poor outcome. Impact of acute therapy for ICH has been studied far less than other types of stroke. Clinical trial studies, such as the INTERACT and ATTACH, observed conflicting results thus causing a puzzle in recommending the optimal systolic BP after acute ICH.  



# What this research adds  

Intensive BP-lowering treatment is clinically feasible with better efficiency and safety compared to normal or ordinary BP lowering methods.   
BP ranging between 120 and $130\mathrm{mmHg}$ serves as an optimal goal for acute ICH by reducing the hematoma enlargement. Cerebral amyloid angiopathy is also an important cause for cerebral hemorrhage. Patients with strong deposition of $\mathrm{A}\beta40$ in the cerebral vessels are associated with subcortical hemorrhage.  



# Introduction  

Cerebral hemorrhage or intracerebral hemorrhage (ICH) is caused by bleeding of the cerebral membrane within the brain tissue [1]. ICH is a type of intracranial bleeding and a life-threatening type of stroke that occurs when the brain is deprived of oxygen and blood supply [2]. It is one of the most severe types of stroke that affected more than one million of the Chinese population in 2010, and a majority of the ICH population lives in Asia [3]. The symptoms of ICH include vomiting, headache, one-sided numbness, unconsciousness, etc. It is commonly caused by high blood pressure, arteriovenous malformations, or injury to the head [4]. The diagnosis of ICH is mainly conducted by CT scan, and the treatment focus is on controlling the bleeding and removing the blood clots [5]. ICH accounts for only $15\%$ of all strokes; about one-third of those affected die early, while the majority of ICH survivors face life-long disability. ICH is a major cause of disability compared to other forms of strokes. Moreover, hemorrhagic stroke causes a higher number of disability and deaths compared to ischemic stroke [6]. Despite knowledge of the magnitude and burden of ICH as well as the cost of treating ICH, there is still a lack of effective medical therapy and surgery for altering the clinical outcome of ICH [7]. Furthermore, the impact of acute therapy for ICH lags far behind that of other types of strokes. On the other hand, various studies, including the ATTACH (Antihypertensive Treatment of Acute Cerebral Hemorrhage) and INTERACT-2 (Second Intensive Blood Pressure Reduction in Acute Cerebral Hemorrhage Trial) assessment analysis, observed conflicting results, which has caused a puzzle in suggesting optimal systolic blood pressure after acute ICH [8, 9]. Thus, the present study aims to establish a correlation between the clinical outcome and acute systolic blood pressure in patients with cerebral hemorrhage. The study is intended to help in identifying the optimal systolic blood pressure goal. The observations and monitoring included radiological outcomes, which include CT scan, adverse events, and observation of acute systolic blood pressure obtained after ICH.

# Subjects and study design  

This exploratory analysis started in July 2014 and ended in June 2018. A total of 1,500 patients diagnosed with ICH in the Critical Care Center of General Hospital, Shanghai Jiaotong University, Shanghai, China were assessed in the present study. The neurosurgeons of the hospital evaluated all patients for neurological symptoms, and the diagnoses were based on CT scans in all patients. The patients were randomized, and systolic blood pressure (maximum/minimum) was measured every half hour during the first day of admission. Further, the patients were categorized into five different groups within intervals of $10\mathrm{mmHg}$ based on the mean value of the average systolic blood pressure measured every hour during the first 24 hours of admission. The patients were randomly assigned to receive different blood pressurelowering treatments. The National Institutes of Health Stroke Scale (NIHSS) score was used to assess neurological status based on the CT scan taken during the first 24 hours. The CT scan images were further processed in the imaging analysis center and assessed by two specialists: a radiologist and a neurosurgeon who were not aware of the diagnosis and the patient’s clinical symptoms.

# Ethical approval and standards  

All procedures were carried out in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki, 1964 and its later amendments. The protocol and procedures were approved by the Hospital Ethical Research Committee (HERC-2014-  

The clinical outcomes were based on the following observations: hematoma enlargement, adverse events, modified Rankin scale of 4 – 6, and the death of the subject between 0  and  90 days. Hematoma enlargement is considered as an increase in the volume of the hematoma above $6~\mathrm{cm}^{3}$ within the first 24 hours on the CT scan in comparison with baseline characteristics. Adverse events were defined as a gradual impact on the renal and cardiac systems during the first week. The cardiorenal adverse events are comprised of mostly type-1 (acute cardiorenal syndrome) and type-2 (chronic cardiorenal syndrome).

# Immunohistochemical analysis  

For the analysis of cerebral amyloid angiopathy (CAA), the hematoma samples were evacuated from the patients underwent surgery, and the sample tissue were treated with $90\%$ alcohol for dehydration. Further, the samples were treated with $70\%$ alcohol and washed with xylene and prepared for paraffin sectioning, and the tissues were cut with a thickness of $5\upmu\mathrm{m}$ and stained with hematoxylin and eosin. For the immunohistochemical analysis, the samples were blocked with the respective primary antibodies for amyloid $\upbeta$ -protein: $\mathrm{A}\S{-}40$ and $\mathrm{A}\S\l\mathbf{-}42$ . The slides were incubated with an immunostainer (Ventana Discovery, Ventana Medical Systems, Tucson, AZ, USA) and visualized as per the manufacturer’s protocol. The slides were counterstained with hematoxylin, then they were further allowed to dehydrate and were mounted.  



# Statistical analysis  

All analyses were carried out using SPSS Statistics 25.0. Analyses were performed on the pooled individual patient data using the following factors as covariates: sex, age, BMI, NIHSS score, the volume of hematoma and its lobar location. Group comparisons between the clinical outcomes were carried out with the $\chi^{2}$ -test. Statistical significance was fixed at $\mathrm{p}<0.05$ .  



# Results  

From July 2014 through June 2018, a total of 3,145 patients with ICH were enrolled in the study. 1,500 patients were randomly assigned to the procedure for intensive treatment of blood pressure reduction, and 1,645 patients were assigned to the guideline-recommended therapy. Table 1 represents the management of blood pressure in the first 24 hours after the onset of ICH. The median time for the treatment with intravenous administration was shorter in the intensivetreatment group compared to the guideline group (4.2 hours vs. 4.5 hours). The patients in the intensive group were also given higher numbers of multiple intravenous agents to lower their blood pressure. On the other hand, the number of patients who received any kind of treatment for lowering blood pressure after ICH onset was comparatively lower in the guideline-recommended group (Table 1). The mean systolic blood pressure gradually became different between the two groups after 1 hour of the intensive treatment. 1,062 $(70.80\%)$ patients received an intravenous agent by infusion in the intensive group compared to 463 $(28.15\%)$ in the guideline-recommended group. However, 816 $(54.40\%)$ patients in the intensive group and 383 $23.28\%)$ patients in the guidelinerecommended group had their blood pressure controlled with bolus injections alone. Urapidil used in 507 $(33.8\%)$ patients and nicardipine used in 263 $(17.53\%)$ patients were the most commonly used intravenous agents among the patients in the intensive blood pressure-lowering group (Table 1). It was also observed that the use of oral agents was higher in the intensive group compared to the guideline-recommended group.

# Discussion  

In the present investigation, the outcome of the patients assigned to the intensivetreatment group showed a significant better functionality compared to the guideline-recommended group. In fact, the management strategy was performed and applied with extreme care and monitoring of the patients with reasonable efficiency. Moreover, the intensive treatment showed a reduction of the hemorrhage compared to the guidelinerecommended treatment. The expansion of hematoma is a strong indicator for mortality among ICH patients. Therefore, this study provides vital information on the beneficial effects of rapid control of high blood pressure and the intensive-treatment strategy. In fact, the patients in the intensive-treatment group showed improved physical and psychological condition.  

It was observed that the modified Rankin scale ranged between 4 and 6 when the average minimum systolic blood pressure ranged between 120  and $130\mathrm{mmHg}$ compared to $140\mathrm{~-~}150~\mathrm{mmHg}$ . The study also observed that the modified Rankin scale ranging between 4  and  6 was directly associated with the risk for hematoma enlargement and represented a gradual increase in the average minimum systolic blood pressure from 120  to $130\mathrm{mmHg}$ . Additionally, the modified Rankin scale ranging between 4  and  6 was also directly associated with cardiorenal adverse events within a week among the subjects with systolic blood pressure $\ge140$ mmHg compared to the patients ranging between 120  and $130\mathrm{mmHg}$ . Therefore, it is deduced that there is no direct benefit to the clinical outcome by suppressing the hematoma enlargement and lowering the systolic blood pressure between 120  and  130 after ICH [10]. This may be due to the complications caused by cardiac and renal dysfunction arising from the stroke. The modified Rankin scale of 4 – 6 at 90 days also leads to the identification of a positive association between the systolic blood pressure and the average minimum systolic blood pressure of two different groups: 120 – 130 and $140-150\mathrm{mmHg}$ [11].

In the present study, the group under the intensive treatment, which comprised $\sim57\%$ of the population that had a target of $>140\mathrm{mmHg}$ for the minimum systolic blood pressure, showed a better result compared to the standard-treatment group having a target of $140-160~\mathrm{mmHg}$ . This indicated a positive adjustment even after the treatment assessment, which is also consistent with the results of INTERACT data and analysis [12].  

On the other hand, Shi et al. [13] reported that hematoma enlargement among the investigated ICH subjects can be assumed to be a good predictor for poor clinical outcome, and Arima et al. [14] observed that speedy lowering of systolic blood pressure leads to a better reduction of the hematoma enlargement. This is because the clinicians had prescribed nicardipine, which is a wellknown antihypertensive drug for lowering blood pressure [15]. However, the results in our study showed an apparent effect on the suppression of hematoma enlargement thereby indicating an enhanced clinical result. In ATTACH-2, there was a decrease in the level of systolic blood pressure of $10\mathrm{mmHg}$ in the first 24  hours compared to INTERACT-2. Moreover, the systolic blood pressure based on an hourly minimum was equivalent to  

$140\mathrm{mmHg}$ in the ATTACH-2 compared to $150\mathrm{mmHg}$ in INTERACT-2 [16], thus causing a reduction of hematoma enlargement in the ATTACH-2, which may have been due to the exhaustive lowering of systolic blood pressure. However, it does not show any correlation with the decrease in the modified Rankin scale from 4 to 6. In the present study, $\sim15\%$ of the subjects who had adverse events developed renal injury, which included renal impairment and renal failure. These subjects also tended to have a gradual rise in serum creatinine level in their blood, thus indicating symptoms of acute kidney failure because of the sudden decrease in blood pressure. The same has been observed in the ATTACH-1 study where a sudden decrease in blood pressure led to renal ischemic injury [17]. However, Pannu et al. [18] reported that prerenal kidney injury can be overcome by suitable fluid replacement and that it does not cause major renal damage morphologically. However, the present study suggests that very careful observation and care of the kidney is a necessary step after a gradual decrease in blood pressure level.  

The study also found that adverse events in the cardiac system had a direct influence in the range of 4 – 6 of the modified Rankin scale. Both renal and cardiac injury disturbs the positive effects of lowering blood pressure. Moreover, it was observed that the prevalence of adverse effects in the kidney and cardiac systems was increased in the subjects with $4-6$ modified Rankin scale in the low systolic blood pressure group [19]. Interestingly, it was also observed that lowering systolic blood pressure is directly associated with a reduction of hematoma enlargement followed by the increase in adverse events of both the kidney and cardiac system [20]. In the present study, the outcome of hematoma enlargement is seen in a small amount of patients with systolic blood pressure reduction compared to patients with adverse events in the kidney and cardiac system. However, considering that the renal injury is curable, due care should be taken in hypotension-related patients who may benefit from lowering systolic blood pressure. On the other hand, the analysis by Hemphill et al. [21] suggests an acute lowering of the systolic blood pressure to $140\mathrm{mmHg}$ for ICH patients. However, this value was doubtful from the analysis by Boulouis et al. [22]. Whereas in the Stroke Acute Management with Urgent Risk-factor Assessment and Improvement study, systolic blood pressure $<130\mathrm{mmHg}$ was oppositely associated with modified Rankin scale range between 4 and 6. However, in the present study, the most achievable average hourly minimum systolic blood pressure between 120 and 130 $\mathrm{mmHg}$ was observed to be the most favorable value for an improved clinical outcome. Thus, considering all the accounts of these findings and reports, a mean systolic blood pressure of $130\mathrm{mmHg}$ might be the ultimate goal for patients with acute ICH during the first 24  hours. In the present study, we attempted to evaluate the association in the range of $4-6$ in the modified Rankin scale and systolic blood pressure. The main findings include that in the range of 4 – 6 in the modified Rankin scale, the lowest chance for hematoma enlargement was observed, while the average and systolic blood pressure of $120-130~\mathrm{mmHg}$ serves as an optimal goal for reducing hematoma enlargement in acute ICH compared to other categories. Another finding was that the modified Rankin scale 4  –  6 was directly associated with the risk of hematoma expansion, and an increase in the systolic blood pressure from 120 to 130 $\mathrm{mmHg}$ in a linear fashion was observed. The last finding is that cardiorenal injury, which is directly associated with modified Rankin scale 4  –  6 also occurred the least in the 140 – 150 mmHg group.  



# Conclusion  

The study concludes that intensive blood pressure-lowering treatment is clinically feasible with better efficiency and safety for the patients. Blood pressure in the range of $120-130~\mathrm{mmHg}$ serves as an optimal goal for acute ICH by reducing the hematoma enlargement. However, although systolic blood pressure between 120 and $130\mathrm{mmHg}$ serves as an optimal goal during the first 24 hours of acute ICH by suppressing hematoma enlargement, the study also observed that the reduction of high mean systolic blood pressure after blood pressure-lowering therapy gradually caused cardiorenal injury. Lastly, our study found that CAA is also an important cause of cerebral hemorrhage among patients. Patients with a strong deposition of $\mathrm{A}\beta40$ in the cerebral vessels are associated with subcortical hemorrhage. Thus, our findings will provide a new strategy for treating patients with ICH and CAA.
