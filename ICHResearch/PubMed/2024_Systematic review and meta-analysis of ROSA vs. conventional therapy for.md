# Systematic review and meta‑analysis of ROSA vs. conventional therapy for intracerebral hemorrhage  

Li Luo1 $\cdot$ Chuan‑long ${\mathsf{He}}^{1}$ · Wei Li2 · Xiao‑ping Tang  

Received: 20 July 2024 / Accepted: 31 July 2024 / Published online: 21 August 2024   
$\circledcirc$ The Author(s), under exclusive licence to Springer-Verlag London Ltd., part of Springer Nature 2024  

# Abstract  

The purpose of this systematic review and meta-analysis was to evaluate the perioperative and short-term results of the Robot of Stereotactic Assistance (ROSA) compared to traditional approaches in individuals with intracerebral hemorrhage (ICH). We will perform a comprehensive computerized search of PubMed, CNKI, Embase, and Google Scholar to identify relevant literature on ROSA vs. conventional therapy for intracerebral hemorrhage, covering publications from the inception of each database until July 2024. This study will include both English and Chinese language studies. Literature screening will adhere strictly to inclusion and exclusion criteria, focusing on randomized controlled trials (RCTs) and cohort studies. The ROBINS-I tool is utilized for evaluating bias risk in non-RCTs. Analysis of the data from the studies included will be conducted with Review Manager 5.4.1. The final analysis included 7 retrospective cohort studies and 1 randomized controlled study, involving a total of 844 patients. Among these, 433 patients underwent ROSA, while 411 received conventional treatment (conservative treatment, conventional craniotomy, or stereotactic frame-assisted surgery). Compared to conventional therapy, patients treated with ROSA showed improvements in operative time, postoperative rebleeding, postoperative extubation time, and intracranial infection. Nonetheless, there was no notable contrast in mortality or central hyperthermia outcomes between the two treatments. ROSA is a safe and viable option for treating patients with cerebral hemorrhage, showing significant advantages in terms of surgery duration, postoperative rebleeding, time to remove the breathing tube, and intracranial infection compared to conservative treatment, traditional craniotomy, or stereotactic surgery.

# Introduction  

Intracerebral hemorrhage (ICH) occurs due to the rupture of brain blood vessels, often linked with arterial hypertension [1]. It is the second most common cause of death from stroke globally [2], responsible for around $8\%$ to $30\%$ of all stroke incidents [3]. Current treatment guidelines from AHA/ASA encompass a range of approaches including medical management and various surgical interventions such as endoscopic surgery, craniotomy, and minimally invasive procedures [4].  

Traditional skull surgery (TSS) provides advantages such as efficient removal of blood clots, decreased swelling in the brain, enhanced flow of cerebrospinal fluid, stopping of bleeding, and management of pressure inside the skull. However, CC is associated with longer operation times and risks of complications including brain injury and infection [5]. Since its introduction for treating hypertensive ICH in 1978 [6], stereotactic neurosurgery has consistently shown superior outcomes compared to traditional methods. This includes better functional recovery, lower mortality rates, reduced complications, shorter hospital stays, faster operations, and improved hematoma evacuation [7–9]. Nevertheless, traditional stereotactic frame systems do not provide clear three-dimensional views of hematoma morphology, posing challenges for precise drainage tube placement and potentially increasing the risk of postoperative infections [10].  

Medtech’s ROSA (Robot of Stereotactic Assistance) integrates advanced technologies such as six-dimensional robotic arm sensing, marker-free automated registration, and sophisticated software programming. These features enable precise robotic manipulation, accurate positioning without the need for traditional frames, automated navigation, and surgical planning. ROSA’s live camera allows surgeons to plan and adjust pathways in real time, thereby expanding the surgical field, reducing errors, saving localization time, and enhancing precision. Compared to CC, ROSA minimizes damage to critical brain areas, reduces operation times, improves hematoma clearance rates, and lowers risks of vascular injury, especially when guided by CT angiography [11]. Studies, such as those by Sun [12] (2018), have demonstrated ROSA’s superiority over stereotactic frame surgery in terms of operation duration, postoperative infection and rebleeding rates, complication rates, and a significant reduction in 30-day postoperative mortality. Xiong’s (2020) analysis across seven studies further supports robotic surgery as a safe and effective approach for treating cerebral hemorrhage.  

So far, there has not been a comprehensive assessment or meta-analysis focused on examining the effectiveness of ROSA in the treatment of intracerebral hemorrhage. Given the increasing global adoption of robotic surgery, our study aims to provide substantial evidence supporting ROSA’s role in advancing neurosurgical practices.  

# Literature search  

The research follows the protocols of systematic review and meta-analysis and is recorded in the PROSPERO database under the registration number CRD42024568731. Two researchers (LL and HCL) independently conducted literature screening and data extraction to find studies that met the inclusion criteria. We conducted a comprehensive search of the PubMed, CNKI, Embase, and Google Scholar databases from their inception to July 2024, encompassing both English and Chinese literature. Search terms included “cerebral hemorrhage”, “stereotactic”, “robot” and similar terms, utilizing a combination of subject headings and freetext keywords. In addition, relevant articles were identified through manual searches and by reviewing the references of retrieved studies for additional eligible studies, which were included in the detailed analysis.  

# Inclusion and exclusion criteria  

The inclusion criteria for this study were as follows: adult patients diagnosed with cerebral hemorrhage, where the intervention involved ROSA, and the control group received traditional treatments such as conservative therapy, conventional craniotomy, or stereotactic frame surgery. The primary outcomes of interest were operative time, postoperative rebleeding, and mortality. Secondary outcomes included postoperative extubation time, intracranial infection, and central hyperthermia. Study designs considered for inclusion were RCTs and cohort studies. Exclusion criteria included studies that did not have original data, studies involving animals, studies with only one group, abstracts from conferences, and reports of individual cases.  

# Conducting analysis and extracting information  

Two researchers independently conducted literature screening based on pre-defined inclusion and exclusion criteria. Initially, they identified eligible studies by evaluating titles and abstracts. Subsequently, both researchers independently reviewed the full text of eligible articles. In cases where discrepancies arose during the screening process, they resolved them through discussion or sought the opinion of a third researcher to achieve consensus. The ultimate compilation of studies will encompass information like the primary author, year of publication, patient count, breakdown of patient age and gender, and primary and secondary outcome assessments of the studies.  

# Statistical analysis  

Statistical analysis will be performed utilizing Review Manager 5.4.1 software. Weighted mean differences (WMD) will be used to present continuous variables, while odds ratios (ORs) will be used for binary variables, along with their respective $95\%$ confidence intervals $95\%$ CI). When studies provide median, quartiles, or extreme values, we will transform them into mean and standard deviation (SD) using the techniques outlined by Wan [13] et al. Categorical variables will undergo testing with the Cochran-Mantel–Haenszel technique, whereas continuous variables will be analyzed using the inverse variance approach. Given the anticipated heterogeneity among studies, we will employ a randomeffects model for all analyses. The I2 statistic will assess heterogeneity, with values exceeding $50\%$ indicating significant heterogeneity. A $p$ value below 0.05 will determine statistical significance. The quality of cohort studies will be assessed using the ROBINS-I tool due to the inclusion of seven cohort studies and one randomized controlled trial (RCT). Sensitivity analyses will involve one-by-one exclusion of studies to test result robustness and identify potential sources of instability. Because there are only a few studies included ( $\leq$ ten), we will not conduct additional analysis for publication bias since the statistical test power may not be adequate [14, 15].  

# Result  

# Baseline characteristics  

Following our search strategy and adherence to inclusion and exclusion criteria, we identified and included a total of eight studies in this meta-analysis: seven cohort studies [12, 16–21] and one [22]RCT. These studies collectively involved 844 patients, with 433 undergoing ROSA and 411 receiving conventional therapy. Notably, all studies originated from China. Table 1 provides an overview of the features and main results of these research projects. Table 2 showed that there were no notable discrepancies in age $(P=0.79)$ ), gender distribution $\scriptstyle{\left(P=0.28\right)}$ ), preoperative Glasgow Coma Scale (GCS) score $_{\overset{}{\calP}=0.88,}$ ), and hematoma volume $\scriptstyle{\calP}=0.82,$ ) between the ROSA and traditional therapy groups, indicating that the study groups were similar. The PRISMA flowchart detailing our literature search process is presented in Fig. 1, illustrating the selection and inclusion of studies according to PRISMA guidelines.  

# Assessment of quality  

This study assessed the quality of the seven cohort studies by utilizing the ROBINS-I tool. The risk of bias assessment revealed that the study by Li18 et al. was categorized as ‘Low’ risk, while the study by Sun12 et al. was rated as ‘Critical’. Five studies that were left were evaluated to have a ‘Moderate’ risk of bias. Detailed results of the quality assessment can be found in Fig. 2.  

# Primary outcome measures  

According to the meta-analysis of the included studies, ROSA demonstrated superior performance compared to conventional treatment on several key indicators. ROSA was linked to a notably reduced duration of surgery (weighted mean difference $-47.41$ , $95\%$ CI  – 59.92 to $-34.89\$ ; $p<0.00001,$ (Fig.  3A) and a notably decreased occurrence of postoperative rebleeding (odds ratio 0.14, $95\%$ $\mathrm{CI}0.06{-}0.34$ ; $p<0.00001,$ (Fig. 3B). However, there was no statistically significant difference in mortality between the two treatment modalities (odds ratio 0.30, $95\%$ CI $0.09{-}1.04$ ; $p{=}0.06,$ (Fig. 3C).  

# Secondary outcome measures  

The meta-analysis findings showed that ROSA outperformed traditional treatment in various important metrics. ROSA was linked to a notably reduced extubation time after surgery (weighted mean difference $-1.75$ , $95\%$ CI  – 2.34 to $-1.16$ ; $p<0.00001.$ ) (Fig. 3D) and a significantly decreased risk of intracranial infection (odds ratio 0.06, $95\%$ CI 0.01–0.26; $p{=}0.0002\$ (Fig. 3E). Nonetheless, there was no notable variance in the occurrence of central hyperthermia among the two treatment approaches, with an odds ratio of 0.82 and a $95\%$ confidence interval of 0.48–1.40, yielding a $p$ value of 0.46 as shown in Fig. 4A.  

# Sensibility analysis  

The meta-analysis we conducted showed significant variability in multiple findings, with I2 values of $98\%$ for surgery duration, $97\%$ for extubation time after surgery, and $78\%$ for mortality rates. To identify the sources of this heterogeneity, we conducted a sensitivity analysis using the one-byone exclusion method. The results indicated no significant change in heterogeneity after excluding Wang20 et al. In 2019, it was shown that patients who received ROSA had a decreased mortality rate (weighted mean difference 0.16, $95\%$ C $\mathrm{~T~}0.06{-}0.43$ ; $p=0.00002)$ as depicted in Fig.  4B. Further analysis suggested that the higher mortality rate observed in the ROSA group in the Wang et al.study (four out of 35 patients in the ROSA group vs. Two out of 49 in the conservative treatment group) could be attributed to the severity of thalamic hemorrhages, which are more severe and likely to cause death compared to other types of intracerebral hemorrhage. The examination of additional research demonstrated that there was no significant alteration in heterogeneity or conclusions when excluding other studies, suggesting that the results of our meta-analysis remained stable.  

# Discussion  

The meta-analysis of the included studies demonstrated that ROSA significantly reduced operative time compared to conventional treatments. For instance, Han [17] reported that ROSA required smaller incisions, and shorter operative times, and caused less brain tissue damage than conventional craniotomy. The mean operative time was markedly reduced to $40.3\pm7.0$ min in the ROSA group compared to $143.1\pm61.3$ min in the conventional craniotomy group.

In the same way, Li [18] discovered that the average duration of the operation was $30.15\pm8.73$ min for the ROSA cohort compared to $48.03\pm7.69\mathrm{{min}}$ for the traditional frame cohort $(p<0.05)$ . Time is crucial for individuals with cerebral hemorrhage, and ROSA efficiently saves valuable time for treatment. Due to its minimally invasive nature and reduced operation duration, ROSA enables surgery for patients with poor physical conditions and multiple organ injuries, thereby expanding the applicability of surgical interventions compared to traditional craniotomy for hematoma evacuation. The study highlights that minimizing surgical trauma, achieving rapid and thorough hematoma evacuation, reducing damage to surrounding brain tissue, maintaining cerebrospinal fluid circulation, and lowering systemic and local intracranial pressure are crucial for improving patient prognosis [23].  

According to our meta-analysis of the included literature, ROSA significantly reduced postoperative rebleeding compared to conventional treatments. Cerebral hemorrhage causes blood to enter brain tissue, where thrombin can lead to secondary parenchymal damage [24]. Surgical procedures aim to reduce hematoma size, decrease ischemia, eliminate harmful substances, stop additional bleeding, relieve pressure, and ensure proper cerebrospinal fluid flow to prevent further neurologic damage [25]. ROSA’s planning capabilities allow for precise design based on the hematoma’s morphology, enabling the avoidance of eloquent brain areas and blood vessels. This helps minimize the risk of postoperative bleeding [26]. The advanced surgical planning and navigation technology provided by ROSA enhances the safety and efficacy of surgery, particularly in reducing postoperative complications.  

According to our meta-analysis, ROSA significantly outperformed conventional treatments in terms of time to drain removal after surgery. This advantage has been validated in multiple studies. For instance, Han [17] demonstrated that the ROSA group experienced significantly shorter durations for both postoperative drain removal and hospital stay. Furthermore, assessments like BI (Barthel Index), GOS (Glasgow Outcome Scale), KPS (Karnofsky Performance Status), and MRS (Modified Rankin Scale) demonstrated the advantages of ROSA compared to conventional craniotomy for the treatment of cerebral hemorrhagic stroke. In Li [18], the time to extubation after surgery was significantly shorter in the ROSA group $(1.13\pm0.37$ days) compared to the conventional craniotomy group $(3.65\pm0.88$ days) $(P<0.05)$ . Similarly, in $\mathrm{Xu}$ [21], a comparison between 22 patients who underwent traditional infratentorial craniotomy hematoma evacuation and 21 patients who received ROSA hematoma evacuation revealed that ROSA was superior in both operation time and postoperative extubation time. These advantages are likely due to ROSA’s ability to ensure accurate drainage insertion along the long axis of the hematoma through precise three-dimensional morphology observation and planning. This precision reduces delays in postoperative drainage removal and lowers the risk of intracranial infection. In contrast, traditional treatments, especially those using stereotactic frame systems, struggle with accurately observing hematoma morphology, which can lead to imprecise drainage tube insertion and negatively impact postoperative management and rehabilitation outcomes.  

An analysis of the compiled studies revealed that ROSA outperformed traditional treatment in lowering the chances of intracranial infection. In the four articles describing intracranial infection, no infection events were reported in the ROSA group. According to Han’s research, the use of ROSA in surgery resulted in decreased preparation times, fewer local anesthetic procedures, smaller brain exposure areas, and ultimately a lower rate of postoperative intracranial infection $0\%$ compared to $8.9\%$ ). Related studies have highlighted that inflammation around the hematoma site exacerbates brain tissue damage and neurologic deterioration in patients with hypertensive cerebral hemorrhage [27]. For example, Liang [22] demonstrated that patients treated with ROSA had significantly lower serum levels of tumor necrosis factor- $\mathbf{\nabla}\cdot\mathbf{{\alpha}}\propto$ , hs-CRP, and IL-6 after surgery compared to those receiving traditional treatment. The decrease in inflammatory markers can be linked to the features of decreased surgical trauma, shorter exposure time of brain tissue during surgery, lower surgical pressure, and a milder inflammatory reaction in ROSA treatment. These factors combined help lower the chances of postoperative intracranial infection, speed up postoperative recovery, and lower the incidence of complications [28]. Moreover, the ROSA system offers multiple registration and registration modes (including body surface markers, skull implant markers, frame markers, and laser automatic registration without markers), allowing the selection of the most appropriate registration mode based on individual circumstances during the operation [29]. This flexibility further enhances the accuracy and safety of the procedure. The personalized surgical planning enabled by ROSA can effectively avoid unnecessary damage to important functional areas and visible blood vessels, reduce the mass effect of the hematoma, improve local blood flow, and minimize the inflammatory response and cerebral edema following nerve tissue injury [30, 31]. These advantages promote better brain function recovery and are crucial for the long-term prognosis of patients with cerebral hemorrhage.  

An analysis of the compiled literature demonstrated that ROSA was more effective in lowering the mortality risk when compared to standard treatment methods. For example, a clear trend was observed in Tang [19], with significantly lower mortality rates in both the craniotomy and minimally invasive surgery groups than in the conservative treatment group ( $17.6\%$ (9/51) and $79.7\%$ (59/74), respectively). However, a small number of deaths were also reported in Han, including one death due to frailty in the CC group and one death due to lung infection in the RM group, possibly related to underlying cardiac disease. Some studies did not report the cause of death or whether there was a death event, which may be related to different follow-up times and study designs. To comprehensively address these issues, more randomized controlled studies are needed in future. In the Bao [16] study, the surgical group showed significant advantages in survival and functional scores. For example, 60 days after surgery, a higher proportion of patients had a GCS score of 5 in the surgical treatment group $58.6\%$ vs. $69.9\%$ ), whereas a higher proportion of patients had a GCS score of 3 in the conservative treatment group ( $100\%$ vs. $65.2\%$ ). Moreover, Han’s data showed that the postoperative GCS score was notably higher in the ROSA group compared to the conventional treatment group $13.5\pm2.1$ vs. $11.6\pm3.1\$ , indicating that robot-assisted minimally invasive surgery offers substantial benefits in enhancing neurologic recovery and safeguarding brain tissue.  

Factors such as the accuracy of ROSA, choice of treatment time, and optimal timing of surgery may influence study results. Nevertheless, additional comprehensive research is required to validate the enduring clinical impacts of ROSA, given the restricted article count and short patient follow-up duration in this meta-analysis.  

# Limitations  

This is the first meta-analysis to compare ROSA vs. usual care for intracerebral hemorrhage. Despite this, the study has some significant limitations. Initially, the selection of articles was restricted to just eight studies, with only one being an RCT. The lack of more RCTs and blinded designs, as well as data inclusion and exclusion influenced by subjective factors, leads to relatively low article quality and contributes to a moderate to high degree of heterogeneity. Second, all studies came from China, which may lead to geographic bias. China has adopted a more aggressive surgical strategy in the treatment of cerebral hemorrhage and has some time differences in surgical equipment and technical upgrades, which may affect the applicability of the results to other regions. Finally, this meta-analysis failed to perform further subgroup analysis of the highly heterogeneous results. In addition, there was insufficient examination of the patient’s quality of life after surgery and their long-term outlook. To enhance the study’s generalizability and reliability, it is recommended to incorporate additional multicenter trials and data from diverse countries, while also considering the longterm prognosis and quality of life for patients.  

# Conclusions  

A meta-analysis of eight studies has demonstrated that ROSA (Robot of Stereotactic Assistance) is a safe and feasible option for patients with cerebral hemorrhage. ROSA has shown significant advantages over conservative management or conventional craniotomy across several key measures, including operative time, postoperative rebleeding, postoperative extubation time, and intracranial infection rate. These findings suggest that ROSA effectively reduces operation time, decreases the incidence of postoperative complications, and enhances the surgical safety and recovery speed of patients. However, despite these positive outcomes, further studies are warranted to thoroughly validate the broader advantages of ROSA in treating patients with cerebral hemorrhage, particularly focusing on its long-term effects.
