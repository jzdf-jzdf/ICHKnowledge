# Blood Pressure after Successful Endovascular Therapy: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis of Randomized Control Trials  

Khaled Gharaibeh, MD $\circledcirc,1,2$ Nameer Aladamat, MD,1 Adam T. Mierzwa, MD,1,2 Rahul Rao, MD,1,2 Hisham Alhajala, MD,1,2 Sami Al Kasab, MD,3 Mohammad Anadani, MD,4 Richard Burgess, MD,1 Syed Zaidi, MD,1,2 and Mouhammad Jumaa, MD1,2  

Objective: There are limited data evaluating the optimum blood pressure (BP) goal post mechanical thrombectomy (MT) and its effect on outcomes of patients with large vessel occlusions (LVO). The objective of this study was to compare the efficacy and safety of intensive versus conventional BP control after reperfusion with MT via a systematic review and meta-analysis of randomized controlled trials (RCTs).  

Methods: We searched PubMed and Embase to obtain articles related to BP control post MT through September 2023. The primary outcome was functional independence (modified Rankin Scale [mRS] 0-2) at 3 months, while secondary outcomes included excellent outcome (mRS 0-1), symptomatic intracranial hemorrhage (sICH), and mortality.  

Results: Four RCTs with 1,566 patients (762 randomized into intensive BP control vs. 806 randomized into conventional BP control) were included. Analysis showed that there was a lower likelihood of functional independence (mRS 0-2: odds ratio [OR]: 0.68, $95\%$ confidence interval [CI] $0.51\substack{-0.91}$ , $p=0.009)$ in the more intensive treatment group compared with the conventional treatment group. There was no statistically significant difference in achieving excellent outcome (mRS0-1: OR: 0.82, $95\%$ CI: 0.63–1.07; $p=0.15$ ), risk of sICH or mortality.  

Interpretation: This systematic review and meta- analysis Indicates that in patients who achieved successful MT for acute ischemic stroke with LVO, intensive BP control was associated with a lower likelihood of functional independence at 3 months without significant difference in likelihood of achieving excellent outcome, sICH risk, or mortality.  

ANN NEUROL 2024;95:858–865  

echanical thrombectomy (MT) for ischemic stroke due to large vessel occlusion is now the standard of care.1 Successful recanalization, defined as thrombolysis in cerebral infarction (TICI) 2b or better, can be achieved in up to $80\%$ of patients.2 While recanalization is undoubtedly beneficial, there are several factors that can influence functional outcome in patients who undergo MT, yet not all can be modified. One such factor that has garnered significant attention in recent years is peri-procedural and post MT blood pressure (BP) management.  

In the broader context of stroke care, guidelines have long provided recommendations for BP targets following  

intravenous thrombolysis, with a consensus advocating systolic BP levels not exceeding $180\mathrm{mm}\mathrm{Hg}$ and diastolic BP levels at $105~\mathrm{mm}\mathrm{Hg}$ or lower during the initial 24 hours.4 However, when it comes to the distinct population of patients who have undergone successful MT, it is widely accepted practice to lower SBP below ${<}140\mathrm{mmHg}$ or ${<}160\mathrm{mmHg}$ due to concern of reperfusion injury. This clinical equipoise led to multiple randomized controlled trials (RCTs) assessing the benefit of BP lowering post successful reperfusion.  

To the date, 4 RCTs6–9 have been published, providing valuable insights on BP management post successful reperfusion. In this systematic review and metaanalysis, we encompass the findings of these trials to provide a deeper understanding of the optimal BP management approach after successful MT.  

# Methods  

We conducted this systematic review and meta-analysis based on the guidelines of the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-analysis (PRISMA).10 Our analysis relied on the aggregation of data from 4 RCTs. Data are available on request to the corresponding authors.  

# Data Source and Search Strategy  

We searched PubMed and Embase to obtain articles in all languages from inception until September 15, 2023. “Stroke” or “Acute Ischemic Stroke”, “Mechanical Thrombectomy” or “Endovascular therapy”, and “Intensive blood pressure” were the search terms. Synonyms were obtained from PubMed and Embase with elimination of duplicates. Table S1 describes the full search term used in each database searched.  

# Eligibility Criteria  

The inclusion criteria for our systematic review and metaanalysis were as follows: (1) RCTs, (2) that performed a direct comparison between intensive and conventional BP management in patients treated with MT, (3) reporting of the modified rankin Scale (mRS) score of 0–2 at 3 months and symptomatic intracranial hemorrhage (sICH). Conference abstracts, single-arm studies, case reports, case series, and observational studies were excluded. Two investigators (K.G. and N.A.) independently screened and selected the studies for the final review. Discrepancies were resolved by a third investigator (M.J.).  

# Data Extraction  

Extracted data included study design; country and year of the study; baseline characteristics; and primary, secondary, and safety endpoints. Two investigators (K.G. and N.A.)  

independently extracted the data from the included studies. Any disagreement was resolved by consensus.  

# Risk of Bias Assessment and Quality of Evidence  

The methodological quality of the clinical trials was evaluated using the Jadad composite scale, which considers randomization, blinding, and withdrawals.11 This scale is scored from 0 to 5 points. Clinical trials with a cumulative score of ${\ge}3$ were classified as having a low risk of bias, Table S2. Funnel plot analysis was used to evaluate publication bias. The funnel plots for the functional independence and sICH appeared asymmetric by visual inspection (Figs S1 and S2). However, Egger’s regression analysis did not show evidence of publication bias ${\bf\nabla}_{,\phi}=0.12$ , $\ensuremath{p}=0.81$ respectively).  

# Effect Measures  

The primary outcome was functional independence (mRS 0-2). The secondary outcomes were excellent outcome $(\mathrm{mRS~}0{-}1)$ , sICH defined according to study criteria, and mortality at 90 days.  

# sICH Definition  

The definition of sICH varied across the 4 studies included in our analysis. In BP TARGET, sICH was defined as any evidence of hemorrhage associated with a notable increase of ${\ge}4$ points on the NIHSS at $24\mathrm{~h~}$ . Similarly, both OPTIMAL BP and BEST II utilized the same criterion of ${\ge}4$ points on the NIHSS but at $36\mathrm{~h~}$ . ENCHANTED II specified sICH as a hematoma occupying ${\geq}30\%$ of the infarcted tissue with evident mass effect as judged as per Heidelberg criteria.  

# Statistical Analysis  

Statistical analysis was performed using Review Manager 5.3 (Cochrane Collaboration, Copenhagen, The Nordic Cochrane Centre). Data were reported as intention-to-treat analysis. Absolute counts are provided in addition to effect estimates, which are expressed as odd ratios (ORs) with corresponding $95\%$ confidence intervals (CIs). The heterogeneity of the results was evaluated using the $\mathrm{I}^{2}$ statistic as defined by the Cochrane handbook for systematic reviews. It was considered that an $\mathrm{I}^{2}<50\%$ and $\mathrm{\Delta}p_{\mathrm{\Delta>0.1}}$ indicated that the combined results were homogeneous.

# Study Selection  

A total of 67 studies were retrieved by our search strategy. Among these, 8 were eligible for the systematic review. Four studies met our inclusion criteria and were included in the meta-analysis, Figure 1 shows the PRISMA flow chart that illustrates how the final studies were selected.

# Randomization and BP Parameters  

The 4 RCTs had slightly different BP parameters (Table). The Safety and efficacy of intensive BP lowering after successful endovascular therapy in acute ischemic stroke (BP-TARGET) randomized patients with successful recanalization to either intensive BP (SBP $\mathrm{{100-129mmHg}}$ or standard BP (SBP $130{-}185\mathrm{mm}\mathrm{Hg}$ . The Intensive BP control after endovascular therapy for acute ischemic stroke (ENCHANTED2/MT) randomized patients to intensive BP (SBP $<120\mathrm{mmHg},$ ) or standard BP (SBP 140– $180\mathrm{mmHg}$ ). The outcome in patients treated with intraarterial thrombectomy optimal blood pressure control (OPTIMAL-BP) trial randomized patients to intensive BP (S $\mathrm{BP}<140\mathrm{mmHg})$ or standard BP $\mathrm{140–180mmHg)}$ . Finally, the BP management after endovascular therapy for acute ischemic stroke (BEST-II) trial randomized patients into 3 SBP groups $\mathrm{(<140~mmHg,}$ $<160\mathrm{mmHg},$ and ${<}180\mathrm{mmHg)}$ ). In our study, the patient cohort was stratified into 2 distinct groups through a dichotomization process, employing the criteria specified by each individual study. This categorization aimed to differentiate between intensive and conventional BP control strategies. Specifically, in the case of BEST II which included 3 groups, those with $\mathrm{SBP}<140\mathrm{mmHg}$ were assigned to the intensive BP control group, while individuals with SBP ${<}160\mathrm{mmHg}$ and SBP ${<}180\mathrm{mmHg}$ were categorized into the conventional group.

These results indicate that patients subjected to intensive BP control exhibited a lower likelihood of achieving functional independence after 3 months when compared to those undergoing conventional BP control (OR: 0.68, $95\%$ CI 0.51–0.91, $p=0.009,$ ) (Figure 2). # Secondary Outcomes  

Excellent Outcome $(mRSO-1)$ . This result was reported in 4 RCTs. 263 out of 747 patients had an excellent outcome in the intensive BP group, while in the conventional BP control group, 312 out of 782 patients achieved the same outcome. Random-effect model was used. There was no statistically significant difference between the 2 groups (OR: 0.82, $95\%$ CI: $0.63\substack{-1.07}$ ; $\ensuremath{p}=0.15$ ) (Fig 3A). There was no notable heterogeneity detected $(\mathrm{I}^{2}=26\%$ , P for heterogeneity $=0.25$ ).  

# Symptomatic Intracranial Hemorrhage  

Our analysis using random-effect model also showed that in the intensive BP control group, 56 out of 753 patients experienced sICH, while in the conventional BP control group, 51 out of 787 patients encountered this complication. There is no statically significant difference in the risk of sICH between these 2 groups (OR: 1.14, $95\%$ CI $0.77\substack{-1.69}$ , $\begin{array}{r}{\rho=0.51,}\end{array}$ ) (Fig 3B). The heterogeneity was observed to be very low $(\boldsymbol{\mathrm{I}}^{2}=0\%$ , $\mathrm{P}$ for heterogeneity $=0.71$ ).

# Mortality  

Four RCTs were combined and analyzed using the random-effect model. Mortality did not differ between the intensive BP group $(114/750)$ and the conventional BP group $\left(110/784\right)$ , with a pooled effect estimate of 1.16 $(95\%$ CI: 0.87–1.55, $\ensuremath{p}=0.33)$ (Fig 3C), showing an extremely low heterogeneity $(\mathrm{I}^{2}=0\%)$ ).  

# Discussion  

BP management after thrombectomy is a key part of post thrombectomy care. Elevated BP may contribute to reperfusion injury by exacerbating blood–brain barrier disruption and promoting hemorrhagic transformation.12 Conversely, excessively low BP may jeopardize cerebral perfusion and lead to infarct expansion.13 Therefore, achieving an optimal BP range is crucial to balance these competing risks and maximize patient recovery.  

In this systematic review and meta-analysis, our analysis was based on the combined data of 4 RCTs, encompassing a cohort of patients from various regions worldwide presenting with acute ischemic stroke due to LVO. Our study found that intensive BP control post thrombectomy was associated with lower likelihood of functional independence at 3 months, comparable sICH, and comparable mortality rate at 3 months when compared to conventional BP management.  

The divergence in functional independence, which favored a conventional BP target in our meta-analysis is highly derived from the results of OPTIMAL-BP and

ENCHANTED2 and contrasts with findings of BPTARGET and BEST-II which failed to show significant difference in clinical outcomes between conventional and intensive BP targets. In BP-TARGET and OPTIMALBP, approximately one-third to one-half of participants in conventional cohorts fell outside the intended therapeutic range. Furthermore, the average systolic BP difference following randomization was $10\mathrm{mmHg}$ (128 vs 138 and 129 vs 138; respectively) between the intensive and conventional cohorts. Considering these observations, efforts to discern treatment effect size between the 2 groups become more difficult.  

The outcome differences are suspected to be multifactorial. First, the intensive BP cohort in OPTIMAL-BP trial was, interestingly, associated with significantly higher rates of malignant cerebral edema $(7.7\%$ vs $1.3\%$ ; $\begin{array}{r}{\gamma=0.01.}\end{array}$ ), attributed by the authors to infarct extension in the lower BP parameters arm. This contrasts with prior studies, which associated uncontrolled hypertension following successful thrombectomy with malignant cerebral edema. Second, the population in OPTIMAL-BP was South Korean which is associated with higher prevalence of intracranial atherosclerotic disease (ICAD).15,16 This is particularly relevant as patients with ICAD often exhibit lower tolerance for aggressive BP reduction measures.17 Third, while both studies (BP-TARGET and OPTIMALBP) only enrolled patients with successful recanalization scores, the proportion of final TICI 2b and TICI 3 scores were numerically different between each study. Forty-five percent of patients achieved TICI 2b recanalization in BPTARGET compared to only $22\%$ in OPTIMAL-BP. TICI 2B recanalization implies the presence of significant residual ischemic vascular territories that may or may not be adequately perfused by collateral circulation. Intensive BP control will likely worsen infarcts at the expense of penumbral tissue in such territories. A similar effect can occur in the subset of TICI 2C and TICI 3 patients with no re-flow, which accounted for approximately $25\%$ of similar patients in a pooled analysis of ENTEND- IA, EXTEND IA-TNK and EXTEND IA- TNK II patients.18  

Our results also correspond to the findings in the ENCHANTED2 study. This study evaluated a Chinese population and has similarly higher prevalence of ICAD compared to Western populations.19 Underlying ICAD accounted for $53\%$ and $44\%$ of LVO mechanisms in the conventional and intensive BP groups, respectively. Patients with ICAD who undergo MT are more likely to experience re-occlusion due to underlying lesion, and very frequently require aggressive antiplatelet regimens which can increase the risk of ICH. Those patients also frequently suffer from ICAD in other vascular territories, making them more vulnerable to ischemic injuries with sharp BP fluctuations. When considering that ENCHANTED2 had the most aggressive BP target $(120\mathrm{mmHg})$ , this effect could have been exaggerated in this study compared to others.  

The intensive treatment cohort in the BEST-II study targeted systolic BPs below $140\mathrm{mmHg}$ but had better collaterals and smaller baseline infarct volumes compared to the other cohorts within this study. This baseline imbalance may have impacted the functional outcomes of this cohort and potentially confounding the overall results, BEST- II also had an exploratory futility design with small sample size, which limits validity of conclusions derived from that study.  

Our analysis demonstrated similar odds of developing sICH between aggressive and conventional BP cohorts. While all the studies involved in this metaanalysis had failed to reject the null hypothesis, the ENCHANTED2 trial produced the largest weight due to enrolling the most patients and having a narrow confidence interval.  

While the absolute BP level is a crucial predictor of outcomes following MT, it is worth noting that the variability and fluctuations in these values also play a significant role in predicting the clinical outcomes of stroke patients after endovascular thrombectomy. 20–22 The maintenance of consistent BP levels before and after the procedure emerges as a critical factor for optimizing outcomes. Consequently, the precise management of BP, frequently achieved through the administration of antihypertensive medications or vasopressors, becomes indispensable in striking a delicate equilibrium between mitigating the risk of complications and ensuring adequate cerebral blood flow. There is a lack of data on evaluating various strategies for lowering BP after MT. Yet, previous studies suggest that some antihypertensive drugs can impact BP variability in secondary stroke prevention. For example, calcium-channel blockers have demonstrated a capacity to decrease BP variability, whereas beta-blockers might increase it.23,24 The 4 RCTs we included each utilized different antihypertensive agents as part of their respective treatment protocols. Notably, nicardipine emerged as the predominant choice among these agents, and it was consistently utilized in 3 of the included trials: OPTIMALBP, BP-TARGET, and BEST II. In contrast, the fourth trial, ENCHANTED-II, exhibited a distinct preference, with urapidil being the primary antihypertensive agent of choice.  

Our study has notable limitations that should be considered when interpreting its findings. First, the number of trials included in the analysis was limited, which may have constrained the robustness of the results and

limited our ability to detect subtle effects. Second, there was considerable variation in the systolic BP cutoff points employed across the different treatment groups, introducing potential heterogeneity, and making direct comparisons more challenging. The differences in evaluated targets highlight the lack of a standardized approach in the medical community, emphasizing the complexity in establishing universal guidelines. By acknowledging this variation, we aim to provide a more comprehensive and insightful interpretation of our study’s findings. Third, our analysis is primarily shaped by ENCHANTED2/MT and BP-TARGET, given their large patient cohorts. Notably, these trials implemented aggressive systolic BP (SBP) targets $(<120\mathrm{mmHg}$ and ${<}130\mathrm{mmHg},$ respectively) in the intensive arms. In contrast, OPTIMALBP and BEST-II opted for less aggressive SBP targets $(<140\mathrm{mmHg})$ . This divergence in BP control strategies could notably impact our observed results. Fourth, the issue of generalizability arises, as some of the included studies were conducted on Eastern populations, which exhibit distinct disease characteristics, such as ICAD, that may not fully represent the broader patient populations encountered in other regions. These limitations underscore the need for cautious interpretation and highlight the importance of considering the specific context and demographics when applying the study’s findings.  

In conclusion, our meta-analysis underscores the critical role of BP management in optimizing outcomes post-thrombectomy, emphasizing the delicate balance required to mitigate reperfusion injury while maintaining cerebral perfusion. Our findings suggest that conventional BP control after successful MT is associated with better functional outcome compared with intensive BP control.
