- METHODS: AcomprehensivesearchofPubMed, CochraneLibrary, EMBASE, andWebofScienceuptoJuly11, 2022wasperformed.RCTscomparingDTandBTwereincluded.Therelativeriskorratedifferenceandtheir $95\%$ confidenceintervalsinaMantel-Haenszelfixedeffectsmodelwereusedastheeffectindexofeachoutcome.Thenoninferiormarginwasspecifiedas $80\%$ fortherelativeriskor $-10\%$ fortheratedifference.Theprimaryoutcomewastheproportionofpatientswithafavorablefunctionaloutcome, definedasamodifiedRankinscale (mRS) scoreof $_{0-2}$ orareturntobaselineat90days.Additionalefficacyandsafetyoutcomesincludedsuccessfulrecanalizationattheendofthrombectomy, excellentclinicaloutcomes (definedasanmRSscoreof0e1), deathwithin $90\pm14$ days, symptomaticintracerebralhemorrhage, anytypeofintracerebralhemorrhage, andclotmigration.

The incidence of intracranial atherosclerotic stenosis in the Asian population is much higher than that in Western populations.37,38 Patients with AIS due to atherosclerotic LVO had better collateral circulation and a larger volume of ischemic penumbra with stronger survivability,39 which might cover the advantages of early and partial recanalization after IVT in the RCTs from China. The MR CLEAN-NO IV study had a higher percentage of patients with atrial fibrillation or terminal internal carotid artery occlusions and a slightly older age in the EVT-alone group, which was reported to be associated with poor clinical outcomes. In addition, that RCT had enrolled more patients with M2 occlusion than the other included RCTs, in which the site of IVT tended to bring more benefit.40,41 The prehospital triage system is more complicated in China than that in many Western countries, and more patients are in late time window of IVT in China. The time dependence of the benefit from IVT can be an important reason for the difference in the results between China and other countries. The delay of EVT caused by IVT could also cover the advantage of BT because of the longer time from door to groin puncture in the Chinese RCTs. However, a significant benefit for BT was found among Asian patients with similar baseline characteristics in the latest RCT of the DIRECT-SAFE trial. Although our meta-analysis of the 6 RCTs showed noninferiority of DT for favorable functional outcome rates with either an RR margin or an RD margin in a previous report and showed no statistically significant differences in the excellent clinical outcome rate and mortality. Further confirmation of the influence of these factors is necessary to make a precise screening for patients who could potentially benefit.  

In our analysis, the RR was used as the effect index for each outcome, similar to that in a previous study.29 RR is the ratio of the outcome events incidence in the intervention group to that in the control group and is the most useful and intuitive indicator reflecting the correlation between intervention factors and outcome events. The OR is the comparison of the odds of the outcome events in the intervention group with the odds in the control group. The OR is always farther from 1 (more extreme) than the corresponding RR. For rare outcomes, the OR will be equivalent to the RR, and they can be used interchangeably. However, when the outcome is common, such as in the present study, ORs were not suitable as a substitution for RRs. Therefore, our results might provide more reliable evidence using RRs than the metaanalysis using ORs.42  

The present meta-analysis has several important limitations. First, this was only a study-level rather than a patient-level analysis, which provides limited information. Second, the subgroup analysis was not performed according to age, etiology of occlusion, or onset time, which might influence the effects of IVT. In addition, our study only provides treatment decision reference for stroke centers capable of EVT. Still evidence is lacking whether to perform IVT or transfer patients to a comprehensive stroke center immediately when patients first present at a primary stroke center incapable of EVT.  



# CONCLUSIONS  

The results from the present study showed the noninferiority of DT to BT for favorable functional outcomes. The incidence of successful recanalization and any ICH were more common in the BT group. The incidence of other outcomes, including excellent clinical outcomes, distal embolization, sICH, and mortality, were not significantly different statistically between the 2 groups. Patient-level pooled analyses and subgroup analyses are needed to verify the role of IVT and for which patients the benefits from BT would outweigh the risks compared with DT.
