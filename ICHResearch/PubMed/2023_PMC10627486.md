# Effect of Magnesium on Deterioration and Symptomatic Hemorrhagic Transformation in Cerebral Ischemia: An Ancillary Analysis of the FASTMAG Trial  

Shawn Fan a, Michael Jang b,c, May Kim-Tenser b,c, Kristina Shkirkova d, David S Liebeskind e,f, Sidney Starkman e, Juan Pablo Villablanca e, Scott Hamilton g, Andrew Naidech h, Jeffrey L Saver e, Nerses Sanossian b,c,\*  

Author information Article notes Copyright and License information PMCID: PMC10627486  PMID: 36599321  

# Abstract  

# Background  

Magnesium $(\mathrm{Mg})$ is a neuroprotectant in preclinical models. Lower serum $\mathrm{Mg}$ levels have been associated with symptomatic hemorrhagic transformation (HT) in patients with ischemic stroke. Early treatment of acute ischemic stroke with $\mathrm{Mg}$ may reduce rates of symptomatic HT.  

# Methods  

In this post hoc study of the Field Administration of Stroke Therapy Magnesium (FAST-MAG) trial, 1,245 participants with a diagnosis of cerebral ischemia received $20\mathrm{g}$ of $\mathrm{Mg}$ or placebo initiated in the prehospital setting. Posttreatment serum $\mathrm{Mg}$ level was measured for 809 participants. Cases of clinical deterioration, defined as worsening by ${\ge}4$ points on the National Institute of Health Stroke Scale (NIHSS), were imaged and evaluated for etiology. Symptomatic HT was defined as deterioration with imaging showing new hemorrhage.  

Results  

Clinical deterioration occurred in 187 and symptomatic HT in 46 of 1,245 cases of cerebral ischemia. Rates of deterioration and symptomatic HT were not significantly lower in those who received $\mathrm{Mg}$ ( $15.7\%$ vs. $14.4\%$ , $p=0.591$ ; $2.8\%$ vs. $4.6\%$ , $p=0.281\$ ). In cases where serum $\mathrm{Mg}$ level was obtained posttreatment, lower serum $\mathrm{Mg}$ level $(<1.7\mathrm{mg/dL})$ ) was associated with significantly higher rates of deterioration and symptomatic HT $(27.5\%$ vs. $15.5\%$ , $p=0.0261$ ; $11.6\%$ vs. $3.65\%$ , $p=0.00819^{}$ ).  

# Conclusions  

Treatment with $\mathrm{Mg}$ did not significantly reduce rates of clinical deterioration or symptomatic HT. Future analysis should address whether treatment with $\mathrm{Mg}$ could have influenced the subgroup with low serum $\mathrm{Mg}$ at baseline.  

Keywords: Magnesium, Cerebral ischemia, Hemorrhagic transformation  

# Introduction  

Magnesium (Mg) has been shown to be neuroprotective for acute ischemic stroke within $2\textrm{h}$ of symptom onset in preclinical models [1]. Furthermore, lower serum $\mathrm{Mg}$ levels have been associated with higher rates of symptomatic hemorrhagic transformation (HT) [2, 3]. Thus, intravenous $\mathrm{Mg}$ sulfate could be an attractive pharmacologic treatment for the prevention of symptomatic HT because it is well tolerated and inexpensive.  

The NIH-funded Field Administration of Stroke Therapy Magnesium (FAST-MAG) phase 3 clinical trial randomized subjects to treatment with high dose of $\mathrm{Mg}$ or matched placebo with treatment infusion in standard paramedic ambulances in prehospital patients with acute stroke symptoms [4]. We aimed to determine if treatment with intravenous $\mathrm{Mg}$ reduced rates of clinical deterioration and symptomatic HT.  

# Methods  

Details of the multi-center FAST-MAG trial have been previously described [5, 6]. Patients 40–95 years of age were eligible for inclusion if they had a suspected stroke and if treatment could be initiated within $2\mathrm{{h}}$ of symptom onset. The active Mg dose regimen was designed to rapidly double the normal serum $\mathrm{Mg}$ level and maintain that higher concentration for the first $24\mathrm{~h~}$ . Study protocol did not prespecify serum $\mathrm{Mg}$ evaluation; however, a subgroup of participants did have their posttreatment $\mathrm{Mg}$ levels measured.  

Upon hospital arrival, all patients underwent acute brain imaging and neurologic status evaluation by the National Institute of Health Stroke Scale (NIHSS), which was followed up on days 1, 2, 4, and 90. Deterioration is defined as ${\ge}4$ point increase on the NIHSS between the time of arrival and the day 4 follow-up visit. Follow-up imaging was collected on all cases of clinical deterioration. Symptomatic  

HT is defined as deterioration accompanied with imaging showing new hemorrhage and was centrally adjudicated by study neuroradiologist (JPV).  

We compared the rates of deterioration and symptomatic HT between acute ischemic stroke patients who received $\mathrm{Mg}$ versus placebo treatments. In addition, for the subgroup of participants who had posttreatment serum $\mathrm{Mg}$ measurements, we compared the rates of deterioration and symptomatic HT between participants with high/normal and low serum $\mathrm{Mg}$ level defined as ${\leq}1.7\mathrm{mg/dL}$ [7]. Differences in the rates of deterioration and symptomatic HT were primarily assessed using the Cochran-Mantel-Haenszel test with adjustment for age $\mathord{\left[<70\right]}$ years vs. $\ge70$ years), baseline severity of stroke (Los Angeles Motor Scale [LAMS] score ${<}4$ vs. ${\ge}4$ ), and prior history of diabetes and hypertension. For the comparison of treatment group characteristics, the independent $t$ test was used for continuous variables that were normally distributed and the Mann-Whitney U test for those that were not, and the $\chi^{2}$ test was used for categorical variables. The criterion for statistical significance was set at an alpha level of 0.05 throughout the analysis.  

# Results  

A total of 1,245 cases of cerebral ischemia were evaluated by paramedics a median of $24\mathrm{{min}}$ (interquartile range: $14{-}43\operatorname*{min}$ ) and had study drug initiated $44\mathrm{{min}}$ (interquartile range: $34{-}59\operatorname*{min})$ after symptom onset. There were 46 cases of symptomatic HT. Patients with symptomatic HT had a higher incidence of diabetes $(p<0.001)$ and scored higher on prehospital LAMS $(p<0.001)$ and NIHSS $(p<0.001)$ upon arrival.  

Table $\underline{{1}}$ compares the characteristics of the $\mathrm{Mg}$ and placebo treatment groups. There was no significant difference in the overall rate of clinical deterioration between treatment groups $(p=$ 0.591). Symptomatic HT was the leading cause of deterioration in both groups. The rate of symptomatic HT was numerically lower in those who received $\mathrm{Mg}$ treatments, but this was not statistically significant $(p=0.281)$ .

# Discussion  

We found that ultra-early treatment with high-dose $\mathrm{Mg}$ was not associated with significantly reduced rates of clinical deterioration or symptomatic HT. Previous observational studies have documented an association of higher serum $\mathrm{Mg}$ with reduced rates of symptomatic HT in general, postthrombolysis, and improved clinical outcomes [2, 3]. We conducted an analysis of the subgroup of study participants with post-infusion $\mathrm{Mg}$ levels and found that low serum $\mathrm{Mg}$ levels were associated with deterioration and symptomatic HT.  

In the FAST-MAG study population, low serum Mg was associated with a 3-fold increase in rates of symptomatic HT, consistent with previous observational studies [2, 3]. Numerically, there were fewer cases of symptomatic HT in the $\mathrm{Mg}$ group compared to placebo. The most likely explanation for these findings is that only the subgroup of individuals with low serum $\mathrm{Mg}$ at baseline has the potential to receive a protective benefit from $\mathrm{Mg}$ infusion. Rates of symptomatic HT and incidence of low serum $\mathrm{Mg}$ were low in general in the studied population, prohibiting this analysis from describing any significant association. Future analysis should address whether treatment with $\mathrm{Mg}$ could influence participants with low pretreatment serum $\mathrm{Mg}$ levels.  

Limitations of our analysis include the lack of systematic follow-up imaging in those who did not experience clinical deterioration, preventing us from evaluating the effect of $\mathrm{Mg}$ on nonsymptomatic HT. The studied population may not be reflective of the general stroke population.  

In summary, treatment with high-dose Mg did not lead reduced rates of symptomatic HT. We did confirm the association of low serum $\mathrm{Mg}$ with symptomatic HT in our population, consistent with prior studies predominantly in the Chinese population [2, 3].
