# Benefit of dual antithrombotic therapy with direct oral anticoagulants in patients with atrial fibrillation undergoing percutaneous coronary intervention: a systematic review and metaanalysis of randomized clinical trials  

Giacomo Zoppellaro1 $\circledcirc$ · Giuseppe Maria Marchese2 · Alessandro Squizzato3 · Gentian Denas4 · Giuseppe Patti5 · Raffaele De Caterina6 · Vittorio Pengo4  

Received: 14 April 2020 / Accepted: 6 June 2020 / Published online: 2 July 2020   
$\circledcirc$ Società Italiana di Medicina Interna (SIMI) 2020  



# Abstract  

Antithrombotic treatment in patients with atrial fibrillation undergoing percutaneous coronary intervention is still debated. We conducted a meta-analysis of recent randomized controlled trials to evaluate the benefit of different antithrombotic strategies. Data were analyzed between May and September 2019. Efficacy outcomes were trial-defined major adverse cardiovascular events (MACE); its individual components; stent thrombosis. Safety outcomes were trial-defined primary bleeding outcome; TIMI and ISTH major bleeding; clinically relevant non-major bleeding; intracranial hemorrhage. Differences in outcomes among groups were expressed as pooled odds ratio (OR) and corresponding $95\%$ confidence interval (CI). Four randomized studies were included (10,969 patients). The mean age ranged from 69 to 72 years, prevalence of acute coronary syndrome (ACS) varied from 48 to $62\%$ . Comparing dual antithrombotic therapy (DAT) with a direct oral anticoagulant (DOAC) versus triple antithrombotic therapy (TAT) with vitamin K antagonist (VKA), OR for trial-defined MACE and primary bleeding outcome were 1.03 $95\%$ CI, 0.86–1.24) and 0.59 $95\%$ CI, 0.41–0.86), respectively. There was a $68\%$ lower risk of intracranial hemorrhage and a non-statistically significant higher risk of stent thrombosis with DAT. DAT was as effective and safer than TAT in patients with stable coronary artery disease, while a trend towards increased ischemic events was seen in ACS patients. DAT with a DOAC showed similar efficacy and less bleeding than TAT with a VKA. However, increased stent thrombosis with DAT may be present, and TAT should be considered in patients at high ischemic risk, such as ACS patients.  

Keywords  Direct oral anticoagulants $\cdot$ Dual and triple antithrombotic therapy $\cdot$ Atrial fibrillation $^{\ast}$ Percutaneous coronary intervention  



# Introduction  

Atrial fibrillation (AF) is a major global health problem that increases the risk of ischemic stroke and systemic embolism [1]. Approximately $5{-}10\%$ of these patients also undergo percutaneous coronary intervention (PCI) for concomitant coronary artery disease (CAD) [2]. Oral anticoagulation with either a vitamin K antagonist (VKA) or a direct oral anticoagulant (DOAC) is indicated for stroke prevention, while dual antiplatelet therapy (DAPT) with a $\mathrm{P2Y}_{12}$ inhibitor and aspirin is used to prevent recurrent coronary events and stent thrombosis [3, 4].  

Consequently, most patients with AF following PCI are treated with the so-called ‘triple antithrombotic therapy (TAT)’, a combination of DAPT and anticoagulation. While this approach is reasonable and endorsed by European Society of Cardiology, American College of Cardiology and American Heart Association guidelines, the level of evidence for its efficacy is not robust [5, 6]. On the other hand, TAT exposes patients to a higher risk of bleeding, which in turn may induce physicians to stop antithrombotic treatment with consequent further increase in thromboembolic risk.  

A strategy of the so-called ‘dual antithrombotic therapy (DAT)’ has been more recently attempted, demonstrating its ability to reduce bleeding events occurring in the first weeks of therapy, and apparently with no increase in major adverse cardiovascular events [7, 8]. In the last several years, a number of randomized controlled trials (RCTs) have attempted to evaluate strategies of DAT with a DOAC versus TAT with VKA [9–12]. Overall, these studies have suggested reduced bleeding with DAT and “similar” incidence of major adverse cardiovascular events. However, none of these trials was powered to assess efficacy outcomes.  

Recently, attempts have been made to draw definite conclusions on efficacy and safety of these treatment strategies [13, 14]. In this systematic review and metaanalysis of the main RCTs, we focused our attention on patients with different ischemic and bleeding risk profiles and balancing the risks of major hemorrhages (especially intracranial) against the risk of ischemic complications (especially stent thrombosis).  



# Methods  

We followed the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analysis (PRISMA) guidelines for the systematic review and meta-analysis. The review protocol was submitted to PROSPERO on 9th June 2019 (CRD42019138433).  



# Search strategy  

Using the MEDLINE and the EMBASE electronic databases (from January 2015 up to September 2019), we identified all published RCTs on antithrombotic therapy in AF patients undergoing PCI. The search strategy was developed without any language restriction and run on www.embase.com. We used the following Medical Subject Headings (MeSH) and EMTREE terms and text words: ‘anticoagulant agent’, ‘percutaneous coronary intervention’, ‘atrial fibrillation’ and the following limits: ‘Cochrane review’, ‘systematic review’ and ‘randomized controlled trial’. We supplemented our search by manually reviewing the reference list of all retrieved articles.  



# Study Selection  

Two reviewers (GZ and GM) performed study selection independently, with disagreements solved through discussion and by the opinion of a third reviewer (AS), when necessary. Studies were considered potentially eligible for this systematic review if they met the following criteria: (i) studies were phase III RCTs; (ii) included patients were treated with oral anticoagulant therapy because of documented AF and have indication to PCI or medically-managed ACS (iii) trials were comparing different antithrombotic strategies. Data were also extracted from articles on subgroups of the included studies [15–17]. Trials in which anticoagulated patients have an indication different than non-valvular atrial fibrillation were excluded from the main analysis. Observational nonrandomized studies, registry data, ongoing trials without results, editorials and case series were excluded from the analysis.  



# Data extraction and quality assessment  

The same two reviewers independently extracted data on study and population characteristics. All data were collected using a standardized form. The following outcomes at the longest available follow-up were obtained from the main trial publications and supplemental appendices: trialdefined primary bleeding outcome (definitions are reported in Supplementary Table 1), major bleeding and clinically relevant non-major bleeding as defined by ISTH (International Society of Thrombosis and Haemostasis), major bleeding according to TIMI (Thrombolysis in Myocardial Infarction) definition, intracranial hemorrhage (ICH) or type 3c bleeding according to BARC (Bleeding Academic Research Consortium) definition; trial-defined major cardiovascular acute events (MACE, definitions are reported in Supplementary Table 1), stroke, myocardial infarction, definite stent thrombosis (ST), cardiovascular death and allcause mortality. When possible, we performed analyses with the intention-to-treat population for efficacy and bleeding outcomes. All outcomes occurring during active treatment and during follow-up period were collected. We assessed comparative trial-defined primary bleeding outcome and trial-defined MACE in patients stratified for clinical presentation (PCI in the setting of acute coronary syndrome vs.  

PCI in stable coronary artery disease) and for age (elderly vs. younger patients, according to trial definition).  

The same two reviewers assessed the methodological quality of selected studies based on Cochrane criteria (random sequence generation, allocation concealment, blinding of participants and personnel, blinding of outcome assessment, incomplete outcome data, selective reporting) [18]. Each of the criteria was scored as ‘low,’ ‘high,’ or ‘unclear’ (where ‘low’ indicates that the study is less open to bias) and reported in a risk of bias table. Each criteria was scored as ‘low,’ ‘high,’ or ‘unclear’ (where ‘low’ indicates that the study is less open to bias) and reported in a risk of bias table. Due to the small number of included studies, publication bias was not assessed.  



# Statistical analysis  

Analyses were carried out using Review Manager (RevMan [Computer program]. Version 5.3. Copenhagen: The Nordic Cochrane Centre, The Cochrane Collaboration, 2014). Differences in the outcomes among groups were expressed as mean differences or pooled odds ratio (OR) and corresponding $95\%$ confidence interval (CI), which were calculated using a fixed-effects and a random-effects model (DerSimionan and Laird method) [19]. Statistical heterogeneity was evaluated using the $I^{2}$ statistic, which assesses the appropriateness of pooling the individual study results. Random-effects model was used in case of moderate $(I^{2}$ $30\mathrm{-}50\%\cdot$ or severe $(I^{2}>50\%)$ heterogeneity. A funnel plot of the effect size versus the standard error was designed, to highlight possible publication bias. Number needed to treat to benefit (NNTB) or to be harmed (NNTH) were calculated according to Cochrane’s recommendations: [1/ $(1-\mathrm{OR})\times\mathrm{ACR}]+[\mathrm{OR}/(1-\mathrm{OR})\times(1-\mathrm{ACR})]$ for $\mathrm{OR}<1$ ; $[1/(\mathrm{OR~-~}1)\times\mathrm{ACR}]+[\mathrm{OR/(OR~-~}1)\times(1\mathrm{~-~}\mathrm{ACR})]$ for $\mathrm{OR}>1$ ; ACR stands for assumed control risk [20].  

Main analysis was conducted comparing DOAC-based DAT versus VKA-based TAT. To test results for consistency, we run supplementary analyses comparing (i) DAT treatments with any anticoagulant (DOAC or VKA) versus TAT treatment with any anticoagulant (DOAC or VKA) and (ii) DAT with VKA versus TAT with VKA. For these analyses, we included also other two published RCTs, even if not every patient included was anticoagulated because of AF (i.e., AF was present in $69\%$ of patients in WOEST trial and in $84\%$ of patients in ISAR-TRIPLE trial) [7, 8]. We run subgroup analyses according to age (elderly and nonelderly patients) and clinical presentation (ACS and stable CAD). The analysis stratified per clinical presentation was conducted comparing DAT versus TAT strategy. Patients with medically-managed ACS were not included in this subanalysis. We calculated the absolute risk difference as the difference between the risk of an outcome (namely, ISTH major bleeding, ICH, MACE and ST) in the DAT group and the TAT group. Benefit was calculated as the sum of risk difference in bleedings (DAT-TAT) and risk difference in ischemic events (DAT-TAT). Negative values describe a risk reduction with DAT, whereas positive values describe a risk increase with DAT.  



# Results  

A total of 273 studies were screened for eligibility. Four phase 3 RCTs were included in the final analysis, accounting for 10,969 patients (Fig. 1). These trials included: the Prevention of Bleeding in Patients with Atrial Fibrillation Undergoing PCI (PIONEER-AF PCI trial); Dual Antithrombotic Therapy with Dabigatran after PCI in Atrial Fibrillation (RE-DUAL PCI trial); Antithrombotic Therapy after Acute Coronary Syndrome or PCI in Atrial Fibrillation (AUGUSTUS trial); Edoxaban-based versus vitamin K antagonist-based antithrombotic regimen after successful coronary stenting in patients with atrial fibrillation (ENTRUST-AF PCI trial) [9–12]. Characteristics of the individual trials included in the analysis are depicted in the Supplementary Table 2.  

In PIONEER-AF PCI study, three arms were described: rivaroxaban $15/10~\mathrm{mg}$ daily (according to dose reduction criteria) plus a $\mathrm{P2Y}_{12}$ inhibitor; warfarin plus aspirin and a $\mathrm{P2Y}_{12}$ inhibitor; rivaroxaban $2.5~\mathrm{mg}$ bid plus aspirin and a $\mathrm{P2Y}_{12}$ inhibitor. To have a valid comparison among studies, the latter arm was not included in the analysis.  

In RE-DUAL PCI, two doses of dabigatran plus a P2Y12 inhibitor ( $110~\mathrm{mg}$ DAT and $150~\mathrm{mg}$ DAT) were compared with warfarin plus aspirin and a $\mathrm{P2Y}_{12}$ inhibitor. We excluded from the main analysis the $110~\mathrm{mg}$ DAT arm to avoid the merging of benefit and risk of different doses and the mixing of different randomization models. However, we also ran sensitivity analyses with low-dose dabigatran, and combining both high and low doses.  

In AUGUSTUS, four arms were described: apixaban plus aspirin and a $\mathrm{P2Y}_{12}$ inhibitor (‘TAT with DOAC’), apixaban plus a $\mathrm{P2Y}_{12}$ inhibitor (‘DAT with DOAC’), warfarin plus aspirin and a $\mathrm{P2Y}_{12}$ inhibitor (‘TAT with VKA’) and warfarin plus a $\mathrm{P2Y}_{12}$ inhibitor (‘DAT with VKA’). Apixaban dose was $5~\mathrm{mg}$ bid or $2.5\mathrm{mg}$ bid according to dose reduction criteria. To have a valid comparison among studies, we included in the main analysis the arms ‘DAT with DOAC’ and ‘TAT with VKA’. The other two arms were included in the supplementary analyses.  

In ENTRUST-AF PCI, two different antithrombotic strategies were compared: edoxaban $60/30~\mathrm{mg}$ daily (according to dose reduction criteria) plus a $\mathrm{P2Y}_{12}$ inhibitor and warfarin plus aspirin $100\mathrm{mg}$ daily and a $\mathrm{P2Y}_{12}$ inhibitor. All study patients were included in the main analysis.

# Quality assessment  

Supplementary Table 3 summarizes quality items according to Cochrane criteria. All the trials were judged to be at low risk of bias except for the performance bias, because of the open-label design for the anticoagulant treatment.  



# Baseline characteristics  

Table 1 describes the characteristics of patients enrolled in the four RCTs included in this meta-analysis. Mean age of patients was 69 to 72 years. Women accounted for $22-30\%$ of total population. Elderly patients (defined as 80 years of age or more in RE-DUAL PCI and AUGUSTUS, 75 years or more in PIONEER-AF PCI and ENTRUST-AF PCI) represented $23-36\%$ of patients (except in the high-dose dabigatran arm where elderly patients accounted for $1\%$ of population). Patients undergoing PCI for ACS were $48-62\%$ , whereas the remaining $38-52\%$ underwent PCI in stable coronary artery disease (except $24\%$ of ACS patients in AUGUSTUS who were medically managed).  

Duration of dual antiplatelet therapy in the TAT arm was different among studies and variable within each study (Supplementary Table  2). While some RCTs opted for fixed duration of aspirin treatment (1 month for BMS and 3 months for DES in RE-DUAL PCI, 6 months in AUGUSTUS), in the other two RCTs the duration of aspirin or clopidogrel treatment (i.e., from 1 to 12 months) was decided by the treating physician. The duration of follow-up ranged from 6 to 14 months.  



# Safety outcomes  

DAT with DOAC showed a significant and consistent reduction in the risk of bleeding for all the pre-specified outcomes (‘trial-defined’ primary bleeding outcome: OR 0.59, $95\%$ CI 0.41–0.86; ISTH major bleeding OR 0.59, $95\%$ CI 0.40–0.88; TIMI major bleeding OR 0.60, $95\%$ CI 0.41–0.88; CRNM bleeding: OR 0.63, $95\%$ CI 0.43–0.92) as compared with TAT with VKA (Fig. 2). Calculated absolute risk reduction (ARR) for primary bleeding outcome was $7.5\%$ with a NNTB of 13.  

DAT with DOAC was also associated with a significant reduction in intracranial bleeding (OR 0.32, $95\%$ CI 0.14–0.71) in comparison with TAT with VKA (NNTB: 197). These outcomes were similar when low-dose or the combined doses of dabigatran were analyzed (Supplementary Table 4).

# Efficacy outcomes  

As shown in Fig. 3, DAT has not an increased risk of total ischemic events when compared to TAT (‘trial-defined MACE’ OR 1.03, $95\%$ CI 0.86–1.24) but showed a nonstatistical increase in ST (OR 1.42, $95\%$ CI 0.88–2.29) with a NNTH of 280. Myocardial infarction, stroke and overall mortality did not significantly differ between the two groups (OR 1.13, $95\%$ CI 0.86–1.48; OR 0.82, $95\%$ CI 0.51–1.31 and OR 1.06, $95\%$ CI 0.83–1.36, respectively).  

Sensitivity analyses including low-dose or the combined doses of dabigatran obtained similar results (Supplementary Table 4) except that, in the low-dose dabigatran analyses, there was a statistically significant increase in ST with DAT compared to TAT (OR 1.65, $95\%$ CI 1.04, 2.60). The comparison of DAT treatment with any anticoagulant versus TAT treatment with any anticoagulant was also similar (Supplementary Table 5).  



# Subgroup analyses  

Primary efficacy and safety outcomes stratified for the index event leading to PCI (Fig. 4) revealed that, in patients with stable CAD, DAT is as effective and safer than TAT (OR 0.65, $95\%$ CI ${}_{0.49-0.86}$ for safety outcome; OR 0.86, $95\%$ $\mathrm{CI}0.62\substack{-1.19}$ for efficacy outcome). Similarly, in patients with an ACS, there was a significant reduction in bleeding (OR 0.62, $95\%$ CI 0.50–0.77). However, there was a non-significant trend towards an increased risk of ischemic events with DAT (OR 1.21, $95\%$ CI 0.92–1.59 for efficacy outcome).  

The analysis on non-elderly patients suggested a good safety and efficacy profile with DAT in comparison to TAT (OR 0.88, $95\%$ CI $0.68{-}1.16$ for efficacy outcome; OR 0.6 $95\%$ CI 0.58–0.81 for safety outcome). In elderly patients, ischemic events with DAT were non-significantly increased (OR 1.91, $95\%$ CI 0.86–4.23 for efficacy outcome) but the analysis included only two studies of which one with only 16 patients. Bleeding events were non-significantly decreased (OR 0.78, $95\%$ CI 0.58–1.04) (Supplementary Fig. 1).  

Data on overall mortality stratified for age or index event were not available.  



# Risk difference  

Overall, DAT reduces by $2.53\%$ the risk of a major bleeding according to ISTH criteria and increases by $0.19\%$ the risk of MACE, with a total benefit of $2.34\%$ . Focusing on hard events, DAT reduces the risk of ICH by $0.51\%$ and increases the risk of ST by $0.36\%$ , with a total benefit of $0.15\%$ . Included trials showed variable absolute risk differences for these outcomes, as displayed in Fig. 5.  



# Discussion  

The present systematic review on more than 10.000 AF patients undergoing PCI or with an ACS compared different antithrombotic strategies evaluating their safety and efficacy profiles. Results confirm the hypothesis that a strategy of DAT with DOAC reduces the risk of bleeding (especially intracranial hemorrhage) without an increase in the total risk of ischemic complications and overall mortality.

On the other hand, reduction in ICH is probably due to the use of DOAC over VKA as suggested by the absence of ICH reduction when comparing DAT with VKA and TAT with VKA. ICH, although rare, is an appalling complication of anticoagulant therapy, undoubtedly increased by TAT, and it frequently bears a poor prognosis with very high fatality rate [22]. Therefore, the observed reduction in intracranial bleeding with DAT has a definite impact on prognosis and quality of life, even if the absolute risk reduction is quite small $(0.51\%)$ .  

While included RCTs were designed to assess the safety of DAT, none of them was powered to detect differences in ischemic outcomes. Indeed, this uncertainty is probably the main reason of the discrepancy in guideline recommendations. ESC documents are more concerned by the risk of ST with DAT [5], while North American documents by the higher bleeding risk with TAT, especially in the first month of therapy [6]. By pooling together trial results, we enhanced the power to detect statistical differences for ischemic complications, and we found that DAT is comparable to TAT in reducing the trial-defined MACE, while a trend towards an increase in ST was observed. The absence of significant MACE increase in the DAT group may be partly explained by the relevant reduction in bleeding events. Bleedings may reduce the compliance to therapy that in turn exposes patients to thrombotic complications [21]. Moreover, physicians may be induced to decrease or temporary withdraw the antithrombotic treatment. Same reasoning may apply to the reduction rate of CRNMB.

DAT increases ST consistently in different sensitivity analysis. Although rare (with an incidence of roughly $1\%$ ), this event is potentially catastrophic. Several factors not considered in the included studies may be involved, such as stent type and dimension, implantation technique and the way it is deployed in the vascular wall. $\mathrm{P2Y}_{12}$ inhibition is also important and clopidogrel was by far the most used in these trials while the use of newer $\mathrm{P2Y}_{12}$ inhibitors was limited to a small number of patients. Timing of initiation of DAPT is a major determinant in ST, as this event is more frequent in the first weeks after PCI. Notably, included RCTs randomized patients to DAT or TAT from 3 to 14 days after PCI, a time window in which patients could stay on DAPT (Supplementary Table 2). Type of DOAC may also play a role: as shown in Fig. 5, DOACs behave differently regarding the reduction in the considered outcomes compared to their control group. While DAT with apixaban seems to maximize the reduction of bleeding risk, DAT with high-dose dabigatran appears to minimize the risk of ST.  

Overall, DAT reduces the risk of ICH and increases the risk of ST, but these events are very rare. We therefore estimated the benefit of DAT by looking at the absolute risk difference as compared with TAT. DAT reduces the risk of an ischemic or hemorrhagic event by $2.33\%$ and reduces the risk of ICH or ST by $0.15\%$ . DAT is thus associated with a reduction of overall events and, in a small proportion, of harder events.  

In some patients the balance between ischemic and bleeding risk may be shifted towards the first (i.e., patients presenting with an ACS) or towards the second (i.e., elderly patients). To determine the best medical strategy in these subgroups of patients, we performed several subgroups analyses. In the elective PCI, DAT regimen resulted to be better than TAT as to the bleeding risk, and as effective as TAT. Conversely, in patients undergoing PCI because of ACS, DAT confirmed its safety, but with a trend towards increased ischemic events. Further research is here needed but, relying on presently published data, we feel that ACS patients should receive a variable short-period of triple therapy, for a minimum of 1 month.  

In this study-level meta-analysis, there was little or no heterogeneity in most endpoints, except for trial-defined bleeding, ISTH major bleeding and CRNM bleeding; moreover, follow-up durations were similar, and definitions of the various outcomes were consistent across all studies. However, our analysis has some limitations. In particular, we had no access to individual patients’ data; therefore, it was not possible to perform further investigations on patient subgroups, such as: type of antiplatelet drugs used, coronary stent type, coronary anatomy complexity, stent length, left main stenting, thromboembolic and bleeding risk profiles, and various durations of dual antiplatelet therapy in TAT patients. Other metaanalyses were recently performed on the same subject [13, 14, 23] but, unlike them, we restricted our analysis comparing DAT with a full-dose DOAC versus TAT with a VKA. Finally, we run subgroups analyses according to the type of index event and the age at presentation, but results may be biased by the lack of randomization for these characteristics (even if well balanced across groups) and some missing data.  



# Conclusions  

In summary, the present systematic review confirms that DAT reduces the bleeding risk in AF patients following PCI without overall increasing the ischemic risk and should be regarded as the default strategy especially in non-elderly patients undergoing PCI for stable CAD. TAT may be considered in patients presenting with an ACS or at very high risk of ST and for the limited time (currently about 1 month) when this event has a reasonable probability to occur. When DAT is chosen, physician should use newer generation drugeluting stents, consider a more potent $\mathrm{P2Y}_{12}$ inhibitor and select patient undergoing an uneventful PCI procedure with optimal stent apposition.
