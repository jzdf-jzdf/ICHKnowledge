# Intravenous Alteplase Versus Best Medical Therapy for Patients With Minor Stroke: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis  

Yang Zhang, MPH\*; Tian Lv , MD\*; Thanh N. Nguyen , MD, PhD; Simiao Wu , MD, PhD; Zhi Li, MD, PhD; Xue Bai , MPH; Dan Chen, MPH; Chuansheng Zhao , MD, PhD; Wanyi Lin, MD; Shiqin Chen , MD; Yi Sui , MD, PhD  

BACKGROUND: The efficacy of thrombolysis (IVT) in minor stroke (National Institutes of Health Stroke Scale score, 0–5) remains inconclusive. The aim of this study is to compare the effectiveness and safety of IVT with best medical therapy (BMT) by means of a systematic review and meta-analysis of randomized controlled trials and observational studies.  

METHODS: We searched the PubMed, Embase, Cochrane Library, and Web of Science databases to obtain articles related to IVT in minor stroke from inception until August 10, 2023. The primary outcome was an excellent functional outcome, defined as a modified Rankin Scale score of 0 or 1 at 90 days. The associations were calculated for the overall and preformulated subgroups by using the odds ratios (ORs). This study was registered with PROSPERO (CRD42023445856).  

RESULTS: A total of 20 high-quality studies, comprised of 13 397 patients with acute minor ischemic stroke, were included. There were no significant differences observed in the modified Rankin Scale scores of 0 to 1 (OR, 1.10 $[95\%$ CI, 0.89–1.37]) and 0 to 2 (OR, 1.16 $[95\%$ CI, 0.95–1.43]), mortality rates (OR, 0.67 $[95\%$ CI, 0.39–1.15]), recurrent stroke (OR, 0.89 $[95\%$ CI, 0.57–1.38]), and recurrent ischemic stroke (OR, 1.09 $[95\%$ CI, 0.68–1.73]) between the IVT and BMT group. There were differences between the IVT group and the BMT group in terms of early neurological deterioration (OR, 1.81 $[95\%$ CI, 1.17–2.80]), symptomatic intracranial hemorrhage (OR, 7.48 $[95\%$ CI, 3.55–15.76]), and hemorrhagic transformation (OR, 4.73 $[95\%$ CI, 2.40–9.34]). Comparison of modified Rankin Scale score of 0 to 1 remained unchanged in subgroup patients with nondisabling deficits or compared with those using antiplatelets.  

CONCLUSIONS: These findings indicate that IVT does not yield significant improvement in the functional prognosis of patients with acute minor ischemic stroke. Additionally, it is associated with an increased risk of symptomatic intracranial hemorrhage when compared with the BMT. Moreover, IVT may not have superiority over BMT in patients with nondisabling deficits or those using antiplatelets.

# METHODS  

# Approval and Registration of Standard Protocols  

For this systematic review, we followed the Meta-analysis of Observational Studies in Epidemiology11 proposal and adhered to the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analysis12 guidelines. This study was registered in the  

PROSPERO database ([International Prospective Register of Systematic Reviews]; https://www.crd.york.ac.uk/prospero/ display_record.php?ID $\vDash$ CRD42023445856). Data are available on request to the corresponding authors.  

# Data Source and Search Strategy  

We conducted a systematic search using keyword combinations in the PubMed, Embase, Cochrane Library, and Web of Science databases from inception up to August 10, 2023. The search strategy employed a combination of key terms including stroke, thrombolytic therapy, thromboly\*, tissue plasminogen activator, tenecteplase, and urokinase-type plasminogen activator (I Search strategies in Supplemental Materials). Synonyms were retrieved from PubMed, Embase, Cochrane, and Web of Science databases, and redundant entries were removed.  

# Eligibility Criteria  

The inclusion criteria for this study were as follows: (1) population: patients with minor ischemic stroke (NIHSS score, 0–5), aged 18 years and over, could receive thrombolysis within 4.5 hours after onset of stroke; (2) intervention: IVT with alteplase; (3) comparison: BMT (the BMT included most commonly used medications such as dual antiplatelets, single antiplatelet agent, systemic anticoagulants treatment of statins, blood pressure lowering therapy, blood glucose control therapy, and other risk factor modifications as described in the national and institutional guidelines for the management of acute ischemic stroke except for the thrombolytic therapy); (4) outcome: functional outcomes and any safety outcomes; (5) study design: randomized controlled trials (RCTs) and observational studies.  

Exclusion criteria were (1) studies that did not include a control group; (2) patients who received endovascular thrombectomy or bridging therapy; (3) literature classified as case report, guideline, review, or conference report; (4) definitions of minor stroke and outcome metrics that did not meet the specified inclusion criteria; and (5) studies with flawed designs or of low quality (RCTs: algorithm result is high; observational studies: score of ${<}6$ ).  

# Study Selection and Data Collection  

Both level 1 (title and abstract) and level 2 (full text) screening of articles were conducted and cross-validated against predetermined eligibility criteria that were independently formulated by 2 reviewers (Y.Z., T.L.). Disagreements were resolved through discussion with the senior author (Y.S.) when necessary. Data on the baseline characteristics, primary end points, secondary end points, and safety end points were extracted from each study and subsequently analyzed by 2 independent investigators (Y.Z., T.L.). Among the studies presenting duplicate data, the ones with the largest data set were retained, while the others were excluded. Data extraction was performed on structured reports, including author names, publication year, study design, sample size, types of drugs used for IVT, patient characteristics, and outcome events (Table S1). Patients with missing data with regard to the variables and outcomes of interest were excluded from our analysis.  

# Assessment of Bias and Quality of Evidence  

The Cochrane risk assessment tool (RoB2)13 was employed to evaluate the quality and bias of RCTs. The Newcastle-Ottawa  

Scale14 was employed to assess the cohort and case-control studies. For observational studies, a minimum assessment score of ${\ge}6$ was considered indicative of good quality and included in the meta-analysis. Two researchers, Y.Z. and T.L., conducted an assessment of the overall quality of the studies. Any potential conflicts were resolved under the guidance and assistance of the senior author, Y.S.  

# Measures of Effect  

The primary outcome was excellent functional outcome at 90 days, defined as a modified Rankin Scale (mRS) score of 0 to 1. Secondary outcomes included favorable functional outcome at 90 days (mRS score of 0–2), mortality at 90 days, early neurological deterioration (END), recurrent stroke, and recurrent ischemic stroke. Safety outcomes included symptomatic intracranial hemorrhage (sICH) and hemorrhagic transformation. The mRS is an ordinal scale incorporating 6 levels of function from 0 to 5 with death assigned to 6. END was defined as an increase of NIHSS score ${\ge}4$ within 24 hours after onset,15 in the first 24 to 48 hours after admission16 or an increase of 2 or more points within 24 hours but not as a result of ICH.17 Symptomatic ICH was defined as any type of intracranial bleeding accounting for clinical deterioration or an increase of NIHSS score ${\ge}4$ within 22 to 36 hours, according to the ECASS (European Cooperative Acute Stroke Study) II trial,18 clinical deterioration defined by an increase of ${\ge}4$ points in NIHSS score within 22 to 36 hours or any ICH leading to death as per ECASS III criteria,19 or any computed tomography or magnetic resonance imaging documented bleeding with clinical deterioration of $\geq1$ point on the NIHSS or leading to death ${<}7$ days according to National Institute of Neurological Disorders and Stroke criteria.20 Hemorrhagic transformation was defined as extravascular blood identified on brain computed tomography or magnetic resonance imaging scan within 24 hours after treatment,18 or categorized into hemorrhagic infarction (HI 1, HI 2), parenchymal hemorrhage (PH 1, PH 2), and remote parenchymal hemorrhage.19  

# Statistical Analysis  

Statistical analysis was conducted using Stata software (version 17.0). We computed the odds ratios (ORs) along with their corresponding $95\%$ CIs to explore the association for all the outcomes. Adjusted ORs were preferably selected over unadjusted ones for the analysis. To determine and evaluate sources of heterogeneity, subgroup analyses performed for the primary outcome by age $(>80$ years old excluded or included), nondisabling (yes or not mentioned), compared with antiplatelet agents (yes, dual antiplatelet therapy or not mentioned), large vessel occlusion (excluded, all/partly included or not mentioned), thrombolytic time window $(0{-}3\$ or 0–4.5 hours), baseline NIHSS score (0–3 or 0–5), study design (RCT, prospective study or retrospective study), and premorbid mRS scores (0, $_{0-2}$ or not mentioned). Grouping factors were determined based on known and reported clinical factors and confounders potentially influencing heterogeneity and the stability of results. The heterogeneity of the results in each study was analyzed using the Q statistics and $I^{2}$ test. $I^{2}$ values of at least $50\%$ or $\mathcal{R}{<}0.10$ for the Q test were considered to represent substantial heterogeneity. If there was substantial heterogeneity present, a random effects model was employed. A fixed effects model was selected otherwise.21 Publication bias was evaluated through the use of funnel plots and the Egger linear regression test. If the funnel plot exhibited a standard symmetrical distribution, it indicated the absence of publication bias. If the funnel plot had a nonstandard or asymmetrical distribution, it suggested the presence of publication bias. Quantitative analysis was conducted to assess the asymmetry of the funnel plot using Egger regression test. Sensitivity analyses were performed by sequentially removing one study each.  

# RESULTS  

# Results of the Conducted Search  

A total of 5393 publications were acquired using the search strategy that was devised. Out of these, 2103 studies were excluded due to duplication. Following an initial screening of article titles and abstracts, a total of 3256 articles that did not meet the inclusion criteria were excluded. There were 14 articles further excluded after conducting a thorough examination of their full text (Table S2). Ultimately, we included 3 RCTs and 17 observational studies (Figure 1).  

# Study Characteristics and Quality Evaluation  

The essential features of the studies included in this meta-analysis are presented in Table S1. Totally 20 studies with 13 397 patients (age range, sex proportion, baseline NIHSS range, and time from onset to treatment) were included, with 4972 patients receiving IVT and 8425 patients receiving BMT. The reports originated from various countries including China, the United States, Australia, and others, and were published between 2011 and 2023. The 3 RCTs had a low risk of bias. The 17 observational studies were evaluated using the Newcastle-Ottawa Scale, which assigned a score ranging from 7 to 9. All studies included in this metaanalysis were deemed to be of high quality and met the criteria for inclusion (Tables S3 and S4, respectively).

# Risk of Bias  

Publication bias was subsequently evaluated through the utilization of funnel plots (Figures S2 through S9). Funnel plots depicting the outcomes of excellent functional outcome, favorable functional outcome, mortality, END, recurrent ischemic stroke, sICH, and hemorrhagic transformation exhibited a general trend of approximate symmetry. Egger measurement did not show any indication of publication bias (Egger test: $P{>}0.05$ ). The funnel plot depicting recurrent stroke exhibited asymmetry, and the application of Egger measurement (Egger test: $P_{=0.045}$ ; Table S6) indicated the presence of publication bias for this outcome. Since different definitions of sICH were used by the included RCTs and observational studies, a subgroup analysis was conducted, stratifying for the sICH definition used, confirming similar results between the 3 subgroups (ECASS II: OR, 7.76 $[95\%$ CI, 2.28–26.35], $P\mathrm{=}0\%,$ $\scriptstyleP=0.893$ ; ECASS III: OR, 4.22 $[95\%$ CI, 1.19–14.92], $P{=}0\%,$ $_{P=0.602}$ ; National Institute of Neurological Disorders and Stroke: OR, 10.90 $[95\%$ CI, 2.62–45.28], $P{=}0\%,$ , $P{=}0.736$ ; Figure S10).  

# DISCUSSION  

Evidence supporting the application of IVT in acute minor stroke remains inconclusive. In this meta-analysis, we showed no difference between IVT and BMT in treating patients with minor ischemic stroke (NIHSS score ≤5) in terms of mRS score of 0 to 1 as well as mRS score of 0 to 2 at 90 days. Subgroup analyses remained consistent with the main findings. IVT was associated with an increased risk of safety outcomes such as END, sICH, and hemorrhagic transformation, while BMT was similar to IVT in preventing recurrent stroke and recurrent ischemic stroke.  

Subgroup analysis of the primary outcome (mRS score, $0{-}1$ ) showed that there was considerable overlap for the $95\%$ CIs of the measured association in each group, indicating that there was no significant interaction between the combined association (mRS score, 0–1) between IVT or BMT and each subgroup.40 The result was consistent across subgroups and sensitivity analyses. The sensitivity analysis also showed there were no significant differences between the 2 groups after the sequential removal of each study, indicating that the conclusion was stable and reliable (Figure S11).  

In the present study, IVT was found to be associated with a higher incidence of END when compared with BMT. Important predictors of END such as the presence of large vessel occlusion, a more proximal occlusion site, or an increased thrombus length have been identified in previous studies as potential factors.41,42 Nevertheless, the limited number of patients in our analysis makes us unable to further analyze the cause of END based on distinct causes. In line with an increased frequency of hemorrhagic transformation and sICH, we surmised the increased END seen in the IVT group was partially due to the occurrence of intracranial bleeding. Additionally, considering the shorter half life of intravenous alteplase (compared with oral antiplatelet), vessel re-occlusion after the clearance of plasma alteplase may be another factor to explain END in the absence of antithrombotic coverage in the early 24 hours after the index stroke. It is also possible that the use of dual antiplatelet therapy in the BMT group might reduce the frequency of END, as reported in some studies.17,43  

An increasing number of studies are exploring the safety and efficacy of antiplatelet therapy with or without a comparison to IV alteplase in patients with minor stroke. The 2013 CHANCE study (Clopidogrel in HighRisk Patients With Acute Nondisabling Cerebrovascular Events)44 demonstrated that treatment with aspirin given in combination with clopidogrel during the acute phase may reduce the risk of recurrence within 90 days in patients with TIA or minor stroke, without increasing the risk of bleeding in patients. The results of the 2018 POINT study (Platelet-Oriented Inhibition in New TIA and Minor Ischemic Stroke)45 confirmed CHANCE findings, with clopidogrel in combination with aspirin significantly reducing the major ischemic event risk, providing additional clinical benefit for patients with acute nondisabling stroke. The PRISMS study (Potential of r-tPA for Ischemic Strokes With Mild Symptoms)22 evaluated the efficacy and safety of alteplase versus oral aspirin alone in patients with minor stroke (NIHSS score ${\le}5\$ ) and no disabling deficit. Preliminary results showed that alteplase did not increase the likelihood of a good outcome at 90 days compared with aspirin, and the incidence of sICH was higher in the alteplase-treated group than in the aspirin control group. Unfortunately, the trial was prematurely terminated for administrative reasons, and ${<}50\%$ of patients completed follow-up, limiting the study’s findings. The recent ARAMIS study17 explored the efficacy and safety of aspirin in combination with clopidogrel versus alteplase for the treatment of acute minor nondisabling stroke. It demonstrated that dual antiplatelet therapy was noninferior to alteplase in the nondisabling minor ischemic stroke population in terms of 90-day functional outcomes and with fewer sICH events. In our study, we showed that antiplatelet therapy did not differ from IVT in favorable functional outcome across observational studies, which is in accordance with the PRISMS and ARAMIS trials.  

New generation thrombolytics are promising in improving functional outcomes for acute ischemic stroke. However, after searching the relevant databases according to our predetermined search strategy, we did not uncover any reports of RCTs and observational studies investigating the use of thrombolytic drugs aside from alteplase, in the management of minor stroke. This implies that there remains a significant knowledge gap regarding the use of intravenous thrombolytics such as tenecteplase in patients with minor stroke. TEMPO-2 ([TNK-tPA Versus Standard of Care for Minor Ischemic Stroke With Proven Occlusion] NCT02398656) aims to investigate the efficacy of tenecteplase in comparison to antiplatelet therapy in patients with mild stroke and confirmed vascular occlusion. The TRUST trial (Thrombolysis of Urokinase for Minor Stroke)46 will assess the effectiveness and safety of intravenous urokinase in comparison to antiplatelet agents for the management of minor ischemic stroke. This trial has the potential to offer a promising and cost-effective thrombolytic therapy for minor stroke in developing regions across the globe.  

The strength of the study lies in its most updated literature, the excellent homogeneity of the original data, consistent findings across various subgroup analyses and its generalizability based on a pragmatic BMT definition.  

Our study is subject to several limitations. First, synthesized data contains inherent bias associated with the included retrospective and observational studies. Owing to the observational nature of some included studies, the decision of patients to accept IVT or BMT was not randomized. Patients who had minor disabling ischemic stroke may have had a higher likelihood of being treated with IVT. Additionally, propensity-matched analyses were only conducted in a subset of the studies, resulting in notable allocation bias and the presence of unmeasured confounding variables that were not accounted for. Second, there were inconsistencies among the included studies in terms of the time of treatment initiation, baseline NIHSS score, definitions of minor stroke and dosing paradigms of antiplatelets et al. These inconsistencies could potentially influence the outcome assessment of the synthesized analysis. Therefore, additional large-scale prospective randomized studies with uniform definitions may be warranted. Thirdly, the lack of vessel imaging makes us unable to further specifying the subpopulations that may potentially benefit from BMT or IVT, which may provide insights to endovascular thrombectomy research in low-NIHSS patients. We also excluded studies containing patients undergoing endovascular thrombectomy in addition to IVT, which may bias the comparison between BMT and IVT due to its potential treatment effect size. Therefore, our findings may not apply to patients with proven large vessel occlusion, particularly for IVT with new generation thrombolytics. Lastly, we were unable to draw conclusions regarding minor ischemic stroke with disabling symptoms due to a lack of classification based on the original literature.  

# CONCLUSIONS  

This study presents a systematic review and metaanalysis that examines the efficacy of IVT therapy in improving functional outcomes in patients with acute minor ischemic stroke, in comparison to BMT available. To our knowledge, this study is the most comprehensive meta-analysis to date, suggesting IVT is not associated with an excellent functional outcome at 90 days for acute minor stroke, but is associated with an increased risk of intracranial hemorrhage instead.
