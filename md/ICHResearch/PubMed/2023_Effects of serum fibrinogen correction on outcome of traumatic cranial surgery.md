# ABSTRACT  

Keywords:   
Traumatic brain injury (TBI)

Background: Traumatic brain injury (TBI) is strongly associated with coagulopathy that occurs in $25{-}35\%$ of patients. This complication is linked to higher mortality and morbidity. Recent lines of evidance have supported administration of fibrinogen concentrate (FC) in patients with severe TBI, while its efficacy remains contro­ versial. In this study we aim to evaluate the effectiveness of serum fibrinogen level correction from 1.5 and $2.0g/$ l to more than $2.0~g/1$ in patients with severe TBI undergoing traumatic cranial surgery.  

Method: This randomized, single-blind, placebo-controlled clinical trial included trauma patients who had abbreviated injury scale (AIS) more than 3 in head and below 3 in other organs. FC was administered intrave­ nously to patients with severe TBI undergoing TBI to correct the fibrinogen level above $2~g/1$ . Patients were randomly assigned to FC and control groups. The amount of intra-operative blood loss, packed cell (PC) trans­ fusion, formation of new intracranial hemorrhage, and hemovac drainage were compared between the two study groups.  

Results: Forty-seven of 65 participants received the study intervention within $40{-}112\mathrm{min}$ of admission. Intraoperative PC transfusion was higher in FC group $(80\%)$ compared to control group $(55.5\%)$ while the differ­ ance was not statistically significant $(\mathtt{p}>0.05)$ . Intra-operative blood loss was significantly higher in control group than FC group $(\mathbf{P}=0.036)$ . Chance of re-operation and new intracranial hematoma were not significantly different between two study groups.  

Conclusion: Early delivery of FC, decreases intraoperative bleeding. Although based on our findings it has no other effect on other parameters, further multicenter studies are recommended to investigate the role of early FC administration in management of post traumatic coagulopathy.  

# 1. Introduction  

Traumatic brain injury (TBI) is one of the most important causes of mortality and morbidity all around the world that impose economic and social consequences. Based on the World Health Organization (WHO) calcification, road traffic accidents, suicides and homicides are major root causes of injury and death. In the USA, 2.2 million annual visits in the emergency department, 50,000 deaths, 280,000 hospitalizations, and about 3.2–5.3 million people live with TBI associated complications [1,2]. Intracranial hemorrhage is an important and unfavorable subsequence of TBI that needs a medical or surgical intervention that might be induced or deteriorated due to coagulopathy. Coagulopathy in TBI is a frequent complication $(32-35\%)$ that is shown as a poor prog­ nostic factor [3]. In patients with traumatic intracerebral hemorrhage (ICH) and brain parenchymal damage, the progression of hemorrhagic injury-induced after trauma is a catastrophic upcoming consequence that needs early diagnosis and treatment of coagulopathy. Death and multiple organ failure are significantly increased in coagulopathy state [3,4].  

Several mechanisms are theorized for post-traumatic coagulopathy such as dilution of coagulation factors iatrogenically, lack of coagulation factors due to consumption in the acute phase, hypothermia and acidosis. Although in new approaches to patients with traumatic injury, tailored transfusion of the packed cell, plasma, platelet (Plt) and infusion of crystalloids, many causes of post-traumatic coagulopathy are addressed. Several studies have attempted to find other causes of coa­ gulopathy because this kind of coagulopathy occurs before significant loss of coagulation factors and is associated with the severity of trau­ matic injury and level of shock [5–7].  

Fibrinogen is a newly described principal factor for clot formation and clot strength that is synthesized in hepatocytes [8]. The depletion of fibrinogen levels occurs earlier and more frequently rather than other common coagulation factors. Insufficient amounts of fibrinogen in a bleeding situation is related to inappropriate fibrin clots firmness and Plt aggregation (acts as the ligand for glycoprotein IIb/IIIa receptor) [5,9]. Major causes of fibrinogen depletion in the massive hemorrhagic state include dilution, factor consumption, fibrinolysis and fibrinogenolysis [10]. It seems the most important factor to start and deteriorate acute traumatic coagulopathy is fibrinolysis that is probably due to an increased level of tissue plasminogen activator (tPA) after injury and blocking plasminogen activator inhibitor-1 (PAI-1) by activated protein C. Prevalence of decreased plasma fibrinogen level on arrival is about $25\%$ in severe trauma patients. Recent studies recommended that plasma fibrinogen concentration should preserve more than $1.5{-}2g/1$ in severe trauma and it’s better to keep more than $2.0~g/1$ to prevent massive bleeding [2,11–13]. Recent studies have been shown hypofi­ brinogenaemia is an important part of traumatic coagulopathy, thus we decide to evaluate effectiveness of correction of serum fibrinogen level between 1.5 and $2.0\:g/1$ to more than $2.0\:g/1$ in traumatic brain injury.  

# 2. Materials and methods  

# 2.1. Study population  

We conducted a single-blinded, randomized trial study to evaluate effectiveness of correction of serum fibrinogen level $(1.5{-}2\S/1)$ to $2<{\bfg}/$ l (Fibrinogen concentrate group (FC group)) compared with the control group (without serum fibrinogen level correction, who underwent traumatic cranial surgery. Participants were recruited from who brought to Shahid Rajaee Hospital affiliated to Shiraz University of medical sciences (SUMS), Shiraz, Iran due to traumatic brain injury and need cranial surgery within first $24\mathrm{~h~}$ after patient or next of kin written consent. Between March 2018 and October2019, Adult patients (16–60 years old) were eligible to participate if needed emergency cranial sur­ gery while serum fibrinogen level was between 1.5 and $2~\mathbf{g}/1$ . We exclude patients who were already received fresh frozen plasma (FFP), cryoprecipitate, vitamin K and etc., or had a history of hypercoagula­ bility or coagulopathy disorders, consumption of anticoagulant drugs or finding any abnormality in coagulation profile. Patients who need major surgery by other surgical services or received more than 2 L fluid before hospital arrival or had Abbreviated Injury Scale (AIS) more than 3 in other organs were excluded too. SUMS Ethic Committee approved the study protocol and all patients and/or their families provided written informed consent. The study was review and approved by Shiraz Uni­ versity of Medical Sciences Ethic Committee: (IR.SUMS.MED. REC.1397.340) and the Iranian Registry of Clinical Trial (IRCT) (IRCT20190129042541N1).  

# 2.2. Randomization and blinding  

Randomized assignment to treatment groups was electronically performed. Patients and surgeons were blinded to treatment assignment, although anesthesiology technicians were not blinded. In FC group we gave fibrinogen concentrate in order to correct serum fibrinogen level more than $2.0~g/1$ . patients with serum fibrinogen level $1.75{-}2~g/1$ received $^\textrm{\scriptsize1g}$ fibrinogen concentrate, and those with serum fibrinogen level $1.5{-}1.75~g/1$ received $_\mathrm{~2~g~}$ fibrinogen concentrate and in control group, patients received no fibrinogen concentrate.  

# 2.3. Intervention  

For all patients who assigned to study, 1.8 cc bloods were drawn from the antecubital vein, after 10-minute centrifugation, samples kept in $37\mathrm{c}^{\circ}$ for 2 min and then serum fibrinogen level was measured. We also checked Plt, prothrombin time (PT), partial thromboplastin time (PTT), international normalized ratio (INR) to find out if participants have coagulation abnormality or not. Hemoglobin (Hb) level measured preoperation and post-operation. Perioperative packed cell transfusion, intra-operative bleeding, pre-operation and post-operation size and site of hematoma in brain computed tomography (CT) scan, post-operation Hemovac drainage, reoperation due to previous operated site rebleed­ ing and reoperation cranial surgery in other sites of cranial due to he­ matoma expansion were recorded. For hemorrhage volume measurement in brain CT scan, $\mathbf{A}{\times}\mathbf{B}{\times}\mathbf{C}{\times}\mathbf{\delta}0.5$ formula used. A and B are the largest diameter, C is the thickness of hemorrhage in vertical. Blood loss estimated via study of $\mathrm{Fu}$ ‑Qiang Gao et al. at early post operation (within first $^{6\mathrm{~h~}}$ ) and $24\mathrm{~h~}$ after operation (14).  

Hb loss t $\mathrm{\Gamma_{\mathrm{{tal}}}=BV\times(Hb_{i}-Hb_{e})\times0.001+Hb_{t}}.$  

V loss total $=1000\times{\mathrm{Hb}}$ loss total/Hbi.  

Generally, 1 U banked blood is considered to contain $52\pm5.4~\mathrm{Hb}$ [14].  

Follow up assessment performed within operation and postoperation period.  

# 2.4. Statistical analysis  

To analyze values, numerical variables were presented as mean $\pm$ SEM (standard error of mean), while categorical variables were sum­ marized as numbers (percentages). The variables were compared be­ tween two groups by Student’s t-test and the Chi-square/Fisher’s exact test. The data were analyzed using the statistical package for social sciences (SPSS software version 22.0 for Windows (SPSS Inc.). The dif­ ferences among groups were assessed using one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) with post-hoc Tukey test as well as Chi-square exact tests. A pvalue $<0.05$ was taken as the significant level.  

# 3. Results  

# 3.1. Baseline characteristics  

During the study time table 65 patients with head trauma were screened to confirm the eligibility criteria. Forty-seven patients were enrolled and randomly assigned to receive fibrinogen concentrate $\mathbf{(N=}$ 20) or placebo $\mathrm{(N\:=\:27.}$ ) (Fig. 1). For the primary endpoint, after excluding patients based on exclusion criteria; patients who underwent cranial surgery including decompressive craniectomy (DC), subdural hematoma (SDH) evacuation, epidural hematoma (EDH) and intracra­ nial hematoma (ICH) evacuation enrolled to the study between March 2018- October2019, with mean age of 39.94 years (standard deviation, 16.06), $83\%$ male and $17\%$ female. The demographic and admission parameters based on serum fibrinogen level are demonstrated in Table 1 (patients may have more than one pathology in brain CT scan). No significant differences were found in terms of age and sex between two studied groups $(\mathtt{p}>0.05)$ . In FC group the proportion of patients with admission levels between 1.5 and $1.75\:g/1$ and $1.75{-}2\:8/1$ was $65\%$ and $35\%$ , respectively. Although volume of EDH in FC and control groups were statistically similar $(\mathtt{p}>0.05)$ . However, other hemorrhage factors did not show significant differences between groups $(\mathbf{p}>0.05)$ .  

# 3.2. Intra-operative measures  

Intra-operative values included PC administration during operation and blood loss volume those were recorded by anesthesiology technician (Table 2). Intra-operation PC administration in FC group $(80\%)$ is more than control groups $(55.5\%)$ $(\mathbf{p}>0.05)$ . Bleeding volume loss in control group was significantly more than patients who received fibrinogen concentrate $(\mathbf{p}=0.036)$ (Table 2).

# 4. Discussion  

TBI remains an important cause of mortality and morbidity all around the world. Traumatic-induced coagulopathy has a key role in bleeding tendency, also it is preventable [9,15]. This is the first RCT study evaluating serum fibrinogen level correction in TBI cases. Mean sample taking was $23\mathrm{min}$ and fibrinogen concentrate administration was $58\mathrm{min}$ despite the range varied $(40{-}112\operatorname*{min})$ . There was some evidence of average similarity on admission between groups in pre-operation values such as age, gender, pre-op Hb and Plt which likely reflects the equivalent distribution of patients in groups. Average SDH thickness and ICH and EDH volume in the control group were similar to F.C. Patients, except the control group received fibrinogen concentrate before or within the operation.  

Intra-operative values show more PC transfusion demand in FC group but overall PC transfusion has no significant differences between FC and control group. Although intra-operative PC transfusion partially depends on surgeon and anesthesiologists, it might be due to the small sample size. Mean bleeding volume loss during the operation in control group was significantly more than FC, which reflects fibrinogen correction effect on post TBI coagulopathy in acute phase. Postoperation Hb and Plt amount those checked within $^{6-8\mathrm{~h~}}$ after opera­ tion had no significant differences, while post-operation PC transfusion requirements (during post-operation hospitalization period) in FC group was more than control group. In contrast, some studies showed that fibrinogen therapy reduces PC transfusion demand [6,11].  

The post-operation brain CT scan revealed that new intracranial hemorrhage occurred in 22 patients (One new EDH and 21 new ICH). The average volume and distribution incidence among groups were statistically similar. Further operation due to new intracranial in­ dications after initial surgery had no significant difference between groups. Calculated intra-operative blood loss by researchers demon­ strate more blood loss in control group but it wasn’t statistically sig­ nificant more than FC group.  

Data that mentioned before, demonstrate that fibrinogen adminis­ tration has advantages in TBI patients. In participants who did not received fibrinogen concentrate, intraoperative bleeding was signifi­ cantly more than FC group. Several studies consist on post-traumatic low fibrinogen level on admission to the emergency room is associated with the severity of injury, dilution, and shock. Fibrinogen level decreases clinically earlier than other coagulation factors and associated with more blood loss and PC transfusion demand. Thus early fibrinogen administration in acute phase of trauma will help to manage hemostasis and trauma induced coagulopathy [5,6,13]. Only early fibrinogen administration is considered as crucial supplement because fibrinogen level begins to rise after the second day of trauma and increases due to upregulated fibrinogen synthesis in the liver as an acute phase reactant. Therefore, elevated fibrinogen level could be detected in days 5 and 7 post-trauma regardless of initial fibrinogen supplementation [6]. Rudi­ man et al. found that there was no significant association between mortality and the values of fibrinogen level; but Initial fibrinogen level is strongly associated with coagulopathy [12].

# 5. Conclusion  

The present study revealed that the treatment of TBI patients with fibrinogen supplement decreases intraoperative bleeding, but has no effect on P.C transfusion demand, post-operation new intracranial hemorrhage, and reoperation. We suggest that further multicenter and prospective studies with a large sample size are needed to evaluate the fibrinogen effect in TBI patients. Also, better evaluation could be done via restriction of number of surgeons and splitting day and night oper­ ation data.
