# Mechanical thrombectomy alone versus with thrombolysis for ischemic stroke: A meta-analysis of randomized trials  

Hassan Kobeissi $\textcircled{1}$ , Gautam Adusumilli $\circledcirc$ , Sherief Ghozy1 $\circledcirc$ Cem Bilgin1 $\circledcirc$ , Ramanathan Kadirvel1,3 $\circledcirc$ , Waleed Brinjikji1, Jeremy J Heit $\circledcirc$ , Alejandro A Rabinstein5 and David F Kallmes1 $\circledcirc$  

# Abstract  

Introduction: Mechanical thrombectomy (MT) is the standard of care in eligible patients presenting with acute ischemic stroke (AIS). The question of whether intravenous thrombolysis (IVT) improves outcomes in conjunction with MT remains unanswered. We performed a systematic review and meta-analysis of published randomized controlled trials (RCT) to explore outcomes of MT with and without IVT.  

Methods: Following the PRISMA guidelines, a systematic literature review of the English language literature was conducted using PubMed, Embase, Web of science, and Scopus. Outcomes of interest included 90-day modified Rankin Scale (mRS) 0–2, thrombolysis in cerebral infarction (TICI) score $2b-3$ , symptomatic intracranial hemorrhage (sICH), distal embolization, and mortality. We calculated pooled risk ratios (RRs) and their corresponding $95\%$ confidence intervals (CI).  

Results: Six RCTs with 2334 patients compared outcomes of patients treated with MT alone and MT with IVT. Both treatments resulted in comparable rates of $\mathsf{mRS~}0^{-2}$ ( $\mathsf{RR}=0.96$ , $95\%$ ${\sfC}|=0.88\substack{-1.04}$ ; $p$ -value $=0.282^{}$ ), sICH ( $\mathsf{RR}=0.80$ , $95\%$ $\mathsf{Cl}=0.55\AA-$ 1.17; $p\mathtt{-value}=0.253\$ , mortality at 90-days $\mathsf{RR}=1.06$ , $5\%~{\sfCl}=0.88\substack{-1.28}$ ; $p$ -value $=0.529$ ), and distal embolization $\mathsf{\Omega}(\mathsf{RR}=$ 1.10, $95\%$ ${\sfCl}=0.79\substack{-1.52}$ ; $p$ -value $=0.572$ ). MT alone was associated with a lower rate of TICI 2b-3 compared to MT with IVT $\mathrm{\mathit{\Omega}}^{\prime}\mathrm{RR}=0.96$ , $95\%$ $\%{\sfCl}=0.93–0.99$ ; $p\mathtt{-value}=0.006\$ ).  

Conclusions: In this meta-analysis of six RCTs, MT alone was comparable to MT plus IVT for mRS 0–2, sICH, mortality, and distal embolization; however, MT alone resulted in lower rates of TICI $2b-3$ . Further trials are needed to determine which patient populations benefit from MT plus IVT and to increase the power of future meta-analyses.

# Introduction  

Since 2015, several randomized clinical trials (RCT) have established the utility of mechanical thrombectomy (MT) in emergent large vessel occlusions (ELVO).1–7 As a result, MT has become a standard of care in eligible patients. However, despite the increased utilization of MT, intravenous thrombolysis (IVT) still has widespread use as a primary, rescue, and bridging therapy.  

IVT alone has a modest benefit for ELVO, with successful recanalization rates ranging between $11\%$ and $33\%$ .8,9 Nevertheless, IVT is widely employed prior to MT as bridging therapy due to its complementary advantages. IVT has a rapid onset of action, and therefore, it could potentially shorten brain ischemia time. Additionally, MT can fail to achieve successful recanalization in up to $29\%$ of ELVO patients.10 In such cases, bridging therapy ensures that the patient has received timely thrombolytic treatment.  

Current guidelines recommend bridging therapy in all eligible ELVO patients (class 1 recommendation).11,12  

However, bridging therapy can also exert several unfavorable effects. It might delay the initiation of MT and may increase the risk of hemorrhagic complications and cost.13 Additionally, IVT can lead to clot fragmentation and embolization.14 These partially dissolved clot components can move distally with thrombectomy attempts. Thus, a treatable lesion might become untreatable.  

Due to potential benefits and risks, the role of bridging therapy in ELVO has been a topic of interest. We conducted a systematic review and meta-analysis of published RCTs to eliminate selection bias. In this study, we sought to compare the efficacy and safety outcomes of MT alone compared to MT plus IVT.  

# Methods  

# Search strategy  

On 14 October 2022, following the PRISMA guidelines for performing systematic reviews, a systematic literature review of the English language literature was conducted within the Nested Knowledge Autolit software per the drafted protocol, from inception, using PubMed, Embase, Web of science, and Scopus.15 Based on each database, different combinations of possible keywords and/or MeSH terms were used for that purpose. Keywords and MeSH terms included: “without intravenous,” “thrombectomy,” and “endovascular.” The publication type for the search was “clinical trial.” Moreover, we did an extensive manual search through the references of the included articles to retrieve any missed papers.  

# Screening process  

We included all original studies fulfilling our predetermined PICO. Population was patients with AIS, Intervention treatment with MT and IVT, Control group treatment with MT alone, the Outcomes of interest were the modified Rankin Scale (mRS) score 0–2, thrombolysis in cerebral infarction (TICI) score 2b-3, distal embolization, mortality, and symptomatic intracranial hemorrhage (sICH). We excluded papers where patients did not have AIS, were not treated with MT with and without IVT, review articles, duplicate studies including the same patients presented in other included paper, case reports, case series with less than five patients, and conference abstracts. We included RCTs and excluded all other study designs.  

Two authors did the title and abstract screening against the pre-defined criteria. This was followed by a full text screening of any retained studies of the first screening step. In both stages, the senior author was consulted to resolve any conflicts in the decisions.  

# Data extraction  

Following a pilot extraction, an extraction sheet was built, and the extraction was performed by at least two authors. The extracted data included study characteristics, baseline data of the included patients, and the aforementioned outcomes of interest. After performing the extraction, a third author did an extensive revision of the extracted data to avoid any prior mistakes. The data that support the findings of this study are available from the corresponding author on reasonable request.  

# Risk of bias  

The “Cochrane RoB 2: a revised tool for assessing risk of bias in randomised trials” was used to assess the risk of bias, with two independent reviewers evaluating all studies.16 Two authors evaluated the quality of each study and adjudicated by a third one, whenever needed.  

# Statistical analysis  

Using the package “meta,” we conducted a pairwise meta-analysis to calculate the pooled risk ratios (RRs) and their $95\%$ confidence intervals $95\%$ CI). A random-effects model was used to perform the meta-analysis due to methodological heterogeneity contradicting with the common effect assumption and because the number of included studies was greater than five.17 The pooled effect sizes were considered heterogenous whenever $\mathrm{I}^{2}$ was higher than $50\%$ and/or $p$ -value< 0.05, as assessed by Q-statistic. Due to small number of the included studies (less than 10), assessing publication bias using Egger’s regression test was not possible.  

# Results  

# Search and screening results  

We retrieved 266 papers for screening from the initial search. Moreover, we excluded 229 records through the title and abstract screening stage, to retain 37 records for full-text screening. Another five papers were identified via expert recommendation and retained for full-text screening. Following full-text screening, six papers were determined to satisfy our inclusion criteria with the appropriate outcomes of interest (Supplementary Figure 1).  

# Study characteristics and risk of bias  

All six studies included in our analysis were RCTs. The size of the included studies ranged from 204 patients to 656 patients. All six RCTs included in our analysis were deemed to have a “low” risk of bias (Supplementary Table 1). Study characteristics, such as age, baseline NIHSS, sex, and procedural outcomes are detailed in Table 1 and Supplementary Table S2.  

# Good functional outcome (mRS 0–2)  

Six RCTs, with 2334 patients reported rate of mRS 0–2 at 90 days. MT alone resulted in comparable rates of mRS $0-$ 2 compared to MT plus IVT $\mathrm{\mathit{RR}}=0.96$ ; $95\%\mathrm{CI}=0.88-$ 1.04; $p$ -value $=~0.282$ ). There was no heterogeneity among the included studies $(\mathrm{I}^{2}=0\%$ ; $p$ -value $=0.467^{}$ ) (Figure 1).  

# Successful reperfusion (TICI 2b-3)

# Distal embolization  

Four RCTs, with 1676 patients reported rate of distal embolization. MT alone resulted in comparable rates of distal embolization compared to MT plus IVT $\mathrm{(RR=}$ 1.10; $95\%$ $\mathrm{CI}=0.79-$ 1.52; $p$ -value $=0.572\$ ). There was no heterogeneity among the included studies $(\mathrm{I}^{2}=0\%$ ; $p$ -value $=0.723$ ) (Figure 5).  

# Discussion  

Our meta-analysis demonstrated a number of important findings. Our analysis revealed that, based on available evidence, MT alone versus MT plus IVT resulted in similar rates of good functional outcome, mortality, sICH, and distal embolization, even though MT alone produced lower rates of successful reperfusion.  

Previous meta-analyses investigated the role of MT alone versus MT plus IVT in ELVO with inconsistent results. Three studies suggested that MT alone resulted in functional independence rates similar to MT plus IVT.18–20 On the other hand, four studies suggested that MT plus IVT is associated with better functional outcomes with a relatively similar safety profile to MT alone.21–24 However, these studies did not perform adjustment procedures and included studies in which MT alone was performed for many IVT ineligible patients. Adusumilli et al. previously conducted a meta-analysis of DIRECT-MT, DEVT, SKIP, and MR CLEAN-NO IV and reported similar results to our present study, though the previous meta-analysis did not include DIRECT-SAFE and SWIFT DIRECT.25 Since the publication of DIRECT-SAFE and SWIFT DIRECT, analyses have been performed incorporating the trials.26,27 Our results are in agreement with the previously published meta-analyses.  

Because sICH has a detrimental impact on clinical outcomes, the potential increased rates of sICH hypothesized to occur with MT plus IVT has been one of the major concerns for the combined treatment. However, completed RCTs on the role of direct MT found that bridging therapy does not significantly increase the risk of sICH.28–33 Likewise, in our study, the rates of sICH did not significantly differ across groups, even with the addition of IVT, although there was a trend toward lower rates of sICH with MT alone.

It is important to note that, while the results were statistically insignificant, MT alone trended toward lower rates of mRS 0–2 and higher rates of mortality and distal embolization. Additionally, MT alone resulted in lower rates of successful reperfusion. These trends suggest that MT plus IVT is a reasonable treatment for eligible patients. Yet, it is possible that certain subgroups of patients may benefit from the omittance of IVT, such as those with occlusion of the terminal bifurcation of the internal carotid artery, where prompt initiation of MT can result in better outcomes.34 Furthermore, previous literature has reported that in AIS due to M2 occlusions, bridging therapy may exert a favorable effect on outcomes.26  

Among the published RCTs, different statistical designs were employed to test MT alone versus MT plus IVT. For example, the DEVT trial was designed with a noninferiority margin of $10\%$ , whereas the SKIP trial was designed with a noninferiority odds ratio of 0.74. The DIRECT-MT and MR CLEAN-NO IV trials also tested noninferiority, although the margin in those trials was an odds ratio of 0.8. DIRECT-SAFE used a noninferiority margin of $-0.1$ , and SWIFT-DIRECT used a noninferiority margin of $12\%$ . The heterogeneity in noninferiority margins among the published RCTs contributed to our decision to not employ a noninferiority design in our meta-analysis. Future RCTs should work to increase transparency regarding the selection of noninferiority margins, as there is often confusion in the neurointerventional community regarding these margins.35  

Our meta-analysis has several limitations. First, we only included published RCTs. Safety and efficacy outcomes of MT alone may change in a real-world setting. Second, IVT was administered at the participant thrombectomy-capable centers after randomization in most of the included RCTs. Consequently, drip and ship patients were not enrolled in these RCTs. Therefore, our results do not exclude the potential benefit of bridging therapy in transfer patients. Third, most of the included RCTs employed alteplase for bridging therapy. However, tenecteplase and glycoprotein $2\mathrm{b}/3\mathrm{a}$ inhibitors may exhibit a different safety profile with combined treatment, and therefore, could potentially alter outcomes.  

Fourth, IVT doses varied between included RCTs, potentially affecting the performance and safety profile of IVT and combined treatment. Additionally, all RCTs focused on anterior circulation ELVO. Final, we did not have access to patient-level data, limiting the analysis we were able to perform.  

With future RCTs, a new meta-analysis with higher power may clarify the role of IVT conjunction with MT. Future studies should focus on different patient populations and occlusion locations to better pinpoint who may benefit from inclusion and omittance of IVT. Presently, the use of MT plus IVT is associated with a similar safety profile compared to MT alone, and results in higher rates of successful reperfusion. Thus, MT plus IVT is a reasonable treatment option in eligible patients with AIS.  

# Conclusions  

In this meta-analysis of six RCTs of patients treated for AIS, we found comparable rates of good functional outcome and mortality at 90 days, sICH, and distal embolization between MT alone and MT plus IVT; however, we found lower rates of successful reperfusion with MT alone. Future RCTs should focus on determining subgroups of patients who may benefit from IVT.
