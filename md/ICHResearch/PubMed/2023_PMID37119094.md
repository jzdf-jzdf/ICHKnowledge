# Early stellate ganglion block for improvement of postoperative cerebral blood flow velocity after aneurysmal subarachnoid hemorrhage: results of a pilot randomized controlled trial  

\*Youxuan Wu, MD,1 Fa Lin, MM,2 Yang Bai, MM,1 Fa Liang, MD,1 Xinyan Wang, MM,1 Bo Wang, MD,1 Minyu Jian, MD,1 Yunzhen Wang, MD,1 Haiyang Liu, MD,1 Anxin Wang, MD,3 Xiaolin Chen, MD,2 and Ruquan Han, MD1  

Departments of 1Anesthesiology and 2Neurosurgery, Beijing Tiantan Hospital, Capital Medical University, Beijing; and 3Department of Statistics, China National Clinical Research Center for Neurological Diseases, Beijing, China  

OBJECTIVE  Stellate ganglion block (SGB) is a commonly used sympathetic nerve block technique that may have benefits for patients with aneurysmal subarachnoid hemorrhage (aSAH) in the early stage. Cerebral vasospasm (CVS), one of the most common complications of aSAH, is accompanied by an abnormal increase in cerebral blood flow velocity (CBFV) and neurological dysfunction. In this pilot study the authors sought to determine the feasibility of early SGB for CVS in aSAH patients by observing the incidence of symptomatic CVS.  

METHODS  Prior to receiving surgical treatment, patients with aSAH were randomly assigned to the SGB group or the non-SGB group. The primary outcome was the incidence of symptomatic CVS within $14\pm2$ days after the onset of aSAH. As a higher CBFV is often associated with CVS and a poor prognosis, the mean CBFV of the middle cerebral artery was observed immediately after surgery and on postoperative days 1, 2, 3, 5, and 7. Other secondary outcomes included transcranial Doppler (TCD)/CTA-type CVS, delayed cerebral ischemia during hospitalization, new cerebral infarction within 3 months, adverse events (AEs), and clinical prognosis.  

RESULTS  Symptomatic CVS occurred in $40\%$ of patients in the non-SGB group and in $20\%$ in the SGB group (RR 0.50, $95\%$ CI 0.22–1.16). Continuous TCD sonography revealed that the postoperative mean CBFV was lower in the SGB group than in the non-SGB group $\mathrm{\Omega}(\mathrm{F}=3.608\$ , $p=0.02)$ ). In addition, the percentages of patients with CVS evaluated by TCD (TCD-CVS) and total new infarctions within 3 months were also significantly lower than those in patients with CVS (TCD-CVS $36.7\%$ vs $70\%$ , RR 0.52, $95\%\mathbb{C}|0.31\mathrm{-}0.89$ , and total new infarctions $26.7\%$ vs $53.3\%$ , RR 0.50, $95\%$ CI 0.25–0.99). In terms of AEs and mortality, there were no significant differences between the two groups.  

CONCLUSIONS  This pilot study demonstrated for the first time, to the authors’ knowledge, that early SGB is feasible and has the potential to reduce the risk of CVS and improve the prognosis of aSAH. This method may be a new treatment for patients with aSAH that may have more advantages than traditional therapeutic drugs and is worth further study.

D espite advances in the standard treatment of aneurysmal subarachnoid hemorrhage (aSAH) and a decline in mortality, more than one-third of ini  
tial bleeding survivors may still face death or long-term  

neurological disability due to delayed cerebral ischemia (DCI).1–3 Cerebral vasospasm (CVS) is one of the leading causes of DCI after hemorrhage. Therefore, improved patient prognosis can be achieved by preventing CVS and  

DCI, but little progress in prevention has been made over the past decade. Advancement will require new ideas and methods for the treatment of aSAH.  

The effect of nimodipine on aSAH is worth considering. In the past, we have mainly relied on cerebrovascular dilation to reduce the occurrences of CVS and DCI. However, cerebrovascular dilation alone did not significantly improve the prognosis of aSAH, and only treatment with nimodipine showed beneficial effects, possibly because it offered neuroprotective effects that other vasodilators did not have.4–6 These findings indicate that multiple pathophysiological mechanisms lead to CVS or DCI, and therefore aSAH treatment based on merely a single pathophysiological process is not feasible. To treat aSAH, it is necessary to consider multiple potential pathogenic mechanisms simultaneously, such as neuroinflammation, early brain injury (EBI), and microcirculatory disturbance.  

Stellate ganglion block (SGB), a sympathetic nerve block technique, has been widely used in treating vascular diseases in clinical practice. In fact, studies have also found that SGB has more extensive therapeutic effects on other diseases, such as posttraumatic stress syndrome,7 ulcerative colitis,8 and cancerous hot flashes.9 It seems that SGB exerts extensive systemic effects by inhibiting the sympathetic ganglion, including dilation of the cerebral vessels, reduction of the stress response, regulation of immunity, and improvement of microcirculation.10–15 Several case reports and retrospective studies have revealed that SGB has promising effects on CVS that is refractory to traditional treatment.16–19 This finding indicates that SGB may be more advantageous than traditional methods for treating CVS. Most likely similar to the neuroprotective effect of nimodipine, the effect of SGB on CVS cannot be explained simply by secondary macrovascular dilation after a sympathetic ganglion block. The capacity of SGB to prevent CVS may be based on multiple mechanisms, making it more advantageous than traditional treatment in refractory CVS. The early stage of aSAH is the key period of treatment; thus, we speculated that the benefits of early SGB for aSAH might be an effective measure for reducing the risks of CVS and DCI. Unfortunately, existing studies have shown that SGB has only been used as a rescue therapy for refractory CVS, and few studies have focused on the effects on aSAH of its early application.  

A causal relationship between early SGB treatment and CVS or DCI prevention has not yet been established. Therefore, we designed a prospective randomized, openlabel, blinded endpoint trial (BLOCK-CVS) to explore whether early application of SGB is effective in treating aSAH in terms of reducing the risk of CVS, preventing DCI, and improving patient prognosis. In this pilot study of the BLOCK-CVS trial, we explored the feasibility and potential efficacy of the early application of SGB for preventing CVS in aSAH by observing the incidence of symptomatic CVS and the change in cerebral blood flow velocity (CBFV).  



# Methods  



# Study Design and Patients  

The BLOCK-CVS trial is a prospective, randomized, open-label, blinded endpoint assessment study that is in line with the SPIRIT (Standard Protocol Items: Recommendations for Interventional Trials) statement. The study protocol of the BLOCK-CVS trial has been evaluated and approved by the Medical Ethics Committee of Beijing Tiantan Hospital, Capital Medical University, and registered with ClinicalTrials.gov (no. NCT04691271). The legal representatives of all participants signed informed consent forms before patient enrollment.  

In the BLOCK-CVS trial, all eligible patients were $\geq18$ years old with a clear diagnosis of aSAH by CTA or DSA, a preoperative Hunt and Hess grade of II–III, and a scheduled procedure for clipping of an intracranial aneurysm within 48 hours after hemorrhage. Patients were excluded if they had a preoperative American Society of Anesthesiologists physical status class $>$ III, posterior circulation aneurysm, multiple aneurysms, coagulation disorder, tumor or infection in the nerve block area, anatomical structure changes in the neck, a poor temporal window confirmed by preoperative transcranial Doppler (TCD) sonography examination, allergy to known local anesthetics, or were pregnant or lactating.  



# Randomization and Allocation  

The randomization method was blocked randomization at a ratio of 1:1. Due to the small sample size of the pilot study, stratification was not carried out, and the number of blocks was 4. The table of random digits was completed by dedicated independent staff via a computer (Stata 17). After the enrolled patients were admitted to the operating room, the designated anesthesiologist in charge of the intervention made the random assignment according to the information received through the networking software and performed SGB or a sham operation on the respectively assigned patients. Because the anesthesiologist and neurosurgeons needed to safely provide relevant patient care, they were not blinded. Patients were blinded unless there were serious adverse events (AEs) requiring additional visits.  

In the intervention group, the designated experienced anesthesiologist performed SGB on the patients by using ultrasonic visualization technology before anesthesia induction. After routine disinfection, $0.5\%$ ropivacaine $(8{-}10\mathrm{ml})$ was injected into the surface of the longus colli muscle under the prevertebral fascia at the C6 plane, and then the puncture point was covered with a sterile dressing. To prevent bilateral recurrent laryngeal nerve block from endangering the safety of the patient, a unilateral SGB was adopted for the craniotomy side. The craniotomy side is often the side with more bleeding. For communicating segment aneurysms, the neurosurgeon chose the craniotomy side based on habit or aneurysm direction if the amounts of hemorrhage on the left and right sides were similar. The success of SGB was determined by the presence of Horner’s syndrome, which is characterized by miosis, ptosis, enophthalmos, conjunctival hyperemia, and facial redness without sweating. After undergoing SGB, patients were observed for at least 30 minutes to determine whether they were safe and to confirm that the block was successful.  

For the control group, a blank control was adopted to reduce unnecessary invasive operations. However, to ensure the smooth implementation of the blinded endpoint evaluation, the control group underwent a “camouflage” sham operation. The designated anesthesiologist disinfected the skin of the patient’s neck and covered it with a sterile dressing. In this way, the follow-up staff would not be aware of the grouping of the patients by puncture point. As Horner’s syndrome usually disappeared at the end of the surgery, it was not necessary to perform special measures to maintain blinding. Although SGB was not performed, the craniotomy side was regarded as the treated side.  



# Standardized Treatment  

All patients were admitted to the ICU after surgery and received unified standardized treatment, including intensive care, maintenance of normal blood pressure (systolic blood pressure $140{-}160\mathrm{mm}\mathrm{Hg}$ , fluid management to maintain normovolemia (positive balance $>50\bar{0}\mathrm{ml/day})$ and reduce intracranial pressure (ICP), and nimodipine treatment. In this treatment center, the preferred treatment was continuous intravenous infusion of nimodipine $(0.8-$ $2.0\mathrm{mg/hr)}$ via a microsyringe pump. We did not aggressively treat drug-induced hypertension to prevent CVS. The intensive care physician in charge, who was blinded, used such therapy only when necessary because of ischemic symptoms. Norepinephrine was the first-choice vasopressor. Likewise, hypervolemia and hemodilution were not used in any of the patients. Patients with severe cerebral edema or ICP greater than $20\mathrm{mm}\mathrm{Hg}$ were treated with mannitol or $3\%$ sodium chloride saline.  



# Outcomes  

The primary outcome of the BLOCK-CVS trial was the incidence of symptomatic CVS within $14\pm2$ days after the onset of aSAH. Because a higher mean CBFV is often associated with CVS risk and a poor prognosis,20 we focused on the mean CBFV on both sides immediately after surgery and on postoperative days 1, 2, 3, 5, and 7 as another indicator of both patient improvement and the effectiveness and feasibility of early SGB. In addition, other outcomes, such as cerebral perfusion in the early postoperative period, TCD/CTA-type CVS and DCI during hospitalization, total new cerebral infarctions within 3 months, AEs, and 90-day neurological functional status, were also monitored.  



# Adverse Events  

From the onset of SGB to hospital discharge, all AEs in the patients were monitored. AEs of particular interest included the occurrence of complications such as recurrent hemorrhage, pneumonia, and neurological infections, and SGB-related complications, including local anesthetic toxicity, carotid/vertebral artery injury, and hematoma or infection at the puncture site. All AEs were taken as safety indicators to evaluate the safety of SGB.  



# Clinical Evaluation  

All of the follow-up staff were blinded to the treatment assignment. All patient CBFV data were recorded by the same experienced examiner using the same mobile TCD (Delica; EMS-9D) on postoperative days 1, 2, 3, 5, and 7. The corresponding parameters were as follows: 2-MHz probe: middle cerebral artery (MCA) at a detection depth of $40{-}60~\mathrm{mm}$ ; anterior cerebral artery (ACA) at a detecting depth of $60{-}80~\mathrm{mm}$ ; and 16-MHz probe: intracranial segment of the internal carotid artery (ICA). The highest CBFV within the scanning range was obtained and recorded.  

CVS was classified as symptomatic CVS, TCD-CVS, or CTA-CVS, with symptomatic CVS defined as a new focal or global cranial nerve dysfunction or a decrease in the patient Glasgow Coma Scale score by 2 points that was not explainable by reasons such as primary hemorrhage, secondary hemorrhage, hydrocephalus, fever, infection, electrolyte or metabolic disorder, or surgical complications.21,22 TCD-CVS or CTA-CVS indicated the presence of vascular stenosis. New infarctions on the next CT examinations after symptom occurrence were defined as instances of DCI, regardless of whether there were TCD- or CTA-supported vasospasms. These conditions were evaluated by two independent, blinded investigators. TCD-CVS was defined as a spasm with a mean CBFV greater than $120\mathrm{cm/sec}$ . CTA-CVS was defined as follows: compared with the baseline CTA results at hospital admission, there was new arterial stenosis or an increased ratio of arterial stenosis that was not associated with arterial stenosis caused by atherosclerosis or the mechanical action of the artery clip or coil. Patients underwent CT examinations at admission, within 24 hours after aneurysm clipping, $5\pm2$ days postoperatively, before discharge from the hospital, 3 months after discharge, and when neurological deterioration occurred. CT scanning at hospital admission was mainly used to evaluate the type and severity of aSAH, and follow-up CT scanning after surgery was used to evaluate new infarction. Cerebral perfusion was evaluated by routine postoperative CT perfusion (CTP) imaging $5\pm$ 2 days after clipping. All imaging evaluations were performed by an independent neuroimaging expert.  

The patients’ 90-day neurological prognosis assessments were completed by telephone by a certified neurologist  who was not involved in the intervention process and did not know the grouping information.  



# Statistical Analysis  

In the BLOCK-CVS trial, the estimated sample size for adequate power was 228 cases in total.23 Taking $20\%$ of the number as the sample size of the pilot study, the result indicates 46 cases. Because there was no reference for the SGB effect size to evaluate sample size accurately, the total BLOCK-CVS sample size estimate may have been inaccurate. Therefore, we planned to obtain the SGB effect size through this pilot study. Another purpose of the pilot study was to evaluate the feasibility of early SGB. Therefore, we considered the highest mean CBFV to guarantee a sufficient sample size and power to test the hypothesis in the pilot study. The results of this pilot study will be used to guide the correction of sample size in the BLOCKCVS trial. Previous studies showed that the highest mean CBFV after aSAH surgery was $149\pm47\mathrm{cm/sec}.$ 24 We assumed that SGB could reduce the highest mean CBFV to the normal level, namely, below $120~\mathrm{cm/sec}$ (down $20\%$ , approximately $117\pm47\mathrm{cm/sec})$ ). Using PASS, version 15 (NCSS), considering a $5\%$ dropout rate, the sample size calculated was 30 cases per group $(\upalpha=0.05,\upbeta=0.\mathbf{\hat{8}})$ , with 60 cases in total.  

All data acquired in this study were analyzed using IBM SPSS version 23 (IBM Corp.). Categorical variable data, such as the incidences of CVS, DCI, and new infarction and the neurological prognosis, were tested using the chi-square test. Postoperative mean CBFV was analyzed using repeated ANOVA, a random-intercept linear mixedmodel analysis, to assess the difference between groups in response to intervention with an unstructured covariance matrix. Intervention, time, and intervention $\times$ time as fixed effects together with a random intercept for each patient were included in this model. The specific differences at baseline and each time point were verified using the Student t-test. Two-sided $\mathrm{p}\bar{\le}0.05$ was considered statistically significant. For multiple comparisons, we used the Bonferroni method to adjust the $\mathsf{p}$ value. Both intentionto-treat and per-protocol analyses were performed in this trial. For patients with successful SGB, we conducted statistical analysis in line with the per-protocol principle. For missing data, the latest observed data were used for insertion. The latest CTP examination was performed before surgery, so missing CTP data were not included.  



# Results  

From July 2021 to May 2022, a total of 136 aSAH patients were admitted to Beijing Tiantan Hospital and underwent aneurysm clipping therapy. In total, 76 patients were excluded from the study for various reasons, including 25 patients with a time of onset to emergency exceeding 48 hours, 24 patients with serious consciousness disorders, 11 patients with poor temporal windows, 7 patients with neurological deficits, 5 patients with multiple aneurysms, 3 patients refusing to participate, and 1 patient for whom a temporary decision was made to use coil treatment. Sixty patients were randomly enrolled in the SGB group or the non-SGB group (Fig. 1). All 60 patients underwent the intervention and assessment for the primary endpoint, and no patient withdrew prematurely during the trial. In the SGB group, apparent Horner’s syndrome was observed in all 30 patients 5–10 minutes after SGB, and the block success rate was $100\%$ . There were no baseline differences between the two groups (Table 1).  

For symptomatic CVS, DCI, and 90-day modified Rankin Scale (mRS) score, although there was no statistically significant difference, the SGB group exhibited a consistent favorable trend (Fig. 2). The incidence rates of symptomatic CVS and DCI were $40\%$ and $30\%$ overall and $20\%$ and $16.7\%$ in the SGB group. The incidence decreased by nearly $50\%$ in the SGB group. The SGB group also had more asymptomatic survivors (mRS score 0) evaluated at 90 days $66.7\%$ vs $43.3\%$ , RR 1.69, $95\%$ CI $0.94\substack{-3.12})$ .  

There was a significant intervention $\times$ time interaction effect ( $\mathrm{\DeltaF}=3.608\$ , $\mathrm{p}=0.02)$ found for mean CBFV on the treated side (Fig. 3). The two groups had different change trends in terms of postoperative mean CBFV, which was significantly lower in the SGB group on the treated side immediately and 1–5 days after clipping (Table 2). The mean CBFV reached its peak around day 5 after the operation. The highest mean CBFV in the non-SGB group was $136.6\pm50.1~\mathrm{cm/sec}$ , whereas in the SGB group, it was $105.0\pm36.7\mathrm{cm/sec}$ $(\mathbf{p}=0.007,$ ). However, on the contralateral side, the intervention $\times$ time interaction effect (F $=2.002$ , $\mathrm{p}=0.120;$ ) was not obvious. The overall level of mean CBFV in the SGB group was lower than that in the non-SGB group. Due to the improvement in mean CBFV on the treated side, the two groups showed significant differences in TCD-CVS $36.7\%$ vs $70\%$ , RR 0.52, $95\%$ CI 0.31–0.89). The SGB group also had fewer total new infarctions within up to 3 months $26.7\%$ vs $53.3\%$ , RR 0.50, $95\%$ CI 0.25–0.99).  



# Safety Assessment  

In 7 patients in the SGB group, recurrent laryngeal nerve block was observed 15 minutes after SGB. The patients presented with hoarseness, foreign body sensation, and cough, but their normal breathing and circulation were not affected. The symptoms of recurrent laryngeal nerve block disappeared after completion of the surgery, and no AEs were observed. None of the patients presented with other SGB-related complications. Ultrasound-guided SGB greatly improved the safety and success rate of the operation.  

Complications of hemorrhage recurrence were the primary safety concern in this study. As the intervention was conducted before aneurysm clipping, increased cerebral perfusion meant a higher risk of hemorrhage. Therefore, we counted all types of hemorrhage but found that SGB did not increase the risk of hemorrhage $20.0\%$ vs $16.7\%$ , $\ensuremath{\mathbf{p}}=0.739$ ; RR 1.20, $95\%$ CI 0.41–3.51). One patient in the non-SGB group presented with severe recurrent hemorrhagic complications resulting in a persistent vegetative state after surgery. There were 3 cases of death, 2 patients in the non-SGB group and 1 patient in the SGB group. One patient died of massive cerebral infarction after surgery, 1 patient died of circulatory failure after sudden severe epilepsy, and 1 patient died of an unknown cause. In addition, there were fewer incidents of pneumonia and intracranial infection in the SGB group than in the non-SGB group ( $16.6\%$ vs $36.7\%$ and $10\%$ vs $20\%$ , respectively), but there was no statistically significant difference. Regarding other complications, such as electrolyte disturbance and liver function disorder, there were no differences between the two groups (Table 3).  



# Discussion  

To our knowledge, this study is the first randomized controlled trial to explore the efficacy and feasibility of SGB as an early treatment measure in aSAH patients. In this study, we found that early application of SGB significantly improved cerebral hemodynamics on the treated side after aneurysm clipping surgery, with decreased rates of TCD-CVS during hospitalization and 3-month total new infarctions. The highest mean CBFV decreased by more than $20\%$ , which was consistent with our initial assumption. In addition, we did not find that SGB caused any additional AEs affecting the condition of any of the patients. These outcomes indicated that the early SGB for CVS was feasible and probably had a potential beneficial effect on aSAH. Although there were no statistically significant differences in symptomatic CVS or DCI, the lower proportions in the SGB group also supported the possible benefit of SGB. Compared with the non-SGB group, in the SGB group symptomatic CVS decreased by nearly $50\%$ , and the DCI decreased by nearly $45\%$ . Of course, more samples are needed to confirm this difference. At 90 days, we observed that there were more asymptomatic patients (mRS score 0) in the SGB group. All of these findings support our initial conjecture that the early extensive SGB postsympathetic blockade effect may treat aSAH through a variety of potential mechanisms, reduce complications such as CVS and DCI, and improve patient prognosis.

In the classic clazosentan phase 3 study, Macdonald et al.30 noted that one of the possible reasons why clazosentan failed to reduce EBI was that clazosentan merely dilated large vessels but did not improve microcirculation. Gupta et al.10 found that SGB can significantly decrease cerebral vascular tone, which means that the resistance of small vessels is improved and, therefore, may have beneficial effects on microcirculation in the early stage of aSAH. In this study, we also found that nearly $40\%$ of the patients presented with abnormal cerebral perfusion detected by CTP scan when admitted to the hospital but without any radiological large-vascular stenosis. This indicated that early cerebral ischemic injury may indeed be more associated with microcirculatory disturbance. However, in the SGB group, the percentage of regional abnormal cerebral perfusion at postoperative days 3–5 was lower than that in the control group, also suggesting that SGB may be conducive to cerebral perfusion and microcirculation in aSAH patients.  

SGB may subdue molecules that activate and exacerbate neuroinflammation or EBI. Toll-like receptor 4 (TLR4), a pivotal molecule of neuroinflammation in aSAH, plays a crucial role in damage-associated molecular patterns (DAMPs).31 Animal experiments have revealed that SGB can act on TLR4 while inhibiting nuclear factor kappa B signaling pathway to mediate inflammation.13,14,32 Similarly, it was noted in an animal study that SGB diminished TLR4 expression, effectively reduced brain infarct volume in mice with acute stroke, and protected against ischemic injury and oxidative stress.14 Such findings may be the reason why the percentage of patients with new infarctions at 3 months in the SGB group was lower than that in the control group. Finally, some other proven effects of SGB may also be beneficial to treating aSAH patients, such as reducing the stress response. Of course, these possible potential action mechanisms still need to be verified by further research.  

This preliminary exploration of the BLOCK-CVS trial also has some limitations. First, unlike other SGB-related studies, the use of a single SGB may limit its efficacy. In this pilot study, it seems that unilateral SGB only had an obvious effect on improving mean CBFV on the same side. Therefore, continuous block or bilateral block may be more effective for CVS or DCI, and further research is needed. Second, we have not limited the new infarctions to the treated side, which may be more suitable to explain the results of unilateral block. Third, we did not monitor biochemical surrogates previously defined as predictors of vasospasm and outcome, such as von Willebrand factor and $\dot{\varsigma_{100\upbeta}}$ . Finally, although in this pilot study we calculated the sample size based on the highest mean CBFV, the sample size is still small, and it is still necessary to carry out randomized controlled trials, such as BLOCK-CVS.  

Finally, combined with the preliminary results and the implementation process, we proposed the following suggestions or basis for the next step of research. First, the incidence of symptomatic CVS in our center was approximately $40\%$ , and the potential therapeutic effect size of a single SGB might be nearly $50\%$ . Second, a single SGB also has a good effect on CBFV, and it is more important to block pathophysiological deterioration in an early and timely manner. Third, unilateral block may only work on the treatment side, so bilateral block may be more effective. Fourth, we did not observe more bleeding in the SGB group, and it is necessary to conduct SGB under ultrasonic guidance. Finally, the role of SGB is diversified, so it is necessary to comprehensively evaluate brain metabolism, cerebral perfusion, inflammation, and other variables.
