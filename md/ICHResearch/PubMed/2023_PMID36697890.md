# Association between intravenous tirofiban and intracranial hemorrhage in acute large vessel occlusion stroke: insight from the RESCUE BT randomized placebo‑controlled trial

# Abstract  

Background  The aim of this study is to investigate the association between intravenous tirofiban and symptomatic intracranial hemorrhage (SICH) in patients with acute ischemic stroke (AIS) secondary to large vessel occlusion (LVO) receiving endovascular thrombectomy (EVT) within $^{24\mathrm{h}}$ of time last known well (LKW).  

Methods  Patients with AIS-LVO who were randomly assigned to receive intravenous tirofiban or placebo before EVT within $^{241}$ of time LKW and had follow-up brain non-contrast computed tomography within $^{24\mathrm{h}}$ after stopping tirofiban treatment were derived from “RESCUE BT”: a multicenter, randomized, placebo-controlled, double-blind trial. All eligible patients were divided into SICH and NO-SICH groups. Subgroup analyses were performed to explore for heterogeneity.  

Results  Of 945 patients included in this cohort, there were 76 $(8.0\%)$ in the SICH group and 869 $(92.0\%)$ in the NO-SICH group. The incidence of SICH was not higher in patients receiving intravenous tirofiban compared with placebo (adjusted risk ratio (RR), 1.51; $95\%$ confidence interval (CI), 0.97–2.36; $P{=}0.07$ ). Subgroup analyses showed that age greater than 67-year-old (adjusted RR, 2.18; $95\%$ CI 1.18–4.00), NIHSS greater than 16 (adjusted RR, 1.88; $95\%$ CI 1.06–3.34), and cardioembolism (adjusted RR, 3.73; $95\%$ CI 1.66–8.35) were associated with increased SICH risk.  

Conclusions  In patients with acute large vessel occlusion stroke, intravenous tirofiban before EVT within $^{24\mathrm{h}}$ of time from last known well is not associated with increased risk of SICH. Patients who are older, have more severe neurological deficits, or with cardioembolism are at higher risk of SICH with intravenous tirofiban.

# Introduction  

Endovascular thrombectomy (EVT) has been proven to be an effective treatment for improving neurological outcomes of acute ischemic stroke (AIS) patients with large vessel occlusion (LVO) [1]. However, EVT also results in some adverse events, such as intracranial hemorrhage (ICH), artery dissection, artery perforation, emboli to new territory, and vasospasm [2]. Symptomatic ICH (SICH) is the most worrisome of these complications, which may lead to significant neurological deterioration and increase mortality [3]. The prevalence of SICH ranges from 4 to $22\%$ [4, 5], which may be related to differences in the treatment protocol, study population, and criteria for the adjudication of SICH between studies. Several studies reported that older age, higher NIHSS and low ASPECTS at admission, poor collaterals, incomplete reperfusion, and periprocedural antiplatelet therapy with tirofiban are correlated with higher incidence of SICH [3, 6].  

Tirofiban, a highly selective glycoprotein IIb/IIIa inhibitor, blocks the binding of fibrinogen and the IIb/IIIa receptor, the common pathway of platelet aggregation. Recently, it has been increasingly used as an adjunctive medication of EVT in treating patients with AIS secondary to LVO. Nevertheless, the role of tirofiban in AIS-LVO remains controversial [6–9]. Some studies showed that tirofiban decreased the risk of re-occlusion and improved clinical outcomes without promoting ICH [8, 9]. Other studies suggested that tirofiban was associated with a higher frequency of SICH and poor clinical outcomes [6, 7].  

Thus, we investigated the relationship of tirofiban with SICH using data from The Endovascular Treatment With versus Without Tirofiban for Patients with Large Vessel Occlusion Stroke (RESCUE BT) Trial [10], a multicenter, randomized, placebo-controlled, double-blind clinical trial.  



# Treatments  

All enrolled patients were randomized to treatment with intravenous tirofiban or placebo ( $0.9\%$ saline solution) before EVT. The dose of tirofiban or placebo was as follows: an intravenous bolus of $10\upmu\mathrm{g}$ per kilogram of body weight within the first $3\mathrm{min}$ followed by a continuous intravenous infusion of $0.15{\upmu}\mathrm{g}$ per kilogram of body weight per minute for up to $^{24\textrm{h}}$ . Meanwhile, endovascular procedures were prepared and initiated in all patients. The thrombectomy procedure included contact aspiration and/or stent retriever, intra-arterial medication, balloon angioplasty with or without stenting, or various combinations of these approaches, conducted by experienced neurointerventionalists. Intraarterial administration of tirofiban, alteplase, or urokinase was allowed, however, not recommended. For detailed treatment information, please refer to the trial protocol. [11]  

Intravenous heparin was allowed during thrombectomy. Subcutaneous heparin or low-molecular-weight heparin for deep vein thrombosis prophylaxis was permitted postprocedure. However, the use of other anticoagulants or antiplatelet agents was prohibited during the first $^{24\mathrm{~h~}}$ after randomization.  



# Methods  



# Study design and patient selection  

RESCUE BT, aiming to assess the efficacy and safety of intravenous tirofiban in AIS due to anterior circulation LVO who underwent EVT within $^{24\textrm{h}}$ of time since last known well, was an investigator-initiated, multicenter, double-blind, placebo-controlled, randomized trial conducted in China between October 10, 2018, and October 31, 2021 [10]. A total of 948 AIS patients with an occlusion of the internal carotid artery (ICA) or the first or second segment of middle cerebral artery (MCA) were randomly assigned in a 1:1 ratio to receive intravenous tirofiban or placebo treatment before EVT. Detailed patient selection criteria and results of RESCUE BT have been described previously [10, 11]. RESCUE BT was registered on the Chinese Clinical Trial Registry (http://www.chictr.0rg.cn; ChiCTR-INR-17014167). The trial protocol was previously published and approved by the medical ethics committees of Xinqiao Hospital and all participating hospitals [11]. Written informed consent was provided by each patient or their legal representative. All imaging data including computed tomography (CT), magnetic resonance imaging (MRI), and digital subtraction angiography (DSA) were adjudicated by an independent Imaging Core Laboratory who were unaware of the treatment assignment. All patients included in this study cohort were from the RESCUE BT trial and without missing data on ICH.  



# Intracranial hemorrhage  

The adjudication of ICH was determined by the Imaging Core Laboratory based on follow-up CT within $^{24\textrm{h}}$ after stopping tirofiban treatment. SICH was defined according to the Heidelberg Bleeding Classification [12]. SICH was diagnosed if the newly detected ICH correlated with any of the following conditions: (1) National Institutes of Health Stroke Scale (NIHSS) score increased more than 4 points than the NIHSS immediately before worsening; (2) NIHSS score increased more than 2 points in one category; (3) Deterioration led to intubation, hemicraniectomy, external ventricular drain placement or any other major interventions; (4) The neurological deterioration could not be explained by causes other than the observed ICH. Patients were divided into a SICH group and NO-SICH group according to whether they were diagnosed with SICH.  



# Statistical analysis  

A comparison of baseline characteristics, imaging data, and treatment profiles between both the tirofiban versus placebo group and SICH group versus NO-SICH group was conducted using the Mann–Whitney $U$ and chi-square test as appropriate. Multivariable Poisson regression was performed to identify independent predictors of SICH taking factors with marginal significance $\scriptstyle{T<0.10)}$ in univariable analysis into account. The association between tirofiban treatment and hemorrhage was analyzed using a Poisson regression model adjusting for age, serum glucose, onset-torandomization time, baseline NIHSS, ASPECTS, number of passes, balloon angioplasty, and location of occlusion. The same analyses were repeated after excluding patients who had procedure-related ICH. Moreover, analyses for hemorrhages were conducted both in the intent-to-treat and astreated population to evaluate for consistency of the results. The intent-to-treat population was defined as all randomized patients grouped according to the initial randomization assignment, regardless of the actual treatment they received. The as-treated population analyzed all randomized patients according to the treatment the patient actually received (regardless of the group initially assigned). The Wald chisquare test was used to examine the interaction between subgroups, tirofiban treatment and SICH. Unadjusted and adjusted risk ratios (RR) were reported with $95\%$ confidence interval (CI) to indicate precision. A Bonferroni adjustment was used for multiple comparison. Multiple imputation was performed for missing data of baseline variables included in the multivariable regression model. All tests were twosided with $P<0.05$ considered as statistical significance. All analyses were performed using SPSS 23.0 (IBM) and figure were drawn with Microsoft Excel 2019.  



# Results  



# Patients  

Among the 948 randomized patients, 3 patients were excluded due to missing data on ICH, and the remaining 945 patients were included in the present study. The median age of the 945 patients was 67 (interquartile range [IQR], 57–74) years; 390 patients $(41.3\%)$ were women, and the median baseline NIHSS was 16 (IQR, 12–19). Substantial reperfusion (defined as an Expanded Treatment in Cerebral Ischemia (eTICI) score of 2b, 2c or 3) at final angiogram was present in $91.6\%$ (866/945) of patients. At 90 days after EVT, 447 patients $(47.3\%)$ achieved functional independence (defined as a modified Rankin scale score of 0–2) and all-cause mortality was $17.4\%$ (164/945). Any ICH was observed in 296 patients $(31.3\%)$ . According to the Heidelberg hemorrhage criteria, 76 patients $(8.0\%)$ were diagnosed with SICH and 869 patients $(92.0\%)$ without SICH.  

Comparisons between both the tirofiban versus placebo group and SICH versus NO-SICH groups are shown in Table 1. Baseline characteristics, such as age, baseline NIHSS score, baseline international normalized ratio, history of anticoagulant, medical history, baseline ASPECTS, occlusion site, collateral circulation status (measured by the American Society of Interventional and Therapeutic  

Neuroradiology/Society of Interventional Radiology (ASITN/SIR)), and workflow metrics were well balanced between the tirofiban and placebo groups. Regarding EVT characteristics, the tirofiban group had fewer number of passes (1 versus 2; $P{=}0.03.$ ), lower proportions of balloon angioplasty $(22.3\%$ versus $28.6\%$ ; $P{=}0.03)$ and intra-arterial thrombolysis ( $1.7\%$ versus $4.4\%$ ; $P{=}0.02$ ) than the placebo group.  

Patients in the SICH group were older (70 versus 67 years; $P{=}0.04,$ ) and had higher baseline serum glucose (7.4 versus 6.8; $P{=}0.004,$ ) compared to the NO-SICH group. The median (IQR) baseline ASPECTS (7 (6–8) versus 8 (7–9); $P<0.001\$ ) and ASITN/SIR collateral circulation grade (2 (1–2) versus 2 (1–3); $P=0.005^{\circ},$ of patients in the SICH group were lower than those in the NO-SICH group. Compared with the NO-SICH group, patients in the SICH group had higher frequency of ICA occlusion $32.9\%$ versus $19.4\%$ ; $P{=}0.005^{}$ ) and lower rate of M1 MCA $48.7\%$ versus $66.2\%$ ; $P{=}0.002.$ ). There was no significant difference in the time course between the two groups, but the SICH group had numerically more thrombectomy passes compared to the NO-SICH group (2 versus 1; $P=0.09\$ ). Moreover, 11 of 76 patients with SICH and 230 of 869 patients without SICH were treated with balloon angioplasty ( $14.5\%$ versus $26.5\%$ ; $P=0.02\$ ), respectively. Patients with SICH had a lower proportion of substantial reperfusion than those who without SICH ( $82.9\%$ versus $92.4\%$ ; $P{=}0.004$ ). Compared with patients without SICH, patients with SICH had a significantly higher mortality at 90 days $(61.8\%$ versus $13.5\%$ ; $P<0.001\$ ), and a much lower proportion of functional independence $3.9\%$ versus $51.1\%$ ; $P<0.001\$ ). Figure 1 shows the distribution of the 90-day modified Rankin scale scores in the SICH and NO-SICH groups.  



# Predictors of symptomatic intracranial hemorrhage  

The unadjusted and adjusted risk ratio of SICH by tirofiban treatment, baseline characteristics, and treatment profiles are presented in Table 2. Variables with $P$ -values $<0.10$ in univariate analysis were included in multivariate analysis. On the multivariable Poisson regression analysis, after adjusting for these variables, increasing age (adjusted RR, 1.02; $95\%$ CI 1.00–1.03; $P=0.05$ ), and higher baseline serum glucose (adjusted RR, 1.06; $95\%$ CI 1.02–1.11; $P{=}0.01$ ) were associated with SICH. Conversely, a higher baseline ASPECTS (adjusted RR, 0.84; $95\%$ CI 0.76–0.94; $P{=}0.002,$ ), MCA M1 occlusion (adjusted RR, 0.60; $95\%$ CI 0.36–0.99; $P{=}0.048,$ ), and substantial reperfusion (adjusted RR, 0.53; $95\%$ CI $0.28\mathrm{-}1.00$ ; $P=0.05$ ) were associated with a decreased risk of SICH. Multivariable analysis did not identify tirofiban treatment (adjusted RR, 1.51; $95\%$ CI 0.97–2.36; $P=0.07$ ) as an independent predictor of SICH.

# Tirofiban and hemorrhage  

Tirofiban treatment caused a significant higher frequency of any ICH $(34.8\%$ versus $28.0\%$ ; adjusted RR, 1.25; $95\%$ CI $1.04{-}1.50\$ ; $P{=}0.02,$ ). After excluding patients with procedure-related ICH, however, tirofiban treatment was not associated with increased risk of any ICH (adjusted RR, 1.20; $95\%$ CI 0.99–1.46; $P{=}0.07$ ; Table 3). The tirofiban group demonstrated a trend toward an increased proportion of SICH, fatal SICH, or non-intracranial hemorrhage, although this was not significant. (Table 3). In the as-treated analysis, tirofiban treatment was associated with a higher risk of SICH (adjusted RR, 1.58; $95\%$ CI 1.01–2.45; $P{=}0.04$ ; Table 3).

# Discussion  

Using data from the multicenter RESCUE BT double-blind, randomized placebo-controlled trial, we investigated the association between tirofiban treatment and ICH in patients with acute ischemic stroke secondary to anterior large vessel occlusion who underwent endovascular thrombectomy within $^{24\textrm{h}}$ of time last known well. Our results suggested that intravenous tirofiban did not significantly increase the risk of SICH, however, it increased the risk of any ICH after adjusting for potential confounding factors. A modification of tirofiban treatment effect by age, baseline NIHSS, and stroke etiology was observed in subgroup analysis of SICH. Consistent with prior studies [3, 13], increased age, higher baseline serum glucose, lower baseline ASPECTS, ICA occlusion, and failure of substantial reperfusion were independently associated with increased risk of SICH after endovascular procedure.  

Tirofiban is a fast-acting, non-peptide, highly selective glycoprotein platelet IIb/IIIa receptor inhibitor. Given its benefits in acute coronary syndromes [14–16], it has been increasingly used to treat acute ischemic stroke, particularly to prevent local platelet aggregation and early re-occlusion due to potential endothelium injury after mechanical thrombectomy, angioplasty and/or stenting. However, tirofiban has also been associated with an increased risk of ICH, one of the most common and worrisome complications after EVT, which often leads to unfavorable outcomes [3, 6, 7]. Our study showed that intravenous tirofiban may increase the risk of SICH, although it did not reach statistical significance. The higher risk of ICH in our study may be due to the higher dose or the longer duration of tirofiban treatment used. Higher doses of tirofiban are known to increase the risk of SICH or ICH compared to lower doses [7]. For instance, in our trial, tirofiban was administrated intravenously as a bolus dose of $10\upmu\mathrm{g/kg}$ followed by a continuous infusion at a rate of $0.15\upmu{\mathrm{g/kg/min}}$ for up to $^{241}$ , similar to what is commonly used in percutaneous coronary intervention [17]. Although the same maintenance dose of tirofiban $(0.15~{\upmu\mathrm{g}}/{\mathrm{kg}}/{\mathrm{min}})$ was used in Pan et al.’s study, their rates of ICH were lower ( $6.1\%$ rate of SICH and $12\%$ rate of ICH) compared to our study. This may be due to the longer treatment duration in our study $(16{-}24\mathrm{~h~})$ , and that there was no bolus loading dose in Pan et al.’s study [8]. The use of a lower dose of tirofiban in another study [18] (bolus dose of $0.25{-}0.5\mathrm{mg}$ followed by a continuous infusion of 0.2 to $0.4\mathrm{mg/h}$ for $12{-}24\mathrm{h})$ was also associated with lower rates of SICH $(6.9\%)$ and ICH $(29.7\%)$ compared to our study. Therefore, the tirofiban dosing regimen used in this current study may not be safe. Nevertheless, the optimal dosing and protocol administration of tirofiban as an adjuvant to endovascular treatment for acute large vessel occlusive stroke needs to be further investigated.  

MR CLEAN-MED (Safety and efficacy of aspirin, unfractionated heparin, both, or neither during endovascular stroke treatment) was an open-label randomized controlled trial and was stopped early because periprocedural administration of aspirin or unfractionated heparin was associated with an increased risk of SICH with no evidence for a beneficial effect on functional outcome. These findings were similar with ours. However, the incidence of SICH in the aspirin or unfractionated heparin group of MR CLEAN-MED was slightly higher than that in the tirofiban group in our trial (aspirin vs unfractionated heparin vs tirofiban: $14\%$ vs $13\%$ vs $9.7\%$ ) due to different patient selection criteria and the action mechanism of the investigational medication (aspirin and unfractionated heparin vs tirofiban) [19]. In the present study, the overall incidence of SICH was $8.0\%$ , which is comparable to the DEFUSE-3 (Endovascular Therapy Following Imaging Evaluation for Ischemic Stroke, $6.5\%$ ) [20] and DAWN (DWI or CTP Assessment With Clinical Mismatch in the Triage of Wake-Up and Late Presenting Strokes Undergoing Neurointervention With Trevo, $5.6\%$ ) [21] randomized controlled trials, and slightly higher than that in ESCAPE-NA1 (Efficacy and safety of nerinetide for the treatment of acute ischemic stroke; $3.9\%$ ) trials [22]. Despite using non-advanced imaging in our study, our results are comparable to DAWN and DEFUSE-3 trial that used advanced imaging. The ESCAPE-NA1 trial used the same strategy as the RESCUE BT trial to enroll subjects. In addition, the ESCAPE-NA1 trial also used multiphase CT angiography to randomize patients with moderate-good collateral circulation. This has been shown that favorable collateral circulation is associated with a lower incidence of SICH in AIS patients [3].

The mechanism of stroke was noted to be a factor in explaining higher SICH risk. Although the baseline international normalized ratio and history of anticoagulant usage were well balanced between the tirofiban and placebo groups, subgroup analysis revealed that patients with cardioembolic stroke treated with tirofiban had an increased risk of SICH compared with placebo. A plausible explanation is that patients with cardioembolic stroke usually have a small penumbra and large core infarction due to sudden occlusion of the arteries [23], resulting in a higher risk of SICH, compared with patients with large artery atherosclerosis occlusion related stroke in which the gradual development of stenosis may permit the development of collaterals over time and relatively smaller core infarction in the event of LVO [24]. In the present study, $42.9\%$ of patients with cardioembolic stroke contributed to one third (26/76) of the SICH group, which may have offset the potential benefit of tirofiban antiplatelet therapy.  

To our knowledge, this is the largest study on the association of intravenous tirofiban with intracranial hemorrhage. The strength of this study is that its data were from a national multicenter, randomized, placebo-controlled, double-blind trial with well-balanced baseline characteristics and few missing data. Furthermore, ICH was adjudicated by an independent Imaging Core Laboratory that was blind to randomization and treatment information. Our study has limitations. First, one dose regime of tirofiban was investigated in this study. The efficacy and safety of other doses merit evaluation in future studies. The efficacy and safety of intra-arterial tirofiban treatment are also undetermined, as the protocol of this study did not recommend the use of tirofiban via this route. Second, this study was designed to recruit patients with large vessel occlusion stroke. Therefore, the results may not be suitable for stroke patients without large vessel occlusion. Lastly, all the patients in the present study were Chinese with a high prevalence of intracranial atherosclerosis, and the findings may not be generalizable to other populations.  

In conclusion, intravenous tirofiban treatment was not associated with an increased incidence of SICH in acute ischemic stroke patients with large vessel occlusion who received endovascular thrombectomy within $^{24\textrm{h}}$ of time since last known well. Older age, higher baseline serum glucose, lower baseline ASPECTS, higher NIHSS, ICA occlusion, failure of substantial reperfusion, and stroke etiology of cardioembolism may increase the risk of SICH. Tirofiban might not be suitable in these patients. More studies are needed to assess the safety and efficacy of lowdose tirofiban in acute ischemic stroke.
