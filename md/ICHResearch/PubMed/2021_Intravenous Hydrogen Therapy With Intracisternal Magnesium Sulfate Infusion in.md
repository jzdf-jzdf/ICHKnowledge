# CLINICAL TRIAL  

# Intravenous Hydrogen Therapy With Intracisternal Magnesium Sulfate Infusion in Severe Aneurysmal Subarachnoid Hemorrhage  

Satoru Takeuchi , MD, PhD; Kosuke Kumagai , MD, PhD; Terushige Toyooka, MD, PhD; Naoki Otani, MD, PhD; Kojiro Wada, MD, PhD; Kentaro Mori, MD, PhD  

BACKGROUND AND PURPOSE: Poor-grade subarachnoid hemorrhage still has a poor prognosis. This randomized controlled clinical trial evaluated intracisternal magnesium sulfate infusion combined with intravenous hydrogen therapy in patients with poor-grade subarachnoid hemorrhage.  

METHODS: Thirty-seven patients with poor-grade subarachnoid hemorrhage were randomized to $\mathsf{Mg}+\mathsf{H}_{2},$ Mg, and control groups. Mg and $\mathsf{Mg+H}_{2}$ groups received intracisternal magnesium sulfate infusion $(2.5\mathrm{mmol/L})$ at 20 mL/h for 14 days. $\mathsf{Mg}+\mathsf{H}_{2}$ group also received intravenous hydrogen-rich solution infusion for 14 days. Primary outcome measures were occurrence of delayed cerebral ischemia and cerebral vasospasm. Secondary outcome measures were modified Rankin Scale and Karnofsky performance status at 3 and 12 months, Barthel index at 12 months, and serum and cerebrospinal fluid malondialdehyde and neuron-specific enolase.  

RESULTS: Serum neuron-specific enolase levels were significantly lower in the $\mathsf{Mg}+\mathsf{H}_{2}$ group from days 3 to 14 than in the control group. Cerebrospinal fluid neuron-specific enolase levels were also significantly lower in the $\mathsf{Mg}+\mathsf{H}_{2}$ group from days 3 to 7 than in the control group. Incidences of cerebral vasospasm and delayed cerebral ischemia were significantly higher in the control group than in other groups. Modified Rankin Scale and Karnofsky performance status did not significantly differ between the three groups at 3 months. Modified Rankin Scale scores 0 to 2 were more common in the $\mathsf{Mg}$ and $\mathsf{Mg}+\mathsf{H}_{2}$ groups at 1 year. Barthel index was higher in the $\mathsf{Mg}+\mathsf{H}_{2}$ group than in the control group.  

CONCLUSIONS: Intracisternal magnesium sulfate infusion started immediately after surgery reduces the incidence of cerebral vasospasm and delayed cerebral ischemia and improves clinical outcomes without complications in patients with poor-grade subarachnoid hemorrhage. Intracisternal magnesium sulfate infusion combined with intravenous hydrogen therapy decreases serum malondialdehyde and neuron-specific enolase and improves Barthel index, indicating hydrogen has additional effects.

Patients with poor-grade SAH have higher risk of early brain injury (EBI) and delayed cerebral ischemia (DCI), which are both associated with poor outcomes. EBI is the result of the acute consequences of the sudden increase of intracranial pressure associated with SAH, which can cause decreased cerebral perfusion and transient global cerebral ischemia during the first 72 hours after SAH.5 The major mechanisms of EBI are neuronal cell death and endothelial damage, which result in cytotoxic edema and blood-brain barrier breakdown.6 DCI is believed to result from multiple factors such as angiographic vasospasm, cortical spreading ischemia, microthrombosis, and microcirculation vasoconstriction.5 In addition, EBI may contribute to the development of DCI.7,8 Therefore, a new therapeutic strategy against both EBI and DCI is needed to improve the outcomes of poor-grade SAH.  

Increasing evidence has suggested that enhanced oxidative stress is involved in EBI following SAH.9–12 Recent experimental studies showed that hydrogen, which has antioxidant properties,13 can alleviate EBI after SAH via attenuation of neuronal apoptosis.14–17 Hydrogen has been reported to selectively reduce hydroxyl radicals and peroxynitrites, which are highly reactive oxygen species that react indiscriminately with nucleic acids, lipids, and proteins, resulting in DNA fragmentation, lipid peroxidation, and protein inactivation.13,18 Malondialdehyde (MDA)—the end product of lipid peroxidation of polyunsaturated fatty acids in cellular membranes—indirectly reflects damage to the cells and so is a reliable marker of oxidative stress. Serum MDA increases during the acute phase in patients with SAH,19 and hydrogen can decrease MDA in the brain cortex in the rabbit SAH model.14 In addition, hydrogen is highly diffusible and could potentially reach subcellular compartments, such as the mitochondria and nuclei, which are the primary sites of generation of reactive oxygen species and subsequent DNA damage.13,18  

Magnesium sulfate has several beneficial effects such as vasodilation of vessels and attenuation of neuronal death. However, the recent randomized controlled MASH-2 study (Magnesium for Aneurysmal Subarachnoid Haemorrhage 2) failed to confirm that intravenous magnesium sulfate infusion improves functional outcomes.20,21 The direct intracisternal magnesium sulfate infusion method can deliver an effective ${\mathsf{Mg}}^{2+}$ concentration in the cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) using only the minimum dosage of Mg but does not increase ${\mathsf{Mg}}^{2+}$ concentration in the serum. Recently, we have reported that intracisternal magnesium sulfate infusion can inhibit cerebral vasospasm (CV) in patients with SAH.22  

These findings suggest that combination therapy using intravenous hydrogen infusion and intracisternal magnesium sulfate infusion may be effective to treat both EBI and CV. We present the results of a randomized controlled clinical trial of intravenous hydrogen therapy combined with intracisternal magnesium sulfate infusion in patients with poor-grade SAH.  

# METHODS  

This study was a randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial conducted in the National Defense Medical College Hospital and included 3 arms: (1) intravenous hydrogen-rich solution infusion with intracisternal magnesium sulfate infusion $\left(\mathsf{Mg}+\mathsf{H}_{2}\right.$ group), (2) intracisternal magnesium sulfate infusion only (Mg group), and (3) placebo (control group). The protocol was approved by the Ethics Committee of the National Defense Medical College and was registered on the University Hospital Medical Information Network Clinical Trials Registry (https://www.umin. ac.jp/ctr/index.htm; unique identifier: UMIN000014696). The data that support the findings of this study are available from the corresponding author upon reasonable request.  

The study protocol and intervention description were reported previously.23 Briefly, inclusion criteria were age of 20 to 80 years, ruptured aneurysm, Hunt and Kosnik grade 4 or 5, aneurysm treated by surgical clipping or coiling within 72 hours after the onset, and written informed consent from the patient or family member. Exclusion criteria were severe brain edema, heart dysfunction, renal insufficiency, Fisher grade 4 with massive intracerebral hematoma, and rejection of randomization. Written informed consent was obtained from family members before inclusion in the study. Patients fulfilling the eligibility criteria were assigned to either the control group, Mg group, or $\mathsf{Mg}+\mathsf{H}_{2}$ group by stratified block randomization. Randomization was stratified by sex, age (20–60 years and ${>}60$ years), Hunt and Kosnik grade, and Fisher group. The allocation ratio was 1:1:1. During aneurysm clipping, a ventricular drainage tube was placed in the lateral ventricle, and a cisternal drainage tube was placed in the basal cistern. For aneurysm coiling, ventricular drainage was performed before coiling, and a cisternal drainage tube was placed through a supraorbital keyhole minicraniotomy after coiling.24 A spinal drainage tube was placed in the lumbar spine immediately after the clipping or coiling procedure. Fasudil hydrochloride (90 mg/day, 14 days) was administered but not triple-H (hypertensive-hypervolemichemodilution) therapy.  

Patients assigned to the $\mathsf{Mg}+\mathsf{H}_{2}$ group or the Mg group received intracisternal magnesium sulfate infusion. Continuous infusion of $2.5\mathrm{~mmol/L}$ magnesium sulfate in Ringer solution was administered at 20 mL/h from days 1 to 14. Irrigation was performed mainly through the cisternal to spinal drainage. The cisternal drainage tube and the pressure control system at 20 cm $\mathsf{H}_{2}\mathsf{O}$ was connected by a T-connector for safe irrigation to avoid excessive infusion. The ventricular drainage was set at a pressure of $10~\mathrm{cm}\ H_{2}\mathrm{O}$ as a safety outlet in case of unexpected increase in the intracranial pressure. The level of the spinal drainage was adjusted according to the drainage volume, which was recorded hourly. Patients assigned to the control group received continuous infusion of only Ringer solution at 20 mL/h from days 1 to 14. Patients assigned to the $\mathsf{Mg}+\mathsf{H}_{2}$ group received intravenous hydrogen infusion immediately after randomization (within 1 hour after admission). Hydrogenrich solution was produced using a nondestructive hydrogen diffusion apparatus (MiZ, Co, Ltd, Kamakura, Kanagawa, Japan) as reported before.25 Bags of glucose-electrolyte solution (SOLDEM1 200 mL; Terumo Corp, Tokyo, Japan) were immersed, without opening or altering the bag, in a water tank in which water was electrolyzed periodically to produce water with hydrogen concentrations of up to 1.6 ppm. The concentration of hydrogen in the bag reaches saturation, increasing to ${>}1.0$ ppm, because of diffusion through the wall of the bag. Intravenous hydrogen solution $\mathrm{\'{200mL}}$ ) was administered at 200 mL/h BID for 14 days. Patients assigned to the control group and the Mg group received glucose-electrolyte solution (SOLDEM1 $200\mathrm{mL}$ ) without added hydrogen at $200\mathrm{mL/h}$ BID for 14 days.  

Head computed tomography was performed on admission and at 3, 7, 10, and 14 days after surgery to assess the cerebral infarction related to DCI and complications including rebleeding and $\approx1$ and 3 months after surgery to assess chronic hydrocephalus, which was diagnosed if clinical deterioration with no detectable causes other than hydrocephalus occurred after day 14 and the ventricular size progressively increased with Evans Index ${>}0.30$ . All patients with chronic hydrocephalus underwent shunt surgeries. Head computed tomography angiography was performed on admission and at 7 days after surgery. Head computed tomography perfusion imaging was performed at 7 days after surgery. Cerebral digital subtraction angiography was performed on admission, and follow-up digital subtraction angiography was performed at 7 to 10 days after surgery or within 8 hours after the onset of DCI to assess angiographic CV. Transcranial Doppler imaging was performed daily until 14 days after surgery.  

# Study End Points  

Outcome measures were evaluated by 2 physicians who were unaware of study-group assignments and were not involved in the care of the patients. Primary outcome measures were occurrence of DCI and CV. DCI was defined as new focal neurological deficits (motor or speech deficits) that developed after SAH or decrease in Glasgow Coma Scale score of ${}^{\ge2}$ points for ${>}6$ hours.26,27 The deficit could not be present immediately after aneurysm treatment and could not be attributed to other causes including rebleeding, hyponatremia, and infection.26,27 CV was defined as angiographic vasospasm manifesting as moderate-to-severe arterial narrowing on computed tomography angiography or digital subtraction angiography not attributable to atherosclerosis, catheter-induced spasm, or vessel hypoplasia and transcranial Doppler vasospasm defined as a mean flow velocity in the M1 of $>120~\mathsf{cm}/\mathsf{s}.$ 28 Secondary outcome measures were modified Rankin Scale score and  

Karnofsky performance status at 3 and 12 months, Barthel index at 12 months, and the biochemical markers MDA and neuron-specific enolase (NSE) in serum and CSF.  

# Statistical Analysis  

Statistical analysis was performed using commercially available software (SPSS Statistics, version 22.0; IBM, Armonk, NY). Data are expressed as mean±SD. Differences between the three groups were assessed using univariate analysis with the $\chi^{2}$ test for categorical variables and the 1-way ANOVA followed by Tukey test for continuous variables, with $R{<}0.05$ considered to be statistically significant.  

# RESULTS  

A total of 151 patients with SAH caused by a ruptured aneurysm were treated at the National Defense Medical College Hospital from January 2015 to December 2018. This study enrolled 39 eligible patients, but 2 patients were later excluded because of rerupture before aneurysm treatment in 1 case in the Mg group and injection failure of the magnesium sulfate solution in 1 case in the $\mathsf{Mg}+\mathsf{H}_{2}$ group. The clinical data of the remaining 37 patients (13 patients in the control group, 12 patients in the Mg group, and 12 patients in the $\mathsf{Mg}+\mathsf{H}_{2}$ group) are summarized in Table 1. No significant differences were detected between the three groups in age, sex, comorbidities, current smoking, family history of SAH, outpatient medications, Hunt and Kosnik grade, Fisher grade, aneurysm location, and treatment. The mean daily volume was $468.3~\mathrm{mL}$ from the spinal drainage, $64.9\mathrm{mL}$ from the ventricular drainage, and $22.7~\mathrm{mL}$ from the cisternal drainage.  

Serum and CSF magnesium levels at baseline (day 1) and during the course of treatment (days 2–14) are shown in Table  2. CSF magnesium levels during treatment were significantly higher in the Mg and $\mathsf{Mg}+\mathsf{H}_{2}$ groups than in the control group. The temporal changes of serum MDA concentrations are shown in Table  3. Serum MDA concentrations were significantly lower in the $\mathsf{Mg+H}_{2}$ group from days 3 to 14 than in the control group and from days 3 to 7 than in the Mg group. The temporal changes of serum and CSF NSE levels are shown in Table 4. Serum NSE levels were significantly lower in the $\mathsf{Mg}+\mathsf{H}_{2}$ group from days 3 to 14 than in the control group. CSF NSE levels were also significantly lower in the $\mathsf{Mg}+\mathsf{H}_{2}$ group from days 3 to 7 than in the control group.  

Incidences of CV and DCI are shown in Table 5. Both angiographic and transcranial Doppler examinations for CV after surgery were performed in all patients. Incidence of CV was significantly higher in the control group $(62\%)$ than in the Mg group $(8\%)$ and $\mathsf{Mg+H}_{2}$ group $(25\%)$ . Incidence of DCI was also significantly higher in the control group $(54\%)$ than in the Mg group $(8\%)$ and $\mathsf{Mg}+\mathsf{H}_{2}$ group $(17\%)$ .

No complication associated with cisternal infusion with lumbar spinal drainage, such as brain herniation, was observed in any patient in the three groups. No complications associated with magnesium sulfate administration, such as bradycardia or hypotension, were observed in the Mg and $\mathsf{Mg}+\mathsf{H}_{2}$ group patients. No complications associated with hydrogen administration, such as heart failure and diarrhea, were observed in the $\mathsf{Mg}+\mathsf{H}_{2}$ group. Rerupture occurred in 1 patient in the $\mathsf{Mg}+\mathsf{H}_{2}$ group. Meningitis occurred in 1 patient in the control group, 2 in the Mg group, and 1 in the $\mathsf{Mg}+\mathsf{H}_{2}$ group. There were no significant differences between the three groups regarding the occurrence of postoperative complications, rerupture, meningitis, and hydrocephalus.  

The clinical outcomes at the 3-month and 1-year follow-up examination are shown in Table  6. Modified Rankin Scale and Karnofsky performance status did not significantly differ between the three groups at 3 months. Modified Rankin Scale scores 0 to 2 were more common in the Mg group $(67\%)$ and $\mathsf{Mg}+\mathsf{H}_{2}$ group $(75\%)$ than in the control group $(23\%)$ at 1 year. Barthel index was higher in the $\mathsf{Mg}+\mathsf{H}_{2}$ group than in the control group.  

# DISCUSSION  

The results of this study suggest that intracisternal magnesium sulfate infusion started immediately after surgery can reduce the incidence of both CV and DCI and improve clinical outcomes without complications in patients with poor-grade SAH. In addition, intracisternal magnesium sulfate infusion combined with intravenous hydrogen therapy can further decrease serum MDA and NSE and improve Barthel index at 1 year, indicating that hydrogen therapy may have additional effects.

Magnesium sulfate has several beneficial effects such as vasodilation of vessels and attenuation of neuronal death. The mechanisms of these effects include blockage of voltage-dependent calcium channels, inhibition of excitatory glutamate release, and interference with N-methyl-D-aspartate-glutamate receptors.29 However, the recently conducted randomized controlled MASH-2 study failed to confirm that intravenous magnesium sulfate infusion improves functional outcomes despite such vasodilatory effects.20,21 Update of a meta-analysis of 7 randomized controlled trials found intravenous magnesium infusion did not improve clinical outcome after SAH. The neuroprotective effects of magnesium depend on reaching adequate CSF levels. However, intravenous magnesium infusion produces only limited increases in CSF levels. Conversely, serious adverse events, such as bradycardia and hypotension, occur mostly at serum magnesium levels ${>}2$ mmol/L.30 In addition, recent reports have suggested the importance of treatments that focus on EBI during the first 72 hours after SAH, and magnesium can also affect EBI. However, time from onset to intravenous magnesium administration was ${\approx}30$ to 40 hours in most randomized controlled trials, and more time may be required to achieve significant CSF magnesium levels after infusion.31,32  

Our previous randomized controlled trial started intracisternal magnesium sulfate infusion on day 4 after the ictus, which significantly inhibited CV in patients with SAH but failed to reduce either the incidence of DCI or improve the functional outcomes in patients with SAH.22 The present randomized controlled trial started intracisternal magnesium sulfate infusion immediately after surgery, leading to beneficial effects on functional outcome through the prevention of DCI, as well as CV. We consider that this treatment protocol has the potential to resolve the problem of time latency before effective CSF magnesium sulfate levels are reached.  

Intravenous hydrogen therapy with intracisternal magnesium sulfate infusion reduced serum MDA more than intracisternal magnesium sulfate infusion only, which indicates that hydrogen could ameliorate reactive oxygen-mediated oxidative damage. Several experimental studies have suggested that enhanced oxidative stress is involved in EBI following SAH.9–12 Intravenous hydrogen administration with intracisternal magnesium sulfate infusion showed the same functional outcome for modified Rankin Scale and Karnofsky performance status as intracisternal magnesium sulfate infusion only but also improved Barthel index at 1 year. We consider that the improvement in Barthel index may reflect some additional beneficial effect against EBI through suppression of oxidative stress. In fact, intravenous hydrogen administration with intracisternal magnesium sulfate infusion reduced NSE levels in the serum and CSF, which can provide information about the extent of neurological injury. Rat experimental studies reported that hydrogen gas inhalation can maintain high hydrogen concentrations in the brain for longer than intravenous hydrogenrich solution administration.33 However, the present study used intravenous administration because hydrogen gas inhalation could not be applied for humans at that time. Now, hydrogen gas inhalation is approved for several conditions.34 We consider that hydrogen gas inhalation may achieve beneficial effects other than improvement of Barthel index. Further studies are required on this point.

Our previous study used intracisternal infusion of 5 mmol/L magnesium sulfate,19 but the present study adopted 2.5 mmol/L for the concentration of magnesium sulfate solution to maintain the beneficial effects and minimize any side effects because our recent experimental study showed that intracisternal infusion of 2.5 mmol/L (5 mEq/L) magnesium sulfate could prevent CV after SAH.35 In addition, the present dose regimen of hydrogen-rich solution was based on our previous study, in which 38 patients with acute ischemic stroke received intravenous infusion of hydrogen-rich solution and adverse events included diarrhea in 1 patient $(2.6\%)$ ) and heart failure in 1 $(2.6\%)$ ).25 Heart failure was considered to result from volume overload, so that patients with heart dysfunction and renal insufficiency were excluded in the present study. The present treatment regimen caused no complications associated with magnesium and hydrogen therapy, indicating that our therapeutic protocol is safe.

# Limitations  

Selection bias was caused by our exclusion of patients with SAH of Fisher grade 4, Hunt and Kosnik grades I to III, or severe surgical complications and should be considered in the interpretation of our results. The present study was a single-center trial, with too few cases to evaluate the functional outcome in SAH attributed to multifactorial etiologies. Fortunately, no significant differences were found between the three groups regarding aneurysm location, although randomization should have also considered location because ruptured posterior circulation aneurysms are associated with a lower incidence of DCI. The relatively high incidence of aneurysm trapping and proximal ligation was due to the high incidence of posterior circulation aneurysm (vertebral artery dissection aneurysm), which would affect outcome. This small sample prevented investigation of the effect of intravenous hydrogen therapy only and might explain the finding of no beneficial clinical outcome at 3 months. The Barthel index was not assessed at 3 months because we adopted it later. Hydrogen is reported to be safe, although potential drug-drug interactions between hydrogen and magnesium sulfate were not assessed. We could not clarify whether Mg and $\mathsf{H}_{2}$ had more effect than lumbar spinal drainage and administration of intravenous fasudil, both of which are considered to be beneficial in SAH, because no group was treated without lumbar spinal drainage and fasudil. Nimodipine has been the standard of care in other countries including the United States, but no patients received it in the present study because this agent is not approved for use in Japan. Conversely, fasudil is approved for human use in Japan and China but not in the United States and Europe. Therefore, further large studies are required to investigate whether our protocol can become standard therapeutic strategy.

# Conclusions  

The results of this study suggest that intracisternal magnesium sulfate infusion started immediately after surgery can reduce the incidence of both CV and DCI and improve clinical outcomes without complications in patients with high-grade SAH. Intracisternal magnesium sulfate infusion combined with intravenous hydrogen therapy decreases serum MDA and NSE and improves Barthel index at 1 year, indicating that hydrogen therapy may have some additional effect.
