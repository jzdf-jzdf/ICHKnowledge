# Effects of clazosentan on cerebral vasospasm–related morbidity and all-cause mortality after aneurysmal subarachnoid hemorrhage: two randomized phase 3 trials in Japanese patients  

Hidenori Endo, MD,1,2 Yasushi Hagihara, MD,3 Naoto Kimura, MD,4 Katsumi Takizawa, MD,5 Kuniyasu Niizuma, MD,1,6–8 Osamu Togo, MSc,9 and Teiji Tominaga, $MD^{1,8}$  

1Department of Neurosurgery, Tohoku University Graduate School of Medicine, Miyagi; 2Department of Neurosurgery, Kohnan Hospital, Miyagi; 3Department of Neurosurgery, Rinku General Medical Center, Osaka; 4Department of Neurosurgery, Iwate Prefectural Central Hospital, Morioka; 5Department of Neurosurgery, Japanese Red Cross Asahikawa Hospital, Hokkaido; 6Department of Neurosurgical Engineering and Translational Neuroscience, Graduate School of Biomedical Engineering, Tohoku University, Miyagi; 7Department of Neurosurgical Engineering and Translational Neuroscience, Tohoku University Graduate School of Medicine, Miyagi; 8Research Division of Advanced Diagnosis and Treatment for Subarachnoid Hemorrhage, Tohoku University Hospital, Miyagi; and 9Idorsia Pharmaceuticals Japan Ltd., Tokyo, Japan  

OBJECTIVE  Clazosentan has been investigated globally for the prevention of cerebral vasospasm after aneurysmal subarachnoid hemorrhage (aSAH). The authors evaluated its effects on vasospasm-related morbidity and all-cause mortality following aSAH in Japanese patients.  

METHODS  Two similar double-blind, placebo-controlled phase 3 studies were conducted in 57 Japanese centers in patients with aSAH, after aneurysms were secured by endovascular coiling in one study and surgical clipping in the other. In each study, patients were randomly administered intravenous clazosentan $(10\mathsf{mg}/\mathsf{hr})$ or placebo (1:1) starting within 48 hours of aSAH and for up to 15 days after aSAH. Stratified randomization based on World Federation of Neurosurgical Societies grade was performed using a centralized interactive web response system. Vasospasm-related morbidity and all-cause mortality within 6 weeks post-aSAH, including new cerebral infarcts and delayed ischemic neurological deficits as well as all-cause mortality, were the first primary endpoint in each study. The second primary endpoint was all-cause morbidity (new cerebral infarct or delayed ischemic neurological deficit from any causes) and all-cause mortality (all-cause morbidity/mortality) within 6 weeks post-aSAH. The incidence of individual components of the primary morbidity/mortality endpoints within 6 weeks and patient outcome at 12 weeks post-aSAH (including the modified Rankin Scale scores) were also evaluated. The above analyses were also performed in the population pooled from both studies.  

RESULTS  In each study, 221 patients were randomized and 220 were included in the full analysis set of the primary analysis (109 in each clazosentan group, 111 in each placebo group). Clazosentan significantly reduced the incidence of vasospasm-related morbidity and all-cause mortality after aneurysm coiling (from $28.8\%$ to $13.6\%$ ; relative risk reduction $53\%$ ; $95\%$ CI $17\%{-}73\%)$ and after clipping (from $39.6\%$ to $16.2\%$ ; relative risk reduction $59\%$ ; $95\%$ CI $33\%{-}75\%)$ . All-cause morbidity/mortality and poor outcome (dichotomized modified Rankin Scale scores) were significantly reduced by clazosentan after preplanned study pooling. Treatment-emergent adverse events were similar to those reported previously.  

CONCLUSIONS  Clazosentan significantly reduced the combined incidence of vasospasm-related morbidity and allcause mortality post-aSAH with no unexpected safety findings.

neurysmal subarachnoid hemorrhage (aSAH) is a A sudden, life-threatening condition caused by the rupture of an aneurysm.1,2 An urgent operation involving endovascular coil embolization or microscopic clipping is required to prevent rerupture.1 Clot hemolysis and the release of vasoconstricting agents can contribute to cerebral vasospasm, which typically begins approximately 3 days after aSAH onset, peaks in severity at days 8–11, and resolves by day 21.3 If untreated, cerebral vasospasm can be a key factor of morbidity and mortality in patients with aSAH.1 Cerebral vasospasm may lead to delayed ischemic neurological deficit (DIND) in $17\%-40\%$ of aSAH cases, and half of the patients with vasospasm develop cerebral infarction.2 With an occurrence of 22.5 per 100,000 person-years, aSAH is 2–3 times more frequent in Japan than in the rest of the world4 and is therefore a significant problem in this country.  

The calcium channel blocker nimodipine (oral/intravenous) has been the standard treatment in the United States and in Europe for improving neurological outcomes after aSAH, but there is no convincing evidence that it affects the incidence of vasospasm or DIND.1 In Japan, intravenous fasudil hydrochloride, a Rho-kinase inhibitor, and intravenous ozagrel sodium, a thromboxane A2 synthase inhibitor, have been prescribed to prevent vasospasm. However, these agents have been investigated in only a limited number of trials, and their effects on DIND and outcomes are not established.5 Recent studies have disputed several therapies,6 exemplifying the need for efficient and safe management of cerebral vasospasm and DIND.  

Endothelin-1 (ET-1), which has potent, long-lasting vasoconstrictor effects,7 is overproduced after aSAH and is a key contributor to cerebral vasospasm development.1 Clazosentan, an endothelin receptor antagonist (ERA), has been widely investigated for the prevention of cerebral vasospasm in patients with aSAH.8–11  

The Clazosentan to Overcome Neurological Ischemia and Infarction Occurring After Subarachnoid Hemorrhage (CONSCIOUS-1) study demonstrated a dose-dependent decrease in moderate or severe vasospasm, with up to a $65\%$ relative risk reduction (RRR) in patients with aSAH secured by aneurysm clipping or coiling.9 The subsequent CONSCIOUS-2 and CONSCIOUS-3 trials investigated the effect of clazosentan on the incidence of cerebral vasospasm-related morbidity and all-cause mortality.10,11 CONSCIOUS-2 showed that the $5{\cdot}\mathrm{mg/hr}$ dose of clazosentan did not produce a statistically significant treatment effect, resulting in the premature termination of CONSCIOUS-3. However, an exploratory analysis of CONSCIOUS-3 demonstrated a $44\%$ RRR in the occurrence of cerebral vasospasm–related morbidity and allcause mortality with the $15\mathrm{mg/hr}$ dose of clazosentan. In parallel, a phase 2 dose-finding study, conducted in Japanese and Korean patients with aSAH secured by surgical clipping, suggested that clazosentan at $10\mathrm{mg/hr}$ reduced the incidence of vasospasm-related morbidity and all-cause mortality events compared with placebo while maintaining the same tolerability as clazosentan at $5~\mathrm{mg/hr!}$ 12 We now present the results of two randomized, placebo-controlled pivotal phase 3 studies, which were designed to confirm the efficacy of clazosentan at a dose of $10\mathrm{mg/hr}$ in reducing vasospasm-related morbidity and all-cause mortality events and its safety in patients with aSAH secured by endovascular coiling in one study and surgical clipping in the other. These pivotal studies supported the approval of clazosentan in Japan.  



# Methods  



# Design  

Two prospective, multicenter, double-blind, randomized, placebo-controlled, pivotal phase 3 studies were conducted in parallel in 57 centers in Japan. These studies were registered with the Japan Clinical Trials Information database (https://www.clinicaltrials.jp) and their registration numbers are JapicCTI-163369 and JapicCTI-163368. Protocols were identical except for the securing intervention, which was either endovascular coiling (JapicCTI-163369; the “coiling study”) or surgical clipping (JapicCTI-163368; the “clipping study”) (Supplemental Fig. 1). Following a blinded review of the incidence of vasospasm-related morbidity and all-cause mortality events during the conduct of the study, the sample size was increased from 80 to 110 patients per group to maintain sufficient power (see Statistical Analyses section). Other changes implemented after the start of the studies included the addition of the incidence of moderate to severe angiographic vasospasm within 14 days post-aSAH as another efficacy endpoint.  

The studies were approved by the institutional review boards of each center and were conducted in accordance with Good Clinical Practice guidelines and the Declaration of Helsinki. Prior to study drug initiation, written informed consent was obtained from each patient or legal guardian.  



# Patient Population  

Eligible patients were Japanese adults between the ages of 20 and 75 years with aSAH due to a ruptured saccular aneurysm, which had been successfully secured by endovascular coiling or surgical clipping. The main inclusion criteria were Fisher grade 3 (the most severe grade of SAH) on preoperative CT, irrespective of the presence of an intracranial or intraventricular hematoma, and World Federation of Neurosurgical Societies (WFNS) grades I–IV.13,14 Women of childbearing potential were included only after a negative serum pregnancy test. The main exclusion criteria included moderate to severe cerebral vasospasm present on digital subtraction angiography (DSA) prior to the surgical intervention and a significant complication during the procedure, including new large cerebral infarct and new significant neurological deficits lasting 12 hours or more after the procedure. Concomitant use of intravenous fasudil hydrochloride hydrate, ozagrel sodium, milrinone, or nicardipine hydrochloride was not permitted from 4 hours before treatment start to the end of administration of the study drug. Inhibitors of organic anion transporting polypeptide 1B1 or 1B3 (i.e., OATP1B1 or OATP1B3) were not permitted before or during the study drug’s administration.  



# Interventions  

Patients were randomized (1:1) to receive $10\mathrm{mg/hr}$ of intravenous clazosentan or placebo starting within 48 hours after aSAH. The study drug was administered for up to 15 days after aSAH. The study drug was to be permanently discontinued in the case of safety concerns; use of forbidden concomitant medication; inability to follow patient management guidelines (see Appendix 1, Supplemental Data); occurrence of any adverse event (AE) considered by the investigator to require discontinuation; and lack of efficacy (defined as disease progression, treatment failure, or worsening of patient’s condition).  

Rescue therapy, such as triple-H therapy (hypervolemia, hemodilution, hypertension); hyperdynamic therapy; percutaneous angioplasty; intrathecal/intracisternal administration of nicardipine hydrochloride; or intraarterial administration of vasodilators (e.g., fasudil hydrochloride, papaverine hydrochloride, milrinone, ozagrel sodium) for up to 15 minutes was allowed at cerebral vasospasm onset per standard care. Rescue therapy involving intravenous vasodilator or antiplatelets required permanent discontinuation of the study drug.  



# Randomization and Masking  

Patient eligibility characteristics were entered in a centralized interactive web response system, and the central enrollment center generated a random allocation table. The study drug was allocated to clazosentan or placebo by stratified randomization in which a minimization method of dynamic allocation based on preprocedural WFNS grade was used as an allocation factor. Patients, investigators, medical experts, review committee members (except members from the Independent Data Monitoring Committee), study staff, contract research organization personnel, and sponsor employees were blinded to study treatment until study closure.  



# Assessments  

DSA and CT scans were obtained before the aneurysmsecuring procedure. DSA was performed at $9\pm2$ days post-aSAH, at rescue therapy implementation, or in case of symptoms suggesting DIND. CT scans were scheduled between 24 and 48 hours after the procedure, at 6 weeks after aSAH, at rescue therapy implementation, or in case of symptoms suggesting DIND. Neurological assessments based on the modified Glasgow Coma Scale (mGCS) and the abbreviated National Institutes of Health Stroke Scale (aNIHSS) (see Appendices 2 and 3, Supplemental Data) were performed within 1 hour before treatment start, every 6 hours up to the 15th day post-aSAH, within 1 hour after the end or discontinuation of study treatment, in case of symptoms suggesting DIND, and at 6 weeks post-aSAH. At 12 weeks post-aSAH, functional outcome was assessed with the Glasgow Outcome Scale–Extended (GOSE) and the modified Rankin Scale (mRS), and cognitive status was evaluated with the Mini-Mental State Examination (MMSE).  



# Committees  

The Imaging Review Committee comprised five radiology/neurosurgery specialists who evaluated the presence or absence and the severity of cerebral vasospasm and the incidence and cause of new cerebral infarcts by using DSA/CT images in a blinded manner. The severity of cerebral vasospasm was rated as “none to mild” or “moderate to severe” (i.e., inner artery reduction $\geq34\%$ ) based on DSA images.  

The Events Review Committee comprised three neurosurgery specialists who worked in a blinded manner to confirm that patients met the definition of DIND and that they needed rescue therapy. The Events Review Committee also evaluated the relationship between cerebral vasospasm and 4 items: death, new cerebral infarcts, DIND, and rescue therapy initiation.  

The Clot Size Assessment Committee comprised three radiology/neurosurgery specialists who evaluated the preprocedure clot size by assessing CT images in a blinded manner. Clots were defined as local (long axis $<20~\mathrm{mm}$ ) or diffuse (long axis $\geq20\mathrm{mm}$ or across both hemispheres) and as thin (short axis $\ge1\mathrm{mm}$ and $<4\mathrm{mm}$ ) or thick (short axis $\geq4~\mathrm{mm}$ ).  

The Independent Data Monitoring Committee comprised four cardiology/neurosurgery/biometry specialists, who were unblinded to monitor safety.  



# Endpoints  

The first primary efficacy endpoint was the proportion of patients with at least one vasospasm-related morbidity or one all-cause mortality event within 6 weeks post-aSAH, defined as vasospasm-related new cerebral infarcts, vasospasm-related DIND, and all-cause death. The second primary efficacy endpoint was the proportion of patients with at least one all-cause morbidity (new cerebral infarcts or DIND from any cause) or one all-cause mortality (all-cause morbidity/mortality) event within 6 weeks post-aSAH.  

DIND was defined as a decrease of 2 points or more on the mGCS or an increase of 2 points or more on the aNIHSS, lasting for 2 hours or more in line with expert consensus15,16 (see Appendices 2 and 3, Supplemental Data). If the neurological score (mGCS or aNIHSS) was not assessable, DIND was defined as cerebral vasospasm on DSA with rescue therapy initiation.  

The secondary endpoints were the occurrence of the individual components of the vasospasm-related and the all-cause morbidity/mortality endpoints within 6 weeks post-aSAH. Secondary endpoints also included clinical outcomes assessed by the GOSE, dichotomized as good $(>$ 4) and poor $(\leq4)$ outcomes, and by mRS, dichotomized as good $(<3)$ and poor $(\geq3)$ outcomes, along with cognitive function based on MMSE at 12 weeks post-aSAH. The incidence of moderate to severe angiographic vasospasm within 14 days post-aSAH was another efficacy endpoint. Moderate to severe cerebral vasospasm was defined as any inner artery reduction by $\geq34\%$ . Additionally, the proportion of patients receiving rescue therapy was evaluated.  

The safety endpoints included death up to 12 weeks post-aSAH and treatment-emergent AEs up to 30 days after the end or the premature discontinuation of study treatment.  



# Statistical Analyses  

The sample size was calculated based on the results reported for the vasospasm-related morbidity and all-cause mortality events in the previous phase 2 clinical study.12 To detect a significant difference between groups $90\%$ power at a 2-sided significance level of $5\%$ ), a sample size of 80 patients per group and per study was required. However, in a blinded review performed in 2018, the observed rate of events was lower than expected. Consequently, the sample size was reassessed, assuming the original RRR of $43\%$ (event rate of $6.2\%$ in the clazosentan group and $14.2\%$ in the placebo group): 110 patients per group were required to provide a power of at least $50\%$ at a 2-sided significance level of $5\%$ for the first primary endpoint.  

Two null hypotheses were established in each study: 1) the incidence of vasospasm-related morbidity and allcause mortality (first primary endpoint), and 2) the incidence of all-cause morbidity/mortality (second primary endpoint) in the clazosentan arm is not different from that in the placebo arm. These hypotheses were tested using the hierarchical closed testing procedure at 2-sided $5\%$ type I error as described elsewhere:17 the second null hypothesis was tested if the first null hypothesis was rejected.  

Efficacy analyses were based on assigned treatment and on the full analysis set, defined as all patients who started infusion with the study drug. Regarding the primary analysis, no imputation of missing data was performed, because missing data on morbidity/mortality events are unlikely to result from the efficacy or lack of efficacy of the study drug, and the propensity to have missing data can be assumed to be completely random. The primary endpoints were analyzed using the CochranMantel-Haenszel test stratified by WFNS grade prior to surgical intervention. The consistency of the treatment effect was evaluated on the primary endpoint for several subgroups based on WFNS grade, clot size, sex, age, and surgical procedure, and the results were displayed in forest plots. The secondary and exploratory endpoints were analyzed using the same statistical test as the primary endpoints. Fisher’s exact test was used to compare dichotomized variables (i.e., GOSE and mRS). The level of significance for statistical testing was $5\%$ . Safety analyses were based on the patients who started study drug infusion. The incidences of AEs were compared descriptively between treatment groups.  

Pooling of the data from both individual studies was anticipated in a separate statistical analysis plan prior to database lock. The same hierarchical testing procedure defined above for the primary endpoint hypothesis testing was considered in the pooled analysis. Efficacy and safety analyses performed in the individual studies were repeated for the pooled analysis. For the pooled primary efficacy analysis, WFNS grade prior to surgical intervention and individual study (i.e., coiling and clipping) were used as strata in the Cochran-Mantel-Haenszel test.  



# Results  



# Patient Disposition and Characteristics  

Overall, 442 patients were randomized (221/study) between December 2016 and June 2020 and followed up for 12 weeks. Two randomized patients withdrew before study drug administration (1/study), and 220 patients were included in the full analysis set of each study (109 in each clazosentan group, 111 in each placebo group) (Fig. 1).  

Patient demographic and disease characteristics before the aneurysm-securing procedure were similar across all clazosentan and placebo groups (Table 1). Two-thirds of the patients were women, and most $(>90\%)$ had an aneurysm located in the anterior circulation. More than $70\%$ of the patients had WFNS grade I or II, and approximately half had thick and diffuse clots.  



# Efficacy  

The median treatment duration was 12 days for the clazosentan- and placebo-treated groups in both studies (from treatment initiation occurring within 48 hours of aSAH until up to 15 days after aSAH). Reasons for premature treatment discontinuation are presented in Fig. 1. The first primary endpoint reporting vasospasm-related morbidity and all-cause mortality events within 6 weeks after aSAH occurred in $13.6\%$ (14 of 103) of the clazosentan-treated patients and in $28.8\%$ (32 of 111) of the placebo-treated patients (RRR $53\%$ ; $95\%$ CI $17\%-73\%$ ; $\mathrm{p}=$ 0.0055) in the coiling study (Fig. 2 upper panel). In the clipping study, the first primary endpoint occurred in $16.\dot{2}\%$ (17 of 105) of the clazosentan-treated patients and in $39.6\%$ (42 of 106) of the placebo-treated patients (RRR $59\%$ ; $95\%$ CI $33\%-75\%$ ; $\begin{array}{r}{\bar{\bfp}=0.0001,}\end{array}$ ). In the preplanned pooled analysis, the first primary endpoint occurred in ${\hat{1}}4.9\%$ (31 of 208) of the clazosentan-treated patients and in $34.1\%$ (74 of 217) of the placebo-treated patients (RRR $56\%$ ; $95\%$ CI $36\%-70\%$ ; $\overset{\vartriangle}{\mathbf{p}<}0.0001\$ ). Similar results were observed when the analyses were performed on the perprotocol population set.  

Given that the first primary endpoint was statistically significantly different between clazosentan and placebo, the second primary endpoint reporting all-cause morbidity/mortality events within 6 weeks after aSAH was tested at the $5\%$ level (Fig. 2 lower panel). All-cause morbidity/ mortality events occurred less frequently in clazosentantreated versus placebo-treated patients in both coiling $(33.0\%$ vs $41.4\%$ ; $\mathrm{p}=0.19)$ and clipping $45.7\%$ vs $57.5\%$ ; p $=0.088)$ studies. In the pooled analysis, all-cause morbidity/mortality events occurred in $39.4\%$ (82 of 208) of all clazosentan-treated patients and in $49.3\%$ (107 of 217) of all placebo-treated patients (RRR $20\%$ ; $95\%$ CI $1\%-36\%$ ; $\mathrm{p}=0.032)$ .

# Safety  

In the pooled safety analysis (Table 2), the incidence of treatment-emergent AEs was not different between the clazosentan group $(94.5\%)$ and the placebo group $(94.6\%)$ . In both groups, a similar number of deaths $1.4\%$ for clazosentan and $2.3\%$ for placebo) and AEs leading to the discontinuation of study drug $11.5\%$ and $11.7\%$ were observed. Treatment-emergent AEs occurred in $\geq5\%$ of the patients in the clazosentan group (Table 3), with a difference of $\geq2\%$ compared with placebo, included vomiting ( $7.3\%$ for clazosentan and $3.6\%$ for placebo) and signs of hemodilution or fluid retention, such as hyponatremia $(26.1\%,22.5\%)$ , hypoalbuminemia $(17.4\%$ , $12.6\%)$ , anemia ( $16.5\%$ , $9.5\%$ , pleural effusion $16.5\%$ , $3.6\%$ ), pulmonary edema $11.9\%$ , $4.5\%)$ , and brain edema $(6.0\%,\dot{2}.7\%)$ . The AE profile reported in the Japanese population in this study is in line with what has been observed previously in studies performed in Japan and globally, and no unexpected study drug–associated safety findings were reported.

# Discussion  

These studies confirm that treatment with clazosentan at a dose of $10~\mathrm{mg/hr}$ significantly reduces the combined incidence of vasospasm-related morbidity and allcause mortality, in Japanese patients with aSAH secured by endovascular coiling or surgical clipping. Morbidity and mortality events including new cerebral infarcts and DINDs are relevant clinical endpoints in patients with aSAH, because both are predictive of cognitive impairment and poor functional outcome.18–21 These endpoints were objectively evaluated by an independent committee, and their decreased occurrence provides evidence of the efficacy of clazosentan in preventing vasospasm-related ischemic events after aSAH.

Although many therapeutic approaches have been investigated for the prevention and treatment of cerebral vasospasm following aSAH,6,22–24 few treatments are recommended by current guidelines.5,6 Nimodipine prevents DIND, improves outcomes, and decreases mortality, but it has not shown a consistent effect on reducing cerebral vasospasm.6 In Japan, intravenous fasudil and ozagrel have been approved for the prevention of vasospasm5 after patients who received these treatments showed a reduced rate of angiographic and symptomatic vasospasm, but large randomized clinical trials are needed to confirm the benefit of these agents. For patients who fail to respond to medical management, the only currently available treatment option for reversal of established cerebral vasospasm is endovascular therapy, an invasive procedure consisting of balloon angioplasty and/or intraarterial administration of vasodilators.6 In this context, clazosentan is a promising new therapeutic approach supported by a strong worldwide development program that has consistently demonstrated a decrease in the occurrence of cerebral vasospasm after aSAH.9–12  

In the large, placebo-controlled studies reported here, clazosentan significantly reduces combined morbidity and mortality. Furthermore, clazosentan showed a consistent trend for improvement in clinical outcome (GOSE and mRS scores) at 12 weeks post-aSAH versus placebo. With the increased power gained by pooling the two studies, a significant treatment effect could be demonstrated on the reduction of poor outcome assessed by mRS, supporting the argument that a large number of patients would be needed to establish the effect of an antivasospastic drug on long-term outcome.18–21 These results suggest a pronounced improvement in clinical outcome with clazosentan despite a significantly decreased need for rescue therapy compared with placebo, whereas the higher use of rescue therapy in the placebo group may have contributed to improved outcomes in these patients.25

Previous studies suggest that poor WFNS grade (≥ III) and the presence of thick and diffuse subarachnoid blood before the securing procedure portend a worse clinical course and outcome in patients with aSAH,10,11,26 which was confirmed in the current studies. Despite this, clazosentan had a significant impact on vasospasm-related morbidity and all-cause mortality in the pooled analysis, independently of WFNS grade and presence of thick and diffuse clots.  

The treatment effect of clazosentan on all-cause morbidity/mortality events (the second primary endpoint) was maintained, although with a smaller effect size than that observed in vasospasm-related morbidity and all-cause mortality events. This suggests that the nonvasospastic components of morbidity and mortality, which are unlikely to be affected by clazosentan, did not compromise the overall benefit. The effect on all-cause morbidity/mortality events may have been driven by the significant decrease in vasospasm-related events, but non–vasospasm-related causes may have influenced the overall effect.  

The most common AEs in the pooled clazosentan group were pulmonary complications and hemoglobin decrease. These events have been reported in previous studies and are adequately managed in the hospital.9–12 Indeed, the rate of study drug discontinuation due to an AE was the same in both the clazosentan and placebo groups. These events have been suggested to be class effects of ERAs, with fluid retention and vascular leakage occurring more frequently with selective $\mathrm{ET_{A}}$ receptor antagonists than with dual ERAs.9,27–29 Anemia is more likely to be caused by plasma volume expansion, given that there is no evidence of hemolysis, bleeding, or bone marrow depression. The decrease in hemoglobin induced by clazosentan is typically reversible after drug discontinuation. Serious cardiovascular AEs suggestive of arrhythmia were not observed. No new safety concerns were identified.  

These studies were not individually powered to detect a treatment effect on clinical outcome at 12 weeks, yet a trend for improved outcome was seen in each study, and a significant reduction in poor outcome assessed by mRS could be demonstrated for clazosentan after the preplanned pooling of the two studies. The MMSE performed at 12 weeks did not indicate differences in cognitive outcomes between the study groups, which scored very highly, independently of treatment (median score of 29 or 30 on a scale of 0 [worst] to 30 [best]). This may be due to the MMSE not being sensitive enough to capture the subtle neuropsychological changes observed in patients with aSAH, or it may be due to the examination being difficult to perform in the more severely affected patients, thus leading to some missing assessments in this particular group. The ongoing phase 3 clinical trial known as the Prevention and Treatment of Vasospasm with Clazosentan trial (i.e., REACT; NCT03585270) will investigate changes in cognitive status using the Montreal Cognitive Assessment (MoCA test), which has been validated in patients with $\mathrm{aSAH^{30}}$ and may perform better in this population.31 The REACT study will also explore long-term health-related quality of life, while providing further evidence on the impact of clazosentan in a global population.  



# Conclusions  

These two prospective, multicenter, double-blind, randomized, placebo-controlled, pivotal phase 3 studies, which were conducted in Japanese patients after aSAH, support the efficacy of clazosentan to reduce the incidence of cerebral vasospasm and the combined incidence of vasospasm-related morbidity and all-cause mortality, which is a relevant clinical endpoint. In addition, the safety and tolerability of clazosentan were confirmed. Therefore, clazosentan has the potential to be an effective new treatment option for the prevention of cerebral vasospasm and delayed ischemic events in patients with aSAH.
