# Outcomes after Thrombectomy for Minor Stroke: A Meta-Analysis  

Xiao $\mathbf{}\pmb{W}\pmb{u}^{\eta}.$ , Mihir Khunte1, Sam Payabvash1, Chengcheng Zhu2, Alexandria Brackett3, Charles C. Matouk1, Dheeraj Gandhi4, Pina Sanelli5, Ajay Malhotra1  

- PURPOSE: To determine the outcomes after mechanical thrombectomy (MT) versus medical management in patients with minor stroke symptomatology.  

- METHODS: A meta-analysis was performed for studies reporting outcomes after MT, either as stand-alone therapy or with intravenous thrombolysis in patients with minor stroke and large-vessel occlusion.  

- RESULTS: Fourteen studies with 2134 patients met the selection criteria and were included. Two studies compared immediate thrombectomy versus best medical management (with rescue thrombectomy) and the odds ratios of excellent outcomes, good outcomes, mortality and incidence of symptomatic intracranial hemorrhage (sICH) after immediate thrombectomy versus best medical management were 1.07 $195\%$ confidence interval [CI] $0.93\substack{-1.22\%}$ ), 1.15 $(95\%$ CI 1.05e 1.25), 0.65 $(95\%$ CI 0.30e1.38), and 2.89 $95\%$ CI 0.82e10.13), respectively. Among the 8 studies that compared MT outcomes versus medical management (without thrombectomy), odds ratios of excellent outcomes, good outcomes, mortality, and incidence of sICH after MT versus medical management were 0.98 $195\%$ CI 0.89e1.07), 0.94 (95% CI 0.89e1.00), 1.61 $95\%$ CI 1.08e2.41), and 2.59 (95% CI 1.35e4.96), respectively. Among all 14 studies, pooled proportions of excellent outcomes, good outcomes, mortality, and sICH after thrombectomy were $58.7\%,$ $76.2\%,$ $6.82\%,$ and $3.23\%,$ respectively.  

- CONCLUSIONS: Our study shows significant selection bias and heterogeneity in the literature with differences in baseline characteristics (age, stroke severity, prestroke modified Rankin Scale score, side of infarct, vessel and site of occlusion, use of intravenous thrombolysis, criteria for clinical deterioration, and selection bias for rescue MT and rates of reperfusion), emphasizing the need for a randomized controlled trial.

Multiple recent, nonrandomized studies have investigated the safety and utility of mechanical thrombectomy (MT) as a supplement or an efficacious alternative to IVT in this patient population. In a meta-analysis of studies published until September 2017, Griessenauer et al. found no significant difference in functional outcome between MT and medical therapy using IVT for patients with minor stroke and LVO. Additional studies published since that meta-analysis have reported conflicting results. Sarraj et al.,10 in a retrospective review of 214 patients, did not find a benefit of MT over medical management (MM). Nagel et al.,11 in contrast, reported an adjusted odds ratio (OR) for good outcome of 3.1 $(95\%$ confidence interval [CI] $\mathbf{I}.4^{-6.9)}$ favoring immediate thrombectomy. Dargazanli et al.12 found similar outcomes between urgent thrombectomy versus best medical management (BMM, including IVT if applicable and rescue thrombectomy in cases of clinical deterioration). However, there is significant variability in factors among studies that may impact outcomes including patient selection, eligibility for IVT, and criteria for immediate versus rescue thrombectomy, amongst others. The purpose of this meta-analysis was to systematically review, analyze, and summarize the current literature to determine the utility of MT in patients presenting with mild stroke symptomatology with LVO.  



# METHODS  

This meta-analysis strictly followed the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses (PRISMA) guidelines.13  



# Data Search  

A medical librarian (A.B.) performed a comprehensive search of multiple databases: Ovid Embase (January 1, 2010, to December 21, 2018), Ovid MEDLINE (January 1, 2010, to December 21, 2018), Web of Science (January 1, 2010, to December 21, 2018), and Cochrane Central (January 1, 2010, to December 21, 2018). To formulate the search, we began with the institutional MeSH Analyzer using key articles to produce relevant controlled vocabulary and key word terms.14 The search included 2 concepts: minor stroke (defined by the NIHSS) and MT. The authors also culled references of articles meeting inclusion criteria. The final search results were exported into EndNote (version X, Clarivate Analytics, Philadelphia, Pennsylvania, USA), where they were deduplicated, and then uploaded to Covidence (Veritas Health Innovation, Melbourne, Australia) for screening.  

An additional search was performed on October 18, 2019, to extend the search results to October 15, 2019. Similar search strategies were employed.  



# Study Selection  

A senior radiologist (A.M.) with more than 10 years’ experience reviewed reports for inclusion. A separate screener (X.W.) independently reviewed all included studies and performed a full-text screen. Observational studies including case series with more than 5 patients published in English reporting the outcomes after thrombectomy in patients with minor stroke (exact NIHSS thresholds vary across studies) were included, regardless of whether there was a control group (MM with or without rescue thrombectomies). Patients of all ages and occluded vessels were included. When one cohort of patients was published partially or in full in more than 1 study, the study with the largest number of patients was included. Abstracts were excluded due to the lack of enough information, including the institution(s) patients were collected from. A flowchart of the search is presented in Figure 1 in accordance with PRISMA.  



# Data Extraction  

All data extraction was performed by one author (X.W.) and crosschecked by another author (A.M.) for accuracy. For studies that reported only on outcomes after thrombectomy, the following information from each of the included studies was collected: study period dates and institutions from which patients were selected, definition of minor stroke (NIHSS threshold), reason given to perform thrombectomy, number of patients who underwent thrombectomy, patient age and sex breakdown, baseline NIHSS, baseline Alberta Stroke Program Early CT Score, number of patients who received IVT, time from symptom onset to groin puncture, number of patients with excellent outcome (modified Rankin Scale [mRS] score $_0\mathrm{-}\mathrm{\Delta}_{\mathrm{I}}$ ), functional independence (mRS $_{0-2)}$ , and mortality at discharge and/or at 90-day follow-up and number of patients with symptomatic intracranial hemorrhage (sICH). For patients who included a control group of either BMM or MM alone, similar data were extracted for the control group, as well as time to intravenous thrombolysis.  



# Quality Assessment  

Two reviewers (A.M., X.W.) independently assessed the quality of each study, using the Quality in Prognosis Studies. Adjudication was performed through consensus after discussion between the 2 authors when opinions differed.  



# Data Synthesis  

For all studies, we calculated the pooled proportion of excellent outcomes $\scriptstyle(\mathrm{mRS0-I})$ , good outcomes $\left(\mathrm{mRS}0\mathrm{-}2\right)$ , mortality (mRS 6), and risk of sICH after thrombectomy. For studies that included BMM or MM as the control group, ORs of excellent outcomes, good outcomes, mortality, and sICH compared with the thrombectomy group were calculated.  



# Statistical Analysis  

Forrest plots and funnel plots were constructed with RStudio (RStudio, Inc., Boston, Massachusetts, USA) using the meta (version 4.11-0; Schwarzer 2020). $\mathrm{I}^{2}$ was calculated as a measure of statistical heterogeneity, with $\mathrm{I}^{2}$ values of $0\%{-40\%}$ , $30\%{-}60\%$ , $50\%{-}90\%$ , $75\%\mathrm{-}\mathrm{-}\mathrm{I00\%}$ representing not important, moderate, substantial, and considerable inconsistency, respectively.16

# RESULTS  

The literature search returned a total of 4153 results after deduplication, the abstracts of which were screened. In total, 73 papers were identified for full-text screening. A total of 14 studies with 2134 patients were included in the final analysis. The PRISMA flowchart is presented in Figure 1. The information extracted from each study is presented in Table 1.10-12,17-27  

Of the 14 studies that investigated outcomes after thrombectomy in patients with minor stroke, $64\%\mathrm{~(9/{\pi}4)}$ studies defined minor stroke as $\mathrm{NIHSS}<6$ at presentation. Dargazanli et al. and Kaesmacher et al. used an NIHSS threshold ${<}8$ , Kastrup et al.  

used NIHSS $\leq\mathbf{I}0$ , Shang et al. used ${\le}8$ , and Wolman et al. used $\leq6$ .12,20,24,27,28 The median NIHSS at baseline was between 3 and 5 for $86\%\left(\mathtt{I}2/\mathtt{I}4\right)$ studies. In the remaining 2 studies, Kastrup et al. reported medians of 8 and 7 in the MT and MM group, respectively, whereas the median values in Shang et al. were 7 and $4$ , respectively. Of the 14 studies, 8 included MM as control groups, 2 included BMM as control groups, and $4$ only reported on outcomes after thrombectomy.  



# Immediate Thrombectomy Versus BMM (and Rescue Thrombectomy)  

Among the 2 studies that compared immediate medical thrombectomy (without intravenous thrombolysis [IMT]) versus BMM, Nagel et al.11 defined minor stroke as $\mathrm{NIHSS}\leq5$ whereas Dargazanli et al.12 defined it as NIHSS ${<}8$ with subgroup analysis provided for NIHSS ${<}6$ . The decision to assign patients to either group (IMT or BMM) was based on clinicians’ discretion in the study by Nagel et al.,11 whereas Dargazanli et al.12 included patients from 3 centers assigned to MT in first instance and BMM from the fourth center. In Dargazanli et al.,12 more patients in the IMT group received IVT $(\mathrm{\Omega}_{\mathrm{I}03}/\mathrm{\Omega}_{\mathrm{I}70}$ , $60.6\%)$ as compared with the BMM group $\left(53/\mathrm{I}3\mathrm{I},40.5\%\right)$ , whereas in Nagel et al.,11 the proportions were $38/80$ $(47.5\%)$ in the IMT group and $\mathrm{II}4/220\left(5\mathrm{I.8\%}\right)$ in the BMM group.

# MT Versus MM  

Eight studies compared MT outcomes versus MM. The decision to assign patients to either group was not specified in 4 studies (Urra et al., Goyal et al., Manno et al., and Shang et al.) was based on local clinicians’ decisions in 2 (Sarraj et al. and Wolman et al) and based on different time intervals in 2 studies (in the study by Kastrup et al. and Da Ros et al.). $\mathrm{\{10,17,20,2I,23,25^{-}27}}$ In 3 studies (Urra et al., Da Ros et al., and Manno et al.), a larger proportion of patients in the MM group received tissue plasminogen activator (tPA) than the MT group,17,21,25 whereas in 3 other studies (Goyal et al., Shang et al., and Wolman et al.), a large proportion in the MT group received tPA. $^{23,26,27}$ The proportions of patients who received IVT were not specified in Kastrup et al.20 and similar between MT and MM in Sarraj et al.10  

The OR of excellent outcomes after MT versus MM was 0.98 $(95\%\mathrm{CI}$ $_{0.89^{-1.07}})$ using the fixed-effect model and 1.03 $\left(95\%\mathrm{CI}0.88\mathrm{-I}.2\mathrm{I}\right)$ using the random-effect model, although in $4$ of the 8 studies, the proportion of excellent outcome was equivalent or greater in the MT group. The OR of good outcome was 0.94 $(95\%\mathrm{CI}0.89\mathrm{-I.00})$ using the fixed-effect model and 0.97 $(95^{0}/\mathrm{oCIo.88-I.o6})$ using the randomeffect model, with 5 of 8 studies reporting a lower proportion of good outcome after MT. The OR for mortality was 1.61 $(95^{0}/0\mathrm{CIr}.08{-2.4\mathrm{I}},$ ) in the fixed-effect model and 1.95 $(95\%$ CI $\mathbf{I}.\mathbf{I}2-3.42\ $ in the randomeffect model. The OR for sICH was 2.59 $(95^{0}/0\mathrm{CI}\mathrm{r}.35^{-}4.96)$ and 2.71 $(95\%$ CI $\mathtt{I}.32\mathrm{-}5.57)$ , respectively, in the fixed- and random-effect models. The $\mathrm{I}^{2}$ tests showed moderate heterogeneity for the former 2 ORs and insignificant for the latter 2 (Figure 3A-D).  



# Mechanical Thrombectomy

# DISCUSSION  

A previous large, multicenter study has shown high rates of excellent functional outcomes $(77\%)$ after IVT in patients with minor stroke symptoms $({\mathrm{NIHSS}}<5)$ ). However, only $28\%$ of patients in that study harbored LVO at admission.29 Similarly, high rates of good outcomes after IVT have been reported in a recent meta-analysis without reporting on proportion of LVO patients.30 Delay in care, including mode of transport, imaging, and initiation of treatment, has been shown in patients with minor strokes.29  

Multiple nonrandomized studies have been recently published on the safety and efficacy of MT in patients with minor stroke at presentation. Among the 14 studies included in this meta-analysis, pooled proportions of excellent outcomes, good outcomes, mortality, and sICH after thrombectomy were $58.7\%$ , $76.2\%$ , $6.82\%$ , and $3.23\%$ , respectively. Our meta-analysis did not show MT to have better outcomes compared with MM, and there was a greater rate of sICH and mortality after MT by the random-effect model. However, a careful review of the included studies showed significant differences between the 2 groups.  

In the 8 studies that included a control arm (MM), the OR of excellent outcomes after MT versus MM was 0.98 $(95\%\mathrm{CIo.89\mathrm{-}\mathrm{I.07}})$ , whereas OR of good outcome was 0.94 $(95\%$ CI $\scriptstyle0.89-\mathtt{I.oo})$ . However, in $4$ of the 8 studies, the proportion of excellent outcome was equivalent or greater in the MT group and in 3 of 8 studies there was a greater proportion of good outcome after MT. Da Ros et al. was an outlier with recanalization reported in $\mathrm{_{IOO}\%}$ with MT and excellent outcomes in $93{\cdot}\mathbf{I}^{0}/\mathbf{0}$ . In contrast, Wolman et al.27 reported excellent outcomes in only $3\mathbf{I}.3\%$ and functional independence in $56.3\%$ .  

MM led to functional independence in $64{\cdot}7\%$ , resulting in the authors commenting on the unfavorable risk-benefit profile compared with MM. Similarly, Kaesmacher et al. reported functional independence in $65.8\%$ patients undergoing thrombectomy, with TICI $2\mathrm{b}/3$ achieved in only 71. $4\%$ . Goyal et al. recently reported on 251 patients from 16 centers and functional independence at 3 months was achieved in $76.7\%$ after thrombectomy and $85{\cdot}2\%$ after MM.  

Greater rates of sICH and mortality were found after thrombectomy compared with MM. Pooled analysis of 14 studies shows the incidence of reported sICH varied from $\tau.2\%$ to $\mathbf{II.7\%}$ . Similarly, the mortality after thrombectomy varied from $0\%$ to $\tau6\%$ with a mean of $7.3\%$ .  

We found 2 recent studies that compared IMT versus BMM (and rescue thrombectomy). Nagel et al.11 results favored IMT, whereas Dargazanli et al.12 reported similar functional outcomes. However, there were significant differences between the 2 study designs. Patients who were not functionally independent at baseline (mRS ${>}2,$ ) were excluded by Dargazanli et al.12 but constituted $6.7\%$ of patients in study by Nagel et al.11 Nagel et al.11 reported similar frequency of IVT use in IMT $(47.5\%)$ and BMM $(5\mathrm{{I}}.8\%)$ groups, whereas Dargazanli et al.12 had greater use of IVT in IMT group $\left(59{\cdot}4^{0}/0\right)$ compared with patients receiving BMM $\left(34.8\%\right)$ due to greater time since onset leading to IVT ineligibility in BMM group. Dargazanli et al.12 specified the definition of neurologic deterioration necessitating consideration for rescue thrombectomy in the BMM group as an increase in NIHSS by 4 points or an installation of a relevant clinical deficit with NIHSS ${\ge}8$ , leading in each case to consider use of thrombectomy.12 Nagel et al.11 did not specify the exact definition of neurologic deterioration; rather, it was determined by the local team from each institution.11 In the study of Nagel et al., $\mathbf{II}.3\%$ $\scriptstyle(25/220)$ of patients in the BMM group received rescue MT, whereas 24 of 131 $(\updownarrow8.3\%)$ patients received it in the study of Dargazanli et al.12 In the IMT group, Dargazanli et al.12 also commented that 33 patients did not receive MT, 24 of 33 due to substantial reperfusion seen on angiogram. Dargazanli et al. reported right-sided occlusion in $44\cdot3\%$ of patients receiving IMT versus $53{\cdot}3\%$ in patients receiving BMM. Nagel et al.11 reported similar reperfusion rates in IMT $(87.5\%)$ versus rescue MT patients $\left(92\%\right)$ . Both studies reported 90-day follow-up outcomes in propensity-matched cohorts.  

In a previous meta-analysis, Griessenauer et al.9 reported better outcomes after MT and IVT versus patients without IVT, based on 3 studies, and no difference in functional outcomes or complications when comparing MT versus IVT based on 2 studies. This included 2 studies from the Haussen et al.,31,32 which reported on 2 nonoverlapping cohort of patients—a retrospective cohort from the STOPStroke and the GESTOR trials, and a prospective cohort from a single institution. We did not include either study because both of these cohorts were fully or partially included in the paper by Nagel et al.11 Similarly, the cohort from the study by Messer et al.33 was also included in the multi-institutional study by Nagel et al.11  

There is considerable heterogeneity and selection bias in the included nonrandomized studies. There are differences in baseline characteristics (age, stroke severity, prestroke mRS, side of infarct, vessel and site of occlusion, use of IVT, criteria for clinical deterioration, and selection bias for rescue MT and rates of reperfusion). The baseline NIHSS was greater in the MT arm compared with MM. The proportion of patients with M2 or distal occlusions were dissimilar, with all studies having a greater proportion in the MM arm. Da Ros et al. included patients with distal occlusion in $45\%$ of MT patients but $\mathrm{_{IOO}\%}$ in the MM arm. Nagel et al.11 had $28.8\%$ in the MT arm but $49{\cdot}3\%$ in the BMM arm. The mean/median Alberta Stroke Program Early CT Score at baseline was greater than 8 in all studies.  

Urra et al.17 included $3\mathrm{{I}}^{0/_{0}}$ of the patients had posterior circulation stroke, with a greater proportion in the MT $(35{\cdot}3\%)$ compared with the MM $(27.3\%)$ groups. Kastrup et al.20 reported similar outcomes between the 2 strategies, but the outcomes were at discharge and no data at 90-day follow-up was reported. They also reported number of patients in $\mathrm{mRS~5}$ and 6 combined, and mortality was not separately reported. Early signs of ischemia were significantly more pronounced in the MT group than in the IVT group, and $\tau2\%$ (17/145) of patients who received MT had an unknown time of stroke onset.  

The decisions to perform IMT/MT on minor stroke were not fully disclosed or specified in all included studies. A significant proportion of the intervention group (as opposed to MM) from STOPStroke trial underwent thrombectomy after clinical deterioration according to Haussen et al. (included in Nagel et al.), and it is unclear whether they were classified as the BMM or the IMT group.  

A greater proportion of patients getting IMT may have been transferred from remote centers.12 The pooled outcomes after MT is likely an underestimate of excellent and good outcomes and overestimate of mortality due to this selection bias. This is especially pronounced in the 4 studies that reported MT only, with many included patients being ineligible for intravenous tPA.  

We attempted to investigate the correlation between time to puncture and outcomes after thrombectomy, but no clear association could be found due to the small sample size and heterogeneity in time interval reporting. Onset to thrombolysis time was not reported in 8 studies. Onset to puncture time was reported in 11 studies, and the remaining 3 studies reported onset to recanalization time, CT to puncture and door to puncture times respectively.  

It is important to recognize that the included studies did not explore the type of initial deficit as a predictor of outcome. NIHSS may not adequately assess severity of minor stroke on admission, for example, complete hemianopia (NIHSS 2) is a symptom associated with severe disability.  

The single-arm prospective pilot study (Mild acute Ischemic Stroke With LVO MISTWAVE; Clinical Trials identifier NCT03731351) recently published their results (September 2019) on 20 patients and deemed MT to be feasible and safe but inadequate to demonstrate efficacy.34 Collateral flow may play a significant role in patients achieving a good outcome with MM. Early recanalization (ER) may be frequent $\left(34^{\mathrm{o}}/\mathrm{o}\right)$ in IVT-treated patients with minor stroke, with thrombus length being a powerful predictor of early recanalization.35 MT has been recommended in patients with clot $4\mathrm{mm}$ or longer, with greater probability of reperfusion after IVT if clot is less than 4 mm.18 Further advances in collateral imaging and clot characterization may help guide patient selection for immediate versus rescue MT.  

In conclusion, there is significant selection bias and heterogeneity in the literature assessing outcomes after thrombectomy in patients with minor stroke, emphasizing the need for a randomized controlled trial. The Minor Stroke Therapy Evaluation (MOSTE) trial is currently underway, and the estimated primary completion date is December 2021.36
