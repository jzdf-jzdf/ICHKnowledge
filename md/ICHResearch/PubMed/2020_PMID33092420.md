# ABSTRACT  

Background: Intraventricular hemorrhage (IVH) is a common condition in preterm neonates and is responsible for substantial adverse neurodevelopmental outcome in preterm neonates. Prevention of IVH is an important intervention for better neurological outcome in these preterm neonates.  

Aims and objective: This study aimed to determine whether delayed cord clamping (DCC) was superior to immediate cord clamping (ICC) for the prevention of IVH in preterm neonates.  

Patients and methods: In this two centered prospective double-blind randomized controlled trial, eligible neonates with gestational age from 26 to 34 weeks were randomized to receive either ICC (cord clamped in 10–15 s) or DCC (cord clamped in 30–45 s) groups. The grading and severity of IVH were evaluated by cranial ultrasound scan done on the 3–4th and 7–10th days after birth.  

Results: Among the 148 enrolled neonates, 79 were in the ICC group and 69 were in the DCC group. There was no difference in maternal and neonatal baseline characteristics except the neonates in the DCC group weighed more (ICC $1528.77\pm365.59$ vs. DCC $1658.11\pm419.529;$ $p=.047)$ at birth. There was no significant difference in the incidence of any grade of IVH in both groups (ICC $12.8\%$ vs. DCC $14.5\%$ $p=.745)$ . There was a significantly higher incidence of grade I IVH (ICC $2.5\%$ vs. DCC $13\%;$ $p=.024)$ in the DCC group. The incidence of grade II IVH (ICC $5.1\%$ vs. DCC $0\%$ ; $p=.123)$ ); grade III IVH (ICC $3.8\%$ vs. DCC $1.4\%$ $p=.623)$ ; and grade IV IVH (ICC $1.3\%$ vs. DCC $0\%$ ; $p{>}.999)$ were comparable between the two groups. The incidence of a significant IVH (grades II, III, and IV) was significantly less in the DCC group (ICC $10.1\%$ vs. DCC $1.4\%,$ $p=.036)$ . The mean initial hemoglobin levels were significantly higher in neonates enrolled in DCC $(15.41\pm2.1$ vs. $16.46\pm2.45{\:}{\mathrm{g}}/{\mathrm{dL}}$ ; $p=.007)$ ). There was a significant reduction in the number of days of hospital stay (ICC $18.78\pm15.42$ vs. DCC $13.21\pm16.16;$ $p=.002)$ . There was no difference in initial hematocrit, platelet count, maximum bilirubin level, and Apgar score $(p>.05)$ .  

Conclusions: Although there was no reduction in any grade of IVH, the incidence of significant IVH (grades II, III, and IV) was significantly decreased with the use of DCC in preterm neonates. Delayed cord clamping also resulted in a significant increase in birth weight, higher hemoglobin levels, and shorter hospital stays without any increase in the risks of hyper-bilirubinemia, low Apgar score, and neonatal mortality.

# Introduction  

Prematurity is one of the most important causes of neonatal mortality and morbidity [1]. The overall incidence of prematurity has been estimated to be around $9.6\%$ [1]. Recently, the incidence of preterm birth has increased significantly predominantly due to multi-fetal pregnancies after infertility treatment techniques [2]. Intraventricular hemorrhage (IVH) is a major cause of adverse neonatal outcomes in premature neonates [3]. The lower the gestational age (GA) and birth weight (BW) of the neonate, the higher the incidence and grade of IVH to develop [4]. It has been reported that $15\%$ of very low birth weight (VLBW) neonates develop severe degree of IVH (grades III and IV), resulting in post-hemorrhagic hydrocephalus, mental retardation, and cerebral palsy [2]. Other risk factors for IVH include respiratory distress syndrome (RDS), fluctuations in cerebral perfusion, increased venous pressure, pneumothorax, thrombocytopenia, metabolic acidosis, shock, hypoglycemia, and hypercarbia [3,5]. Moreover, with the increase in severity of the IVH, the risk of neurodevelopmental disabilities increases exponentially in these preterm neonate [3].  

There is no effective treatment for IVH [6]; therefore, its prevention seems to be the most futile. There are several interventions to reduce the incidence of IVH, including prevention of preterm birth, administration of antenatal corticosteroid [7], intrauterine transport of premature neonates [8], and appropriate resuscitation as per the neonatal resuscitation program (NRP) [9]. Indeed, studies have shown that delayed cord clamping (DCC) protects against IVH in premature neonates [10] and decreases the need for blood transfusion [10–12]. On the contrary, some prospective randomized clinical trials (RCTs) have reported no difference between DCC and immediate cord clamping (ICC) on the rates of IVH among preterm neonates [13,14].  

We conducted this double-blinded RCT to assess the effect of DCC on the development of IVH in neonates from GA of 26–34 weeks for examining the hypothesis that DCC is superior to ICC in the prevention of IVH in premature neonates.  

All preterm neonates born at GA of 26–34 weeks (calculated by the first trimester obstetrical ultrasound scan and the first day of the last menstrual period) born in hospital were assessed for eligibility and enrolled after fulfilling inclusion criteria. The exclusion criteria of the study were refusal by either parents, obstetrician or neonatologist, presence of severe congenital anomalies, need for immediate resuscitation, presence of placental abruption or placenta previa and clamping of the cord before or after the specified reference time intervals.  



# Intervention  

All eligible were randomized to either DCC or ICC at the time of birth. The preterm infants born in the first month of performing the study were placed in the case or control groups by flipping a coin. The infants born in the next month were put in the second group and this sequence was continued monthly till the precalculated neonates were enrolled in the study. The case and control groups were named as codes A and B. The obstetricians of both the hospital were explained about the study and type of intervention to be done. A trained nursery staff noted the correct timing of umbilical cord clamping and recorded the grouping codes in both the hospital.  



# Patients and methods  

This prospective randomized controlled trial was conducted at Hafez and Hazrat-e-Zeynab hospitals affiliated to Shiraz University of Medical Sciences, Iran from September 2012 to December 2013. This study was approved by the Institutional Research Board (IRB) of Shiraz University of Medical Science on 13 September 2012, 1391/06/23 with reference number CT-P-4103. This trial is registered in the Iranian Registry of Clinical Trials (IRCT): IRCT2014031116936N1.  

Written informed consent was obtained from the parents or guardian before the randomization of the newborn in either of the group. They were provided with written and verbal information including the potential risk and benefits involved in the research. One copy of the research fact sheet was kept with the parents. Parents were explained about the voluntary nature of participation in the research, about non-penalization in event of nonparticipation and about the option of withdrawing their infant from the study at any point they wished without offering any reason. Confidentiality of the subjects was maintained.  



# Immediate cord clamping  

Umbilical cord was clamped after 10–15 s of delivery.  



# Delayed cord clamping  

Umbilical cord was clamped after 30–45 s of delivery.  

In both the arms, during normal vaginal delivery, the newborn was held between the mother’s thighs at or below the level of introitus, its nose and mouth were suctioned for any visible oral secretion, and was dried and wrapped in a warm sterile towel. During cesarean delivery, the neonate was placed on the mother’s thighs and received the same care. In both the centers, antenatal steroid is administered to all mothers with preterm deliveries (betamethasone $24\mathrm{mg}$ in two divided doses) if possible or at least single dose of antenatal steroid when there is impending delivery at time of hospital admission.  

The neonates were followed for the development of IVH as the primary outcome. Cranial ultrasound scanning (CUS) was performed twice for every neonate who survived by diagnostic ultrasonography device Mindray M7 by trained neonatologists or radiologists who were not aware of the timing of the umbilical cord clamping. The first CUS was performed on the third or fourth day after birth, and the second was done on the seventh to tenth day after birth. The finding of CUS was recorded in predesigned performa and the highest grade of IVH was considered for analysis.  



# Outcome assessment  

The primary outcome of the study was the incidence of interventricular hemorrhage (IVH). The secondary outcomes of the study were hemoglobin level, hematocrit, platelet count, serum bilirubin level, and Apgar score. The severity of IVH was classified into four grades:  

 Grade I: sub-ependymal hemorrhage;   
Grade II: IVH without ventricular dilatation; Grade III: IVH with ventricular dilatation; Grade IV: IVH with parenchymal hemorrhage [15].  

IVH having grades of II, III, and IV were defined as significant IVH [10].  

The baseline characteristics of the mother and neonates were collected including maternal age, infant BW, GA, Apgar score, gender, need for continuous positive airway pressure (CPAP) or mechanical ventilation, length of hospital stay, and mortality.  



# Sample size calculation  

The sample size was calculated by the standard method and biomedical information. The effect size was $36\%$ in the ICC group and $14\%$ in the DCC group $22\%$ absolute reduction). Considering the power of $80\%$ and alpha error of 0.05 the sample size was calculated to be 114. Assuming a loss rate of $25\%,$ we decided to enroll 148 infants in the study.  



# Statistical analysis  

The data were extracted from the admission charts, recorded in data collecting sheets, and analyzed with codes A and B. The codes were broken after the results were finalized. The researchers who collected the data from the charts, the sonographer, and the statistician were blinded about the neonate group of enrollment. The supervisor nurse who took the time, obstetricians who did the intervention, and neonatologists present in the delivery room were aware of the timings due to the nature of the procedure; however, neither of them had any role in data extraction and analysis.  

The qualitative variables were analyzed by the chisquare test and Fisher’s exact test. For the quantitative variables with equality of variance and normality, Student’s t-test was used. On the other hand, Mann–Whitney’s $U$ test was performed for those that did not have variance equality or normality. Logistic regression (LR) analysis was done for controlling the confounding variables. All the analyses were performed using the SPSS statistical software (version 15, Chicago, IL) and $p<$ .05 was considered to be statistically significant.  



# Results  

In this prospective randomized control study, 291 cases were assessed for eligibility. Fifty-five cases were excluded from the study secondary to different reasons and 236 were allocated for randomization. One hundred and eight neonates were randomized to the ICC group and 128 were randomized to the DCC group. However, 14 cases in the ICC group and 40 in the DCC group did not have cord clamp at the scheduled times after allocation due to inappropriate communication or coordination between the timekeeper and the obstetricians under the stressful conditions of the operating rooms, hence were removed from the analysis. There were 15 lost to follow up in ICC and 19 in the DCC group; therefore finally, analyses were conducted on 79 neonates in the ICC group and 69 ones in the DCC (Figure 1).  



# Comparison of baseline variables between the two groups  

Both the groups were comparable for maternal characteristics and pregnancy-related complications $(p>.05)$ (Table 1).  

The neonatal characteristics including mean GA, gender, intra-uterine growth retardation (IUGR), and different neonatal mortalities were comparable between the two groups $(p>.05)$ . There was a significant difference in BW of the two groups (ICC $1528.77\pm365.59$ vs. $1658.11\pm419.529;p=.047)$ with neonates of DCC being more heavy (approximately $130{\mathfrak{g}})$ at the time of birth. There was also a significant reduction in hospital stay duration in the DCC group by approximately five days (ICC $18.78\pm15.42$ days vs. $13.21\pm16.16$ days; $p=.002)$ (Table 2).  



# Comparison of the primary and secondary outcome between the two groups  

There was no difference in the incidence of any grade of IVH between the two groups (ICC 10 ( $12.8\%)$ vs. 10 $(14.5\%)$ ; $p=.745)$ ). There was a significantly higher incidence of grade I (ICC 2 $(2.5\%)$ vs. DCC 9 $(13\%)$ $p=.024)$ in the DCC group. The incidence of grade II IVH (ICC 4 $(5.1\%)$ vs. DCC 0 $(0\%)$ ; $p=.123)$ ; grade III IVH (ICC 3 $(3.8\%)$ vs. DCC 1 $(1.4\%)$ ; $p=.623)$ , and grade IV IVH (ICC 1 $(1.3\%)$ vs. DCC 0 $(0\%)$ ; $p{>}.999.$ were comparable between the two groups. The incidence of significant IVH (grades II, III, and IV) were significantly less in DCC group (ICC 8 $(10.1\%)$ vs. DCC 1 $(1.4\%)$ , $p=.036)$ (Table 3).  

There was a significant increase in the initial hemoglobin level in the intervention arm when compared to control (ICC $15.41\pm2.1\mathrm{mg/d}$ vs. $16.46\pm2.45\mathrm{mg/dl};$

# Discussion  

In this randomized control trial, we studied the effect of DCC over the incidence of IVH when compared with ICC. The result of the study showed no effect over reduction in the incidence of any grade of IVH in the DCC group. Although there was an increased incidence of grade I IVH in the DCC arm but the incidence of significant IVH (grades II, III, and IV) was significantly lower in the intervention arm. In the majority cases with grade I IVH, the neurodevelopmental outcome is normal with neurological impairment mostly seen with higher grade of IVH. Increase in the grade I IVH finding in the intervention arm could be incidental as differentiating grade I IVH from normal choroid plexus is difficult and it depends over radiologist interpretation and experience. Decrease in significant IVH in the DCC arm can be secondary to extra blood transfused in DCC in preterm infant’s leading to better hemodynamic and blood pressure stability with more circulating volume, higher initial diastolic blood pressure and mean blood pressure, decreased need of vasopressors and less fluctuation in blood pressure. The level of care was provided same in both the groups and neonatal morbidities were comparable between both the groups, thus removing these as confounding factors for decrease in the incidence of significant IVH.  

The study showed a significant increase in the BW of the neonates who were randomized to the DCC group, with this group neonates weighing almost $130{\mathfrak{g}}$ heavier when compared with their counterpart of ICC group. As the mean GA at birth and maternal characteristic that can effect fetal growth were comparable between the two groups; therefore, this increase in the BW can be explained by the extra amount of blood that the neonate received when DCC is being practiced. We were not able to see any significant difference in the maximum bilirubin level as this was expected secondary to higher hemoglobin level in the DCC group. This difference could be because as the neonates were more mature in the study, thus having more mature hepatic conjugation enzymes for bilirubin metabolism.

The neonates randomized in DCC were discharged earlier approximately by five days which was significant statistically. This earlier discharge can be because the neonates in DCC weighted approximately $130{\mathfrak{g}}$ heavier and had better health status than their counterpart and were eligible for early discharge as per the discharge criteria of the hospital. This early discharge of neonates from NICU will lead to a decrease in financial burden over parents and will also decrease the burden over the health care personal who are taking care of these neonates in the NICU.  

In this study, IVH was seen in $13.5\%$ of the preterm neonates. However, in a previous published study from the same center, IVH incidence was approximately $25\%$ of preterm neonates [8]. This improvement can be explained with several preventive strategies that have been implemented in our center like routine administration of antenatal steroids, advancement of delivery room facilities and neonatal care like early rescue surfactant, use of noninvasive mode of ventilation and using patient triggered ventilation.  

The present study results revealed no statistically significant difference between the ICC and DCC groups regarding the development of any grade IVH as the primary outcome. The results of our study are similar as reported by Strauss et al. and Kugelman et al. [13,14]. Cochrane systematic review reported that DCC was associated with less IVH (all grades) when compared to ICC. However, there were insufficient data for reliable conclusions about the comparative effects on severe IVH (grades III and IV) and neurodevelopmental outcomes [10]. We were not able to show any protective effect of DCC over any grade of IVH because of the small sample size and we had a more mature newborn.  

Although the total number of any grade IVH cases was comparable in both groups in our study, $90\%$ of the neonates who developed IVH in the DCC group had IVH grade I. Since the mortality rates and complications of IVH grade I are similar to those of neonates without IVH [16], this finding might be in favor of a probable protective effect of DCC against higher grades of IVH. Yet, further studies are required to confirm our findings.  

The other beneficial effect of DCC that has been reported includes an increase in hemoglobin (Hb) levels, stabilization of hemodynamic status [10,17], better cardiopulmonary adaptations [18], and decrease in the incidence of necrotizing enterocolitis (NEC) [12]. In our study, the mean initial Hb level after birth was significantly higher in the DCC group as delay in cord clamping contributes to more placental transfusion and higher Hb levels in neonates. However, our study demonstrated no significant difference between the two groups regarding the rate of RDS, requirement for CPAP, need for mechanical ventilation support and NEC. This could be because our neonates were more mature with the majority of having GA of 30 weeks and more with the incidence of RDS and NEC being inversely proportional to GA and are more often seen in extreme premature neonates. The other reason could be that neonates in both the group equally received antenatal steroids therefore making them less prone to develop both RDS and NEC.  

The incidence of hyperbilirubinemia is high in DCC due to an increase in transferred red blood cell volume is a major concern in the safety of DCC. This increase in higher bilirubin levels in the DCC group does not lead to an increase in the need for hyperbilirubinemia treatment [14,18]. However, the results of the current study showed that the mean maximum bilirubin levels were comparable in ICC and DCC groups, which is similar to a study published by Dong et al., Ibrahim et al., and Ceriani Cernadas et al. [17,19,20].  



# Strength and limitation of study  

The limitation of our study includes small sample size because of which effect of DCC on neonatal RDS, NEC, and other neonatal morbidities was not seen. Multifetal pregnancies were not eliminated in this study as earlier fetal maturation in these pregnancies could induce bias; however, the numbers of multifetal pregnancies were similar in the case and control groups. Exact time of cord clamping was not noted as time range was used to label ICC and DCC. The strength of our study includes uniform application of protocol in both the hospital and trial being double blinded.  



# Conclusions  

Although, the incidence of any grade IVH in preterm neonates was not decreased by DCC but the incidence of significant IVH (grades II, III, and IV) was statistically lowered. DCC also resulted in higher Hb levels and shorter hospital stays without increasing the risks of hyper-bilirubinemia, low Apgar score, and neonatal mortality. There are still uncertainties and lack of powerful evidence regarding several aspects on the effect of DCC and ICC in neonates. Therefore, further multi-centered, well-designed studies with large sample sizes are required to have definitive conclusions.
