# ABSTRACT  

Background  The efficacy of endovascular therapy (EVT) in patients with large ischemic core has been reported, but it remains unclear whether IV alteplase (IVT) has beneficial effects in addition to EVT in such patients. We evaluated the efficacy and safety of EVT with or without IVT.  

Methods The RESCUE-Japan LIMIT was an open-label, prospective, multicenter, randomized clinical trial to evaluate the efficacy and safety of EVT in stroke patients with large ischemic core, defined as Alberta Stroke Program Early CT Score (ASPECTS) 3–5. This subanalysis evaluated the differences in the effects of EVT with medical care (EVT group) compared with medical care alone (No-EVT group) between those who received IVT (IVT stratum) and those who did not (No-IVT stratum) before EVT.  

Results  Among 202 enrolled patients, 147 $(73\%)$ did not receive IVT. in the No-IVT stratum, the modified Rankin Scale (mRS) score of 0–3 at 90 days was significantly higher in the EVT group than in the No-EVT group $(31.1\%$ vs $12.3\%.$ , OR 3.21 $95\%$ CI 1.37 to 7.53)). In the IVT stratum, the mRS score of 0–3 was $30.8\%$ in the EVT group and $13.8\%$ in the No-EVT group (OR 2.78 $(95\%\mathrm{Cl}0.72\$ to 10.7)) (interaction $\mathsf{p}{=}0.77)$ . The incidence of symptomatic intracranial hemorrhage was not different between the two groups in the No-IVT stratum (OR 1.20 $(95\%\mathrm{C}|0.35\$ to 4.12)), but it was significantly higher in the EVT group than in the No-EVT group in the IVT stratum ( $11.5\%$ vs $0\%$ , $\uprho{=}0.03,$ .  

Conclusions  There was no difference in efficacy of EVT with or without IVT, while IVT before EVT might increase symptomatic intracranial hemorrhage in patients with large ischemic core.

Endovascular therapy (EVT) is now the standard treatment for acute ischemic stroke caused by large vessel occlusion, and current guidelines recommend that patients eligible for IVT should receive IVT even if EVT is being considered.4 6 Although several recent clinical trials which compared EVT with IVT versus EVT alone in patients with mild-to-moderate core volume have been reported,7 their results were not consistent. # INTRODUCTION  

Intravenous alteplase (IVT) is one of the standard treatments for patients with acute ischemic stroke within 4.5 hours from onset, and recent studies have also shown that IVT is effective beyond 4.5 hours from onset, with patient selection based on imaging modalities such as MRI or CT perfusion.1 2 However, previous studies found an increased risk of intracranial hemorrhage (ICH) after reperfusion therapy with IVT, especially in patients with  

The Recovery by Endovascular Salvage for Cerebral Ultra-acute Embolism-Japan Large IscheMIc core Trial (RESCUE-Japan LIMIT) reported the efficacy of EVT in patients with large ischemic core as defined by an Alberta Stroke Program Early Computed Tomographic Score (ASPECTS) of 3–5.10 In addition, the results of two randomized controlled trials demonstrating the efficacy of EVT in patients with large vessel occlusions with large ischemic core were recently reported.11 12 However, it remains unclear whether IVT has beneficial effects in addition to EVT in such patients with large ischemic core.13 14 Therefore, it is important to evaluate whether IVT before EVT for large ischemic core is effective and safe.  

We thus analyzed RESCUE-Japan LIMIT data to evaluate the efficacy and safety of EVT in such patients with or without IVT before EVT.  

# METHODS  

# Study design  

This study reports the differences in the effects of EVT with medical care (EVT group) compared with medical care alone (No-EVT group) between those who received IVT (IVT stratum) and those who did not (No-IVT stratum) before EVT. This subanalysis was prespecified in the protocol.15  

The RESCUE-Japan LIMIT was an open-label, prospective, multicenter, non-industry, randomized clinical trial to evaluate the efficacy and safety of EVT in patients with stroke caused by acute large vessel occlusion with large ischemic core, defined as ASPECTS of 3-5 on CT scanning or diffusion-weighted imaging (DWI)-­MRI.10 15 The study enrolled 203 patients from November 2018 to September 2021, with follow-up ending in December 2021.  

The key eligibility criteria were (1) age $\geq18$ years; (2) National Institutes of Health Stroke Scale (NIHSS) score $\geq6$ on admission; (3) modified Rankin Scale (mRS) score of 0 or 1 before onset; (4) occlusion site at the internal carotid artery (ICA) or initial segment of the middle cerebral artery (M1) on CT angiography (CTA) or magnetic resonance angiography (MRA); (5) randomization could be completed within 6 hours after the time the patient was last known to be well or 6–24 hours after the time the patient was last known to be well if there were no ischemic changes on fluid-attenuated inversion recovery (FLAIR) imaging16; and (6) EVT could be initiated within $60\mathrm{min}$ after randomization. The key exclusion criteria were (1) significant mass effect with midline shift on CT or MRI; (2) evidence of acute ICH on CT or MRI; (3) high risk of hemorrhage (platelets $<40000/\upmu\mathrm{L}$ , activated partial thromboplastin time $>50s$ or prothrombin time-international normalized ratio $>3.0$ ); and other conditions.15  

This study was conducted by the steering committee, other committees reflecting the current ethical standards and guidelines, and 45 participating hospitals in Japan.  

# Interventions  

The method of EVT was selected by treating physicians including stent retriever, aspiration catheter, balloon angioplasty, intracranial stent, and carotid-artery stent based on the current guidelines.6 Local IA therapy was added to EVT at the discretion of the treating physician. Patients with indications for IVT (alteplase at a dose of $0.6\mathrm{mg/kg}$ body weight) were treated according to Japanese guidelines at the discretion of the treating physician. IVT was not recommended for patients whose CT exhibited extensive regions of clear hypoattenuation according to Japanese guidelines.5 Other contraindications to IVT according to Japanese guidelines are shown in online supplemental table S1.  

# Measurements  

The baseline characteristics and treatment details were obtained from treating physicians at the study hospitals. The baseline characteristics included mRS score, NIHSS score, occlusion sites, ASPECTS, type of stroke, and laboratories.  

Imaging studies were conducted with either non-contrast CT (NCCT) or DWI-­MRI. The selection of imaging modalities was determined by the treating physicians. If there was suspicion of acute ischemic stroke due to large vessel occlusion with NCCT, CTA was used to determine the occlusion site. If MRI was used, MRA was simultaneously undertaken to determine the occlusion site. The treating physicians were trained and certified in advance to assess ASPECTS with either NCCT or DWI-­MRI.17 18 Enrolment was based on the initial evaluation by treating physicians, but an imaging evaluation committee independently evaluated all imaging studies and determined the final judgments.  

# Outcomes  

The primary outcome was an mRS score of 0–3 at 90 days after onset, consistent with that of the main report.10 15 Secondary outcomes were any ICH within 48 hours, symptomatic ICH within 48 hours, death within 90 days, ordinal shift across the range of mRS towards a better outcome, and improvement of $\geq8$ points in the NIHSS score at 48 hours. Exploratory outcomes were an mRS score of 0–2 at 90 days, an mRS score of 0–1 at 90 days, recurrence of ischemic stroke within 90 days, and decompressive craniectomy within 7 days. The degree of reperfusion in the EVT group was measured with modified Thromolysis in Cerebral Infarction (mTICI) score $\geq2\mathrm{b}$ .19  

Symptomatic ICH was defined as any ICH type with the NIHSS score worsening by ${\geq}4$ points within 48 hours.20 The $\mathrm{mRS}$ score at 90 days was assessed by a neurologist or physical therapist who was unaware of the treatment assignment.  

# Statistical analysis  

We described the characteristics of patients according to No-IVT and IVT strata. Continuous variables were expressed as either mean and SD or median and IQR and compared using the Student's t-test or the Wilcoxon rank sum test, based on their distributions. Categorical variables were presented as numbers and percentages and were compared using the $\chi^{2}$ test.  

In both the No-IVT and IVT strata, the distributions of ordinal mRS scores were presented and the differences between the EVT and No-EVT groups were assessed with the Wilcoxon rank sum test, respectively. We calculated the proportions of primary, secondary, and exploratory outcomes in the No-IVT and IVT strata and compared the EVT effects on these outcomes separately. The effects of EVT compared with No-EVT were presented as ORs with $95\%$ CIs, except for reperfusion degree with $\mathrm{mTICI}\geq2\mathrm{b}$ and ordinal shift of mRS. In the No-IVT and IVT strata separately, we constructed logistic regression models to estimate of the ORs of the EVT group relative to the No-EVT group for primary and secondary outcomes. In the same strata, we constructed an ordinal logistic model to estimate the common ORs with $95\%$ CIs for one mRS score lower at 90 days after the assessment of proportional odds assumption. We constructed the same logistic regression models and ordinal logistic regression model in the total cohort and estimated the interaction p values between EVT and IVT. As sensitivity analyses, we constructed the same logistic models adjusted for ASPECTS and onset to imaging time.  

As additional analyses, we constructed logistic regression models to estimate ORs of the No-IVT stratum relative to the  

IVT stratum for primary and secondary outcomes adjusting for age, ASPECTS, baseline NIHSS, EVT, and onset to imaging time in the total cohort. We also constructed the same models in the EVT and No-EVT groups separately.  

We divided patients with onset to imaging time within 4.5 hours and 4.5 hours or more and determined the proportion of $\mathrm{mRS}$ scores 0–3 at 90 days with $2\times2$ factorial subgroups of IVT and EVT because IVT was generally recommended for patients who presented within 4.5 hours after onset.4 5  

Randomization was performed centrally with the use of a stochastic minimization algorithm to balance trial group assignments within hospitals on the basis of age, interval between the time that a patient was last known to be well and the time of hospital arrival, NIHSS score at admission, and use of IVT. Therefore, the estimations of EVT relative to No-EVT were not adjusted. Due to the small number of events, exploratory outcomes were presented as numbers and percentages and not statistically examined.  

All statistical analyses were performed using JMP 15.1 and SAS 9.4 (both SAS Institute, Cary, North Carolina, USA). Missing data in the baseline characteristics were not imputed and were eliminated from the corresponding analyses. All reported p values were two-tailed, and p values $<0.05$ were considered statistically significant. We did not adjust the p values considering the multiplicity.  

# RESULTS  

# Patient characteristics  

From 203 patients enrolled in the RESCUE-Japan LIMIT, we included 202 patients in this subanalysis, excluding one patient who withdrew consent (online supplemental figure S1). Among the 202 patients, 147 patients were in the No-IVT stratum and 55 were in the IVT stratum (see online supplemental figure S1). Patients in the No-IVT stratum had significantly larger core volumes ( $115~\mathrm{mL}$ vs $86\mathrm{mL}$ , $\mathrm{p}{=}0.019$ ) and longer onset to imaging time than those in the IVT stratum ( $210\mathrm{min}$ vs $125\mathrm{min}$ , $\mathrm{p}{<}0.001;$ ) (table 1). There was no difference in the percentage of oral anticoagulants $(24.5\%$ vs $16.4\%$ , $\mathrm{p}{=}0.21$ ).  

# Outcomes  

In the No-IVT stratum, the mRS scores at 90 days were significantly shifted to lower disability in the EVT group compared with the No-EVT group $(\mathtt{p}{=}0.005)$ ) (figure 1A). The proportion of patients with an mRS score of 0–3 at 90 days was significantly higher in the EVT group than in the No-EVT group $(31.1\%$ vs $12.3\%$ , OR 3.21 $95\%$ CI 1.37 to 7.53); table 2).  

In the IVT stratum, the mRS scores at 90 days were not significantly different between the EVT and No-EVT groups $_{\mathrm{{p}}=0.051}$ ; figure 1B). The interaction of the mRS scores at 90 days between EVT and IVT was not statistically significant $(\mathrm{p}{=}0.60)$ . The proportion of patients with an mRS score of 0–3 at 90 days was $30.8\%$ in the EVT group and $13.8\%$ in the No-EVT group (OR 2.78 $(95\%\mathrm{CI}0.72\$ to 10.7)), and the interaction was not statistically significant $(\mathrm{p}{=}0.77^{\cdot}$ ).  

The risk of any ICH was prominent in both strata when EVT was conducted (OR 2.39 ( $95\%$ CI 1.23 to 4.64) vs 5.94 ( $95\%$ CI 1.84 to 19.2)), but the interaction was not significant $(\mathrm{p}{=}0.14)$ . The incidence of symptomatic ICH was not significant in the EVT group of the No-IVT stratum (OR 1.20 $(95\%\mathrm{CI}0.35\$ to 4.12)), while symptomatic ICH was observed only in the EVT group of the IVT stratum $(11.5\%$ vs $0\%$ , $\mathrm{p}{=}0.03$ ).

Additional analyses showed no significant differences in all outcomes between the No-IVT and IVT strata in the total cohort (see online supplemental table S3). The incidences of any ICH and symptomatic ICH within 48 hours were numerically lower in the No-IVT stratum relative to the IVT stratum in the EVT group, but these effects were not statistically significant (see online supplemental table S4).  

When onset to imaging time was within 4.5 hours, the achievement of mRS scores of 0–3 at 90 days was similarly high in both the $\mathrm{IVT+EVT}$ and EVT alone subgroups, but the risk of any ICH was statistically different by treatment $(\mathrm{p}{=}0.003)$ and was prominent in the $\mathrm{IVT+EVT}$ subgroup (figure 2).  

# DISCUSSION  

In this subanalysis we explored the efficacy and safety of EVT in patients with large vessel occlusion with large ischemic core according to the presence of preceding IVT. EVT was shown to improve the functional outcome at 90 days to a similar extent in patients with large ischemic core in both the No-IVT and IVT strata. On the other hand, the risk of any ICH was numerically higher in the IVT stratum than in the No-IVT stratum when EVT was performed. The incidence of symptomatic ICH was not different between the two groups in the No-IVT stratum, but was significantly higher in the EVT group than in the No-EVT group in the IVT stratum. In this trial, IVT did not affect the clinical outcomes although the incidence of bleeding was lower in the No-IVT stratum relative to the IVT stratum when patients underwent EVT.  

Among patients with onset to imaging time within 4.5 hours, the achievement of an mRS score of 0–3 at 90 days was similar in the $\mathrm{IVT+EVT}$ and the EVT alone subgroups, but the risk of any ICH significantly increased when treatment modalities were added.

The benefits and risks of IVT have been discussed since its approval. In previous observational cohort studies in patients with EVT, IVT was associated with better functional outcomes but a higher risk of symptomatic ICH.21 Recent studies have suggested that the non-thrombolytic effects of alteplase may contribute to ICH. Alteplase activates the expression of matrix metalloproteases in cerebral vascular endothelium and promotes degradation of the extracellular matrix of blood vessels22 while simultaneously mobilizing peripheral neutrophils and T cells, subsequently exacerbating disruption of the blood–brain barrier23 and promoting ICH. In particular, IVT administration for large ischemic core due to embolic stroke was more likely to cause symptomatic ICH and almost $50\%$ of mortality was reported in cases with symptomatic ICH after IVT.24 A recent meta-analysis of six randomized trials has shown that the effect size of IVT in an EVT setting is considerably smaller than that suggested by observational data.7 Therefore, it is necessary to consider that IVT might actually be less effective than the results of this study. Therefore, IVT administration to patients prone to ICH, including those with large ischemic core, should be considered with care.  

Previous subanalyses of a randomized clinical trial reported that baseline infarct size did not modify the effect of IVT prior to EVT with regard to functional outcomes and adverse events.8 However, the number of patients analyzed was $^{56}$ and the study was not powered to evaluate the effectiveness of IVT with EVT in patients with large ischemic core, defined by ASPECTS of ${\leq}4$ in their report.8 Our subanalysis findings reinforced the results of previous reports with large sample sizes. When in the past EVT had not been available for large vessel occlusion, an increased ICH risk with IVT for large ischemic cores was reported in many studies.3 17 18 After EVT became available, a retrospective analysis showed that bridging therapy combining IVT and EVT in patients with ASPECTS $<6$ was also associated with very poor functional outcomes and an increased risk of symptomatic ICH.25 Another report also found that the use of IVT prior to EVT in patients with ASPECTS ${<}6$ was not associated with better functional outcomes.9 In another randomized controlled trial attesting the efficacy of EVT for large ischemic core, subgroup analyses showed that EVT was effective only in the group without IVT and not in the group with IVT,11 which is consistent with our findings. EVT for patients with acute ischemic stroke with large ischemic core may increase the risk of ICH regardless of IVT. Careful decision-­making is required in view of the results of our subanalysis which show that the addition of IVT to EVT might not improve functional outcomes and might increase the incidence of symptomatic ICH.  

# Study limitations  

There were several limitations in this study. First, the indications for IVT were decided at the discretion of the treating physician according to Japanese guidelines, which contraindicate IVT for extensive cerebral infarction due to concerns about hemorrhagic complications. Other studies reporting the efficacy of EVT in patients with large ischemic core also showed IVT rates of $19\mathrm{-}28\%$ ,11 12 which were similar to or lower than those in the present study $(27\%)$ . The relatively lower use of IVT and the differences in core volumes of patients between the No-IVT and IVT strata might have influenced the effectiveness of EVT. However, the sensitivity analyses adjusting for ASPECTS and onset to imaging time showed similar results, so our observations should be considered reliable. Second, the alteplase dose was low at $0.6\mathrm{mg/kg}$ instead of $0.9\mathrm{mg/kg}$ in most Western countries. However, the use of $0.9\mathrm{mg/kg}$ has been reported to cause more ICHs and may lead to more IVT complications.26 On the other hand, the non-inferiority of $0.6\mathrm{mg/kg}$ of alteplase to $0.9\mathrm{mg/kg}$ was not proven, and the efficacy of alteplase was possibly underestimated.13 Third, although inevitable in any subanalysis, the sample size in each stratum was relatively small. Thus, the effectiveness of EVT especially in the IVT stratum might not reach statistical significance as well as interactions. Fourth, ASPECTS was primarily evaluated by DWI-­MRI. ASPECTS on DWI-­MRI have been reported to be about one point lower than on NCCT,27 and this potential difference should be taken into account in the application of these study results in clinical practice. Fifth, data were unavailable on whether patients were brought in directly or transferred to the hospital as well as where they received IVT. Because EVT and IVT are timesensitive treatments,28 29 patient transferral might influence the results. Finally, all of the included patients were Japanese. Because Asians are reported to have more hemorrhagic complications after IVT than non-Asians,30 differences in ethnicity might have influenced the study findings. Therefore, further investigations with a larger number of patients from diverse ethnicity backgrounds should be conducted.  

# CONCLUSION  

There was no difference in the efficacy of EVT with or without IVT in patients with large vessel occlusion with large ischemic core. Decisions should be carefully made because the addition of IVT might increase the occurrence of symptomatic ICH when EVT is administered.
