# Early Complications and Outcome After Treatment of Ruptured Aneurysms in Patients with Subarachnoid HemorrhageeA Post Hoc Analysis of the EARLYDRAIN Trial  

Anton Fruh1.2, Stefan Wolf, David Wasilewski', Peter Vajkoczy', Peter Truckenmueller', for the EARLYDRAIN study group  

- OBJECTIVE: Aneurysmal subarachnoid hemorrhage (aSAH) from a ruptured intracranial aneurysm is a severe, life-threatening condition, with high morbidity and mortality. The current treatment often involves surgical clipping or endovascular treatment within the first 24e48 hours. Although there is ample evidence of complications in treating unruptured aneurysms, similar data in patients with acutely ruptured aneurysms are limited. The recently completed EARLYDRAIN trial showed improved neurologic results from lumbar drainage after aneurysm treatment in patients with aSAH. Using this data set, we aim to study the frequency and effects of complications and identify associated risk factors.  

- METHODS: A substudy was carried out of the prospective multicenter randomized controlled EARLYDRAIN trial. We analyzed treatment-associated complications (bleeding and/or infarctions) detected on computed tomography on day 1 after aneurysm occlusion. Outcomes were the occurrence of postprocedural complications, secondary infarctions in the acute phase, and the modified Rankin Scale score after 6 months.  

- RESULTS: The EARLYDRAIN trial recruited 287 patients in 19 centers. Of these patients, 56 $\left(19.5\%\right)$ experienced a treatment complication. Twenty-five patients $(8.7\%)$ experienced postprocedural intracranial hemorrhage and 34 patients $\left(11.8\%\right)$ experienced a treatment-associated infarction. Patients with a complication showed more secondary infarctions $(P=0.049)$ and worse neurologic outcomes after 180 days $(P=0.025)$ compared with patients with no complication. Aneurysm location, rebleeding before the treatment, number of patients recruited per center, and the day of the treatment were independent risk factors for the occurrence of complications.  

- CONCLUSIONS: The present study shows that patients with aSAH frequently experience intervention-associated complications associated with aneurysm occlusion required to prevent recurrent hemorrhage. Consequently, patients with aSAH with treatment-related complications more often experience a worse clinical course and poor outcome.

# METHODS  



# Study Design and Patients  

This study was a subsidiary analysis of a prospectively conducted multicenter randomized clinical trial that incorporated patients with subarachnoid hemorrhage (SAH) across 19 distinct centers in Germany, Switzerland, and Canada.12 The principal ethical clearance was granted by the ethics committee of the University of Erlangen, Germany. Subsequently, ethical approval was secured locally from the respective ethics boards of all participating centers, in alignment with the principles laid out in the Declaration of Helsinki. The trial has been officially registered under the ClinicalTrials.gov identifier NCT01258257. Between 2011 and 2016, patients with aSAH, older than 18 years, were enrolled and subjected to a 1:1 randomization. The participants either were administered the standard care or were provided with the standard care augmented with lumbar drainage.  



# Data Collection  

Patients were managed as previously reported12 and in alignment with recognized international guidelines.13,14 On receiving the standard of care in emergency departments, the patients underwent randomization to either the lumbar drainage or the no-lumbar drainage cohort. Baseline demographic information and care, process data from the initial 8 days were gathered, complemented by descriptive radiologic imaging, and followed up with outcomes assessed after 6 months.  



# Outcome Assessment  

The primary outcome of the study was the occurrence of treatment-associated complications that were defined as either new bleeding or a new infarction at the postinterventional computed tomography (CT) scan after the treatment of the aneurysm. No randomization was conducted with respect to the treatment modality. Clipping or coiling was determined based on what the local investigators deemed the most appropriate option. Clinical treatment after the aneurysm was secured was at the discretion of the local team, with the exception of the assignment to the lumbar drainage or no-lumbar drainage groups. Neurologic outcomes were evaluated using the modified Rankin Scale (mRS) 15 at the 6 months after hemorrhage mark. Outcomes were dichotomized into a favorable outcome (mRS score $\leq2$ ) and an unfavorable neurologic outcome (mRS score ${>}2.$ ). In addition, the onset of new infarctions, not initially present in the postinterventional CT scan (executed within $4^{8}$ hours after aneurysm treatment), was identified. These secondary infarctions were discerned using the final cerebral imaging (either CT or magnetic resonance imaging) before discharge from acute care. Radiologists involved in evaluations were kept blinded to patient details.  



# Statistical Analysis  

Discrete variables were represented as counts and percentages and were analyzed using the $\mathrm{X}^{2}$ test. Continuous variables were expressed either as mean with standard deviation or as median with interquartile range and were compared using the t test or Mann-Whitney U test, as appropriate. To identify risk factors, univariate and multivariate analyses were performed. The age and the aneurysm size were scaled to 1 standard deviation. Multivariate analysis of the risk factors for the development of a complication after aneurysm treatment was performed with a generalized linear model. Factors that showed a P value $<0.20$ in univariate analysis and the presence of high-grade SAH (Hunt and Hess [HH] grade ${>}3)$ ) entered the model. The relationship of complications with the established outcome parameters, mRS score at 6 months as well as secondary infarctions, was performed with a generalized linear model as in the EARLYDRAIN study. Adjustment was performed for the identified risk factors HH grade ${>}3$ , age, and presence of intracerebral and intraventricular hemorrhages. Statistical significance was denoted by 2-sided $\mathrm{P}$ values ${<}0.05$ . Analyses were conducted using SPSS version 25.0 (IBM Corp., Armonk, New York, USA), R (version 4.3.1), RStudio version 2023.06.0 (RStudio Inc., Boston, Massachusetts, USA), and GraphPad Prism version 10 (GraphPad, San Diego, California, USA).

# Treatment Modality  

The EARLYDRAIN study protocol did not use randomization in choosing treatment modalities for aneurysm management. Treatment decisions. either coiling or clipping, were made at local treatment centers, based on the specific configuration and location of the aneurysm. Consequently, aneurysms treated with coiling differed inherently from those treated with clipping.  

The occurrence of complications after treatment of the ruptured aneurysm did not differ between the clipping and coiling groups.  

The overall complication rates (hemorrhage and/or infarction) were $22.9\%$ for clipping and $\tau6.3\mathrm{~}^{\mathrm{~o~}}/0$ $(\mathrm{P}=0.\mathrm{r}63).$ for coiling. However, a higher incidence of infarctions was observed in patients undergoing clipping compared with coiling $\left(\mathtt{I}6.4\%\right)$ vs. 7.5 $\%$ ; $\mathrm{P}=0.002,$ ). Table 2 shows the occurrence of complications regarding the treatment modality. Treatment of a patient in 1 of the 2 largest recruiting centers was associated with a lower rate of complications compared with other centers (clipping $\boldsymbol{\mathbf{\ell}}_{1}6.\boldsymbol{\mathbf{I}}^{0}/\boldsymbol{\mathbf{0}}$ , $\mathrm{~P~}=~0.008$ ; coiling $9{\cdot}7\%$ , $\mathrm{~P~}=~0.034,$ ). Supplementary Figure 1 shows the number of recruited patients and corresponding complication rates across all study centers. Complications, irrespective of being a result of clipping or coiling, uniformly influenced patient outcomes with severe impact. Specifically, among patients who experienced early complications, $59{\cdot}3\%$ (19 patients) treated with clipping and $4\mathrm{I}.7\%$ (14 patients) treated with coiling had unfavorable outcomes $\mathrm{(P~=~o.r89)}$ ). Figure 3 details the neurologic outcomes for patients with early complications, segmented by treatment modality. Furthermore, the study found no significant difference in the rate of secondary infarctions caused by complications between clipping and coiling $(37.1\%$ vs. $38.\ I\%$ ; $\mathrm{P}=0.296$ ). A subgroup analysis, focusing on the aneurysm localizations, showed no significant differences in the occurrence of complications between patients undergoing clipping versus coiling. # Risk Factors for Complications During the Treatment of Ruptured Aneurysm  

The univariate analysis showed that the aneurysm location, rebleeding before the treatment, the amount of recruitment in the corresponding center, and the day of the treatment were independent risk factors for the occurrence of treatmentassociated complications. Table 3 shows the results of the univariate risk factor analysis. For multivariate analysis, provided in Table 4, all variables with a $\mathrm{P}$ value $<0.2$ and additional presence of high-grade SAH (HH grade ${>}3$ ) in univariate analysis were considered. The location of single aneurysms was replaced in favor of the inclusion of a more global description of the anterior or posterior circulation. Multivariate analysis showed that rebleeding before treatment $\left(\mathrm{P}=0.03\right)^{}$ , treatment on the day of admission $\mathrm{\check{P}}=0.007$ ), and treatment at a nonlarge recruiting center $\ensuremath{\mathrm{P}}=0.002,$ ) remained significant risk factors for a treatment complication. Factors known to affect outcome, such as World Federation of Neurological Societies (WFNS), age, and the presence of intraventricular or intraparenchymal hemorrhage were not associated with the development of postprocedural complications.

# DISCUSSION  

The principal finding of this study is that intervention-associated complications occur frequently in patients with SAH. These aneurysm treatment complications play a pivotal role in determining the neurologic outcome of the patients. Risk factors in our study for the occurrence of complications were the day of the treatment, the aneurysm localization, a low amount of recruitment in the corresponding center, and recurrent hemorrhage before the definite treatment of the aneurysm.  



# Incidence of Treatment-Related Complications  

The present study highlights that treatment-associated bleeding and/or infarction occur frequently during the treatment of ruptures and aneurysms because approximately one fifth of the patients experienced a significant treatment complication. The data indicate that these complications adversely affect the neurologic outcomes of patients. The consistent incidence of complications underscores the need for identifying risk factors and implementing measures to enhance patient care. There is limited evidence of complications in treating acutely ruptured aneurysms. # Treatment Modality  

The present data indicate that clipping and coiling have comparable risk profiles regarding treatment-associated complications during the intervention. ISAT and BRAT investigated the treatment of ruptured aneurysms considered to be suitable for opensurgical and endovascular treatment equally, both predominantly in patients with good grades.6,11 This is a crucial difference from the present study, in which different aneurysms were treated endovascularly and surgically on the preference of the local centers. Therefore, based on this study, it cannot be evaluated whether clipping or coiling is the better treatment option. Of patients treated by endovascular coiling in ISAT, $73{\cdot}9\%$ showed a good neurologic outcome (defined as mRS score ${<}3,$ ) after 3 months and $76.5\%$ after 1 year. Of patients treated via surgical clipping, $63{\cdot}\mathbf{I}^{0}/\mathbf{0}$ and $69.1\%$ reported a good outcome after 3 months and 12 months, respectively. In the present study, $6\mathrm{r}.3\%$ of the patients showed a good neurologic outcome after 6 months. Therefore, the results seem to be fundamentally comparable. However, the patients in the current study showed a slightly worse outcome. A possible explanation might be that ISAT predominantly recruited patients with WFNS grade $\tau-2$ $(88\%)$ , whereas in the EARLYDRAIN trial, more than one third of the included patients had a SAH of WFNS grade 5. In BRAT, the distribution of initial hemorrhage severity and outcome was more nuanced, but again, only $5\%$ of patients were attributed to WFNS grade 5.

# Risk Factors for the Occurrence of Treatment-Associated Complications  

The data highlighted variations in complication incidence relative to the timing of treatment. Patients undergoing treatment on the day of admission, coinciding with the day of aneurysmal SAH, showed higher rates of hemorrhagic and ischemic complications. This observation remained significant even after adjusting for hemorrhage severity using the WFNS scale, suggesting that the increased complication rates are not solely attributable to the urgency of treating critically ill patients. Regarding the optimal timing for aneurysm treatment, there are no precise data. Treatment within the first $_{24^{-}4^{8}}$ hours is recommended.6 As a limitation, we acknowledge that we did not record the definite hour but only the day of treatment after hemorrhage in EARLYDRAIN. Based on the data, we cautiously advise against treating an aneurysm on the day of admission at least during nighttime, if clinically feasible, to reduce periprocedural complication rates. This advice is in line with a similar observation from the ULTRA (Ultra-Early Tranexamic Acid After Subarachnoid Haemorrhage) trial on the prophylactic use of tranexamic acid in SAH.19  

Furthermore, our results showed that treatment in a nonlarge recruiting center was a risk factor for experiencing a treatmentrelated complication. Although we do not have recruitment logs from all participating centers, this is at least supported by other studies20,21 that show that admission to high-volume centers is a predictor for good outcomes after SAH.  

The study identifies aneurysm location, rebleeding before treatment, treatment at nonlarge recruiting centers, and the treatment day as independent risk factors for complications during the management of ruptured aneurysms. Han et al.10 investigated the risk for intraoperative rupture and bleeding of  

116 rupture aneurysms during endovascular or microsurgical treatment via clipping in China. In this context, history of SAH, history of hypertension, maximum diameter of aneurysm, irregular form, and anterior communication aneurysm were identified as risk factors. Analysis concerning the intraprocedural rupture during endovascular treatment of ruptured and unruptured aneurysms showed aneurysm size and anterior communicating artery aneurysms as independent risk factors for intraprocedural rupture.22  



# Limitations  

In evaluating the results of this study, several limitations need to be considered. First, the last patient was included in 2016. This temporal gap presents a potential drawback because therapeutic methods may have advanced in the subsequent years. Another significant limitation is the absence of long-term data beyond 6 months, which might provide a more comprehensive understanding of the impact of the therapy over extended periods. During the design phase of the EARLYDRAIN multicenter trial, we were not aware of the relevance and frequency of intervention-associated complications, resulting in a lack of detailed data concerning the origin of the bleeding and/or infarction (e.g., perforator injury or parent vessel occlusion). This lack constitutes a significant limitation of the study and needs to be addressed in future research. Further, we did not investigate longterm complications such as aneurysm recurrence. A restriction in the design of the study was the recruitment criteria, which were limited to patients who were treated within the first 2 days after the bleeding event. Thus, it could not be evaluated if later therapy is advantageous or disadvantageous in terms of occurring complications. In addition, our study did not allow distinctions among various coiling techniques, which might affect the granularity of our results. Data on patients requiring stent-assisted coiling are not available, because these patients were excluded per protocol because required double-platelet anticoagulation prohibited insertion of a lumbar drain afterward. A further potential limitation of our study is that not all study centers provided a screening log. Based on the available data, we assume that approximately $40\%$ of the screened patients were recruited. Therefore, the number of included patients (large recruitment center) does not necessarily reflect the size of the treatment center. Because the treatment modality was chosen by the physicians, the analysis concerning the treatment modality is vulnerable to selection bias.  



# CONCLUSIONS  

The primary outcome of our study shows that patients with SAH frequently experience complications related to interventions. These complications arising from aneurysm treatment are instrumental in shaping the neurologic prognosis of these patients. Noteworthy risk factors in our cohort that contributed to the emergence of these complications include the timing of the treatment, the location of the aneurysm, the recruitment contribution of the enrolling center, and incidents of rebleeding before treatment of the aneurysm.
