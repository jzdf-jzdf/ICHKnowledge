Background and purpose: In this post-hoc analysis using acute dual study dataset, the impacts of cerebral microbleeds (MBs) after mild stroke on clinical outcome were investigated.   
Methods: The number of MBs on admission was categorized as 1) no MBs, 2) MBs 1–4, 3) MBs 5–9, and 4) $\mathrm{MBS}\geq10$ . The efficacy outcome was defined as neurological deterioration and stroke recurrence within 14 days. Safety outcomes included ICH and/or SAH as well as extracranial hemorrhages.   
Results: Of the 1102 patients, 780 (71%) had no MBs on admission, while 230 $(21\%)$ had MBs 1–4, 48 $(4\%)$ had MBs 5–9, and 44 $(4\%)$ had ${\mathrm{MBS}}\geq10$ . The number of MBs was not associated with the neurological deterioration and/or stroke recurrence $\mathbf{\tilde{\rho}}=0.934\mathbf{\tilde{\rho}}$ , ICH and/or SAH $\mathbf{\hat{p}}=0.743;$ , and extracranial hemorrhage $\ensuremath{\mathbf{\bar{p}}}=0.205\$ . Favorable outcome was seem in $84\%$ in the No MBs group, $83\%$ in the MBs 1–4, $94\%$ in the MBs 5–9, and $85\%$ in the ${\mathrm{MBS}}\geq10$ $\ensuremath{\mathbf{{p}}}=0.304\$ . Combined cilostazol and aspirin therapy did not alter any rates of efficacy and safety outcomes among the no MBs, MBs 1–4, MBs 5–9, and ${\mathrm{MBS}}\geq10$ groups compared to aspirin alone (all $\mathrm{p}>0.05\$ ). By multivariate regression analysis, a history of ICH and diastolic blood pressure were the independent parameters to all of the MBs criteria (presence, ${\mathrm{MBs~}}\geq{5}$ , and $\mathrm{MBS}\geq10$ ).  

Conclusions: MBs did not alter the clinical outcome at 3 months of onset. Elevated diastolic blood pressure and a history of ICH were the essential parameters related to the MBs.

# 1. Introduction  

Cerebral microbleeds (MBs), shown on gradient-echo $\mathrm{T}2^{*}$ weighted magnetic resonance imaging (MRI), are common phenomena in patients with acute stroke as well as in the healthy elderly population. MBs are histologically characterized as hemosiderin deposits around small cerebral arteries due to lipohyalinosis and cerebral amyloid angiopathy [1,2]. Patients with MBs have a high cumulative risk of intracerebral hemorrhage (ICH) compared to those without it. In addition, MBs were associated with aging, hypertension, and dementia, which are considered as parameters of poor outcome [3]. Therefore, the MBs itself and the clinical background of MBs have been presumed to increase the risk of cerebral hemorrhage as well as various unfavorable outcomes.  

In the acute stroke setting, the risk of ICH has been a major concern for physicians in the administration of antiplatelet therapy, especially in patients with MBs. Indeed, although recent large, randomized trials have shown that dual antiplatelet therapy (DAPT) using aspirin and clopidogrel improved the clinical outcome at 3 months compared to aspirin alone [4,5], a possible increase in moderate to severe bleeding has been reported [6]. Cilostazol, another antiplatelet drug, is a reversible phosphodiesterase type 3 enzyme inhibitor that promotes vasodilation and the inhibition of platelet activation and aggregation through the increase of cyclic adenosine monophosphate [7]. Our previous randomized trial (acute dual study: ADS) failed to show the efficacy of combined cilostazol and aspirin therapy [8], but cilostazol has been prescribed for patients with carotid stenosis [9], peripheral artery disease [10], and stroke at the chronic stage [11], especially in Asian counties.  

Therefore, in this post-hoc study using the dataset of the ADS, we investigated whether the presence and the number of MBs were associated with short-term neurological changes and clinical outcomes, and if the safety and efficacy outcomes of DAPT using cilostazol and aspirin were altered among patients with and without MBs. We also attempted to clarify the clinical characteristics associated with patients with MBs.  

# 2. Methods  

# 2.1. ADS registry  

This post-hoc research extracted the patient data from the ADS registry, a multicenter, prospective, randomized, open-label trial that compared the safety and efficacy of acute aspirin plus cilostazol dual therapy for acute mild stroke within $48\mathrm{~h~}$ of the symptom onset. Briefly, the ADS trial was performed between May 2011 and June 2017, involving 34 centers in Japan. After MRI examination including $\mathrm{T}2^{*}$ , patients were randomly allocated to the DAPT group or the aspirin group. The DAPT group was treated with cilostazol $(200\mathrm{mg/day)}$ and aspirin $\mathrm{80{-}200mg/day)}$ for 14 days, while the aspirin group was treated with only aspirin $\mathrm{\prime80{-}200mg/day)}$ for 14 days. Because neurological status frequently deteriorated within the acute phase and long-term dual therapy might lead to significant hemorrhagic complications, the periods of dual therapy were defined as 2 weeks. After 2 weeks, cilostazol $\mathrm{200~mg)}$ was continued for 3 months after onset. Concomitant warfarin, clopidogrel, ticlopidine, sarpogrelate, and urokinase were prohibited, whereas argatroban, ozagrel, heparin, edaravone, glycerin, and low molecular weight dextran were permitted only when local investigators considered necessary within 2 weeks. The inclusion criteria of the ADS were as follows: age $\geq18$ years old, noncardioembolic stroke, defined by the Trial of Org 10,172 in Acute Stroke Treatment (TOAST) criteria [12], presented within $48\mathrm{~h~}$ of symptom onset, neurological deficits with a National Institutes of Health Stroke Scale (NIHSS) score $<20$ , and a pre-morbid modified Rankin Scale score of 0–2 [8].  

# 2.2. Study purpose  

This retrospective study included three major aims as follows.  

(1) We evaluated whether the number of MBs was associated with the efficacy and safety outcomes as well as clinical outcome at 3 months from onset.  

(2) We studied whether the efficacy and safety of DAPT using cilostazol and aspirin were consistent regardless of the number of MBs. The primary efficacy outcome was similar to that of ADS, defined as neurological deterioration (with an NIHSS score $\mathsf{of}\geq2$ ) and stroke recurrence within 14 days. The clinical outcome at 3 months was also evaluated. Safety outcomes were also similar to those of ADS, as ICH, and/or subarachnoid hemorrhage (SAH) and extracranial hemorrhage.  

(3) Finally, we investigated whether the independent patient characteristics differed based on the presence or number of MBs.  

# 2.3. Inclusion and exclusion criteria for this post-hoc analysis  

We only included patients with acute non-cardioembolic ischemic stroke who received the 14-day assessment. Therefore, patients who did not receive the 14-day assessment were excluded. Patients diagnosed with cardioembolic stroke after admission, transient ischemic attack, or other neurological disease at discharge were also excluded.  

# 2.4. Number of the MBs  

In accordance with previous studies, MBs defined as homogeneous rounded lesions were calculated using $\mathrm{T}2^{*}$ on admission. Previously, $\mathrm{MB}~\geq~5$ was reported to outweigh the benefits of antithrombotic agents [13]. An increased hazard ratio for ICH with increasing MBs classified based on the number as $2{-}4,\geq5,\geq10$ and $\geq20$ were also presented [14]. In this post-hoc study, after modifying these criteria, the number of MBs was categorized as 1) no MBs, 2) MBs 1–4, 3) MBs 5–9, and 4) ${\mathrm{MBS}}\geq10$ .  

# 2.5. Collected variables  

The number and location of MBs (divided into 4 groups: cortical/subcortical, basal ganglia, cerebellum, and brain stem), parameters regarding age, sex, initial neurological deficits scored using the NIHSS, previous medical history, allocated group in the ADS (DAPT and aspirin groups), systolic and diastolic blood pressure, neuro-image on admission, laboratory findings, and clinical outcomes were reviewed. Previous medical histories regarding previous ischemic stroke, previous ICH, hypertension, and diabetes mellitus were examined. Aspirin therapy before onset was also evaluated. Neuroimaging findings on MRI, MR angiography (MRA), and ultrasound examination on admission included number of infarcts (multiple, single, or none), infarct site (cortex, subcortical white matter, internal capsule, cerebellum, thalamus, midbrain, pontine, and medulla), infarct size $:\leq1.5~\mathrm{cm}$ 1.5– $3.0~\mathrm{cm}$ , or $>3.0~\mathrm{cm}$ ), and arterial lesions including symptomatic intracranial and carotid stenosis/occlusion $(\geq50\%)$ . Periventricular hyperintensity (PVH) and deep and subcortical white matter hyperintensity (DSWMH) were graded as reported [15]. Laboratory data on creatinine was collected, and the estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR) was calculated.  

# 2.6. Statistical analyses  

First, the clinical characteristics and imaging findings were compared among the four groups based on the number of MBs (no MBs, MBs 1–4, MBs 5–9, and ${\mathrm{MBS}}\geq10$ ). And, the association of the number of MBs with efficacy outcome, and safety outcomes was investigated. Clinical outcome at 3 months, including favorable outcome, defined as mRS 0–2 was also assessed. Next, we evaluated whether the efficacy and safety of DAPT using cilostazol and aspirin were consistent regardless of the number of MBs. Finally, we evaluated the independent parameters related to ${\mathrm{MBs~}}\geq~1$ , ${\mathrm{MBs~}}\geq{\mathrm{~5~}}$ , and ${\mathrm{MBs~}}\geq10$ . The Mann–Whitney $U$ test was used to analyze differences in continuous variables, and Fisher’s exact test and Pearson chi-square were used to analyze differences in categorical variables. The data are presented as median values (interquartile range [IQR]) or frequencies $(\%)$ . Variables identified on univariate analyses with p values $<0.1$ were entered into the multivariate analysis. The relative risks were expressed as odds ratios (OR) with $95\%$ confidence intervals (CIs). All statistical analyses were performed using the SPSS software program, version 22 (SPSS Japan, Inc., Tokyo, Japan). Results were considered statistically significant at $\mathtt{p}<0.05$ .  

# 3. Results  

Between February 2011 and March 2017, 1,208 patients were enrolled in the ADS trial. Seven patients withdrew their consent after the start of the study. Twelve patients did not receive the assessment on the 14th day assessment. Information on MBs was missing in two patients. Atrial fibrillation was found during hospitalization in 41 patients. At discharge, 11 patients were diagnosed with cardioembolic stroke without atrial fibrillation, 28 with transient ischemic attack, and 5 with other neurological diseases. Finally, 1,102 patients (737 [67%] men; median age [interquartile range], 68 [60–77] years; initial NIHSS score, 2 [1–4] with noncardioembolic stroke were analyzed.  

# 3.1. Efficacy and safety outcomes among the four groups based on the number of MBs  

Table 1 shows the clinical, imaging, and laboratory findings of the MB subgroups. Seven hundred and eighty patients $(71\%)$ had no MBs on admission, 230 $(21\%)$ had MBs 1–4, 48 $(4\%)$ had MBs 5–9, and 44 $(4\%)$ had ${\mathrm{MBS~}}\geq10$ . Age $(\mathbf{p}=0.019)$ , previous stroke histories of ischemic stroke $\mathbf{\chi}^{\prime}\mathbf{p}<0.001$ ) and ICH $(\mathtt{p}<0.001\$ ), and hypertension $\mathbf{\tilde{\rho}}=0.001$ ) were significantly different among the four groups based on the number of MBs. Blood pressure (both systolic $\left[{\ttp}=0.002\right]$ and diastolic $[\mathsf{p}<0.001]$ ), and the degree of white matter signal changes (PVH $[\mathsf{p}<0.001]$ , and DSWMH $[{\ttp}<0.001]$ ) as well as eGFR $\mathbf{\chi}^{\prime}\mathbf{p}<0.001\$ were also inconsistent among the four groups. Table 2 shows the stroke subtypes and information on MRI and MRA findings, except for the MBs parameters. Only infarct size $\mathbf{\tilde{\rho}}=0.019.$ differed among the MBs subgroups.  

Fig. 1 shows the rates of efficacy and safety outcomes among the No MBs, MBs 1–4, MBs 5–9, and ${\mathrm{MBS}}\geq10$ groups. We found that the number of MBs was not associated with neurological deterioration and/or stroke recurrence $(\mathbf{p}=0.934)$ , ICH and/or SAH $(\mathbf{p}=0.743)$ , nor extracranial hemorrhage $\mathbf{\nabla}_{\mathbf{\cdot}}\mathbf{p}=0.205,$ . We also compared these outcomes to evaluate the impact of DAPT using cilostazol and aspirin. The rates of primary efficacy outcome, neurological deterioration, and stroke recurrence within 14 days were not different between the combined cilostazol and aspirin therapy group and aspirin group when any of the cut-off number of MBs was applied (no MBs, $\mathrm{~p~}=~0.491$ ; MBs 1–4, $\mathrm{~p~}=~0.684$ ; MBs 5–9, $\mathbf{p}=0.660$ ; $\mathrm{MBS}\geq10$ , $\mathbf{p}=1.000;$ . Safety outcomes were also not different, as presented in Table 3A. Furthermore, these results were consistent when the locations of MBs were taken into consideration (Table 3B).  

Clinical outcomes were evaluated in 1,037 patients who were followed up after 3 months. The rates of favorable outcomes were not different among the MB subgroups. Indeed, 613 $(84\%)$ of 732 patients in the No MBs group, 180 $(83\%)$ of 218 in the MBs 1–4 group, 44 $(94\%)$ of 47 in the MBs 5–9 group, and 34 $(85\%)$ of 40 in the ${\mathrm{MBs}}\geq10$ group had a favorable outcome $\mathbf{\chi}_{\mathrm{~p~}}^{\prime}=0.304\rangle$ . In addition, the locations of MBs were not related to the favorable outcome. A total of 170 $(86\%)$ of 198 patients with MBs in the basal ganglia and 701 $(84\%)$ of the 839 patients without MBs in the basal ganglia $\mathbf{\tilde{p}}=0.453$ ) had favorable outcomes. Similarly, 122 $(83\%)$ of 147 with MBs at cortical/subcortical lesions and 749 $(84\%)$ of 890 without cortical/subcortical lesions had favorable outcomes $\mathbf{\chi}^{\prime}\mathbf{p}=0.716\$ . Furthermore, 51 $(82\%)$ of 62 patients with MBs in the cerebellum, 820 $(84\%)$ of 975 without MBs in the cerebellum $\mathbf{\chi}_{\mathrm{}}^{\prime}\mathbf{p}=0.721;$ , 48 $(80\%)$ of 60 with MBs in the brain stem, and 823 $(84\%)$ of 977 without MBs in the brain stem $\mathbf{\chi}_{\mathbf{p}}=0.367\$ ) had favorable outcomes.

# 3.2. Parameters related to the presence and the number of MBs  

Clinical, imaging, and laboratory findings on the MB subgroups based on the three different cut-off amounts of MBs are presented in Table 4. Age was statistically associated with the presence of  

MBs $\mathbf{\chi}_{\mathbf{p}}=0.012,$ , but the difference disappeared when different cut-off MBs $\geq5$ and $\geq10$ ) were applied $({\ttp}=0.615$ and 0.825, respectively). In contrast, the impacts of a previous ischemic stroke, previous ICH, systolic blood pressure, diastolic blood pressure, and white matter changes (PVH and DSWMH) were consistent regardless of the cut-off MB amount $(\mathtt{p}<0.05)$ When we focused on the imaging findings, infarct location played a significant role in the presence or absence of MBs. The proportions of cortical infarct $({\bfp}=0.039)$ and medulla infarct $(\mathtt{p}=0.034)$ were slightly higher in patients without MBs than in patients who had MBs. A small infarct defined ${\ttas}<1.5~\mathrm{cm}$ was frequently observed in patients with MBs than in those without it $\mathbf{\chi}_{\mathbf{p}}=0.001\mathbf{\chi}.$ ), and this difference was also apparent when another MB cut-off $(\ge5)$ was applied $({\bfp}=0.011).$ ), although it was not observed when ${\mathrm{MBS}}\geq10$ were used $\mathbf{\rho}(\mathbf{p}=0.076)$ .Table 5.  

Finally, we conducted a multivariate regression analysis to evaluate the independent parameters related to ${\ttMB}\geq1$ , ${\ttMB}\geq5$ , and ${\mathrm{MB}}\geq10$ . Nine parameters including previous ischemic stroke $\mathbf{\chi}_{\mathrm{p}}^{\prime}=0.009,$ ), previous ICH $\mathbf{\dot{p}}=0.002)$ , hypertension $\mathbf{\chi}_{\mathbf{p}}=0.023,$ , diastolic blood pressure $({\bfp}~=~0.030)\$ , PVH $(\mathtt{p}~=~0.006)$ , DSWMH $({\bfp}=0.039)_{.}$ ), eGFR $\mathbf{\tilde{\rho}}=0.035\$ , infarct size $<1.5~\mathrm{cm}$ $({\bfp}=0.003)$ , and medulla infarct $\mathbf{\zeta}_{\mathbf{p}}=0.040^{\ '}$ were statistically significantly associated with the presence of MBs. However, the number of independent parameters decreased as the cut-off MBs increased. Six parameters were related to the ${\bfMBS}\geq5$ , namely, previous ischemic stroke $(\mathbf{p}=0.020)$ , previous ICH $(\mathbf{p}<0.001)$ , DSWMH $(\mathtt{p}=0.046)$ , eGFR $\mathbf{\chi}_{\mathbf{p}}=0.010\rangle$ , diastolic blood pressure $(\mathbf{p}=0.002)$ , and infarct size $<1.5~\mathrm{cm}$ $\mathbf{\tilde{p}}=0.045,$ . When we focused on patients with $\mathrm{MBS}\geq10$ , only three parameters, including the previous ICH $\mathbf{\chi}^{\prime}\mathbf{p}=0.001$ ), diastolic blood pressure $\mathbf{\dot{p}}=0.026,$ , and pontine infarct $\mathbf{\tilde{p}}=0.027,$ , were observed to play a statistically significant role.

# 4. Discussion  

Neither presence, number of MBs, nor the location of MBs were associated with short-term neurological changes and clinical outcomes. The rates of neurological deterioration and stroke recurrence, as well as favorable outcomes at 3 months, were not different among the MB subgroups. Recent meta-analyses and studies have shown that a ${\mathrm{MBS~}}\geq10$ may increase the incidence of sICH after intravenous thrombolysis [16] and unfavorable outcome after intra-arterial thrombectomy [17]. However, the effect of MBs among patients with mild stroke has been lacking. One paper from Japan, including 240 patients, reported that the presence of MBs was a predictive factor of poor outcome in minor stroke patients [18] Our finding conflicted with this study, but the strength of our study is the large cohort from multiple stroke centers. Further data from different cohorts are needed to conclude the impact of MBs on clinical outcomes after mild stroke.  

A previous post-hoc analysis of the acute DAPT study using clopidogrel and aspirin showed that the increase in MBs was significantly correlated with the existence of primary MBs, especially that of primary existing MBs [19]. In addition, chronic antiplatelet therapy in patients with large amounts of MBs could lose the benefits of antiplatelets by increasing the risk of ICH [20]. Therefore, we presumed that there might be a risk of acute DAPT using cilostazol and aspirin in patients with MBs. However, there were no additional risks of ICH and extracranial hemorrhage in patients treated using combined cilostazol and aspirin even when they had MBs of ${\geq}10$ . We should be sure that our study finding does not apply to all DAPT regimens, but at least, based on our study findings, we may not have to take the presence, number, and location of MBs when we administer acute DAPT using cilostazol and aspirin for 14 days.  

Pathologically, MBs represent a rupture of the small cerebral artery induced by lipohyalinosis (1) as well as cerebral amyloid antipathy. (2) Similar to these reports, our study showed that a history of ICH was another independent factor of both the presence and the amount of MBs. A review analysis calculated the prevalence of MBs as 60 $1(57-64)\%$ in patients with previous ICH, which was higher than 34 (31–36) $\%$ in patients with previous ischemic stroke and 5 $(4-6)\%$ in healthy controls. (3) However, there are still several unsolved problems regarding the association between previous ICH and MBs. The etiology of ICH as well as the location of ICH, coexistence of SAH, and intraventricular hemorrhage have not been taken into consideration. In addition, although the MBs in the subcortical lesion and basal ganglia were reported to correlate well with the MBs, infratentorial MBs were not strongly associated with ICH [21].  

Our study findings indicate that the parameters associated with MBs were not consistent among the degree of MBs. Similar to previous studies, hypertension, (3) a history of ischemic stroke [22], white matter disease [22], and renal dysfunction [23] were related to the MBs. Infarct defined as ${<}1.5\mathrm{cm}$ and the absence of medullar infarct, which are representative of small blood vessel disease, are also related to their presence and ${\mathrm{MBs}}\geq5$ . Indeed, among Asian population, dissection is a common cause of medullar infarct. In contrast, pontine infarct was only statistically associated with $\mathrm{MB}\geq10$ , apart from diastolic blood pressure and a history of ICH. This may imply that patients with small vessel disease in the posterior circulation have a severe degree of hypertension.  

Several limitations associated with the present study warrant mention. First, this was a retrospective analysis of a prospective multicenter trial. Second, the majority of our cohort consisted of patients with mild stroke. Third, our cohort consisted of only Asian people who did not receive intravenous thrombolysis or endovascular thrombectomy. Finally, we were unable to analyze the impact of MBs based on the location of MBs in the multivariate regression analysis because patients with multiple MBs had them at multiple locations.  

In conclusion, the presence and number of MBs did not alter the short-term neurological change and the clinical outcome at 3 months of onset, even when DAPT using cilostazol and aspirin was administered. Elevated diastolic blood pressure and a history of ICH were the universal parameters of both the presence and the number of MBs.
