# Effect of Electroacupuncture on the Treatment of Pneumonia in Patients with Hypertensive Intracerebral Hemorrhage  

Aiyu Jiang',Wanzhen $w_{U}\-\l$ Liling $M\pmb{a}^{\prime}$ ，Miaofang Yan²,Zhulin Zhao',Qinping Chen²  

OBJECTIVE:Pneumonia is a serious postoperative complication of  hypertensive  intracerebral  hemorrhage (HICH), and there is no specific treatment for pneumonia. In this study, we conducted randomized controlled trials to evaluate the effects of electroacupuncture (EA) on the treatment of pneumonia in patients with HiCH.  

METHODS: An equal number of patients with HICH complicated  with  pneumonia $(\mathfrak{n}~=~80\$ in total)were randomly placed in either the EA group (EA treatment and routine basic treatment) or the control group (routine basic treatment). After 14 days of treatment, clinical symptoms and signs, blood oxygen saturation, the level of inflammatory factors, the effective rate, the scores of the Barthel Index, National Institutes of Health Stroke Scale and Glasgow Coma Scale, the hospitalization time, and expenses were compared between the groups.  

RESULTS: The general information of the patients in the control and EA groups were similar. After 14 days of intervention, the patients in the EA group showed better symptom and sign scores, blood oxygen saturation levels, Barthel Index scores, Glasgow Coma Scale scores, and National Institutes of Health Stroke Scale scores than the patients in the control group. Furthermore, the EA treatment also lowered the levels of inflammatory factors and white blood cell count. Additionally, the patients in the EA group showed higher effective rates than those in the control group.  

CONCLUSIONS: EA benefits the treatment of pneumonia in patients with HicH.

Acupuncture is an integral part of a 25oo-year-old Eastern medical system and has been used as a treatment option. By conducting a nationwide retrospective cohort study, Chuen et al found that the incidence of pneumonia in stroke patients administered acupuncture treatment decreased significantly compared to that in patients administered sham acupuncture, and the decreased risk of pneumonia was significantly associated with acupuncture therapy.f Animal and clinical studies have demonstrated that acupuncture is effective  in improving dyspnea, lung function, and overall quality of life in patients with pulmonary inflammatory diseases,1? especially in patients with pneumonia.13,14 Acupuncture can also effectively improve dyspnea in patients with chronic obstructive pulmonary disease and asthma.15 “Tongyuan" acupuncture at the acupoints can improve cough reflexes, help remove sputum, and relieve pulmonary inflammation.16 Acupuncture treatment at LU-1, CVI7,ST- $36$ , and SP-6 acupoints showed anti-inflammatory effects on animals suffering from asthma.17 Electroacupuncture (EA) is a type of acupuncture that involves the use of a small electrical current that is passed between pairs of acupuncture needles. This technique is based on traditional acupuncture principles but adds the use of electrical stimulation to enhance the effects of the treatment.8 In general, EA is often used for conditions that are more severe or chronic than those that are typically treated with traditional acupuncture.19 During EA, the needles are inserted into specific points on the body, just as in traditional acupuncture. Once the needles are in place, a small electrical current is passed through them, usually for about 10— 6o minutes. The current is delivered at different frequencies and intensities, depending on the condition being treated and the individual patient's needs.2o,21 However, studies on the effects of EA on pneumonia after surgery for HICH are limited.  

In this study,we evaluated the clinical effectiveness of EA stimulation in treating pneumonia, by measuring changes in the effective rate, inflammation, and clinical symptoms. Our findings support the postsurgical treatment of pneumonia using EA in patients with HICH.  

# MATERIALS ANDMETHODS  

# Study Design  

We conducted a randomized controlled trial at Zhejiang Hospital from August 2018 to December 2021. The participants were randomly allocated to either the EA group (routine basic treatment plusEA, $\mathrm{~n~}=45,$ )or the control group (routine basic treatment, $\mathrm{~n~}=45,$ 0.All participants received 14 sessions of treatment over I4days.  

# Participants  

The study included 472 patients with HICH who underwent minimally invasive intracerebral hemorrhage evacuation at our hospital between August 2021 and December 2022. Inclusion criteria were as follows: 1) first incidence of HICH, and compliance with the diagnostic code for $\mathrm{HICH}^{22,23}$ , 2) age over 18 years, 3) signs of lung infection or pneumonia, as determined by imaging examinations (chest X-rayand/or computed tomography scan), 4） history of hypertension before cerebral hemorrhage, 5) underwent minimally invasive intracerebral hemorrhage evacuation,6） complete clinical information available, 7) hospitalized for at least 3o days during the index stroke admission. Exclusion criteria were as follows: 1) neurological diseases, 2) severe heart, liver, or kidney disease, 3) history of mental illness, 4) other infections besides PIs, such as abdominal cavity infections, urinary tract infections, or intracranial infections, 5) participation in other clinical trials. The study was approved by the Institutional Ethics Committee of Zhejiang hospital. All participants signed a document providing informed consent before participating in the study.  

# Randomization  

The randomization was performed by an investigator. The participants were randomly assigned to the control or EA group. Another investigator used a random number generator (https:// www.randomizer.org/) to assign participants to each group with block randomization, based on a I:I ratio.  

# Blinding  

We used a single-blinded design. The investigators were blinded to group allocation and were not involved in recruitment and treatment procedures, and the statistician analyzed the data without having any information on allocation. Acupuncturists were not blinded to the participants allotted to the EA group so that they could administer acupuncture treatment.  

# Methods  

Based on the guidelines of the Chinese Clinical Practice and Expert Consensus of Emergency Severe Pneumonia (2o16), the patients in the control group received routine basic treatment, including regulating blood pressure and blood sugar, antinfection treatment, and the maintaining of optimum intracranial pressure. The patients were also simultaneously administered supportive symptomatic treatment with atomization, sputum drainage, and oxygen inhalation nursing, depending on their condition.  

The patients in the EA group received EA stimulation based on routine treatments. Sterile disposable stainless steel needles (diameter: $0.25\mathrm{mm}$ ; length: 4o mm; Hwatuo,Suzhou, China) were inserted into the Sanyinjiao $(\mathrm{SP}36)$ , Neiguan (PC6), Fengchi (GB20), Qihai (RN6), Taixi $(\mathrm{KI}_{3})$ ，Zhongwan (RN12), Feishu (BL13), and Fenglong (ST40) points to a depth of $25\mathrm{-}35$ mm. Then, an electric stimulator (Hwato SDZ-IIB electronic acupuncture treatment instrument; Suzhou Medical Appliance Factory; Suzhou) was used to stimulate the acupoints (current intensity: $2~\mathrm{mA}$ ,wave: 5o Hz). The acupoints and the stimulator are illustrated in Figure 1A and B. The EA treatment started on the day PI was diagnosed, and was routinely administered for 3o minutes once a day for 14 days following previous research and our longterm clinical practice.24  

# Observation Indices  

Efficacy Evaluation Criteria. The efficacy evaluation criteria were as follows?5: highly effective: normal body temperature, thin sputum (white or light-colored), reduction in sputum volume by more than two thirds, normal level of blood indicators, chest X-ray absorption $>50\%$ compared to that before treatment; effective: normal body temperature; thin sputum (white or light-colored), reduction in sputum volume by more than two thirds, a reduction in blood indicator levels, chest X-ray absorption ${>}20\%$ compared to that before treatment; ineffective: no change in the above indices. The total effective rate was the sum of the highly effective rate and the effective rate.

Hospitalization Time and Expenses. The clinical indices, including the total hospitalization time and expenses for the patients in the control and EA groups, were recorded and compared.  

# Statistical Analysis  

The Statistical Product and Service Solutions 25.o (SPSS 25.o; IBM Corp., Armonk, NY) statistical software was used for performing statistical analysis. The Shapiro-Wilk normal distribution test was conducted to confirm that the data followed a normal distribution. The parametric data were analyzed by conducting Student t test for differences between the groups. The nonparametric data were analyzed by performing the Mann-Whitney U test for differences between the groups. The categorical data were analyzed by performing the $\chi^{2}$ test and Fisher exact test. Covariance analysis was performed to compare the data of the 2 groups after treatment. All differences between the groups were considered to be statistically significant at $\mathrm{~P~}<0.05$

# DISCUSSION  

Pneumonia is an extremely serious postoperative complication of HICH. However, no specific treatment strategy is known to combat pneumonia. Therefore, a more efficient way to prevent and treat pneumonia needs to be determined.  

Several clinical studies have shown that serum proinflammatory cytokines, including IL-6, TNF- $\alpha$ , and C-reactive protein, released after PI are closely related to pulmonary inflammation,29,30 and cytokine levels are positively correlated with disease severity.31 The levels of inflammatory cytokines were measured to determine the effects of EA on pneumonia, as indicated by the results of ELISA. The TNF- $\cdota$ ,hs-CRP, and IL-6 levels decreased significantly in the patients after EA treatment, indicating that EA effectively regulated the levels of inflammatoryfactors in the serum. A meta-analysis of randomized clinical trials conducted by Li et al showed that acupuncture at acupoints could modulate the secretion of inflammatory mediators, such as hs-CRP, TNF- $\alpha$ ,and IL-6, in rheumatoid arthritis3?; these findings were similar to our results regarding pneumonia. The effect of EA on PI could be attributed to the stimulation of the Zusanli (ST36), considering that $\mathrm{ST}36$ is a key anti-inflammatory EA point.33,34 We also found that EA treatment at Sanyinjiao $(\mathrm{SP}36)$ , Neiguan (PC6), Fengchi (GB20), Qihai (RN6), Taixi (KI3), Zhongwan (RN12), Feishu (BLi3), and Fenglong (ST4o) could also alleviate infection—induced pulmonary  inflammation.  These  findings elucidated more effective therapeutic acupoints of EA for patients with HICH with pneumonia.  

We also determined the level of $\mathrm{SpO}_{2}$ , which is a strong predictor of the severity of PI. Acupuncture can increase the partial pressure of oxygen and ${\mathrm{SpO}}_{2}$ which can improve the breathing function of patients.35 The $\mathrm{SpO}_{2}$ level in the EA group was higher than that in the control group after treatment, suggesting that EA can help in recovering the pulmonary functions of patients. Unlike studies on bronchial asthma,35 the Taixi (KI3) point was considered in this study. The Taixi (KI3) point, a Yuan-source point of kidney meridians, is clinically selected for relieving dyspnea or shallow breathing. The results show that EA can effectively improve the respiratory function ofpatients with postoperative pneumonia and promote the recovery of lung function, which may be effective means to treat pneumonia.  

Several animal experiments and clinical trials have shown acupuncture can effectively relieve common respiratory symptoms, such as cough, breathlessness, and expectoration in asthma,36 chronic obstructive pulmonary disease,37 and pneumoconiosis,38 but studies on the effect of EA on PI in patients with HICH are limited. We found that the effective rate increased significantly after EA treatment. Compared with the Ctrl group, the scores of breathing, coughing, expectoration, wheezing, panting, and rales decreased significantly in the EA group after intervention. Reversible airflow obstruction causes repeated shortness of breath, coughing, and wheezing.39 Acupuncture may not only lower the nasal resistance to air-flow in patients, thereby improving respiratory symptoms, but also enhance the immune system function of patients to reduce phlegm.4° Unlike previous studies on other diseases,36,38 we focused on more clinical respiratory symptoms, such as wheezing and rales. Our results confirmed the protective effect of EA on lung infection and provided more information on postoperative care of patients with HICH.

Acupuncture application in patients with intracerebral hemorrhage showed good clinical efficacy and functional improvement.4 To assess the function of EA in the recovery of postoperative brain function, the BI, GCS, and NIHSS scores were recorded and analyzed in this study. The scores for BI, GCS, and NIHSS were highly significant after administering EA. These results showed that EA significantly altered the BI, GCS, and NIHSS scores, indicating that EA treatment can effectively enhance the postoperative recovery of brain function. Acupuncture therapy at Quchi (LI11), Neiguan (PC6), Zusanli $(\mathrm{ST}36)$ ，Sanyinjiao (SP6)， and Taichong $(\mathrm{LR}3)$ can improve neurological deficit scores in hypertensive cerebral hemorrhage at an early stage.4² Similarly, patients with subarachnoid hemorrhage who received acupuncture at Zusanli $(\mathrm{ST}36)$ and Neiguan (PC6) points experienced a significantly better function of cerebral vasospasm43; these results were similar to our findings regarding the application of EA at the Zusanli $(\mathrm{ST}36)$ and Neiguan (PC6) points. However, we applied EA instead of manual acupuncture. Electrical stimulation is a major basis of EA application. A general electrical stimulation can generate an electric field of an appropriate intensity at a suitable time.44 The high electric field intensity can stimulate the targeted brain area and affect neural activity,45 which in turn can help in the recovery of brain function. Therefore, the effect of EA on promoting the recovery of postoperative brain function might involve an electric field, and our future studies might focus on the preventive effect of EA on avoiding pneumonia, as well as the effects of different current intensities on brain recovery. Overall, our results showed that EA directly promotes the recovery of postoperative brain function and indirectly facilitates the recovery of lung function and the decrease in lung injury. Our findings might provide new insights into the therapeutic effects of EA on treating pneumonia in patients with HICH.  

Our study had several limitations. First, other criteria for efficacy evaluation, such as the neurological deficit score and daily living, should be considered in the study along with the BI, GCS, and NIHSS scores. Second, parameters of pulmonary function, including maximal voluntary ventilation, residual volume, total lung capacity, vital capacity, and forced vital capacity, need to be determined. Third, the study might have insufficient statistical power for evaluating the effective rate, considering that the sample size was determined based on primary outcomes. Additionally, the preventative effects of EA on pneumonia in patients with HICH should be investigated in further studies in the future.  

In conclusion,EA can be used to treat patients with HICH suffering from pneumonia, as it can increase the effective rate, decrease inflammatory factor levels, and alleviate clinical symptoms. Thus, EA is an efficient and cost-effective approach for the clinical treatment of PI and improving the prognosis of patients with HICH, and hence should be promoted for clinical application.
