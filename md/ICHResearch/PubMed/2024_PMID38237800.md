# Targeted Temperature Management for Poor Grade Aneurysmal Subarachnoid Hemorrhage: A Pilot Study  

Yang Liu1, Yanru Li2, Bingsha Han2, Leikai Mei3, Pengzhao Zhang3, Jiaqi Zhang3, Mengyuan $\chi_{U^{3}}$ , Min $\mathbf{\Delta}Gao^{3}.$ Guang Feng2  

- OBJECTIVE: We assessed the effectiveness and safety of target temperature management (TTM) in treating patients with poor-grade aneurysmal subarachnoid hemorrhage (aSAH). The primary objective was to evaluate the neurological outcome at 3 months. Secondary objectives were to assess mortality, delayed cerebral ischemia, cerebral edema, hydrocephalus, midline shift, and laboratory indicators related to TTM.  

- METHODS: A single-blind, nonrandomized controlled trial was conducted. After admission, patients with poorgrade aSAH (Hunt-Hess scores $|\nabla\sim\nabla)$ were assigned to a TTM group or a control group in a 1:1 ratio. TTM with core temperatures ranging from $36^{\circ}C$ to $37^{\circ}\mathbb{C}$ was performed immediately and maintained until microclipping or endovascular embolization. Subsequently, rapid induction to $33^{\circ}\mathtt{C}\sim35^{\circ}\mathtt{C}$ was carried out and maintained for 3 to 5 days. Then, the patients underwent slow rewarming to $36^{\circ}C$ $\sim37^{\mathrm{{o}}}\mathtt{C}$ and maintained at that temperature for a minimum of 48 hours.  

- RESULTS: Sixty patients (30 treated with TTM and 30 with standard treatment) were included in the study. At 3 months, a favorable prognosis (modified Rankin scale score 0 to 3) was significantly higher in the TTM group than in the control group $(\mathtt{n}=14,$ $46.7\%$ vs. $\mathtt{n}=6,$ $20.0\%,$ $P=$ 0.028). Adjusted multivariate logistics regression analysis indicated that TTM (odds ratio $=~0.20$ , $95\%$ confidence interval: 0.05e0.77, $P=0.019)$ reduced the number of unfavorable prognoses 3 months after admission.  

- CONCLUSIONS: This study demonstrated the effectiveness and safety of TTM in patients with poor-grade aSAH, and its implementation improved neurological outcomes. Multicenter randomized controlled studies with a large number of patients are needed to confirm these observations.

Some studies have shown that the total efficacy of microclipping surgery or endovascular embolization is only $33\%\sim56\%$ , and the postoperative mortality rate is $28\%\sim58\%$ .  

Target temperature management (TTM) is an effective therapy for brain protection and requires “rapid cooling,” “stable maintenance,” “precision and controllability,” and “slow rewarming”.5 Numerous previous studies revealed that TTM could control core body temperature as well as reduce brain metabolism, brain cell apoptosis, brain tissue inflammation, and the formation of free radicals. TTM has been shown to improve cardiac arrest after hypoxic brain injury, severe traumatic brain injury, ischemic stroke, neonatal ischemic brain injury, and other diseases, with neuroprotective effects that can improve prognosis.6-9  

Animal experimentation has determined that TTM can reduce brain tissue metabolism, clear free radicals, regulate neuroinflammatory responses, inhibit neuronal apoptosis, reduce excitatory neurotoxic damage, activate cellular autophagy, and inhibit pyroptosis. Animal experiments by Murai et al. demonstrated that early induction of cerebral hypothermia promotes faster recovery of cerebral perfusion pressure, repolarization, and inhibit excessive glutamate release, thereby preventing ultra-early brain injury after SAH.10 Dai et al. demonstrated in animals that low temperature could activate cold-inducedRNA-bindingprotein expression and elicit protective effects against oxidative stress and apoptosis. Liu et al. also reported that TTM in animals could activate neuronal autophagy moderately through the extracellular regulated protein kinases signaling pathway, reduce neuronal cell death, and produce neuroprotective effects.12 Zhou et al. found that TTM inhibits cell pyroptosis through the AMPK/NLRP3 inflammasome pathway, thereby reducing brain edema.13 However, a meta-analysis by Yao et al. concluded that therapeutic hypothermia did not significantly reduce mortality and poor outcomes in patients with hemorrhagic stroke.14 Therefore, the purpose of this study was to define the clinical effectiveness and safety of targeted hypothermia management therapy in patients with poor-grade aSAH.

# Participants  

The control group received routine temperature control, and the TTM group received TTM. All patients underwent endovascular embolization or microclipping surgery within 24 hours of disease onset. All patients underwent computerized vascular imaging or digital subtraction angiography for the ruptured aneurysm and the underlying aneurysm.  

The inclusion criteria for this trial included, 1) age 18 to 80 years; 2) diagnosis of intracranial aneurysm by computerized vascular imaging or digital subtraction angiography; 3) no history of benign or malignant intracranial tumors; 4) Hunt-Hess grade $\mathrm{IV}\sim\mathrm{V};5$ ) stable vital signs after rescue or early treatment; and 6) the patient’s legal agent provided signed informed consent.  

Exclusion criteria for this trial included, 1) patient was known to be allergic to the medical device; 2) diagnosis of systemic inflammatory reaction syndrome or sepsis at enrollment; 3) history of blood disorders, hemorrhage, or coagulopathy; 4) the patient underwent major surgery or had a history of craniocerebral trauma, gastrointestinal bleeding, or urinary bleeding in the past 3 months; 5) patients exhibited advanced renal failure requiring dialysis; 6) life expectancy was less than 6 months; 7) pregnant and lactating patients; or 8) patients were considered unsuitable by the investigator to participate in the project due to other reasons. All patients underwent computerized tomography (CT), and imaging features were interpreted by a neurointensivist.  

From the perspective of ethics and patients’ safety, this study used a nonrandomized method to group patients according to whether or not to use TTM if their families wished.  



# Sample Size  

The sample size estimates were primarily based on a good prognosis rate. Combined with relevant literature, a good prognosis rate for the control group was $\mathrm{II.I^{0/6^{15}}}$ ; the current study was expected to be approximately $29\%$ higher than the control group. Power Analysis and Sample Size 2021 software was used to estimate the sample size. When the test level $\mathfrak{a}$ was 0.05, and the degree of $\mathbf{I}{-}\upbeta$ was $70\%$ , according to the statistical principles, 25 subjects needed to be included in each group, and the total sample size of the 2 groups was approximately 50 cases. Considering the possibility of a patient loss rate of $\mathrm{\Omega}_{\mathrm{{IO}}}\%$ in the study, a total sample size of 60 cases was included in this study.  



# MATERIALS AND METHODS  



# Design  

This single-blind, nonrandomized clinical trial was conducted at the Department of Neurosurgery intensive care unit (ICU), Henan Provincial People’s Hospital, between April 2022 and April 2023. The study was approved by the Institutional Review and Ethics Committee of Henan Provincial People’s Hospital (2022. NO.6) and registered in the Chinese Clinical Trial Registry  



# Data Collection  

All patients underwent a prospective collection of clinical baseline data (median age and gender distribution), laboratory, and demographic characteristics. Temperature data were obtained from the daily medical record system. Laboratory data, neuroimaging reports, and adverse events were reviewed during hospitalization. Moreover, the single-blind method is maintained for the evaluators of the observed indicators, where they do not know the patient groups and corresponding treatment plans.  



# Treatment and TTM Protocol  

The TTM protocol included induction, maintenance, rewarming, and normal temperature control. In our study, all poor-grade aSAH hospital admissions were treated in the neurosurgical ICU. Treatment principles for both groups were performed according to the standard treatment guidelines for aSAH.16 A temperature probe was inserted into the urinary bladder, and the bladder temperature served as the target core temperature.  

The trial group was treated with TTM. The TTM body surface cooling device used a medical temperature control instrument (Arctic Sun 5000) and a medical temperature control blanket (Arctic Gel Pads) to cool the entire body. The medical temperature control blanket wrapped the trunk and limbs, accurately collected the patient’s body temperature every second up to $\mathrm{o.r^{\circ}C}$ and adjusted the water temperature every 2 minutes to achieve the necessary feedback and precise control of the patient’s body temperature.  

Preoperative and intraoperative TTM protocol for intracranial aneurysm embolization or clipping included maintaining the core body temperature at $36^{\circ}\mathrm{C}\sim37^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$ for hypothermic brain protection to avoid effects on intraoperative anesthesia and circulation, as well as surgical and postoperative results. Analgesic sedative drugs such as midazolam, hibernation mixture, remifentanil, and dexmedetomidine were given via a micropump to maintain the Richmond agitation-sedative score at $-\mathbf{\sigma}_{\mathrm{I}}\sim\mathbf{\sigma}_{\mathrm{I}}$ to prevent shivering and avoid aneurysm rupture and bleeding.  

TTM after intracranial aneurysm embolization or clipping protocol included a TTM induction period that used a rate of $\mathbf{I}^{\circ}\mathbf{C}/$ hour $\sim_{2}{}^{\circ}\mathrm{C}_{I}$ hour to rapidly reduce the bladder temperature to $34^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$ , prevent chills, and maintain a Richmond agitation-sedation score of $-4$ . The pumping speed and dose of analgesic sedation were adjusted according to the patient’s temperature, blood pressure, heart rate fluctuations, and presence of chills. The TTM maintenance period was at least 72 hours when CT examination determined that the intracranial condition had improved, then the patient underwent slow rewarming. The rewarming rate did not exceed $0.\bar{1}^{\circ}\mathrm{C/hour}$ , and attention was paid to monitoring other vital indicators and maintaining stability. The core temperature was maintained below $36^{\circ}\mathrm{C}\sim37^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$ for at least 2 days, and conventional body temperature control occurred later. Antipyretic and analgesic medication or physical cooling was utilized when the body temperature exceeded $38.5^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$ .17  

The control group was subjected to routine temperature control that maintained the urinary bladder temperature at $36^{\circ}\mathrm{C}\sim37^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$ . When the body temperature was $37^{\circ}\mathrm{C}\sim38.5^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$ , ice packs or an ordinary ice blanket were applied. When the body temperature rose above $38.5^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$ , antipyretic and analgesic drugs (acetaminophen and indomethacin) were used to reduce the body temperature. If the drug treatment was ineffective, intravenous infusion of cold saline, ice packs, and an ordinary ice blanket maintained the urinary bladder temperature between $36^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$ and $37^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$ . Sedatives and analgesic drugs also were given when necessary. Postoperative antibiotic therapy was utilized according to the clinical situation.18  



# Measures  

Image Analysis. Bedside head CT was performed before and every 3 days after TTM treatment to assess the presence of DCI, intracranial rehemorrhage, cerebral edema, hydrocephalus, and midline shift. The patient was evaluated using imaging by 1 neurosurgeon and 1 intensive care physician.  

DCI was defined as the presence of focal neurological deterioration or a decrease in the Glasgow Coma Scale of at least 2 points and an infarction on the CT scan that was not visible at admission or immediately following treatment.  

Intracranial rehemorrhage was defined as clinical deterioration with evidence of increased hemorrhage on subsequent CT scans, fresh blood from the external ventricular drain, or deterioration before neuroradiological evaluation. Head CTs were used to reveal hydrocephalus, which appeared as enlarged bilateral ventricles, interstitial edema, round and blunted third ventricles; the ratio of the maximum width of the frontal horns of the lateral ventricles and the maximal internal diameter of the skull at the same level employed in axial CT (Evans’ index, A/ B $)>0.3$ was a typical marker for a diagnosis of hydrocephalus.21 We use the software of Vue PACS (Carestream Health Inc, Rochester, NY, USA) of the medical record system for manual measurements. The resolution of $64$ -row CT is 30lp/ cm, and that of 16-row CT is $\mathrm{{\bf17}}.5\mathrm{{lp/cm}}$ .  

A midline shift was defined as a transparent septum deviation from the midline, measured by drawing a line perpendicular to the transparent septum at the maximum deviation point. Progressive cerebral edema could exert mass effect and displace midline structures.22  

Incidence of Adverse Events. The incidence of adverse events during the hospitalization was calculated. All patients underwent an electrocardiogram examination. Vital signs (heart rate, blood pressure, and respiration) were monitored daily in the neurosurgical ICU.23 Bradycardia was diagnosed when the heart rate was $<50$ beats/min. Tachycardia was diagnosed when the heart rate was ${>}\operatorname{Ioo}/\operatorname*{min}$ . Hypertension was diagnosed when the blood pressure was ${>}\mathrm{I}80/\mathrm{I05~mmHg}$ , and hypotension was diagnosed when the blood pressure was ${<}80~\mathrm{mmHg}$ . Gastrointestinal bleeding and gastric retention were monitored by observing fluid collected through the nasogastric tube. Pneumonia was diagnosed when a positive chest radiograph was observed and new, purulent signs of at least 1 acute infection (temperature $>38^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$ , white blood cell count $<4\mathrm{~}\times\mathrm{~}\mathrm{ro}^{9}/\mathrm{L}$ or ${\tt>I2}\times\mathrm{~I0}^{9}/\mathrm{L}$ ) and at least 2 additional conditions, such as worsening symptoms, or dry and wet rales. Serum electrolyte levels were monitored daily for hypokalemia (potassium $<3.5\mathrm{mmol/L}$ ), hyponatremia (sodium <135 mmol/L), and hyperkalemia (potassium $>5.5$ $\mathrm{mmol/L})$ . Thrombocytopenia was monitored by intermittent routine blood assessments (platelets ${<}\mathrm{Iooo/\upmuL})$ . Abnormal coagulation function was determined by prothrombin time, partial thromboplastin time, D-dimer levels, and levels of fibrin degradation products. Hyperglycemia (blood glucose concentration $>\mathbf{\pi}\mathbf{\pi}.\mathbf{I}$ mmol/L) was controlled by continuous insulin therapy, and blood glucose levels were maintained at $5{\cdot}5{\sim}\mathrm{II}.\mathrm{Immol/L}$ . Acute myocardial infarction was identified by elevated electrocardiogram ST and elevated cardiac biomarkers (including creatine kinase isoenzymes or troponin-T levels). Acute renal failure was diagnosed if the blood creatinine levels in the first 7 days were increased by 1.5 times over the baseline value for more than 24 hours. Acute liver injury was diagnosed when either glutamate or alanine transaminase was $>\mathrm{100U/L}$ . Deep vein ultrasounds of both lower limbs were performed before and after treatment with TTM to assess the presence of deep vein thrombosis.24

Outcome Assessment. The prognosis of neurological function 3 months after admission was assessed using the modified Rankin scale (mRS). The mRS scores range from 0 to 6, with the following criteria, 0 points indicate the patient is entirely asymptomatic; 1 point indicates the patient is able to complete all daily work and life despite some deficits. Two points represent mild disability, and the patient is unable to complete all preillness activities but does not need help to take care of their daily affairs; 3 points indicate moderate disability, where the patient requires some help but can walk independently; 4 points represent moderate and severe disability such that the patient is unable to walk independently, and requires help with daily life activities; 5 points indicate severe disability, where the patient is bedridden, incontinent, and completely dependent on others for daily life activities. Six points indicate the death of the patient. A mRS score of $\mathrm{o}\sim2$ represented an excellent neurological prognosis, a mRS score of $\upsigma\sim_{3}$ was a favorable prognosis, and a mRS score of ${4\mathord{\leftrightharpoons}}6$ indicated an unfavorable neurological prognosis. Follow-up assessments were conducted by telephone by an uninformed, independent evaluator. Mortality also was assessed after 3 months.

# Statistical Analysis  

Statistical analysis was performed using SPSS 26.0 software (IBM, Armonk, NY, USA). Data that conformed to a normal distribution were expressed as $(\mathrm{x}\pms)$ or mean $\pm$ standard deviation. Nonnormally distributed data were expressed as medians and quartiles $[\boldsymbol{\mathrm{M}}\left(\mathrm{Q}\boldsymbol{\mathrm{I}},\mathrm{Q}3\right)]$ . Comparisons between groups were performed using t-tests or rank sum tests. Enumeration data were expressed as the number of cases and percentages [n $\left(\%\right)]$ , and comparisons between groups were performed using the $\chi^{2}$ or Fisher exact test. Multivariate analysis was conducted by performing backward stepwise regression on all variables in a univariate analysis to identify the influencing factors of unfavorable outcomes. $\mathrm{P}<0.05$ was considered statistically significant.  



# RESULTS  



# General Demographics  

The final sample consisted of 60 patients who met the inclusion criteria for poor-grade aSAH. Thirty subjects received TTM, and 30 were in the control group (Figure 1). The mean patient age was $59.10~\pm~\mathrm{{I}}0.85$ years. Patient characteristics, such as history of smoking, alcohol consumption, hypertension, diabetes, coronary heart disease, hyperlipidemia, history of stroke, proportion of Hunt-Hess grade $\mathrm{~N~}\sim\mathrm{~V~}_{;}$ , aneurysm location, and therapeutic medication before admission, were not significantly different between the 2 groups. The mean patient age in the TTM group was $57{\cdot}30\pm\:\:\mathrm{{\scriptsize~I0.88}}$ years, and the control group was $60.90~\pm$ 10.14 years, with no significant difference between the 2 groups. The number of women $(\mathrm{n}=39,$ ) was greater than men $[\mathrm{n}=2\mathrm{I}]$ ) in the study, but there was no significant difference between the groups (Table 1). # DISCUSSION  

This study was a pilot nonrandomized controlled trial investigating the safety and effectiveness of TTM in patients with poorgrade aSAH. This study demonstrated that TTM contributed to improved neurological outcomes in patients with poor-grade aSAH who underwent aneurysmal clipping and embolization.  

According to the mRS scores observed 3 months after hospitalization, there was no significant difference in the number of excellent outcomes (mRS score $\mathrm{o}\sim2$ ) for patients with poor-grade aSAH, but TTM significantly reduced the proportion of patients with an unfavorable prognosis (mRS score $4\mathopen{}\mathclose\bgroup\sim\mathclose\bgroup\left(6\aftergroup\egroup\right)$ ). The prognosis of $\ttmRS$ scores $\mathsf{o}\sim\mathsf{3}$ and ${4\mathord{\leftrightharpoons}}6$ were significantly different between the 2 groups $\mathrm{~\textit~{~P~}~}=~0.028,$ . Moreover, TTM improved cerebral edema, midline shift, and hydrocephalus in patients with poorgrade aSAH. The complication rate was comparable in both groups in this study, and there was no significant association between TTM and inflammatory markers. Therefore, TTM appeared to be a safe and feasible treatment protocol.  

The advantages of TTM in severe neurological diseases are increasingly apparent. However, some studies have indicated there is insufficient evidence to determine whether TTM is an appropriate treatment for patients with poor-grade aSAH. Kobata et al. reported that patients with World Federation of Neurosurgical Societies grade $\mathrm{V}$ subarachnoid hemorrhage who underwent 1 week of intravascular TTM after $34^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$ surface cooling did not exhibit any significant adverse events. This management strategy reduced the incidence of vasospasm-related cerebral infarction and improved outcomes in $4\mathrm{I}.7\%$ of the patients.26 Rhim et al. reported that unfavorable prognoses of patients with poor-grade aSAH who received TTM $(\mathrm{n}=7,38.9\%)$ ) were significantly lower than those in the control group ( $\mathrm{\Deltan}=25$ , $69{\cdot}4\%$ ; $\mathrm{P}=0.03\mathbf{I}$ ).15 Won et al. reported that cerebral edema was reduced in TTM patients 1 month after admission, and mortality $\left(20.0\%\right)$ was significantly lower than in the control group $\left(46.8\%\right)$ . However, Anei et al. reported that TTM did not improve the prognosis of poor-grade aSAH patients and proposed that the treatment of poor-grade aSAH patients should focus on maintaining conventional body temperature to prevent brain swelling and eliminate factors that might induce CVS rather than positive cerebral protection through interventional hypothermia. Seule et al. reported that prolonged systemic hypothermia should be considered a last option for patients with SAH with intracranial hypertension. A good functional prognosis was observed for only $\mathbf{I}5.5\%$ of patients.28  

Secondary brain injury occurring within 72 hours after poorgrade aSAH is known as early brain injury, and the main pathological changes include bleeding extravasation, rapid increase of ICP, acute vasospasm, reduced cerebral blood flow, cerebrovascular autoregulation dysfunction, and brain swelling, which can lead to acute neurological dysfunction in poor-grade aSAH patients. The CVS and DCl that often occur after 72 hours usually indicate an unfavorable patient prognosis.29

The optimal target temperature for TTM is currently unknown. In an international randomized controlled trial of TTM, the incidence of adverse events in aSAH patients with target temperatures of $33^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$ and $36^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$ was $93\%$ and $90\%$ , respectively. This study demonstrated no significant difference in adverse effects between the 2 groups of poor-grade aSAH patients at target temperatures of $33^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$ and $36^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$ .30 Temperatures below $32^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$ can cause severe arrhythmias, including atrial tachycardia, ventricular tachycardia, atrial fibrillation, and ventricular fibrillation. After analyzing previous clinical trials with poorgrade aSAH patients, this study set the postoperative target temperature as $33{\cdot}3^{\circ}{\mathrm{C}}\sim35^{\circ}{\mathrm{C}}$ , which avoided the adverse events caused by low temperature and maintained the neuroprotective effect of TTM.17,24,26  

Currently, there are no defined requirements for the timing of TTM initiation in poor-grade aSAH. Karibe et al. reported that intraoperative hypothermia reduced the severity of intraoperative ischemia caused by transient vascular occlusion and brain retraction, thereby reducing postoperative cerebral blood flow (CBF) injury. A randomized trial by Todd et al. reported that intraoperative TTM in patients undergoing craniotomy did not improve neurological outcomes after craniotomy in patients with good-grade aSAH. However, postoperative bacteremia was more common in patients with hypothermia, which prolonged their hospital stay.30  

A meta-analysis suggested that intraoperative hypothermia may reduce mortality and daily life dependency in patients with goodgrade aSAH and did not report evidence to support the routine use of intraoperative hypothermia in patients with poor-grade aSAH.32 These previous observations indicate that it is still controversial whether intraoperative hypothermia can improve the outcomes of poor-grade aSAH patients. However, the postoperative hypothermia in this study revealed promising results. A retrospective study by Rhim et al. demonstrated a therapeutic effect of TTM in poor-grade aSAH patients undergoing endovascular embolization within 8 hours of symptom onset. Won et al. demonstrated that postoperative TTM could effectively prevent cerebral edema and reduce mortality in patients with poor-grade aSAH. Choi et al. reported that induction of hypothermia at 1 hour after surgery reduced the risk of aneurysm rebleeding; hypothermia was initiated earlier compared with induction of hypothermia within $4^{8}$ hours in previous studies.24 An observational study by Kuramatsu et al. found that early postoperative TTM could reduce the flow velocity of middle cerebral artery and occurrence of DCI.33  

Furthermore, the possibility that application of TTM before surgery may confer a severe risk of rebleeding was reported by Choi et al. Conversely, the Neuroprotective Therapy Consensus Review (NTCR) group indicated that neurogenic fever in patients with aSAH could adversely affect patient prognosis. Therefore, we believe that preoperative and intraoperative temperature control at $36^{\circ}\mathrm{C}\sim37^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$ is feasible, and our results indicated that preoperative and intraoperative TTM could facilitate brain protection.  

The ongoing duration of the TTM has not been determined. Choi et al. reported that continuous application of hypothermia for $4^{8}$ hours reduced mortality in patients with poor-grade aSAH and achieved a favorable $({\mathrm{mRS~}}0\sim_{3})$ neurological prognosis.24 Several case series have indicated long-term hypothermia improved acute ICP increase and CVS in patients with poor-grade aSAH.35-37 To further elucidate the effect of long-term TTM on patients with poor-grade aSAH, Lioudmila et al. compared the outcomes of patients receiving TTM and patients not exposed to TTM and found no significant differences in the patients with long-term TTM compared to control patients with respect to end-organ function, infection rates, and adverse events.38 Rhim et al. studied patients who were maintained under hypothermia for 5 days, and the proportion of unfavorable prognoses in the hypothermia group was significantly lower than in the control group $\mathbf{\bar{P}}=0.03\mathbf{I},$ ). This suggests that long-term TTM helps reduce the incidence of unfavorable prognoses in patients with poor-grade aSAH.15 Cerebral edema and CVS were highest in poor-grade aSAH patients from 3 to 14 days after the onset. Therefore, short-term TTM did not influence the most severe pathological processes in these patients. Thus, long-term hypothermia reduces case fatalities and improves patient outcomes. Based on the results described above, we maintained TTM for 3 to 5 days to benefit poor-grade aSAH patients during early brain injury, CVS, and DCI. Our study revealed that maintaining TTM for up to 5 days did not cause serious adverse events compared with the control group.

In addition, recent studies have found that the target temperature for retemperature is primarily $36^{\circ}\mathrm{C}\sim37{\cdot}5^{\circ}\mathrm{C}_{:}$ , and the rewarming rate should not exceed $0.\bar{1}^{\circ}\mathrm{C}/$ hour w $0.25^{\circ}\mathrm{C/hour^{r}}^{\mathrm{\circ}}$ . If the core body temperature rises too rapidly, that can result in systemic vasodilation and hypotension, which will cause cerebral vasodilation and rebound edema, leading to increased ICP. Conversely, if the rewarming rate is too slow, it might cause more hypothermia complications. Therefore, our study utilized a rewarming rate of $\boldsymbol{0.I^{\circ}{\mathrm{C}}}/$ /hour after the end of the TTM maintenance period. The patient’s body temperature was in a slow and controlled process to $36^{\circ}\mathrm{C}\sim37^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$ and maintained for at least 2 days.  

Our study had several limitations. 1) This was a prospective, nonrandomized, controlled pilot trial. 2) A larger sample size might be necessary to determine additional meaningful differences between the TTM and control groups. 3) Despite treating patients according to established guidelines, implicit bias could occur in using analgesic sedation and mechanical ventilation among different individuals. 4) The collection of patient observation indicators could have been improved, and continuous laboratory indicators could not be collected. 5) The dynamic development process of CVS was not observed, and multimodal monitoring such as ICP, cerebral oxygen, and TCD should be used in the future to monitor DCI and CVS.  



# CONCLUSIONS  

In conclusion, this prospective nonrandomized controlled pilot trial demonstrated that TTM could be safely used in patients with poor-grade aSAH. The complication rate during TTM treatment was comparable to the control group receiving standard therapy, indicating that TTM was an acceptable treatment. Furthermore, we found that TTM reduced cerebral edema and hydrocephalus, improved midline displacement, and improved functional outcomes of patients with poor-grade aSAH. In the future, a large, multicenter, randomized controlled study should be conducted to determine the clinical effectiveness and safety of TTM in poor-grade aSAH patients and delineate the clinical application specifications of TTM for treating poor-grade aSAH.
