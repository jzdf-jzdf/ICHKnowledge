# Short Communication  

# An Exploratory Analysis of Biomarkers of Perihematomal Edema in the CN-105 in Participants with Acute Supratentorial Intracerebral Hemorrhage (CATCH) Trial  

Jay B. Lusk, BSc,a Jesse Troy, PhD,b Nathaniel Nowacki, MD, Peter G. Kranz, MD,d Maureen Maughan, PhD,e Daniel T. Laskowitz, MD, MHS,f and Michael L. James, MD  

Objective: To identify biomarkers with potential to indicate severity of perihematomal edema and secondary tissue injury after intracerebral hemorrhage (ICH), and which could be used as surrogate markers in future clinical trials for novel ICH therapeutics. Materials and Methods: This exploratory cohort study compared trends in neuroinflammatory biomarker levels in 18 consecutively enrolled patients with acute supratentorial ICH and 16 patients treated with the investigational neuroprotective therapy CN-105 to identify a panel of 10 biomarkers. Biomarker levels over five days posthemorrhage were then compared with edema volumes in a larger sample of patients treated with CN-105. Results: Mean normalized edema volumes increased over time; higher CRP levels were associated with increased edema volumes $(p=0.006\$ , $r=0.56^{}$ ). Higher IL8, IL10, MCP, and MMP-9 levels were associated with decreased edema volumes $(p=0.005_{,}$ , $r=-0.57,$ ; $p=0.02,$ $r=-0.51$ ; $p=0.02,$ , $r=\--0.52.$ ; $p=.002,$ $r=-0.63,$ , respectively). IL1-RA, IL1-B, IL23, vWF, and IL17 levels were not significantly associated with edema volumes $(p>0.05)$ . Conclusions: This exploratory study provides some of the first insights into the longitudinal associations between markers of neuroinflammation and development of perihematomal edema and secondary tissue injury in human ICH. We hypothesize that these biomarkers could be used as surrogates for treatment effect in novel therapies intended to limit neuroinflammation after ICH.

# Introduction  

Despite tremendous advances in treatments of ischemic stroke, no proven pharmacological therapies exist for intracerebral hemorrhage (ICH). Disappointing results from large Phase 3 trials have catalyzed interest in understanding the pathophysiology associated with neuronal damage and secondary tissue injury following ICH. Identifying biomarkers that reliably indicate target engagement of novel therapies could assist in developing successful disease modifying therapies for ICH.  

Neuroinflammation is strongly implicated in exacerbating secondary tissue injury and neuronal damage after ICH. For example, leukocytosis is associated with worsened ICH outcomes; specifically, monocyte count is associated with increased risk of hematoma expansion.1,2 Markers of neuronal injury (e.g. neuron-specific enolase), glial activation (e.g., glial fibrillary acidic protein), and acute phase and pro-coagulant factors (e.g., D-dimer and brain natriuretic peptide) have been evaluated as biomarkers for ICH.3  

However, few neuroinflammatory markers have been associated with ICH outcome, and none have been validated4. Clinically, a biomarker panel predicting severity of neuroinflammation, edema formation, mass effect, or late clinical deterioration would impact triage and early medical decision-making. Additionally, biomarkers of post-ICH neuroinflammation could be integrated into clinical trials by demonstrating target engagement of potential therapies aimed at reducing neuroinflammatory damage. We present a pilot study to derive a panel of biomarkers and evaluate associations between changes in biomarker concentrations and change in perihematomal edema using the CN-105 in participants with Acute SupraTentorial IntraCerebral Hemorrhage (CATCH) Trial (NCT03168581).  

# Methods  

# Patient cohorts  

Three separate groups were used in analyses. Group 1 $(n=18)$ ) were taken from the Duke University Brain Injury Repository participants, consisting of patients over age 18 years within $^{12\mathrm{~h~}}$ of primary supratentorial ICH onset. Groups 2 $(n=16)$ and 3 $(n=22)$ consisted of CATCH Trial participants. Briefly, the CATCH Trial was a first-in-diseasestate, multi-center, open-label trial evaluating safety and feasibility of CN-105 administration in patients with acute primary supratentorial ICH. Eligible patients were age 3080 years, had confirmed primary supratentorial $\mathrm{ICH,}$ and were able to initiate CN-105 administration $(1.0\mathrm{mg/kg}$ every $^{6\mathrm{h}}$ for $72\mathrm{h}$ ) within $12\mathrm{h}$ of symptom onset.  

# Sample collection  

Blood samples were collected daily up to 7 days after ICH onset (Group 1). Samples were obtained on CATCH enrollment before the first dose of CN-105 and daily after treatment for up to five days (Groups 2 & 3). Blood was collected in EDTA tubes, plasma was separated and stored $-70\pm10^{\circ}C$ until analysis.  

# Biomarker selection and assessment  

We initially identified 37 candidate biomarkers with commercial availability and theoretical relevance to ICH (eAppendix 1). Biomarker concentrations and trends over time were compared for the two groups of patients using a generalized estimating equation (GEE). Ten markers were selected that had p-values lower than $p=0.15$ for the interaction between study and time and were then assessed in the complete CATCH sample (Group 3).  

# Biomarker assay methodology  

Biomarker levels were measured from plasma by Myriad RBM (Austin, TX) using an optimized multiplexed, Luminex-based platform.  

# Edema volume measurements  

CT scans were obtained at the time of presentation (diagnostic CT; $t=0,$ ), and at $24\:\mathrm{h},48\:\mathrm{h},$ and $^{120\mathrm{h}}$ following hemorrhage. Hematoma and edema volumes were measured following semiautomated segmentation by a neuroradiologist (PGK) using commercially available software (AnalyzePro version 1.0, AnalyzeDirect, Overland Park, KS) and previously published methods.5  

# Correlation analysis methods  

Repeated measures correlations coefficients (r) were calculated to assess longitudinal correlations between biomarkers. Alpha was set at $p=0.05$ for two-sided tests.  

# Results  

Cohort characteristics used for the final biomarker panel are shown in Table 1. Median age was 62 (IQR 5468), 15 patients were female $(39\%)$ , 19 White $(50\%)$ and none of Hispanic ethnicity. Fifteen $(39.5\%)$ patients had an ICH Score of 0, 11 $(29.0\%)$ score of 1, 10 $(26.3\%)$ score of 3, and 2 $(6.2\%)$ score of $_{3-4}$ .  

Mean normalized edema volumes increased over time; higher CRP levels were associated with increased edema volumes $(p=0.006.$ , $r=0.56,$ ). Higher IL8, IL10, MCP, and MMP-9 levels were associated with decreased edema volumes $(p=0.005\$ , $r=-0.57$ ; $p=0.02.$ , $r=-0.51\$ ; $p=0.02$ , $r=-$ 0.52; $p=.002$ , $r=-0.63_{.}$ , respectively). IL1-RA, IL1-B, IL23, vWF, and IL17 levels were not significantly associated with edema volumes $(p>0.05)$ . Trends in edema volume and biomarker levels are shown in Fig. 1.  

# Discussion  

In our study, CRP was the only biomarker with a monophasic, positive association between serum concentration and edema volumes. CRP has been extensively studied in ICH and other acute brain injuries, and our findings are concordant with those previously described.6

On the other hand, we observed that IL-8, IL-10, MMP-9, and MCP-1 had negative associations with edema volumes. The few studies evaluating IL-10 in patients with ICH have not performed longitudinal biomarker trends analyses and have primarily observed a negative association between admission IL-10 concentration and functional outcomes.7 One study evaluating IL-8 in acute basal ganglia hemorrhage showed IL-8 concentration was associated with worsened cerebral edema, in contrast to our results, although the precise time points for comparison differ between the studies.8 MCP-1 has been investigated as a risk factor for ischemic stroke, but a large, population-level analysis showed no association between MCP-1 levels and risk of ICH; MCP-1 has not been evaluated longitudinally as a biomarker in acute ICH. Prior studies of MMP-9 have suggested that MMP-9 levels peak once on post-hemorrhage days 13 and again at posthemorrhage day 7.9 We found that MMP-9 levels were high at day 1 and then fell before rising again after day 3, consistent with previous studies.  

IL1- $\upbeta$ , IL-23, and VWF levels appeared to increase during the acute phase of ICH (days 03) and to decrease thereafter. $\mathrm{IL}{-}1\upbeta$ is a pro-inflammatory cytokine which has been the target of a great deal of preclinical study.10 IL-23 is also a pro-inflammatory cytokine associated with the Th17 phenotype of T-cells; its role in human ICH has not been previously described.10 vWF has been linked to worsened ICH outcomes in preclinical studies.11 Our study did not find a significant association between these biomarkers and edema volumes, potentially due to small sample size. IL-17 levels on the other hand, appeared to decrease during the acute phase and increase slightly from days 3 to 5. IL-17, like IL-23, is a pro-inflammatory cytokine associated with Th17 cells, and has not been investigated in human ICH.10  

# Limitations  

This study was a preliminary, hypothesis-generating study, and as such has major limitations. First, this study only assessed longitudinal biomarker trends in patients treated with CN-105. CN-105 is a neuroprotective peptide and is therefore expected to modulate neuroinflammatory responses. It is therefore possible that the trends in this study are different in magnitude or character compared with the expected natural history of a patient with ICH untreated with CN-105. Future work should explicitly characterize neuroinflammatory biomarker trends in patients who did not receive a drug modulating the central nervous system. Furthermore, this study did not include any negative controls (i.e. patients without active brain inflammation but who had systemic inflammation). Therefore, we are not able to determine whether the biomarker patterns observed are unique to ICH or are present in other causes of systemic inflammation. Finally, this study was a very small, preliminary, hypothesisgenerating study, and its results need to be confirmed and explored in more depth in a larger prospective study, to determine if results are generalizable to a broader population of ICH patients. In terms of strengths, this study is one of the first to longitudinally assess association between neuroinflammatory biomarkers and edema in human ICH and the first to examine these relationships in humans for several biomarkers, i.e., IL-10, IL-23, IL-17, MCP-1, and VWF.  

# Conclusions  

This study provides early proof of concept for the use of neuroinflammatory biomarkers to predict perihematomal edema volumes in human ICH and provides the first human ICH data on several promising neuroinflammatory biomarkers. Further investigation in larger numbers of patients is required to better understand the association between neuroinflammatory biomarkers and perihematomal edema volumes.
