# Abstract  

# Background and Objectives  

Observational studies described associations between higher systolic blood pressure (SBP) values and intracranial hemorrhages (ICHs) and worse outcomes after successful reperfusion by endovascular therapy (EVT). However, the BP-TARGET trial [BP-Target in Acute Ischemic Stroke to Reduce Hemorrhage after EVT] found that an intensive SBP target did not reduce ICH rates after successful EVT. The presence of contrast enhancement (CE) immediately after reperfusion is also associated with higher odds of ICH and worse outcomes. Our research question was to investigate the effect of 2 SBP strategies after reperfusion on ICH rates and functional outcomes according to the presence of CE in the BP-TARGET trial. We hypothesized that patients with CE could benefit from an intensive SBP control.  

# Methods  

We included BP-TARGET patients in whom a brain flat panel was performed immediately after reperfusion. We described CE as present or absent, ICH consisted of any radiographic ICH 24 hours after EVT, and unfavorable outcome consisted of a modified Rankin Scale score between 3 and 6 at 3 months.  

# Results  

Among the 324 patients randomized in BP-TARGET, 164 were included in this analysis, of whom 113 $\left(68.9\%\right)$ presented CE after reperfusion. The 24-hour mean SBP was significantly lower in the intensive SBP group compared with the standard group (129.7 vs $138.3\mathrm{mmHg},$ $p<0.001,$ ). Patients with CE and randomized in the intensive and standard SBP group had increased ICH rates: $\mathrm{aOR}=11.26$ , $95\%$ CI 4.59–27.63, and $\mathsf{aOR}=4.08$ , $95\%$ CI 1.75–9.50, respectively. However, the test of heterogeneity did not reach the significant level $\mathrm{\check{aOR}=}$ 2.76, $95\%$ CI 0.80 to 9.48, $p=0.11$ ). Patients with CE and randomized in the intensive SBP group had also higher odds of unfavorable outcomes $\mathrm{(aOR}=2.91\$ , $95\%$ CI 1.24–6.82), but this association was not significant in the standard SBP group $(\mathrm{aOR}=1.89\$ , $95\%$ CI 0.85–4.23). No significant heterogeneity was found between the 2 groups (aOR, 1.54, $95\%$ CI 0.48 to 4.97, $p=0.47.$ ).

# Classification of Evidence  

This study provides Class IV evidence that for adults with contrast-enhancing lesions after successful EVT of an AIS, intensive blood pressure management did not significantly increase the risk of ICH.  

Several recent observational studies have shed light on the role of blood pressure (BP) management after endovascular therapy (EVT), with higher systolic (SBP) and diastolic BP (DBP) values associated with higher odds of intracranial hemorrhage (ICH) and worse functional outcomes after successful reperfusion.1-12 In this context, the Blood Pressure Target in Acute Stroke to Reduce Hemorrhage after EVT (BP-TARGET) was the first randomized controlled trial (RCT) performed in the EVT era, which aimed to evaluate whether an intensive systolic BP (SBP) target of $100{-}129\mathrm{mm}\mathrm{Hg}$ reduced the occurrence of ICH after successful EVT, compared with a standard care SBP target of $130{-}185\mathrm{mm}\mathrm{Hg}.^{1\bar{3}}$ Of interest, and in contrast to observational studies, an intensive SBP target did not reduce ICH occurrence compared with the standard SBP control.13  

On the other hand, blood-brain barrier (BBB) disruption is frequent in patients with acute ischemic stroke (AIS) treated with EVT and is an independent predictor of ICH and worse outcomes.14-17 Of interest, contrast enhancement (CE) assessed on CT immediately after EVT is a validated and well-studied surrogate marker of BBB disruption and has been associated with increased odds of ICH and worse outcomes after EVT.18-21 Considering the presumed deleterious effect of higher SBP and DBP values after successful reperfusion by EVT on ICH rates and functional outcomes, our hypothesis was that patients presenting with CE lesions after EVT (considered more at risk for ICH and worse outcomes) may benefit more from an intensive SBP control. With this as a background, our research question was to evaluate the effect of the BP control strategy (intensive vs standard) on ICH rates and functional outcomes according to the presence or absence of CE lesions immediately after EVT.  

# Methods  

# Study Population  

We performed a post hoc analysis of the BP-TARGET trial, which was a randomized, multicenter, open-label, blindassessed clinical trial evaluating the efficacy and safety of an intensive BP-lowering strategy after successful EVT.13 Briefly, BP-TARGET included patients aged ${>}18$ years, with an AIS consecutive to an anterior LVO (proximal segment of the middle cerebral artery, intracranial internal carotid artery, or both), successfully treated by EVT (mTICI 2b-3). The main exclusion criteria were hemorrhagic complications during EVT, a spontaneous BP decrease after successful EVT $\'{<}130\mathrm{mmHg}$ ), and severe or fatal comorbidities that would have rendered the procedure unlikely to benefit the individual (i.e., malignant cancer with reduced life expectancy and preexisting disability).13,22 The BP-TARGET trial randomly assigned patients to an intensive SBP target ( $100{-}129\mathrm{mmHg},$ ) or a standard SBP target $\mathbf{\langle130-185mmHg\rangle}$ within 1 hour after randomization.13 T he study protocol and consent forms were approved by the Comite de Protection des Personnes Sud Méditerranée IV (N°ID-RCB: 2016-AO1884-47, March 14, 2017). For the present post hoc study, we only considered BP-TARGET centers that systematically performed a cone-beam CT in the angiographic suite using the flat-panel detector of the angiographic equipment immediately after successful EVT.21 Altogether, 1 center was included in the study.  

# Hemodynamic Management  

The hemodynamic management among the included centers has been previously published.13,22 Briefly, BP was measured noninvasively using a validated BP monitor. The treatment goal in the intensive SBP target group was to reduce and maintain SBP from 100 to $129~\mathrm{mm~Hg}$ within 1 hour after randomization and for 24 hours. The SBP target was $130{-}185\mathrm{mm}\mathrm{Hg}$ within 1 hour and for 24 hours after randomization in the standard SBP target. The first-line antihypertensive treatment recommended was IV nicardipine, but the investigators were free to choose other antihypertensive treatments based on their practice to achieve the SBP targets.  

# Flat-Panel CT Analysis  

The flat-panel CT was systematically performed for every patient after successful EVT, as an institutional policy, and therefore independently of the baseline characteristics or EVT outcome. The validation of the imaging protocol and analysis has been previously published.21 Briefly, the flatpanel CT acquisition and postprocessing were performed with an angiographic system (Allura Interventional Angiographic Systems, Philips Healthcare, Best, The Netherlands). A rotational movement of a C-arm in the plane perpendicular to the longitudinal angiographic table axis around the patient’s head acquired 620 fluoroscopic frames in a circular motion, at a frame rate of 30 frame/s, with a rotation speed of $22^{\circ}/s$ and a total rotation time of 20 seconds, using a $1024\times1024$ pixel matrix detector with a $48\mathrm{-cm}$ field of view. Reconstructions could be performed with variable volume matrixes or reconstructed volume dimensions ( $\mathrm{\ddot{~}}512^{3}$ spatial resolution matrix as a maximum). The reconstructed images could then be zoomed and displayed in different volume-rendering formats and display mode (multiplanar reconstructions). Two independent investigators, blinded to clinical and imaging outcomes, assessed for the presence or absence of CE lesions and graded CE lesions according to the ASPECT-CE score, which ranges from 0 (CE involving the whole middle cerebral artery territory) to 10 (no CE).23 An ASPECT-CE score of 6 was chosen as a cutoff to discriminate extensive vs nonextensive CE lesions, as previously published.23 We did not compare the prognostic significance of CE (presence or absence) vs the CEASPECT on the flat panel, as we decided to simultaneously assess these 2 parameters in the analysis to test whether not only the presence of CE was associated with worse outcomes but also its severity (CE-ASPECTS ${<}6.$ ). The presence or absence of CE and the ASPECT-CE were finally compared with ICH occurrence assessed on brain CT within $24-36\mathrm{~h~}$ (see below).  

# Clinical and Radiologic End Points  

Unfavorable outcome was defined as a modified Rankin Scale (mRS) score between 3 and 6 at 3 months, assessed by neurologists or research nurses blinded to the study arm, in telephone contact and/or in-person clinical evaluation. ICHs were assessed on brain CT within 24–36 hours after reperfusion. Although parenchymal hemorrhage types 1 and 2 (PH1 and PH2) are commonly associated with unfavorable outcomes at 3 months, hemorrhagic infarction types 1 and 2 (HI1 and HI2) were also considered in the BP-TARGET trial, as this study evaluated all injuries suggestive of reperfusion lesions.13 As several studies have highlighted the association of mild, asymptomatic (HI1 and HI2) ICH after successful EVT with worse outcomes, all subgroups of ICHs were thus considered.24-27 CTs were forwarded to the core image analysis center, and senior neuroradiologists, who were unaware of the treatment assignments, clinical findings, and time of image acquisition, centrally determined the presence of ICH.  

# Statistical Analysis  

Categorical variables were expressed as frequencies and percentages. Quantitative variables were expressed as mean (SD) or median (interquartile range, IQR) for non-normal distribution. Normality of distributions was assessed graphically and by using the Shapiro-Wilk test. Patients were divided into 2 groups according to the presence or absence of CE. Baseline characteristics were described according to study groups, and the magnitude of between-group differences was quantified using standardized difference; an absolute standardized difference (ASD) $>$ $20\%$ was interpreted as a meaningful difference.28  

We studied the association of CE and the ASPECT-CE score (treated as a binary variable according to ${\le}6$ cutoff level or as a quantitative variable) with main outcomes (ICH and unfavorable outcome), using univariable logistic regression models. Odds ratios (ORs) were derived from logistic models as effect sizes by using patients without CE or ASPECT-CE score ${\ge}6$ as reference groups or per 1-point decrease in the ASPECT-CE score. We assessed the shape of associations between outcomes and ASPECT-CE score treated as quantitative variable using restricted cubic spline function, and no deviation in log-linearity assumption was observed.29 To reduce the effects of potential confounding factors, betweenCE group comparisons were further done using the inverse probability of CE weighting (IPTW) propensity score method (using stabilized inverse propensity score as weighty in logistic regression models).18 The propensity score was estimated using a nonparsimonious multivariable logistic regression model, with CE group as the dependent variable and all the characteristics listed in Table 1 as covariates. The associations of outcomes with the ASPECT-CE score (treated as binary or continuous) were further investigated after prespecified adjustment on age, hypertension, diabetes, IV thrombolysis, and NIHSS score at baseline by using multivariable logistic regression models. Finally, we assessed the heterogeneity in the associations according to BP-TARGET randomization arms (intensive SBP target vs standard SBP target), by including in the logistic regression models the corresponding interaction term; ORs within each allocated arms were estimated using linear contrast. Statistical testing was performed at the 2-tailed α level of 0.05. Data were analyzed using the SAS software package, release 9.4 (SAS Institute, Cary, NC).

# ASPECT-CE Score and ICH or Functional Outcomes  

As shown in Figure 1, patients presenting with an ASPECTCE score ${\le}6$ had higher odds of ICH compared with patients with an ASPECT-CE score ${>}6$ $\mathrm{aOR}=4.54\$ ; $95\%$ CI, 1.42–14.55). This difference remained significant only in the intensive SBP group but not in the standard SBP group $\mathrm{aOR}=7.88\$ ; $95\%$ CI, 1.58–39.23 and $\mathrm{aOR}=2.13$ , $95\%$ CI, 0.35–12.91 in the intensive and standard SBP groups, respectively). The test of heterogeneity was not significant $\scriptstyle{\mathrm{aOR}},$ 3.71, $95\%$ CI 0.33 to 41.98, $p=0.29.$ ). Results were unchanged when the ASPECT-CE score was analyzed as a quantitative variable (Table 3). A lower ASPECT-CE score was associated with higher odds of ICH (median [IQR], 7 [7 to 9] in patients with ICH vs 9 [9 to 10] in patients without ICH) with an aOR per 1-point decrease of 2.18 ( $95\%$ CI, 1.63–2.93). This association remained significant in both the intensive and standard SPB target groups, without significant heterogeneity.  

Overall, an ASPECT-CE score $^{<6}$ was not significantly associated with higher odds of unfavorable outcomes ( $\mathrm{\Delta}\mathrm{aOR=}$ 3.72, $95\%$ CI, 0.62–22.2), Figure 1. Subgroup analyses according to the BP-TARGET randomization arm were not performed given the small number of patients with an ASPECT-CE score between 0 and 6. However, when the ASPECT-CE score was analyzed as a quantitative variable (Table 3), a lower score was associated with higher odds of unfavorable outcomes (aOR per a decrease of 1 point, 1.71, $95\%$ CI, 1.22–2.40). This association remained significant only in the intensive SBP target group $\mathrm{\Lambda}_{\mathrm{aOR}}^{\prime}=2.27$ , $95\%$ CI, 1.33–3.87) but not in the standard SBP target group, with a nonsignificant $p$ value of heterogeneity (aOR 1.70, $95\%$ CI 0.85 to 3.41, $p=0.13$ ).

# Discussion  

In the present study, we showed that the presence of CE on the flat-panel CT performed immediately after EVT was strongly associated with ICH occurrence and worse outcomes, but the statistical comparisons between the 2 BP groups (intensive vs control group) were inconclusive.  

Previous studies described associations between BBB disruption and ICH occurrence or worse functional outcomes.14- 17 However, most of these studies used a conventional brain CT after EVT to assess CE as a surrogate marker for BBB disruption. In the present study, we used the flat-panel CT, immediately after reperfusion, in the angiographic suit to assess for CE lesions, as previously published by our group.21 As randomization in the BP-TARGET was performed after successful reperfusion, directly in the angiographic suit, this flat-panel CT may serve as a reference before BP management (intensive vs standard). In addition, this fast and easy-toperform imaging protocol could help for the design of future RCT, as it may select patients at a higher risk of poor outcome after successful EVT.  

Several recent observational works have described strong associations between higher BP values and ICH occurrence or worse outcomes.1-12 However, it remains challenging to determine whether higher BP values after reperfusion have a direct and deleterious effect leading to ICH occurrence or worse functional outcomes or, on the other hand, whether elevated BP values are an indirect and secondary marker associated with ICH or functional outcomes.30 Among these 2 hypotheses, the former is the most supported to date, justifying several ongoing RCTs which are evaluating different intensive BP reduction strategies after successful reperfusion (NCT04140110, NCT04205305, and NCT04116112). In this post hoc analysis of the BP-TARGET, patients in the intensive SBP target group had significantly lower 24-hour mean SBP compared with the standard SBP control group (129.7 vs $138.3\mathrm{mm}\mathrm{Hg}$ ). However, despite a significant $10\mathrm{mmHg}$ reduction in the SBP compared with the standard group, patients presenting with CE after reperfusion and randomized in the intensive SBP target group did not have lower rates of ICH or worse functional outcomes. Of note, the association between CE and worse functional outcomes was only significant in patients randomized in the intensive SBP target group. Based on the present results, 2 major points should be considered. First, our findings show that an intensive BP reduction strategy after EVT is not always better. If considered a consequence rather than a cause, higher BP values after reperfusion could be accounted for BBB disruption, and an intensive lowering BP strategy in these patients at risk for complication could have a deleterious effect on the overall prognosis. That said, patients presenting with CE after reperfusion and randomized in the standard group had possibly higher BP values spontaneously, because of different underlying and still unknown factors, which could be associated with CE. Further observational studies are therefore needed to assess BP trajectories after successful EVT,31 specifically in patients with or without CE. Second, the present findings suggest that other parameters (such as CE) should be considered for BP management after reperfusion. This point has been recently discussed for the management of BP during EVT (i.e., before reperfusion) based on the collateral status or the anatomy of the circle of Willis,32,33 and ongoing RCT are evaluating an individualized BP management compared with the currently recommended one-size-fits-all approach (NCT04352296 and NCT04578288). Applied to the postreperfusion window, recent studies have compared fixed vs autoregulation-oriented BP thresholds after EVT, using nearinfrared spectroscopy-derived tissue oxygenation response, which allowed for the definition of lower and upper limits of autoregulation.34,35 Of interest, exceeding upper limits of autoregulation was associated with ICH and worse outcomes, but BP below the lower limit of autoregulation was associated with infarct growth, also highlighting that lower is not always better.34 Altogether, these studies and the present findings question the one-size-fits-all approach and the safety of BP lowering after reperfusion, a strategy currently being evaluated in ongoing RCT, some of them aiming at even lower BP thresholds than BPTARGET.  

To move forward from this work, one question at hand is to comprehensively understand the significance of these CE and the reasons why a more stringent BP control seems deleterious in this population. This point is of importance, as CE after successful reperfusion seems frequent, with $68.9\%$ patients presenting with CE in the present study, and ranging from $25\%$ to $84\%$ , according to different techniques or time frames of assessment in other studies.36 These CEs may indeed represent different pathologic changes36 and do not seem to be mediated by the reperfusion status (as patients with unsuccessful reperfusion may also exhibit such CE). As BBB disruption promotes the extravasation of erythrocytes, platelets but also leukocytes such as neutrophils and lymphocytes, these CEs could be associated with early microvascular thrombosis.38,39 In this scenario, higher BP values after reperfusion could be a response to the BBB disruption and early microvascular thrombosis (partly explaining the classic association between higher BP values after reperfusion and worse outcomes12); but an intensive BP treatment could worsen downstream microvascular perfusion impairments and increase the odds of worse outcomes (i.e., treating the consequence, rather than the cause). Conversely, this could also explain why recent studies have highlighted associations between early and spontaneous SBP reduction after reperfusion and good 7,40   
outcomes.  

This study has several strengths. Studies were lacking regarding the effect of 2 different BP strategies (intensive reduction vs standard) on ICH occurrence or functional outcome according to BBB disruption. This point is important, as an intensive BP control after successful reperfusion is supposedly associated with lower odds of ICH. BP-TARGET is a unique opportunity to decipher the mechanisms underlying BBB disruption with BP management and their association with outcomes after EVT. Second, ICH rates were evaluated by an independent core laboratory, thus limiting several biases of previous observational studies. Third, CE was assessed using a flat-panel CT, a reliable and validated technique,21 currently used in most neurointerventional centers for the treatment of intracranial aneurysms or intracranial vascular malformations.41,42 This assessment in the angio suit makes sense, as it facilitates the management of severe and unstable patients, for whom another transfer could be deleterious, and is performed at the same time as reperfusion (defining a clear baseline of the postreperfusion window).  

However, several limitations must be acknowledged. Although BP-TARGET was an RCT, the present analysis is observational, potentially leading to confounding biases in the analysis. Second, the final infarct volume was not assessed, limiting the interpretation of our results. Third, the total volume of contrast injected during EVT was not systematically collected, and it is possible that higher total volumes could be associated with increased and more severe CE. Although randomization may have balanced the 2 groups on this parameter, we cannot exclude an imbalance regarding the amount of contrast injected, especially in the intensive SBP group. Fourth, this was a single-center analysis, with a relatively small sample size, which altogether may limit generalizability of our results. Therefore, further studies are needed to confirm these results.  

The presence of CE on the flat-panel CT performed immediately after EVT was consistently associated with ICH occurrence and worse outcomes. We are unable to conclude that there are increased rates of ICH and worse outcomes in patients presenting with CE and randomized in the intensive BP group. However, the differences in the ORs between the BP groups are large enough to suggest that further study into this question is warranted.
