# Tranexamic acid for acute intracerebral hemorrhage growth predicted by spot sign trial: Rationale and design  

Liping Liu1,2,3, Yilong Wang1,2,3, Xia Meng1,2,3, Na Li1,2,3, Ying Tan1,2,3, Ximing Nie1,2,3, Dacheng Liu1,2,3 and Xingquan Zhao1,2,3; on behalf of the TRAIGE investigators  

# Abstract  

Rationale: Acute intracerebral hemorrhage inflicts a high-economic and -health burden. Computed tomography angiography spot sign is a predictor of hematoma expansion, is associated with poor clinical outcome and is an important stratifying variable for patients treated with haemostatic therapy.  

Aims: We aim to compare the effect of treatment with tranexamic acid to placebo for the prevention of hemorrhage growth in patients with high-risk acute intracerebral hemorrhage with a positive spot sign.  

Design: The tranexamic acid for acute intracerebral hemorrhage growth predicted by spot sign (TRAIGE) is a prospective, multicenter, placebo-controlled, double-blind, investigator-led, randomized clinical trial that will include an estimated 240 participants. Patients with intracerebral hemorrhage demonstrating symptom onset within $^{8\mathfrak{h}}$ and with the spot sign as a biomarker for ongoing hemorrhage, and no contraindications for antifibrinolytic therapy, will be enrolled to receive either tranexamic acid or placebo. The primary outcome measure is the presence of hemorrhage growth defined as an increase in intracerebral hemorrhage volume $>33\%$ or ${>}6\mathsf{m}$ from baseline to $24\pm2\mathrm{h}$ . The secondary outcomes include safety and clinical outcomes.  

Conclusion: The TRAIGE trial evaluates the efficacy of haemostatic therapy with tranexamic acid in the prevention of hemorrhage growth among high-risk patients with acute intracerebral hemorrhage.

# Introduction and rationale  

Intracerebral hemorrhage (ICH) is a common subtype of stroke and has the highest mortality and disability. ICH comprises $17\%-55\%$ of all strokes in China, higher than that in Western countries.2 The China National Stroke Registry indicated that patients with ICH comprises $23.4\%$ of all hospitalized patients in the acute stage; $46\%$ of these patients die or are severely disabled within one year.  

ICH is a dynamic process. More than one-third of the patients with ICH have hematoma expansion within the first $24\mathrm{{h}}$ , most notably in the first $^\textrm{\scriptsize6h}$ .4 Hematoma expansion has been shown to be associated with early neurological deterioration and poor clinical outcome and is a promising intervention target.  

Hematoma expansion therapy with recombinant factor VIIa has been shown to limit hematoma expansion radiographically in a phase II trial.7 However, this finding did not translate to improved clinical outcomes in a larger Phase III trial ; patients with no hematoma growth did not benefit from haemostatic therapy and were also at risk for thromboembolic complications. These findings suggest that patients who are most likely to have hematoma expansion need to be identified as candidates for hemostatic therapy.  

Contrast extravasation as the ‘‘spot sign’’ in computed tomography angiography (CTA) is a recently described biomarker for hematoma growth9,10 and predicts neurological deterioration and poor clinical outcome at 90 days.11 A multicenter prospective study confirmed that the CTA spot sign is a predictor of hematoma expansion and recommended the spot sign as an entry criterion for future trials of haemostatic therapy in patients with acute ICH.12  

Tranexamic acid (TXA) is an antiEbrinolytic drug commonly used to reduce bleeding caused by trauma or surgery. In patients with major bleeding after trauma, TXA significantly reduced mortality with no increase in vascular occlusive events.13 In the subgroup analysis of CRASH-2 (CRASH-2 Intracranial Bleeding Study), patients with traumatic ICH, TXA had neither moderate benefits nor moderate harmful effects.14 Several studies have examined the efficacy of TXA in spontaneous ICH.15 Antifibrinolytic agents have been found to restrict hematoma expansion in a small case series involving patients with primary ICH with acceptable safety.16–18 Among patients with ICH within $24\mathrm{{h}}$ of onset, the combination of rapid administration of antifibrinolytics and strict blood pressure control produced a hematoma growth rate of $4.3\%$ , lower than previously reported.19 Compared to the higher cost of rFVIIa, TXA may be an effective and favorable economic choice.  

The tranexamic acid for acute ICH growth predicted by spot sign (TRAIGE) trial (NCT02625948) is designed to test TXA among patients with acute ICH that are at high risk of hemorrhage growth.

# Patient population  

Adult ICH patients with the spot sign evaluated as a biomarker for ongoing hemorrhage, and no contraindications for antifibrinolytic therapy will be enrolled and randomized within $^\mathrm{8h}$ of symptom onset. The detailed inclusion and exclusion criteria of the trial are listed in Table 1 in the online supplementary material.  

# Methods  

# Design  

TRAIGE is a prospective, multicenter, placebo-controlled, randomized clinical trial of patients with ICH and evidence of a spot-sign defined by CTA. Patients with symptom onset within $^\mathrm{8h}$ , with the spot sign as a biomarker for ongoing hemorrhage, and no contraindications for antifibrinolytic therapy will be enrolled. Patients will be randomized to receive either tranexamic acid or placebo. Participants with ICH with low risk for hemorrhage growth (determined by CTA ‘‘spot sign’’ negative) will be enrolled and receive standard treatment in a parallel prospective observational registry (Figure 1). The TRAIGE trial is anticipated to complete in 36 months from the first subject recruitment. A Data and Safety Monitoring Board (DSMB) will regularly monitor safety during the study. The trial has been approved by the Institutional Review Board/Ethics Committee in Beijing Tiantan Hospital.  

# Randomization  

Eligible patients will be evaluated for ‘‘spot sign’’ in CTA source imaging within $^{6\mathrm{h}}$ of symptom onset. Spot sign positive patients will be randomized to receive either the active treatment or placebo (1:1). The randomization (treatment) code list will be generated centrally by a computer, generated in blocks, to ensure approximate balance. The treatment code will be allocated using a centralized treatment allocation system at the baseline visit. After randomization, the study treatment must be administered to the patient within $^\mathrm{8h}$ of symptom onset.  

# Interventions  

At the baseline visit, eligible patients are evaluated for ‘spot sign’’ on CTA source imaging. The spot sign positive patients will be randomly assigned to one of two treatments groups, and the spot sign negative patients will be enrolled in a prospective observational registry. The schedule of study assessments is shown in Table 2 in the online supplementary material.  

All spot sign positive patients will be administered a $1-\mathrm{g}$ loading dose intravenous infusion of study medication (TXA or placebo in $100\mathrm{ml}$ of $0.9\%$ NaCl over $10\mathrm{min}$ followed by ${\mathrm{~l~g~}}$ of TXA or placebo in $250\mathrm{ml}$ of $0.9\%$ NaCl infused over $^{8\mathrm{h}}$ . The placebo will be $0.9\%$ NaCl.  

The study medications will be prepared in an indistinguishable (identical in size, shape, color, appearance) and labeled with the randomization code in externally indistinguishable sealed treatment kits. After randomization, a treatment number will be given to the investigator, who will open the box of the corresponding package to the patient. The investigators will not have access to the randomization (treatment) code, except in exceptional circumstances, such as occurrence of a serious adverse event for which knowledge of the study medication would be considered essential for treating the subject.  

All patients will be monitored closely in the neurological intensive care unit during infusion, and other medical treatment for all patients, including the patients with no spot sign, will be provided according to the standard guidelines for the management of spontaneous intracerebral hemorrhage in China. These guidelines include standard blood pressure-lowering treatment (SBP $<180\mathrm{mm}\mathrm{Hg}$ , general monitoring, nursing care, and medical complications management. To monitor thromboembolic events, clinical symptoms and physical examination, electrocardiogram will be performed at least once a day. Serum troponin, cerebral imaging, vascular ultrasound, pulmonary arteries CT angiograms, and other auxiliary examinations will be conducted as necessary. A blood sample will be collected within $24\mathrm{{h}}$ of ICH onset for exploratory research on biomarkers associated with hemorrhage growth.  

# Primary outcomes  

The primary outcome measure is the presence of ICH growth measured at $24\pm2\mathrm{h}$ , defined as an increase of hematoma volume $>33\%$ or ${>}6\mathrm{ml}$ from baseline. Hematoma volume will be analyzed by an imaging adjudication committee using a hematoma measurement software program.  

# Secondary outcomes  

Secondary outcomes include the following:  

. Safety endpoints-major thromboembolic events: (1) acute myocardial ischemia; (2) acute cerebral ischemia; and (3) acute pulmonary embolism.  

Absolute ICH growth volume compared to the baseline CT scan by $24\pm2\mathrm{h}$ . Absolute IVH growth volume compared to the baseline CT scan by $24\pm2\mathrm{h}$ .   
. Poor clinical outcome, defined as death or major disability (Modified Rankin Scale (mRS) 4–6) at $90\pm7$ days.   
Other thromboembolic events at $90\pm7$ days, including venous thrombosis and other peripheral arterial embolisms.   
. Death due to any cause by $90\pm7$ days.  

# Data monitoring body  

The management of the trial includes a Steering Committee co-chaired by Dr Liu and Dr Zhao, both whom will provide scientific and strategic direction for the trial and assume overall responsibility for the study design, execution, and publications. The members of the Steering Committee, and additional members including project directors, will be members of the Executive Committee.  

The independent DSMB, chaired by Dr David Wang from the Illinois Neurological Institute, Peoria, IL, USA, will meet regularly and monitor the progress of the study to ensure that the study meets the highest standards of ethics and patient safety. The DSMB comprises academic members, including an independent statistician, who are not otherwise participating in the trial. The DSMB may request an interim analysis if the reported deaths, major thromboembolic complications, and safety concerns must be balanced against efficacy.  

This trial will develop a corresponding data monitoring plan based on the level of risk. All adverse events will be recorded, managed properly, and receive followup until the event is appropriately resolved or reaches a stable condition. The primary researchers will conduct regular reviews of adverse events, risks, and benefits and will convene as necessary. An independent data monitoring committee will be responsible for high-risk research, providing input on whether to continue the project if necessary.  

# Sample size and feasibility  

Based on previous studies, hemorrhage growth was observed in $61\%$ of spot sign positive patients.11,12 Considering the treatment effect of TXA on spontaneous hemorrhagic diseases, it was projected that TXA would reduce the rate of acute hematoma growth by $25\%-35\%$ . The primary null hypothesis is that there will be no difference in hemorrhage growth by $24\pm2\mathrm{h}$ in spot sign positive patients treated with placebo and patients treated with TXA. The study will have $80\%$ power to detect a relative risk reduction of $30\%$ ( $42\%$ vs. $61\%$ ) with a two-sided alpha of 0.05, and a $10\%$ drop out rate. We plan a sample size of 240 patients, with 120 patients in each arm. According to our previous study, spot sign was detected in $21.6\%$ of hypertensive cerebral hemorrhage patients11; therefore, 1200 patients may need to be screened by CTA.  

# Statistical analyses  

The primary analysis will be by intention to treat. Missing outcome values will be imputed assuming the worst possible primary outcome (hemorrhage growth). The presence of hemorrhage growth between the two groups will be compared and adjusted for baseline volume using binary logistic regression. The primary analysis will be unadjusted. The analysis strategy outlined for the primary outcome will be used for the secondary analyses. Univariate analyses will be performed for examining associations of demographic, clinical, laboratory, and imaging variables with clinical outcome. Continuous endpoints will be summarized by means or medians, with treatment effects tested by the Student $t$ test or Mann–Whitney U test. All statistics will be two-sided with $p<0.05$ considered significant.  

# Study organization  

The management of TRAIGE includes an Executive Committee, which is responsible for the execution of the study design, protocol, data collection, and analysis plan, as well as publications. The Executive Committee membership comprises academic leaders and the principal investigators at all sites.  

All investigators will be trained for the TRAIGE protocol and certified for assessment of mRS, the National Institute of health stroke scale, and spot sign detection. The investigators will perform the clinical trial in accordance with this clinical trial protocol, the ICH guidelines for Good Clinical Practice, and all applicable regulatory requirements. All investigators are required to ensure compliance with all procedures required by the Clinical Trial Protocol and with all study procedures.  

# Discussion  

We plan to use CTA spot sign to identify patients at highest risk of hemorrhage growth that may benefit from haemostatic therapy. CTA spot sign is an effective predictor for ICH growth10,20–23 and is associated with poor clinical outcome.9,10 Several trials are now using spot sign as a screening method to identify an optimal treatment population. Two phase II trials of rFVIIa in ICH patients using spot sign for screening, STOP- $\cdot\Pi^{24}$ in the United States and SPOTLIGHT25 in Canada, were designed but halted due to low recruitment rates. A trial with TXA, STOP-AUST,26 is ongoing.  

The CTA scan is performed within $^{6\mathrm{h}}$ after ICH onset, similar to the PREDICT study12 and a previous study in our center,11 since most hemorrhage growth occurred within $^{6\mathrm{h}}$ .4 In a systematic review for spot sign and hematoma expansion, the spot-sign frequencies decreased as scan time onset increased, with the time-to- $\mathrm{CTA}{>}8\mathrm{h}$ , and the sensitivity and positive predictive value (PPV) of the spot sign to predict hematoma growth was only 0.3 and 0.33.27  

Several small cases in randomized control studies tested the safety and efficacy of TXA in hemorrhagic stroke patients. In a randomized controlled pilot trial, TXA treatment for spontaneous intracerebral hemorrhage (TICH) (16 TXA vs. 8 placebo) within $24\mathrm{{h}}$ after ictus was shown to be feasible, and there were no significant differences in adverse events, hematoma expansion, death, and dependency.28 A single-center, single-blind randomized control trial with 30 patients compared the effect of TXA to placebo on the growth of hematomas in patients with spontaneous ICH. Significant hematoma expansion occurred in control group ( $14.33\mathrm{-}17.99\mathrm{mL}$ , $p=0.001\$ ) whereas there was no significant hematoma size expansion in the treatment group.29 Ongoing trials aim to explore the efficacy of TXA in patients with ICH. The emergency management of spontaneous intracerebral hemorrhage (EsICH), a multicenter double-blind RCT, plans to recruit patients within $^{8\mathrm{h}}$ of onset. Patients will receive TXA or placebo and biomarkers will be tested; this trial is not yet recruiting patients.30 The ongoing phase 3 trial of TXA for intracerebral hemorrhage (TICH-2) is a prospective, double-blind randomized trial assessing safety and whether TXA in ICH within $^\mathrm{8h}$ of onset can reduce death and dependency at day 90.31 A phase II randomized, placebo-controlled, double-blind, multicenter trial, STOP-AUST, is recruiting patients to evaluate the efficacy of TXA in ICH within $4.5\mathrm{h}$ of onset with spot sign. The TRAIGE trial has a similar design as the STOP-AUST trial, but includes a longer inclusion window ( $4.5\mathrm{h}$ in STOP-AUST) and a larger sample size because of a prudent estimated treatment effect (in the STOP-AUST design, the estimated treatment effect is $31\%$ ). In TRAIGE trial, hemorrhage with the lateral ventricle involved $\geq50\%$ are excluded, whereas this stipulation is not stated in the STOPAUST trial. Considering the similarity of design, a further pooled analysis may be conducted.  

# Summary and conclusions  

The TRAIGE trial will provide important information on whether TXA is effective for the prevention of hemorrhage growth in high risk patients with ICH, which present with a CTA spot sign. It may open a path for more precise risk stratification and hemostatic therapy for patients with acute ICH and will provide useful information for further studies to assess the safety and efficacy of TXA in ICH.
