# Abstract  

Recombinant human prourokinase (rhPro-UK) is a novel thrombolytic that has been approved to treat patients with acute myocardial infarction. However, the safety and efficacy of intravenous rhPro-UK in patients with acute ischemic stroke (AIS) has not been well established. We aimed to investigate the safety and preliminary efficacy of rhPro-UK in patients with AIS in a multi-center phase IIa trial setting. One hundred nineteen patients within $4.5\mathrm{~h~}$ of AIS onset were enrolled in this randomized, open-label, 23-center phase IIa clinical trial. Patients were randomly assigned to $35~\mathrm{mg}$ $(\mathrm{n}=40)$ or $50~\mathrm{mg}$ $\mathbf{\chi}_{\mathrm{n}=39}$ ) intravenous rhPro-UK or $0.9\mathrm{mg/kg}$ recombinant tissue plasminogen activator (r-tPA; $\mathrm{n}{=}40$ ). The primary endpoint was functional independence defined as a modified Rankin scale (mRS) score of 0 or 1 at 90 days. The secondary outcome was early neurological improvement defined as a reduction of $\geq4$ points on the National Institutes of Health Stroke Scale (NIHSS) score from baseline to $^{24\mathrm{h}}$ after drug administration. Safety endpoints included death due to any cause, symptomatic intracerebral hemorrhage (sICH), and other serious adverse events (SAEs). The proportion of patients with an mRS score of $\leq1$ at 90 days did not differ significantly among three groups $35~\mathrm{mg}$ rhPro-UK: $55.56\%$ vs. $50~\mathrm{mg}$ rhPro-UK: $57.89\%$ vs. vs. r-tPA: $52.63\%$ ; $\mathrm{P}{=}0.92$ ). The rates of treatment response, referring to early neurological improvement, were similar among these three groups ( $36.11\%$ vs. $31.58\%$ vs. $28.95\%$ , respectively; $\mathrm{P}{=}0.85,$ ). There was no difference in mortality at 90 days or in the rate of other SAEs among the three groups. One patient in the $50\mathrm{mg}$ rhPro-UK group suffered sICH. While neither the primary efficacy outcomes nor safety profile differed significantly among the low, high rhPro-UK and control groups, it is a logical step to further test the low-dose rhPro-UK group versus the control group in a well-powered phase III study. # Introduction  

Ischemic stroke is a major cause of disability and death worldwide. Intravenous recombinant tissue plasminogen activator (r-tPA) is widely recommended as a standard treatment for acute ischemic stroke (AIS) in most clinical practice guidelines [1]. However, a narrow therapeutic window, potential hemorrhagic complications and a high cost limit the use of r-tPA, especially in developing countries. There has been high enthusiasm to develop an alternative thrombolytic that is more cost-effective and safer.  

Recombinant human prourokinase (rhPro-UK) is a novel thrombolytic. It is a single-chain zymogenic plasminogen activator that preferentially activates plasminogen on the surface of fibrin and induces fibrin-selective clot lysis. In a rat model of thromboembolic stroke, Hao [2] found that intravenous infusion of pro-urokinase (Pro-UK) in the acute/early stage $1.5\mathrm{h}$ after embolism) reduced neurological deficit and decreased lesion and infarct volume without increasing intracranial hemorrhage (ICH) compared with urokinase and r-tPA. In a rabbit model with cerebral ischemia, researchers demonstrated that intravenous Pro-UK improved thrombolysis and recanalization with a reduced risk of cerebral hemorrhage, exerting therapeutic effects within a 6-h timeframe [3]. Takano et al. [4] showed that both intra-arterial and intravenous Pro-UK significantly promoted reperfusion and decreased lesion in an embolic stroke model, indicating that intra-arterial and intravenous therapy with Pro-UK are equally effective in promoting reperfusion and inhibiting the development of focal ischemic injury in embolic stroke.  

With potential advantages over other thrombolytics, including a greater efficacy and less adverse reactions, rhPro-UK has shown convincing efficacy, as well as a good safety profile, in patients with acute myocardial infarction (AMI) [5]. The Prolyse in Acute Cerebral Thromboembolism trial showed that intra-arterial infusion of local Pro-UK was superior to a control drug in recanalization in patients with AIS with M1 or M2 middle cerebral artery occlusion [6]. However, the benefits and safety of intravenous rhProUK for the treatment of patients with AIS has not been fully established. The aim of this phase IIa clinical trial was to investigate the safety and efficacy of intravenous rhPro-UK in Chinese patients with AIS within the $4.5\mathrm{-h}$ treatment window from AIS onset.  

# Materials and Methods  

# Study design and participants  

Twenty-three study sites in China participated in this phase IIa, randomized, open-label, multi-center clinical trial. The clinical trial was accepted by the National Medical Products Administration (No. 2016L05323) and approved by the institutional review boards of the 23 participating centers. The study was conducted in accordance with the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki. Written informed consent was obtained from all patients prior to enrollment. Xuanwu  

Hospital, Capital Medical University, was the data coordination center. Data management and statistics were conducted by HBR Data Science Co., Ltd. The trial is registered with chictr.org.cn, number ChiCTR1800016519.  

Patients eligible for the trial were aged 18–80 years with a diagnosis of AIS with neurological deficit (National Institutes of Health Stroke Scale [NIHSS] score of 4–25 points) who underwent treatment within $4.5\mathrm{h}$ after AIS onset. Cerebral computed tomography (CT) was performed before randomization to exclude patients with ICH or major ischemic infarction. Patients with contraindications to stroke thrombolysis were not eligible for the study. The exclusion criteria included transient ischemic attack, cardiogenic embolism, an episode of stroke or myocardial infarction within the previous 3 months, a history of ICH, active visceral hemorrhage or a high hemorrhagic risk, anticoagulant use, severe uncontrolled hypertension, a blood glucose concentration of $<2.7\mathrm{mmol/L}$ o $\mathrm{\Deltar>22.2mmol/L}$ . Detailed clinical, CT, and laboratory inclusion and exclusion criteria are described in Table 1.  

# Randomization and masking  

Eligible patients within $4.5\mathrm{~h~}$ of AIS onset were randomly assigned in a 1:1:1 ratio to receive intravenous rhPro-UK at $50~\mathrm{mg}$ (group A), rhPro-UK at $35~\mathrm{mg}$ (group B), or r-tPA at $0.9\mathrm{mg/kg}$ (maximum $90~\mathrm{mg}$ ; group C). An interactive network web system was used at all participating centers, and randomization was carried out online and in a centralized manner by filling out an electronic case report form. This was an open-label trial and did not involve masking of treatment or outcomes.  

# Study procedures  

rhPro-UK (batch No. 20160701) was obtained from Tasly Biopharmaceuticals Co., Ltd.(Tianjin, China), and r-tPA was obtained from Boehringer Ingelheim (Ingelheim am Rhein, Germany). Patients who were randomized to receive intravenous rhPro-UK at $50~\mathrm{mg}$ (group A) were administered a $20{\cdot}\mathrm{mg}$ rhPro-UK bolus within $3\mathrm{min}$ , followed by a continuous infusion within $30\mathrm{min}$ . In group B, patients who were randomized to receive intravenous rhPro-UK at $35~\mathrm{mg}$ were administered a $15\mathrm{-mg}$ bolus within $3\mathrm{min}$ , followed by a continuous infusion within $30\mathrm{min}$ . In group C, patients were administered a full dose of r-tPA according to treatment guidelines [7] $0.9\mathrm{mg/kg}$ , maximum $90~\mathrm{mg}$ ; $10\%$ bolus followed by a $60{\cdot}\mathrm{min}$ infusion). No antiplatelet agents were permitted within $^{24\mathrm{h}}$ after administration of the investigational drug, and no anticoagulants were permitted at any time in this study. All other treatments were guided by the standard of care of thrombolysis in ischemic stroke. The assessment schedule is summarized in Table 2.

Assessments were made at the time of enrollment; at 2, 6, and $^{24\textrm{h}}$ after drug administration; and at 7, 14, 30, and 90 days after drug treatment. Vital signs, including blood pressure, oxygenation, and heart rate, were closely monitored for the first $^{24\textrm{h}}$ . Initial assessments made at the time of enrollment included a physical examination, head CT, quantification of neurologic deficit using the NIHSS, and the level of global disability using the modified Rankin Scale (mRS). During the follow-up period, patients were assessed with the NIHSS on days 1, 7, 14, 30, and 90, and with the mRS on days 30 and 90. The mRS evaluation was performed by a certificated or trained neurological physician, and telephone visits were performed if patients were unable to return to the hospital for a visit.  

CT was performed at baseline and at $24\mathrm{~h~}$ after randomization. Additional CT scans were performed at the discretion of local physicians. The local study team assessed safety data and adjudicated each adverse event to determine if it was treatment-related or not.  

# Outcome assessment  

The primary efficacy endpoint was disability at 90 days (3-month visit), as assessed by the mean mRS score, and dichotomized as a favorable outcome (a score of 0 or 1) or an unfavorable outcome (a score of 2–6). The secondary efficacy endpoint was early neurologic improvement (defined as a reduction in NIHSS score of $\geq4$ points at $24\mathrm{~h~}$ ), NIHSS score measured at different assessment timepoints, and recurrence of AIS within 7 days after treatment.

Safety endpoints included symptomatic ICH (sICH) within 30 days, overall mortality, any ICH, and other serious adverse events (SAEs) within 90 days after treatment. sICH [8] was defined as any apparently extravascular blood in the brain or within the cranium, combined with neurological deterioration of at least 4 points from baseline NIHSS score, or leading to death, that was identified as the predominant cause of clinical deterioration. Any ICH was classified according to the Heidelberg Bleeding Classification [9].

The $\chi^{2}$ test was used to identify differences in categorical variables (the proportion of subjects with an mRS score of 0–1 and an mRS score of 0–2) between groups. For NIHSS score, descriptive methods were used to characterize the sample using the measured value and the value change from baseline. The t-test was used to compare the NIHSS score at each timepoint with the baseline NIHSS score. An analysis of co-variance, with baseline as the covariant and treatment as the fixed effect, was used to compare the change in NIHSS score. In order to study the effects of different factors on efficacy and safety, Logistic regression analysis was used to establish a regression model.

# Results  

From December 2016 to November 2017, 119 patients were enrolled within the 4.5-h therapeutic time window at 23 centers in China. Among these patients, seven were excluded due to consent withdrawal (2 patients) or withdrawal by the enrolling physician before treatment due to an error in assessing patient eligibility (4 patients) or in randomization (1 patient) (Fig. 1). Thirty-eight patients were assigned to receive $50~\mathrm{mg}$ of rhPro-UK (group A), 36 patients were assigned to receive $35~\mathrm{mg}$ of rhPro-UK (group B), and 38 patients were treated with r-tPA (group C). The baseline demographic and clinical characteristics of patients are listed in Table 3. The summary of prior “heart diseases” was presented in Supplementary Table 1. There were no significant differences between groups at baseline.

# Discussion  

We conducted a randomized controlled study to assess the safety and efficacy of rhPro-UK in patients with AIS. The present clinical trial showed no statistically significant difference in the primary efficacy outcomes (proportional patients with $\mathrm{mRS0}$ or 1) and safety outcomes between two intravenous rhPro-UK dose groups and control group ( i.e., r-tPA) in patients with AIS within $4.5\mathrm{~h~}$ of AIS onset. Both the low and high intravenous rhPro-UK appeared to be safe with possible efficacy in patients with AIS within $4.5\mathrm{~h~}$ of AIS onset.  

Minimal deactivation by circulating inhibitors and clotspecific plasminogen activation are two advantages of ProUK. In contrast to r-tPA, Pro-UK induces minimal $(<5\%)$ or no re-thrombosis and exerts no procoagulant effect in blood [10]. The Prolyse in acute cerebral thromboembolism trial assessed the safety and recanalization efficacy of local intraarterial delivery of Pro-UK in patients with AIS within $^{6\mathrm{h}}$ .  

The study demonstrated a partial/complete recanalization rate of initially occluded cerebral vessels of $57.7\%$ with Pro-UK compared with $15.4\%$ in patients receiving placebo [6]. The partial/complete recanalization rate observed with intravenous r-tPA was approximately $34.4\%$ [11]. Furthermore, Furlan et al. [12] found that $40\%$ of patients receiving Pro-UK and $25\%$ of patients in the control group had an mRS score of $\leq2$ at 90 days, despite ICH with neurological deterioration within $^{24\textrm{h}}$ occurring in $10\%$ of patients who received Pro-UK and $2\%$ of control patients. These findings suggest that benefit with intra-arterial Pro-UK within $^\textrm{\scriptsize6h}$ of AIS onset caused by middle cerebral artery occlusion is significant over the risk of early sICH. In our study, $63.16\%$ of patients receiving high-dose rhPro-UK and $66.67\%$ of patients receiving low-dose rhPro-UK intravenously achieved mRS score $\leq2$ at 90 days. The clinical response with intravenous rhPro-UK did not come at a cost of increased ICH.  

In the $4.5{\cdot}\mathrm{h}$ time window, the median time from stroke onset to initiation of intravenous thrombolysis was approximately $195~\mathrm{min}$ . Without an increase in SAEs and sICH risk, the proportion of patients with slight or no neurological disability at 90 days exceeded $50\%$ with rhPro-UK treatment at either dose. In a meta-analysis of five randomized trials [13], the crude cumulative rates of disability-free $(\mathrm{mRS~}0{-}1)$ outcome at 3 months were tenecteplase $57.9\%$ versus alteplase $55.4\%$ , the crude summary sICH rates were tenecteplase $3\%$ versus alteplase $3\%$ , and the crude mortality rates at 3 months were tenecteplase $7.6\%$ versus alteplase $8.1\%$ . We noted a similar proportion of patients with very favorable outcomes compared with previous studies [13–18]. The drug effect was not significantly different from that of r-tPA, indicating that rhPro-UK is likely to be effective as a treatment in patients with AIS within $4.5\mathrm{~h~}$ of symptom onset. Furthermore, the use of rhPro-UK $35~\mathrm{mg}$ is similar in efficacy and safety to rhPro-UK $50\mathrm{mg}$ and r-tPA $0.9\mathrm{mg/}$ kg. These findings are preliminary but encouraging. Unlike r-tPA, rhPro-UK does not require weight-based administration, which may reduce the delay from admission to thrombolysis. It is reasonable to use rhPro-UK at a dose of $35~\mathrm{mg}$ as the comparator to proceed to a phase III trial of rhPro-UK versus r-tPA in the time window.  

The question remains as to whether a compelling costeffective argument can be made for the use of rhPro-UK over r-tPA. Recently, rhpro-UK has been approved by National Medical Products Administration for AMI. For the management of acute ST-segment elevation myocardial infarction, rhPro-UK has been used in 2900 hospitals in more than 300 cities in China. Its use can be fully guaranteed, not only at large hospitals in big cities, but also at primary medical institutions. The price of single-treatment rhPro-UK is $\$787$ , whichishalfthepriceofr-tPA $(\$1577)$ . In emergency cases, the cost of r-tPA can be greater than patients can afford. Thus, some patients cannot afford to undergo thrombolysis, even if they arrive within the treatment window. However, evidence supports the use of other alternatives, such as rhPro-UK, which are also effective in the management of AIS in China.  

This randomized phase IIa clinical trial is limited by its relatively small sample size. We should interpret the study results with cautions. Although the differences were not statistically significant, there were several imbalances at baseline. A more sophisticated method of randomization might have prevented this imbalance, but a simple and rapid randomization process was desired, and it is unlikely that this imbalance would have affected the results. Second, as an open-label study, the patients were not blinded to treatment assignments, and outcomes was not assessed in a blinded fashion. Despite we have taken several measures, such as selection of stroke centers with Good Clinical Practice (GCP) certification, conduction of uniform training for researchers, and regular supervision and inspection on centers, certain measurement bias cannot completely ruled out. Third, the selection criteria in this study were more stringent than the current criteria used for r-tPA. Some patients who were eligible for thrombolysis on the basis of current criteria were excluded from this trial. A phase III study would be needed to determine whether the efficacy and safety of rhPro-UK extends to this broader population of patients. Forth, advance imaging about perfusion lesion or vessel occlusion and post-thrombolysis MRI scan were not mandated. The information of reperfusion and recanalization on imaging was absent, and we were not able to estimate the likely proportion of mimics (number excluded as having non-stroke diagnoses). However, the use of CT scans in the present study gives a wider applicability of this drug, as CT scans are widely available, even in smaller cities.  

Although the efficacy of rhPro-UK does not differ from that of r-tPA, increased ease of administration and cost-effectiveness offer an advantage of rhPro-UK, further investigation of rhPro-UK for AIS is therefore merited. The potential benefit could be more obvious in a larger randomize cohort study. Whether this strategy could be beneficial in improving long-term clinical outcomes needs to be clarified in larger studies. Furthermore, the optimal strategy and the influencing factors of curative effect need to be confirmed in further.  

# Conclusion  

In summary, this phase IIa, open-label, randomized, multicenter clinical trial supports that both low- and high-dose intravenous rhPro-UK are safe in patients with AIS who are within the $4.5\mathrm{-h}$ treatment window. The primary efficacy outcome defined as mRS of 0 or 1 at 90 day does not differ among three groups. This study provides critical data to plan a phase III multi-center clinical trial to further test the safety and efficacy of the $35~\mathrm{mg}$ low dose of rhPro-UK.
