# Safety and efficacy of tirofiban treatment in the endovascular treatment of patients with acute ischaemic stroke – A meta-analysis

# ABSTRACT  

Object: The use of endovascular therapy (EVT) has become a widespread strategy for the clinical management of acute ischemic stroke (AIS). However, the combination of arterial injection of tirofiban with EVT for AIS con­ tinues to be a subject of controversy. This meta-analysis was conducted to assess the safety and efficacy of this treatment approach.  

Methods: Relevant studies were identified through a systematic literature search in Pubmed, EMBASE, Web of Science, and Cochrane Library databases, covering articles published from January 2010 to January 2023. The efficacy outcomes included favorable functional outcomes, recanalization rates, and safety outcomes including mortality and symptomatic intracranial hemorrhage (sICH).  

Results: The meta-analysis consisted of data from 13 studies, which included 1 randomized controlled trial (RCT), 7 prospective cohort studies, and 5 retrospective cohort studies, encompassing a total of 3477 patients. The study results indicate that the intra-arterial (IA) tirofiban $+\mathrm{EVT}$ for AIS is associated with significant improvements in favorable functional outcomes (OR, 1.21; $95\%\mathrm{CI}$ , 1.05–1.40; $\mathbf{P}=0.009.$ and recanalization rate (OR, 1.33; $95\%$ CI, 1.06–1.65; $\mathbf{P}=0.01$ ), as well as significant reductions in mortality rates (OR, 0.65; $95\%\mathrm{CI}$ , 0.53–0.79; $\mathbf{P}=$ 0.0001). Subgroup analysis revealed that administering a maintenance dose of intravenous (IV) tirofiban postEVT was significantly associated with improved functional outcomes and reduced mortality in patients. In addition, there was no increase in the incidence of sICH (OR, 0.92; $95\%\mathrm{CI}$ , 0.71–1.20; $\mathbf{P}=0.54$ ).  

Conclusion: The administration of Intra-arterial tirofiban combined with EVT is an effective and safe treatment strategy for AIS, and postoperative maintenance doses of intravenous tirofiban may be more effective than IA only.  

# 1. Introduction  

Acute ischemic stroke continues to pose a significant threat to patient health, contributing significantly to both mortality and disability rates [1]. As such, endovascular therapy has become a widely adopted treatment option for acute ischemic stroke[2–4]. Despite its widespread use, the outcomes of a significant proportion of patients who are treated with endovascular therapy remain poor, with $30\%$ failing to achieve reperfusion[5]. The potential for intra-arterial platelet glycoprotein (GP) IIb/IIIa receptor inhibitor, tirofiban, to improve the outcomes of endovascular therapy has generated considerable attention. Tirofiban effectively inhibits the final pathway of platelet activation, thus pre­ venting thrombosis[6]. However, there is ongoing debate regarding the safety and efficacy of intra-arterial tirofiban in the treatment of acute  

ischemic stroke.  

The objective of this meta-analysis was to perform a systematic re­ view of the available evidence to assess the safety and efficacy of intraarterial tirofiban in the endovascular treatment of patients with acute ischemic stroke. Furthermore, a subgroup analysis was conducted to investigate the advantages of postoperative maintenance doses of intravenous tirofiban.  

# 2. Methods  

# 2.1. Search strategy  

An extensive database search was carried out to identify relevant literature pertaining to the subject of intra-arterial tirofiban combined with EVT for the AIS. The search was conducted using a combination of subject terms (i.e., stroke, endovascular therapy and tirofiban) and free terms in the following databases: PubMed, Cochrane Library databases, Web of Science, and EMBASE. The search was limited to articles pub­ lished between January 2010 and January 2023 to ensure the results are up-to-date. In this review, the selection of studies was restricted to clinical subjects, and no language limitations were imposed. To elimi­ nate duplicate of articles, a reference management software, Endnote X9, was utilized. Subsequently, the researchers independently reviewed the title, abstract, and full text of each article that was identified by the literature search to determine its eligibility for inclusion in the review. Furthermore, the list of the selected articles was also scrutinized to ensure that no relevant studies were missed.  

# 3. Study selection  

The criteria for the inclusion of articles in this review were as fol­ lows: (1) studies that compared AIS patients who received EVT in combination with intra-arterial tirofiban to those who received EVT alone; and (2) studies with study designs including randomized controlled trials (RCTs) and cohort studies.  

The exclusion criteria for the articles were as follows: (1) duplicate articles and studies sourced from duplicate databases; (2) single-arm trials, editorial pieces, letters to the editor, conference abstracts and posters, review articles, case reports, and animal experimental studies; (3) articles from which relevant data could not be extracted.  

# 4. Data extraction and quality assessment  

The data extracted from the eligible studies were independently evaluated and assessed by two researchers in accordance with the prespecified inclusion criteria. In the event of any disagreements, a consensus was reached through discussion. The data extracted from the eligible studies included the name of the first author, year of publication, country of study, study design, study center, sample size, and informa­ tion on the therapeutic strategies employed, including the strategy of EVT and the strategy of tirofiban use. The quality of the cohort studies was evaluated using the Newcastle-Ottawa Scale (NOS), with a highquality study defined as having an NOS ranking of 7–9[7]. Cohort studies with an NOS score of $\geq7$ and RCTs were considered to be of high quality.  

# 5. Outcomes  

We evaluated the safety outcomes of symptomatic intracranial hemorrhage (sICH) incidence and mortality at 3 month post EVT. The definition of sICH was in accordance with the European Cooperative Acute Stroke Study III criteria[8]. The primary efficacy outcomes assessed in this paper were favorable functional outcomes and post-operative recanalization rates. Favorable functional outcome was defined as a score of 0–2 on the modified Rankin Scale (mRS)[9]. Recanalization was defined as a Tissue Thrombolysis in Cerebral Ischemia (TICI) score of $\geq2\ensuremath{\mathrm{b}}[10]$ .  

# 6. Statistical analysis  

The data were analyzed using the Cochrane Collaboration Review Manager software (version 5.3) and Stata Software Package (version 15.0). In this study, all outcomes were analyzed using the MantelHaenszel method, with pooled odds ratios (OR) and corresponding $95\%$ confidence intervals (CIs) calculated[11]. The choice of fixed-effects or random-effects model was determined based on the heterogeneity among all included studies. The degree of heterogeneity was assessed using the I2 statistic obtained by the $\chi2$ test. A value of I2 greater than $50\%$ was considered indicative of substantial heterogene­ ity, and in such cases, the random-effects model was used. In contrast, if the I2 value was less than $50\%$ , the fixed-effects model according to the Mantel-Haenszel method was applied.Forest plots were used to present the data, and statistical significance was defined as a $P$ value $<0.05$ . In addition, all primary outcomes were assessed in subgroups based on whether a postoperative maintenance dose of intravenous tirofiban was administered. However, this subgroup analysis of recanalization rates was not possible due to the immediate postoperative evaluation. To further explore potential sources of heterogeneity, we conducted sensitivity analyses by leave-one-out cross validation method [11]. Publication bias was evaluated through an assessment of funnel plot symmetry and Egger’s test[12].  

# 7. Results  

# 7.1. Search results and study characteristics  

A total of 630 studies were identified during the initial search. Following screening and evaluation, a final set of 13 studies were deemed eligible for inclusion in the final analysis. (Table 1) The com­ bined sample included 3477 patients, of which 1167 were assigned to the tirofiban group and 2310 to the control group. Our meta-analysis included a diverse set of study designs, encompassing 1 randomized controlled trial (RCT)[13], 7 prospective cohort studies[14–20], and 5 retrospective cohort studies[21–25].  

Of the 13 studies included in the meta-analysis, 10 were conducted in China, 1 in Germany, 1 in Korea, and 1 in the Switzerland. Table 2 summarizes the characteristics and quality assessment results of all included studies. Most of the studies employed intravenous tirofiban after EVT, with the exception by Sun et al. [16], in which the study did not provide details of the intravenous dose used. EVT was predomi­ nantly performed using Mechanical thrombectomy, and the use of remedial treatment such as balloon angioplasty or stent placement was left to the discretion of the operator based on intraoperative field conditions.  

# 7.2. Efficacy and safety outcomes  

# 7.2.1. Efficacy outcomes  

7.2.1.1. Favorable functional outcome. We evaluated the favorable functional outcomes $(\mathrm{mRS}0{-}2)$ of all 3477 patients 3 months after EVT. The results of our analysis revealed significant association between the use of tirofiban in combination with EVT and favourable functional outcomes (OR, 1.21; $95\%\mathrm{CI}$ , 1.05–1.40; $P=0.009_{.}$ ). Subgroup analysis showed that $\mathrm{IA+}$ maintenance dose IV significantly improved functional outcomes (OR, 1.20; $95\%\mathrm{GI}$ , 1.01–1.42; $P=0.04_{.}$ ), but no significant difference was found in the IA subgroup (OR, 1.25; $95\%\mathrm{CI}$ , 0.94–1.64; $P$ $=0.12\AA.$ ). Furthermore, there was no significant heterogeneity observed among the studies included in our analysis. (Fig. I)  

7.2.1.2. Recanalization rate. Our analysis included 12 studies that re­ ported on the recanalization of 2921 patients after EVT. Of these pa­ tients, 1119 were treated with tirofiban in combination with EVT, while 2264 received EVT alone. Our analysis showed that tirofiban signifi­ cantly improved postoperative recanalization rate (OR, 1.33; $95\%\mathrm{CI}$ , 1.06–1.65; $P=0.01$ ). Furthermore, no significant heterogeneity was observed across the studies included in our analysis. (Fig. II)

# 7.3. Sensitivity analysis and publication bias  

# 7.3.1. Sensitivity analysis  

Through sensitivity analysis, we found no significant differences between sensitivity and pooled analyses in terms of safety outcomes for sICH, favorable functional outcome and mortality. These findings sug­ gest that the safety outcomes of our meta-analysis are robust and reli­ able. However, after excluding the two studies conducted by Zhang, Y [12]. et al. and Pan et al. [15], the meta-analysis results showed no significant difference in recanalisation rates between patients treated with IA tirofiban in combination with EVT and patients treated with EVT alone (Supply 1–4).  

# 7.3.2. Publication bias  

We used funnel plots and Egger’s test to assess the potential for publication bias in the studies included in our analysis. Both visual in­ spection of the plot and Egger’s test provided no evidence of publication bias (Supply 5–12).  

# 8. Discussion  

Recently, tirofiban combined with EVT has emerged as a critical development in the management of AIS. However, the efficacy of intravenous administration of tirofiban is limited. A multicentre, rand­ omised, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial conducted by Zhongming Qiu et al.[26] failed to show a significant difference in the efficacy of intravenous tirofiban priorEVT in comparison with EVT alone. More­ over, intravenous tirofiban after EVT investigated by Kellert et al. [27] did not improve recanalisation rates and was associated with an increased risk of fatal cerebral haemorrhage. In contrast, arterial injec­ tion of tirofiban significantly increased intravascular drug concentra­ tions and improved efficacy [28], indicating that this route of administration may represent a superior treatment option for AIS.  

Our meta-analysis investigated the impact of intra-arterial tirofiban tirofiban on clinical outcomes in patients with AIS undergoing EVT. Our results demonstrate that intra-arterial tirofiban is associated with sig­ nificant improvements in recanalisation rates, good functional out­ comes, and reduced mortality in this patient population. Notably, the use of tirofiban did not increase the risk of symptomatic sICH in these patients. These findings suggest that the intra-arterial route of tirofiban administration may be a promising treatment option for patients with AIS undergoing EVT, with potential benefits for clinical outcomes and a favorable safety profile. Moreover, our study demonstrated the absence of publication bias by funnel plot symmetry and Egger’s test, providing further support for the stability of our results.

To explore the potential sources of heterogeneity observed in our meta-analysis, we conducted a sensitivity analysis. We performed a sensitivity analysis of recanalisation rates, which showed no significant difference in recanalisation rates between patients with arterial in­ jections of tirofiban combined with EVT and those with EVT alone after excluding the studies of Pan et al. or Zhang Y et al. [12]. Our findings suggest that the lack of difference in recanalization rates may be attributed to several factors: 1) the study by Pan et al. only included acute ischemic stroke patients with posterior circulation occlusion, whereas all other studies included patients with anterior circulation. This difference is important as patients with posterior circulation – who usually have an indication for EVT – tend to have a lower curative effect than patients with anterior circulation [29]. This difference in patient population may have contributed to the lack of significant difference in recanalization rates between the tirofiban and non-tirofiban groups; 2) the study by Zhang Y et al. [12] was a RCT, which differed from the other 12 studies included in our meta-analysis. This difference in study design may have introduced bias and increased the heterogeneity observed in our analysis [30]. Overall, our findings underscore the importance of carefully considering both study design and patient characteristics when interpreting the results of meta-analyses in the context of stroke treatment.  

As a component of our analysis, we performed a subgroup analysis by dividing patients into two groups based on their maintenance dose of tirofiban IV: the IA group and the $\mathrm{IA+}$ maintenance dose IV group. This approach allowed us to investigate the potential impact of different tirofiban administration protocols on treatment outcomes. This Sub­ group analysis revealed a significant correlation between maintenance dose IV dosing following IA tirofiban and improved functional outcome as well as reduced mortality after EVT for AIS. The two outcomes of mortality and sICH incidence in this study were similar to the metaanalysis by Zhao H et al.[31]. The difference was that the functional outcome in this study was significantly improved. It should be noted that the subgroup analysis by Zhao et al. [31] had some limitations in that their subgroup confounded patients on maintenance dose IV after high dose IV and patients on maintenance dose IV after IA, which may have resulted in no significant difference in functional outcome between the two groups. Therefore, arterial injection of tirofiban combined with EVT treatment followed by maintenance dose IV tirofiban may be a more effective treatment option. Additionally, in the context of EVT, sICH is a significant and potentially life-threatening complication. However, our analysis found that the use of tirofiban did not result in an increainci­ dence of sICH in either the IA subgroup or the $\mathrm{IA+}$ maintenance dose IV subgroup. These results provide evidence supporting the safety of tir­ ofiban as an adjunctive therapy in stroke patients undergoing EVT. These findings are consistent with previous studies [32–34].  

It is important to acknowledge several limitations when interpreting the results of our meta-analysis. Firstly, the included literature consisted of only one RCT and the remaining studies were cohort studies, which may increase the risk of bias. Secondly, there were variations in EVT strategies across the studies, such as mechanical thrombectomy, stent­ ing, and balloon angioplasty. Additionally, the decision to use tirofiban and the EVT strategy were based on operator judgment, which may have influenced our final results. Thirdly, most of the included studies were of Chinese origin, with only three non-Chinese studies, potentially intro­ ducing a geographic and demographic bias. These limitations highlight the need for further well-designed studies to confirm our findings and provide more robust evidence for the use of tirofiban as an adjunctive therapy for stroke patients undergoing EVT.  

In summary, this meta-analysis shows that the combination of EVT and IA tirofiban significantly improves the prognosis and recanalisation of patients with AIS, reduces mortality and does not increase the risk of intracranial haemorrhage compared to EVT alone. In addition, intra­ operative IA tirofiban combined with postoperative maintenance dose IV may be a more effective treatment strategy. More high-quality mul­ ticentre, randomised controlled, double-blind, placebo trials that in­ volves more diversities in demography are needed in the future to confirm this finding.
