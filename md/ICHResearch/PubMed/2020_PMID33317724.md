# abstract  

Chronic subdural haemorrhage (CSDH) is a common neurosurgical entity with complex pathophysiological pathways. The generally favourable surgical outcome may be affected by its associated risks including recurrence rates. We performed a prospective randomized multi-center clinical trial comparing the addition of tranexamic acid (TXA) to standard neurosurgical procedures for patients with symptomatic CSDH. The primary endpoint was CSDH requiring repeat surgery within 6-month post-operatively. Secondary endpoints were comparison of post-operative volumes between the treatment arms and safety evaluation of the dosing regime. 90 patients were analyzed with 49 patients in the observation arm and 41 patients in the TXA arm. The observation arm had five $(10.2\%)$ recurrences compared to two $(4.8\%$ $\mathtt{p}=0.221\$ ) in the TXA arm. Patients in the TXA arm demonstrated a greater reduction of their CSDH volume at 6 weeks follow up $(36.6\%)$ compared to the observation arm $(23.3\%$ , $\mathbf{p}=0.6648^{\cdot}$ . There were no reportable serious adverse events recorded in the observation arm, compared to 4 $(9.8\%)$ patients in the TXA arm. The addition of TXA treatment to standard surgical drainage of CSH did not significantly reduce symptomatic post-operative recurrence. Patients in the TXA arm had a delay in the CSDH recurrence with a comparative reduction of residual hematoma volume at the 6-week follow up although the effect was unsustained. Larger randomized trials with dose adjustments should be considered to investigate subgroups of patients that may benefit from this medical adjunct.

# 1. Introduction  

Chronic subdural haemorrhage (CSDH) is a common neurosurgical entity, especially amongst the elderly [1–4], with an estimated yearly incidence of 5–58 per 100,000 population in the general population [5,6]. It is associated with a predisposing factor of reduced brain volume within the cranial vault, as well as a precipitant traumatic event. The pathophysiology of the propagation of CSDH involves a complex interwoven pathway of angiogenesis, fibrinolysis, inflammation, and local coagulopathy [5,6]. This results in both the progressive accumulation and encapsulation of typically venous blood and an inflammatory response at the dural border cells [7–11]. The mainstay of therapeutic management for symptomatic CSDH is surgical evacuation of the haematoma to relieve brain parenchymal compression and improve neurological manifestations [12]. Surgical perioperative mortality rates have been reported as high as $20\%$ [8,13,14] and up to $38.7\%$ patients require recurrent operations [3,14–16]. Therefore, methods to reduce recurrence and avoid repeat surgery have been explored, ranging from medical adjuncts to alterations in surgical techniques.  

Tranexamic acid (TXA) is an anti-fibrinolytic drug that competitively inhibits the activation of plasminogen and plasmin activity. It also possesses indirect anti-inflammatory effect through the kallikrein-kinin pathway [17–21]. A case series by Kageyama et al. [18] demonstrated successful TXA utilization for conservative resolution of CSDH with no reported adverse effect. Based upon pathophysiological mechanisms and studies suggesting clinical efficacy in reduction of blood transfusion in surgical procedures and trauma [22], we hypothesized that the addition of TXA to standard neurosurgical procedures might reduce the 6-month recurrence of post-operative CSDH. We report the results of the first prospective, randomized, multi-center clinical trial comparing the addition of TXA to standard neurosurgical procedures in patients with symptomatic CSDH requiring surgical evacuation. The primary endpoint of this trial was a return to operating theatre for a recurrent symptomatic CSDH within the 6-month postoperative period. Secondary end points were comparison of postoperative CSDH volumes at 6 week, 12 weeks and 24 weeks between the 2 treatment arms and evaluation of the safety profile of the proposed dosing regime.  



# 2. Methods  



# 2.1. Study design and participant  

This was a prospective, multi-centre, open-label randomized trial evaluating the effects of TXA in reducing post-operative recurrence of CSDH. The trial was conducted in three hospitals in Singapore (National Neuroscience Institute, Singapore General Hospital and Changi General Hospital) between December 2016 to February 2019. Informed consent were obtained from the patients, whenever possible. If the patient was unable to give informed consent at the time of recruitment, consent was obtained from his or her legally appointed representative, and re-consent was obtained from the patient as soon as possible. The study was approved by SingHealth Centralized Intuitional Review Board (CIRB). The trial design is schematically depicted in Fig. 1.  

Patients who were above the age of 21 and admitted for surgical evacuation of their CSDH were assessed for eligibility for the trial. Exclusion criteria included evidence of pre-existing risk or history of venous thrombo-embolic events, presence of neurological or non-neurological condition unrelated to traumatic brain injury, active concomitant neurosurgical intracranial pathologies, significant renal or liver impairment, pregnancy (for females), chronic treatment with steroids, at risk for thrombosis, including a previous thromboembolic event and a family history of thromboembolic disease, known adverse drug reactions to TXA, history of hypercoagulability, expected death within $24\mathrm{{h}}$ , or any disease, in the opinion of the Investigator, that was unstable or could have jeopardized the safety of the patient. The complete inclusion and exclusion criteria for the study are shown in Supplementary Table 1.  



# 2.2. Randomization and masking  

Randomization was performed centrally using an automated computerized random number generator. Patients were randomized in a 1:1 ratio into either standard neurosurgical treatment only (observation arm) or standard neurosurgical treatment with TXA (TXA arm).  

The TXA drug was manufactured by Pfizer Italia and medication packs were prepared and dispensed by the study team pharmacist. Medications were stored at a central site with controlled temperature of $25~^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$ and away from direct light. Both patients and the study team were unblinded to the results of the randomization.  

Patients in the TXA arm were prescribed one capsule of $500~\mathrm{mg}$ TXA twice daily, for a total of 21 days. Study medications for a total of three weeks were dispensed to the patient and commenced on post-operative day one. The TXA dosing regime was decided based on consideration of patient compliance and safety profile. Oral TXA can be administered to a maximum of $6\mathrm{\:g}$ for menorrhagia, $4.5\mathrm{g}$ for hereditary angioneurotic oedema. Kageyama et al. [18] used a lower daily dose of oral TXA $750~\mathrm{mg}$ for a median duration of 58 days (ranging 28–137 days). We chose a daily dose of $^\mathrm{~1~g~}$ $500~\mathrm{mg}$ twice a day) in our study for a maximum period of three weeks to maximise the potential for efficacy, safety and patient compliance.  

Standard surgical management included the surgical evacuation of CSDH via burr-hole or mini-craniotomy (defined as a maximal diameter less than $3\mathrm{cm}$ ). The decision to insert a drain (subdural or subgaleal) and its duration was left to the individual surgeon’s discretion. Patients in both arms were followed up at postoperative day one and 6, 12 and 24 weeks after randomization.  



# 2.3. Data collection  

Patient demographic and perioperative characteristics were recorded, including age, sex, medical history and medications, symptomology, Glasgow Coma Scale (GCS) on presentation, type of surgery and drainage. Cardiac co-morbidity was defined as ischemic heart disease, previous cardiac surgery and/or intervention. Neurological co-morbidity was defined as previous ischemic or haemorrhagic neurologic conditions. All patients received routine surgical and other perioperative care with respect to selection of type of surgery, drains and rehabilitation care.  

Clinical examination of all patients and computed tomography (CT) brain scans were performed on post-operative day 1, weeks 6, 12 and 24, or as clinically indicated. Volumetric analyses of the CSDH sizes were calculated using the ABC/2 estimation technique, which was demonstrated to have excellent correlation with computer-assisted volumetric analysis [23,24] (Supplementary Fig. 1).  

Patients in the TXA arm were required to record the daily timings of medication using study logs. Patient compliance for study medication must meet at least $80\%$ of ideal compliance during the study and any deviations in compliance will be noted with explanation.  



# 2.4. Outcomes  

The primary outcome was the reduction of post-surgery recurrence of chronic subdural hemorrhage within six months after surgical evacuation with the combined use of tranexamic acid and surgery compared to standard surgery alone. Recurrence was defined as symptomatic CSDH recurrence requiring further surgery within the study period of six months [16,25]. Secondary outcomes included volumetric differences in the sizes of CSDH using radiological imaging between the two treatment arms at baseline, 6, 12 and 24 weeks interval post-operatively.  

To assess the safety profile of TXA at the proposed dosing regimen, adverse events were systematically monitored during the patients’ hospital stays and at follow-up outpatient visits. Any event deemed by the treating independent physician to be potentially related to TXA was reported within $^{48\mathrm{~h~}}$ of detection using a standardized Adverse Event Reporting form. Side effects reported by the patients were also documented. An independent board assisted in evaluated the safety data and adverse events to assure the safety of our participants.

# 2.5. Statistical analysis  

The trial was planned to have $80\%$ power to detect a $15\%$ clinically significant difference in the six-months post-surgery recurrence rate between the two treatment arms, assuming a recurrence rate of $25\%$ in the observation arm. Using the twosided chi square test at $5\%$ significance level, we calculated that a total sample size of 266 patients with 133 patients per arm would be required. However due to poor patient accrual, only a larger marginal difference of $>25\%$ is statistically detectable with the final sample size, provided $80\%$ and significance level of $5\%$ .  

Summary statistics are presented as means $\pm$ standard deviation and medians (minimum–maximum) for continuous variables, and frequencies (percentage) for categorical variables. Demographics, presenting symptoms and baseline clinical features were compared between the two arms using two-sided two-sample t test and Mann-Whitney $U$ test for continuous variables, and Chi-square or Fisher’s exact test for categorical variables. Un-adjusted odds ratios and $95\%$ confidence intervals (CI) were calculated for the association between baseline demographics and clinical characteristics and the recurrence outcome using univariate logistic regression analysis. To further investigate the above association analysis, the odds ratios along with their $95\%$ CIs were obtained at various subgroups. Volumetric differences of CSDH between the two arms at pre-op, post-op, 6, 12- and 24-week time-points were reported as least-square (LS) means and $95\%$ CIs over time. Change in volume at 6, 12 and 24 weeks from postoperative baseline were compared between the two treatment arms using Mann-Whitney $U$ test. A two-sided $p$ value $_{<0.05}$ was considered significant. All analyses were conducted using SAS version 24 for Windows (Statistical Analysis System, Cary, North Carolina).  



# 3. Results  

Out of 191 patients who were screened, 96 patients $(50.3\%)$ were recruited. The most common reason for exclusion was due to declined participation from the patient, or his or her legally appointed representative. (Fig. 1). Data from six patients were further excluded for analysis due to consent withdrawal or surgical complications.  

The final sample $\mathbf{\nabla}^{\prime}\mathbf{N}=90$ ) consisted of 49 patients ( $73\%$ men, mean age $70\pm14$ years) in the observation arm, and 41 patients $59\%$ male, mean age $72\pm12$ years) in the TXA arm. The baseline patient characteristics and CSDH volumes were similar in the two arms and summarized in Table 1. All patients, except three with reported SAEs, allocated to the TXA arm were known to have completed the course of TXA treatment. Three patients from the observation arm underwent mini craniotomies while the remaining 87 study patients underwent burr hole drainage for CSDH evacuation. 9 patients were on antiplatelet medications and 1 was on anticoagulant in the observation arm, compared to 11 and none in the TXA arm. All patients had their coagulation profiles corrected and normalized perioperatively, as per our institutional practices. Resumption of their antiplatelet and anti-coagulant medications were determined by the individual clinicians, depending on their patient’s risk profile for bleeding and thromboembolism events.  



# 3.1. Primary outcome  

There were five $(10.2\%)$ recurrences in the observation arm, and two $(4.8\%$ , $\mathfrak{p}=0.221$ ) in the TXA arm. Their clinical and radiological characteristics are summarized in Table 2. The mean age of these patients is $74\pm15.2$ years, of which five $(83.3\%)$ of these patients were males. Two patients had prior antiplatelet therapy with 3 patients having a cardiac co-morbidity and 1 patient with both cardiac and neurological co-morbidities. The median pre-operative volume was $130.34\pm51.3\mathrm{ml}$ and median postoperative volume was $63.8\pm37.5\mathrm{ml}$ . In the observation arm, postoperative recurrences occurred at a median of 2.3 (2–4) weeks, whilst the TXA arm occurred at the 6-week follow up. Univariate logistic regression analysis did not reveal any significant difference in the recurrence between the two treatment arms (OR 0.51, $95\%$ CI 0.11 – 2.47, $p=0.40\AA^{\cdot}$ ).  



# 3.2. Secondary outcomes  

As seen in Fig. 2, a linear mixed model with interaction term of treatment and time found both treatment arms demonstrated a decreasing trend (with different slopes) in the maximum calculated subdural volume over time (interaction $p=0.0032;$ . Patients in the TXA arm demonstrated a greater change in their CSDH volume in relation to their post-operative volume at 6 weeks follow up $(36.6\%)$ compared to the observation arm $23.3\%$ , $\mathbf{p}=0.6648^{\circ}.$ . However, this effect was not sustained at 12 and 24 weeks. The difference in maximum haematoma volumes between the two treatment arms at 6, 12 and 24 weeks were also not found to be statistically significant (Fig. 3).

# 3.3. Subgroup analysis  

There were no significant interactions between the treatment arm and demographic or perioperative characteristics relating to the primary outcome. There was no significant interaction between assignment to the TXA arm and prior antiplatelet (OR 1.61, $95\%$ CI 0.32–8.07, $p=0.56\AA.$ ) or anticoagulation (OR 3.72, $95\%\mathrm{~CI~}0.04{-}360,$ $p=0.57)$ use. Prior cardiac history was thought to be a potential confounder (OR 6.72, $95\%$ CI 1.44–31.3, $p=0.015^{\cdot}$ , however the results of multivariable logistic regression analysis while adjusting for prior cardiac history was not significantly different from the univariate analysis on the recurrence outcome (OR $0.6295\%$ CI 0.12–3.16, $p=0.57$ ) (Tables 3 and 4). Further analysis of other potential confounders also did not reveal any significant effect on the rate of recurrence. Details of the subgroup analysis are presented in Supplementary Table 2.  

There were a significant number of patients with missing data in our trial. At the 6-week follow up, 2 $(4.1\%)$ patients were lost to follow up in the observation arm, and 1 $(2.4\%)$ in the TXA arm. At the 12-week follow up, 13 $(26.5\%)$ patients and 12 $(29.3\%)$ were lost to follow up respectively. Finally, at the 24-week follow up, 34 patients $(37.8\%)$ with 17 from each arm were lost to follow up.

# 3.4. Safety outcomes  

There were no reportable serious adverse events recorded in the observation arm, compared to 4 $(9.8\%)$ out of 41 patients in the TXA arm. One patient had an immediate acute subdural hemorrhage following his initial evacuation and underwent emergent decompressive craniectomy. Another patient had a postoperative complication of subdural empyema requiring a subsequent craniotomy and evacuation of the empyema two weeks after his initial surgical evacuation, whilst on TXA treatment. One patient on routine follow-up for an incidental mucocele was found to have an asymptomatic $5\times4~\mathrm{mm}$ left thalamic infarct. It was determined that the first two events were unlikely related to TXA, but the cerebral infarct may be possibly related. These three patients were excluded from subsequent data analysis as TXA was immediately halted upon diagnoses of the SAEs. One patient in the TXA arm was found to have jaundice one month after completion of his TXA regime. It was subsequently investigated to be due to drug-induced hepatoxicity secondary to valproate. His jaundice resolved with cessation of valproic acid and he was not excluded from the analysis.  



# 4. Discussion  

With the ageing population worldwide and burgeoning use of blood thinning agents, the incidence and burden of CSDH is expected to increase, resulting in a concerning public health issue [2,3,8,12,26]. Optimal clinical management for CSDH remains controversial and the literature demonstrates a highly variable practice of management in terms of operative and post-operative care [2,4,9,12,18,27,28]. In addition, the surgical treatment of CSDH is associated with substantial morbidity, mortality, and recurrence rates. There are multiple factors which can contribute to postoperative recurrence [29], including the presence of coagulopathy or anti-coagulant drug use, radiological appearance and volume of the CSDH, postoperative residual hematoma or percent haematoma change, postoperative pneumocephalus and drain use [15,30,31]. Multiple studies have investigated in the roles of various medical adjuncts [32–35] for non-surgical management of CSDH or to reduce its post-operative recurrence [12,36–38].  

The mean age at presentation in our randomized controlled trial (RCT) was 70.7 years (range 30–91 years) with significant male predominance $(66.7\%)$ , which is comparable to the reported literature [8,36]. Contrary to our hypothesis, we did not find any significant difference between the two arms in the primary outcome of recurrence requiring repeat operations at 6 months with the addition of TXA post evacuation of CSDH. There are several considerations that may account for this. Firstly, this was an interim analysis of our study, which planned to recruit a total of 266 patients, to detect a $15\%$ difference in recurrence rates. We have enrolled 96 patients over a period of 3 years and were unfortunately unable to achieve our planned target recruitment of 266 patients. There was also a higher than expected drop-out rate of $37.8\%$ compared to our initial calculation of $15\%$ . With our current results of a $5.4\%$ difference of recurrence rates between both groups, we will need a minimum of 432 patients per arm to achieve $>80\%$ power to detect a significant difference. In addition, the general overall recurrence rate after surgical evacuation of CSDH in our study was also significantly much lower than reported in the literature. The recurrence rate in the observation arm was $12.2\%$ (6 in 49), compared to the initial postulated $25\%$ based on literature accounts. This would suggest that a larger sample size would be required to detect a reduction in recurrence rates. The lower than expected recurrence rate is similar to the findings of MotieiLangroudi et al. [15] who concluded that a $50\%$ decrease in maximum hematoma thickness on the first postoperative CT is associated with a less than $10\%$ risk of reoperation. In our cohort, CSDH volumes were reduced by $54.5\%$ after initial surgical evacuation on the first post-operative CT brain on day 1. The substantial reduction of CSDH volume after surgical evacuation in our institutes may account for the low initial recurrence rate. The current smaller sample size than initially calculated may thus explain the inadequately powered study to detect significant differences in the two treatment arms.

With regards to the volumetric analysis, our results suggest that the addition of TXA to standard neurosurgical procedures was associated with a greater reduction in the CSDH volume at the 6- week follow up. However, this effect was not maintained subsequently at the 12 and 24 weeks follow-up radiological scans. Both the recurrences in the TXA arm occurred at median of 6 weeks, as compared to the median of 2.3 weeks in four patients in the observation arm. Although the timings of the recurrences in both arms did not reach statistical significance, the team hypothesize that it is possible that TXA may have an effect on reducing the recurrence, but the effect was not sustained due to insufficient dosing or duration of treatment. In the study by Kageyama et al., [18] TXA was administered orally at $750\mathrm{mg}$ /day over a median period of 58 days (range 28–137 days) until clinical resolution. In another retrospective study, 14 patients were given $650\mathrm{mg}$ of TXA/day for a mean of $90.3\pm27$ days after placement of a bedside subdural evacuating port system [29]. A case was also reported of a hemodialysis patient with bilateral chronic subdural haemorrhage requiring three operative evacuations. The patient subsequently achieved clinical and radiological resolution after receiving a total of eight weeks of intravenous TXA (4 weeks at $20\mathrm{mg/kg/48\Deltah}$ followed by 4 weeks of $10\mathrm{mg/kg/48h)}$ [39]. The above studies had administered TXA until resolution of CSDH was achieved. In comparison, our trial administered TXA for a comparatively shorter duration of three weeks, in consideration of patients’ compliance and safety of TXA. This may account for the unsustained effects of TXA after the 6 week follow-ups. It may be, therefore, worthwhile investigating the effects of TXA use for a longer duration of time or until resolution. A currently ongoing Phase IIB multicenter, placebo-controlled Canadian trial investigating the role of TXA in increasing the rate of CSDH resolution, lower the number of required surgical procedures and decreasing the rate of CSDH recurrence following surgical evacuation have chosen a daily dose of $750~\mathrm{mg~TXA}$ , until CSDH resolution or a maximum of 20 weeks [40]. In its usage for other neurosurgical conditions, TXA has also been trialed in various RCTs and suggests a benefit in diminishing rebleeding rate post ruptured cerebral aneurysms. Different study protocols exist and up to $_\textrm{6g}$ per day of TXA have been administered for 3–6 weeks, depending on the individual study protocol [41,42]. Therefore, it may be possible that the duration and/or dosage of our TXA regime may have been insufficient to demonstrate a significant sustained effect between the two treatment arms. As a result, the volumetric difference between the two arms was reduced after the 6-week follow up and subsequently lost at the 24-week mark.  

TXA has been utilized in the management of trauma [17,21,22,43–45], and shown to be associated with reduced allogenic blood transfusion without increased thromboembolic events in neurosurgical, obstetric, orthopedic, liver and cardiac surgeries [41,43,46,47]. Although our TXA dosing regime was well within the recommended therapeutic range, and given for a comparatively short duration of 21 days, we had one patient with a serious adverse event (SAE) of an incidental asymptomatic cerebral infarction. In contrast, despite the significantly longer duration of treatment, none of the patients in the Kageyama et al. [18] nor Tanweer et al. [29] series had suffered any thromboembolic event. The recent TICH-2 study investigating the role of TXA in hyperacute primary intracerebral haemorrhage also did not demonstrate an increase in thrombo-embolic events [48]. Given the MRI findings of our patient demonstrating multiple previous small cerebral infarcts, it is possible that this SAE might not be related to the dosage and administration of TXA.  

To the authors’ knowledge, this is the first prospective randomized clinical trial that investigates the role of TXA in reducing of post-operative CSDH recurrence and volumetric reduction. The treatment arms were balanced for baseline factors, allowing for comparative analysis. Subgroup analysis of the possible confounders, including the use of antiplatelet and/or anticoagulant therapy did not reveal any significant effect on the study’s outcomes. The inclusion criteria were also deliberately left broad to reflect the real-life clinical situation.  

Nevertheless, there were some limitations with our study. Firstly, this was an open-label trial and hence the treating physicians and the patients were aware of the study group assignments. However, it should be noted also that although both patients and the study team were unblinded to the results of the randomization, the need to pursue surgical intervention and/or reoperation was based on objective presence of neurologic symptoms with concomitant clinical or radiological evidence of cerebral compression.  

Second, despite an excellent $96.7\%$ follow up achieved at the six-week mark, a significant proportion of patients $(37.8\%)$ were lost to follow up at the stipulated six months follow up. However, given that there is a centralized electronic health records of our patients available nation-wide, the lack of neurosurgical follow up may suggest that these patients remain clinically well and did not require further surgical interventions, hence electing not to return for further clinical appointments. Third, the second most common reason for patient exclusion was patients with significant co-morbidities. This could possibly introduce the clinicians’ bias, resulting in a pre-selected group of ‘well’ patients. Lastly, there was only one patient in the trial receiving anticoagulation therapy and hence the safety regarding concurrent use of anticoagulation with TXA need to be further assessed as patients with CSDH are increasingly using anti-thrombotics.  



# 5. Conclusion  

In summary, this prospective randomized controlled trial demonstrated that the addition of post-operative TXA treatment to standard surgical drainage of CSH for a total duration of 21 days to our recruited patient population did not have a significant effect in the reduction in of symptomatic post-operative recurrence. It does however suggests the possibility of delaying the timing of CSDH recurrence, and a reduction of residual haematoma volume at 6 weeks, although these effects was not sustained beyond 6 weeks. Larger randomized trials with dose adjustments should be considered to investigate subgroups of patients that may benefit from this medical adjunct to risk-stratify and individualize treatment of increasingly complex neurosurgical condition.
