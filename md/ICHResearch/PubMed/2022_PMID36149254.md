# Intravenous thrombolysis for treatment of pediatric acute ischemic stroke: Analysis of 20 years of population-level data in the United States  

Alis J Dicpinigaitis $^1\textcircled{\scriptsize{10}}$ , Steven D Shapiro2, Rolla Nuoman3, Haris Kamal2, Philip Overby3, Gurmeen Kaur2, Ji Y Chong3, Johanna T Fifi4, Neha Dangayach4, Eliza C Miller5 Shadi Yaghi $\mathbf{6}\Cap$ and Fawaz Al-Mufti1,2  



# Abstract  

Background and Aims: Although intravenous thrombolysis (IVT) represents standard-of-care treatment for acute ischemic stroke (AIS) in eligible adult patients, definitive evidence-based guidelines and randomized clinical trial data evaluating its safety and efficacy in the pediatric population remain absent from the literature. We aimed to evaluate the utilization and outcomes of IVT for the treatment of pediatric AIS using a large national registry.  

Methods: Weighted hospitalizations for pediatric $\phantom{0}\phantom{.0}\phantom{.0}\phantom{.0}\phantom{.0}\phantom{.0}\times18$ years of age) AIS patients were identified in the National Inpatient Sample during the period of 2001 to 2019. Complex sample statistical methods were performed to assess unadjusted and adjusted outcomes in patients treated with IVT or other medical management.  

Results: Among 13,901 pediatric AIS patients, 270 $(1.9\%)$ were treated with IVT monotherapy (median age 12.8 years). IVT-treated patients developed any intracranial hemorrhage (ICH) at a rate of $5.6\%$ $(\mathsf{n}=\mathsf{I}\mathsf{5})$ , and $71.9\%$ $(\mathsf{n}=\mathsf{I}94)$ experienced favorable functional outcomes at discharge (to home or to acute rehabilitation). Following propensity-score adjustment for age, acute stroke severity, infarct location, and etiological/comorbid conditions, IVT was not associated with an increased risk of any ICH ( $5.6\%$ vs $5.4\%$ , $\mathsf{p}=0.93\mathsf{I}$ ; adjusted odds ratio $({\mathsf{aOR}})=1.01$ , $95\%$ confidence interval $(\mathsf{Cl})=0.48-2.14$ , $\mathsf{p}=0.97|$ ), nor with favorable functional outcome $71.9\%$ vs $74.5\%$ , $\mathsf{p}=0.489$ ; $\mathsf{aOR}=0.88$ , $95\%$ ${\mathsf{Cl}}=0.60\mathrm{-}|.29$ , $\mathsf{p}=0.5|\mathsf{l}$ ) in comparison with other medical therapy.  

Conclusions: Twenty years of population-level data in the United States demonstrate that pediatric AIS patients treated with IVT experienced high rates of favorable outcomes without an increased risk of hemorrhagic transformation.

# Introduction  

Although intravenous thrombolysis (IVT) represents standard-of-care treatment for acute ischemic stroke (AIS) in eligible adult patients,1 definitive evidence-based guidelines and randomized clinical trial data evaluating its safety and efficacy in the pediatric population remain absent from the literature. The failure of the 2010 Thrombolysis in Pediatric Stroke (TIPS) trial to enroll patients (and its subsequent closure in $2013)^{2}$ reflects the relative rarity of AIS in childhood and adolescence, which is estimated to occur in 1.0 to 2.0 per 100,000 children in Western developed nations annually.3 Despite lacking evidence, off-label usage of IVT has been reported in as many as $2\%$ of child and adolescent stroke patients,4–6 as practitioners weigh the costs and benefits of treatment circumstantially and by varying institutional protocols. As childhood cerebrovascular disease remains in the leading 10 causes of mortality in the pediatric population,7 it is imperative to investigate optimal treatment strategies. This study utilizes real-world population-level data from the National Inpatient Sample (NIS) to evaluate the utilization, safety profile, and short-term outcomes of pediatric AIS patients treated with IVT in comparison with non-IVT medical management over a two-decade-long interval.  



# Methods  



# Data source  

The NIS, developed and maintained by the Healthcare Cost and Utilization Project (HCUP), is among the largest publicly accessible inpatient care databases in the United States. Yearly unweighted data approximate 7,000,000 patients, reflecting a $20\%$ stratified sample of all HCUPparticipating community hospitals nationally. More information regarding the NIS and data access can be found at: www.hcup-us.ahrq.gov. Given the public accessibility and de-identified nature of the information in this database, this study did not meet the requirements for institutional review board approval. For the same reason, patient consent was neither sought nor required. The data utilized in this analysis as a well as a comprehensive list of billing codes used to define the clinical variables in this study are available upon reasonable request of the corresponding author following completion of onboarding and verification procedures as specified by the HCUP. This manuscript was composed in accordance with Reporting of Studies Conducted Using Observational Routinely-Collected Data (RECORD) guidelines.  



# Patient selection, cohort development, and baseline clinical characteristics  

Pediatric $\mathord{\left.\begin{array}{rl}\end{array}\right.}\mathrm{<}18$ years of age) patients with primary admission diagnoses of AIS were identified in the NIS during the study period (2001–2019) using International Classification of Diseases, Ninth and Tenth Revision, Clinical Modification (ICD-9/10-CM) codes corresponding to “occlusion or stenosis of cerebral or precerebral arteries with mention of infarction” (ICD-9-CM 433.X1, 434.X1; ICD-10-CM I63. XXX). Patients treated with IVT monotherapy (exclusive of subsequent endovascular treatment) were identified using appropriate ICD Procedural Coding System (PCS) identifiers along with diagnosis codes indicating IVT treatment at a previous facility. Exclusion criteria for the analysis include lack of a primary (DX1) diagnosis of AIS, adult age, non-elective admission, endovascular therapy, and absent data for age or outcome. Baseline clinical characteristics identified included demographic information (age, sex, and race/ethnicity) as well as etiology of stroke, comorbid conditions, location of infarct. Missing data for race/ethnicity accounted for $17.3\%$ of the cohort and were not subjected to a multiple data imputation protocol as this parameter was not carried into adjusted analyses.  



# Clinical endpoints and statistical analysis  

The primary clinical endpoints of this analysis included development of any (encompassing both symptomatic and asymptomatic) intracranial (intracerebral and subarachnoid) hemorrhage (ICH) and favorable functional outcome (defined as discharge disposition to home with or without services or to acute rehabilitation). This definition of favorable functional outcome has previously been shown to have high concordance with modified Rankin scale score $\leqslant2$ evaluated at 90 days subsequent to discharge.8,9 In-hospital mortality was evaluated as a secondary safety endpoint. The temporal utilization of IVT was evaluated against a timeline demarcated by the publication of three landmark clinical trials validating the efficacy of IVT in the adult stroke population (National Institute of Neurological Disorders and Stroke rt-PA Stroke Study10 (NINDS) era, 2001–2008; European Cooperative Acute Stroke Study $\mathrm{III}^{11}$ (ECAS-III) era, 2009–2012; Third International Stroke Trial12 (IST-3) era, 2013–2019).  

Complex sample statistical methods were utilized to account for the stratified sampling design of the NIS using discharge and trend weighting. Standard descriptive statistics were performed to measure the variance in distribution of baseline demographic covariates, clinical characteristics, and study endpoints between patients receiving IVT and those not receiving IVT. A control cohort of adult ${\bf\zeta}>17$ years of age) AIS patients receiving IVT, identified using the same inclusion criteria as pediatric patients listed previously, was constructed for comparative evaluation of clinical endpoints following stratification into young adult (including ages 18 through 40) and older adult (ages 41 and older) populations  

Propensity-score adjustment using a nearest-neighbor approach without replacement and matching sensitivity of  

0.001 was performed to balance clinical characteristics significantly different between IVT- and non-IVT-treated patients on univariable comparison to simulate randomized treatment allocation and to mitigate confounding by indication. Standard descriptive statistics were evaluated to assess covariate balance in treated and non-treated groups subsequent to matching. Multivariable logistic regression analysis was performed, with the propensity score as a model covariate, to determine the adjusted, independent association between IVT treatment and clinical endpoints. A Bonferroni correction for statistical significance was implemented to adjust for multiple comparisons. All analyses were performed using IBM SPSS Version 26 software.  



# Results  

Among 13,901 pediatric AIS patients identified in the NIS from 2001 to 2019, $1.9\%$ $\scriptstyle(\mathtt{n}{}=270)$ ) were treated with IVT monotherapy. In comparison with those treated with other forms of medical therapy, IVT-treated patients were older (mean age 12.8 vs 8.2 years, $\mathrm{\Deltap}<0.001\$ ) and were more likely to be of Black/African American race/ethnicity $(35.7\%$ vs $21.2\%$ , $\mathrm{p}<0.001,$ ). In addition, IVT-treated patients presented with higher rates of posterior circulation infarctions $(27.8\%$ vs $17.0\%$ , $\mathrm{\Delta}\mathrm{p}<0.001\$ ) as well as higher rates of aphasia $(60.2\%$ vs $19.5\%$ , $\mathrm{p}<0.001$ ), paresis/plegia $46.3\%$ vs $14.4\%$ , $\mathrm{p}<0.001$ ), and herniation ( $5.6\%$ vs $2.9\%$ , $\mathrm{p}{=}0.011\$ ) (Table 1). The most common etiological condition in the IVT-treated group was congenital cardiac abnormality $(27.5\%)$ . Utilization of IVT significantly increased throughout the duration of the study period, especially following publication of the IST-3 trial (NINDS era (2001-2008) $1.2\%$ vs ECASS III era (2009-2012) $1.4\%$ vs IST-3 era (2013-2019) $3.1\%$ , $\mathrm{p}<0.001\$ ) (Figure 1).  

IVT-treated patients developed any intracranial hemorrhage (ICH) at a rate of $5.6\%$ $(\mathrm{n}=15)$ ) and $71.9\%$ $(\mathtt{n}{}^{=}194\$ ) experienced favorable functional outcomes at discharge (Table 2). The frequency of any ICH in this cohort was lower than those reported in the landmark adult IVT clinical trials (Figure 2). In IVT-treated patients, mortality occurred at a rate of $<2\%$ (specific rate not reported due to low case number). Mortality did not occur in any patients with ICH after receiving IVT. In the control cohort of adult AIS patients treated with IVT monotherapy, the younger adult (ages 18–40) subgroup experiences favorable functional outcomes in $73.8\%$ of cases and progressed to any ICH in $3.4\%$ of cases. The older adult (ages 41 and older) demonstrated favorable outcomes in $49.9\%$ of cases and any in ICH in $7.7\%$ . Following propensity-score adjustment for clinical covariates significantly different on univariable assessment between IVT and non-IVT-treated patients, IVT was not associated with an increased risk of any ICH $5.6\%$ vs $5.4\%$ , $\mathbf{p}{=}0.931$ ; adjusted odds ratio $(\mathrm{aOR}){=}1.01$ , $95\%$ confidence interval $\mathrm{(CI)}{=}0.48{-}2.14$ , $\mathbf{p}{=}0.971$ ), nor with favorable functional outcome $(71.9\%$ vs $74.5\%$ , $\mathbf{p}{=}0.489$ ; $\mathrm{aOR}=0.88$ , $95\%$ $\mathrm{CI}{=}0.60{-}1.29$ , $\mathrm{p}{=}0.511)$ in comparison with other medical therapy (Table 2). Moreover, IVT-treated patients demonstrated lower rates of mortality ( $K2\%$ vs $8.1\%$ , $\mathbf{p}{=}0.001\$ ). Subsequent to matching, rates of baseline clinical characteristics (those significantly different on unadjusted comparison—age, aphasia, paresis/plegia, herniation, infarct location, congenital cardiac abnormality, and obesity) did not differ between patients receiving IVT and those receiving other medical therapy (Figure 3).  



# Discussion  

The present analysis of approximately 14,000 pediatric AIS patients in the United States represents, to our best knowledge, the largest cohort evaluation of its kind over the largest time interval to date and demonstrates that IVT conferred high rates of favorable functional outcomes ( $72\%$ discharged to home with or without services or to acute rehabilitation) without increased risk for any ICH $\mathrm{(aOR=1.01}$ , $95\%$ $\mathrm{CI}{=}0.48{-}2.14$ , $\mathbf{p}{=}0.971$ ). In addition, mortality was exceedingly rare in IVT-treated patients $(<2\%)$ .  

Subsequent to the failure of the TIPS trial, no further randomized clinical trials were undertaken to evaluate the safety and efficacy of IVT in pediatric AIS, limiting existing evidence for comparative assessment to the major adult trials and large-scale observational studies of pediatric patients. Functional outcomes in the present study were encouraging at $72\%$ , markedly higher than those reported in the adult trials (modified Rankin scale score $<2$ evaluated at 90 days of approximately $50\%$ ).10–12 In the comparative analysis with adult AIS patients, favorable outcomes were very similar to those of the young adult (ages 18–40) subgroup $(73.8\%)$ and higher than those of the older adult subgroup (ages 41 and older) $(49.9\%)$ . It should be noted that vastly different etiologies and comorbidity profiles between pediatric and adult AIS patients limit the utility of this comparison; however, it nonetheless provides a basis of reference. Two other population-based registry-based studies (using the NIS and the Kids’ Inpatient Database (KID)) exist in the literature evaluating IVT in pediatric AIS, but these studies only evaluated data as late as 2009 and were also limited to a markedly smaller sample size of 46 and 67 patients, respectively.5,6 These studies reported favorable functional outcomes in $43.5\%$ (NIS study) and $48.5\%$ (KID study) of patients at discharge, markedly lower than rates of our analysis and suggestive of increased efficacy over time. Moreover, although mortality was exceedingly rare in our analysis $(<2\%)$ , the previous analyses reported rates ranging from $10\%$ to $20\%$ . The utilization of IVT in our cohort ( $2\%$ of pediatric AIS) aligns with those previously reported in the literature5,6; however, our analysis also suggests increasing treatment with IVT over time. In this study, only $5.6\%$ of IVT-treated patients experienced any ICH and none of these patients died subsequent to hemorrhage. These rates are substantially lower than those reported in the adult IVT trials for any $\mathrm{ICH}^{10-12}$ (Figure 2), an important determination given the unclear question of whether or not pediatric patients are more susceptible to hemorrhagic transformation following infarction. A limitation of this comparison is the inability of our analysis to discriminate between asymptomatic and symptomatic bleeds as well as uncertainty regarding the ability of ICD billing codes to appropriately capture this endpoint.13 Consequently, assessment of a control cohort of adult AIS patients was made within this analysis as a form of sensitivity testing, and pediatric AIS patients treated with IVT experienced similarly low rates of any ICH in comparison with young adult $(3.4\%)$ and older adult counterparts $(7.7\%)$ . The two previous registry-based studies reported similarly low rates of ICH at $0\%$ (NIS study) and $3\%$ (KID study).5,6

It should be noted that patients treated with endovascular therapy subsequent to receiving IVT were an a priori exclusion from this analysis in order to isolate the treatment effect of thrombolysis. This exclusion very likely eliminated severe large vessel occlusion strokes indicated for thrombectomy which would impact outcomes and rates of hemorrhagic transformation reported in the present analysis. Endovascular therapy for pediatric AIS was evaluated in a previous study published by this group of authors using the same national registry14 and demonstrated that patients administered preceding thrombolytic therapy $\mathbf{\check{n}}\mathbf{=}79$ , $41.6\%)$ trended toward higher rates of favorable functional outcomes ( $63.3\%$ vs $49.5\%$ ; $\mathrm{p}{=}0.060)$ in comparison with those treated with direct endovascular intervention, without an increased risk of any ICH.  

The primary limitation of this analysis is its observational study design to evaluate the safety and efficacy of a treatment modality, without utilization of randomization and prospective evaluation, as well as a lack of important baseline clinical characteristics. Prognostically significant data absent from this analysis include comprehensive neurological exam details, baseline National Institutes of Health Stroke Scale score, radiographic findings, and time of last known well and initiation of treatment. In addition, as mentioned previously, it is unclear how a determination of any ICH was made by use of ICD billing codes alone, and ECASS classification of ICH severity was not available. Although validated data are presented to justify the selection of discharge dispositions for the definition of favorable functional outcome, these analyses were performed in adult stroke cohorts, which calls into question the extension and applicability of this same definition to the pediatric stroke population. More highly disabled patients (with modified Rankin score (mRS) scores of 3, for example) may have been included in the definition of favorable outcome in this analysis. Despite these limitations, this study presents the largest and most comprehensive evaluation of IVT in pediatric stroke to data and the use of a largescale national registry enhances the generalizability of our findings.

# Conclusion  

Our population-level analysis demonstrates a favorable efficacy and safety profile of IVT in pediatric acute ischemic stroke. Children and adolescents fare remarkably well after treatment with IVT in comparison with both older adult patients and those of pediatric patients from older large-scale studies. In the absence of conclusive data from randomized clinical trials, this study provides unique and valuable clinical insight.
