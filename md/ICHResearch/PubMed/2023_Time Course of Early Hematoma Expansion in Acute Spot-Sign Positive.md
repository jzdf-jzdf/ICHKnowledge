# Time Course of Early Hematoma Expansion in Acute Spot-Sign Positive Intracerebral Hemorrhage: Prespecified Analysis of the SPOTLIGHT Randomized Clinical Trial  

Fahad S. Al-Ajlan , MD; David J. Gladstone, MD, PhD; Dongbeom Song, MD, PhD; Kevin E. Thorpe , MMath; Rick H. Swartz, MD, PhD; Kenneth S. Butcher , MD, PhD; Martin del Campo, MD; Dar Dowlatshahi , MD, PhD; Henrik Gensicke , MD; Gloria Jooyoung Lee, BSc; Matthew L. Flaherty , MD; Michael D. Hill , MD, MSc; Richard I. Aviv , MD; Andrew M. Demchuk , MD; SPOTLIGHT Investigators†  

BACKGROUND: In the SPOTLIGHT trial (Spot Sign Selection of Intracerebral Hemorrhage to Guide Hemostatic Therapy), patients with a computed tomography (CT) angiography spot-sign positive acute intracerebral hemorrhage were randomized to rFVIIa (recombinant activated factor VIIa; $80\upmu{\mathfrak{g}}/\upk{\mathfrak{g}})$ or placebo within 6 hours of onset, aiming to limit hematoma expansion. Administration of rFVIIa did not significantly reduce hematoma expansion. In this prespecified analysis, we aimed to investigate the impact of delays from baseline imaging to study drug administration on hematoma expansion.  

METHODS: Hematoma volumes were measured on the baseline CT, early post-dose CT, and 24 hours CT scans. Total hematoma volume (intracerebral hemorrhage+intraventricular hemorrhage) change between the 3 scans was calculated as an estimate of how much hematoma expansion occurred before and after studying drug administration.  

RESULTS: Of the 50 patients included in the trial, 44 had an early post-dose CT scan. Median time (interquartile range) from onset to baseline CT was 1.4 hours (1.2–2.6). Median time from baseline CT to study drug was 62.5 (55–80) minutes, and from study drug to early post-dose CT was 19 (14.5–30) minutes. Median (interquartile range) total hematoma volume increased from baseline CT to early post-dose CT by $10.0\mathrm{mL}$ $(-0.7$ to 18.5) in the rFVIIa arm and $5.4\mathrm{mL}$ (1.8–8.3) in the placebo arm $_{\mathrm{\scriptsize~P=0.96}}$ ). Median volume change between the early post-dose CT and follow-up scan was $0.6\mathsf{mL}$ $-2.6$ to 8.3) in the rFVIIa arm and $0.7\mathrm{mL}$ $(-1.6$ to 2.1) in the placebo arm $_{(P=0.98)}$ ). Total hematoma volume decreased between the early post-dose CT and 24-hour scan in $44.2\%$ of cases (rFVIIa $38.9\%$ and placebo $48\%$ ). The adjusted hematoma growth in volume immediately post dose for FVIIa was 0.998 times that of placebo ( $[95\%$ CI, $0.71-1.43]$ ; $\mathsf{P{=}0.99}.$ ). The hourly growth in FFVIIa was 0.998 times that for placebo ( $[95\%$ CI, 0.994–1.003]; $P{=}0.50$ ; Table 3).  

CONCLUSIONS: In the SPOTLIGHT trial, the adjusted hematoma volume growth was not associated with Factor VIIa treatment. Most hematoma expansion occurred between the baseline CT and the early post-dose CT, limiting any potential treatment effect of hemostatic therapy. Future hemostatic trials must treat intracerebral hemorrhage patients earlier from onset, with minimal delay between baseline CT and drug administration.

Presently, there are no approved hyperacute medical treatments to reduce morbidity and mortality for spontaneous (non-anticoagulant– related) ICH.  

Hematoma volume remains the strongest predictor of poor functional outcomes and mortality after ICH.3 Approximately one-third of patients with ICH diagnosed within 6 hours of onset experience early hematoma expansion (HE), and this is associated with worse clinical outcome.4–8 A carefully designed observational study demonstrated that in nearly a third of patients with HE, clinical deterioration occurred in the first hour after diagnostic computed tomography (CT) scan and that ongoing bleeding beyond 4 hours of symptom onset was uncommon.5 Hence, early prevention or attenuation of HE is an important goal for emergency treatment of ICH. Studies characterizing the natural history and temporal evolution of early HE with surrogate biomarkers like neuroimaging are lacking. Contrast extravasation on CT angiogram (CTA) “CTA spot sign” identifies an ICH population at risk of HE and worse clinical outcome.9  

The SPOTLIGHT trial (“Spot Sign” Selection of Intracerebral Hemorrhage to Guide Hemostatic Therapy) randomized CTA spot sign positive ICH patients to rFVIIa (recombinant activated factor VIIa) versus placebo within 6 hours of onset, with the primary goal to limit HE. The pooled main results of this trial when combined with the STOP-IT study revealed no significant HE prevention with rFVIIa.10 The explanations for this lack of treatment effect may relate to the time delay between the baseline imaging and study drug administration. This delay was the result of several steps required prior to study drug administration: Performing a CTA in addition to non-contrast CT interpretation for the presence of CTA spot sign, awaiting bloodwork results; obtaining consent; and study drug preparation. To assess how much HE might develop during the time period after the baseline CT and before study drug administration, the protocol included a repeat noncontrast head CT scan immediately after study drug administration (post-dose CT).  

This study is a prespecified analysis of the SPOTLIGHT trial aimed at determining the time course of HE. We examined the magnitude of HE during the intervals between the baseline CT and the post-dose CT, and from the post-dose CT to the follow-up 24-hour CT.  

# METHODS  

The data that support the findings of this study are available from the corresponding author upon reasonable request per the journal’s Transparency and Openness Promotion Guidelines. The SPOTLIGHT trial was a multicentre phase III randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled comparison of rFVIIa versus placebo in eligible patients with acute spontaneous ICH diagnosed by CT scan within 6 hours of onset, age $\geq18$ years and who had a spot-sign on CTA. Site study investigators determined the presence or absence of a spot sign by reviewing the CTA source images obtained after the routine noncontrast CT scan. Spot-positive patients were randomly assigned to intravenous rFVIIa $(80~\upmu{{\mathfrak{g}}}/\upk{{\mathfrak{g}}})$ or placebo in a 1:1 ratio using a block randomization scheme, stratified by participating center. Figure S1 shows the CONSORT (Consolidated Standards of Reporting Trials) flow diagram for this analysis. This article follows the CONSORT reporting guideline (including checklist and flow diagram).11 Figure S2 shows examples of ICH volume contraction, between the early post-dose CT and 24-hour CT scan.  

The SPOTLIGHT protocol was approved by the ethics committees at the recruiting centers. The trial was registered with Clinicaltrials.gov (NCT01359202). Informed consent was obtained from the patient or legally acceptable representative, or via waiver of consent if applicable.  

# CT Scan Acquisition  

In addition to the baseline CT, all patients in this study had 2 follow-up scans. The first early post-dose CT was obtained within 1 hour of study drug treatment administration. The second was obtained at 24 hours.  

# Hematoma Volume Measurement  

Planimetric techniques were used to measure ICH volumes at a central core laboratory blinded to treatment assignment and all clinical variables. Experts used Quantomo (Cybertrial Inc, Calgary)12 to measure the hematoma volumes. ICH, intraventricular hemorrhage, and total hematoma volumes ${\mathsf{ICH}}+$ intraventricular hemorrhage) were measured.  

# Statistical Analysis  

Patient characteristics and hematoma volumes were summarized for each group, using descriptive statistics. Statistics software used was R, version 3.5.0. The aim of this analysis was 2-fold: (1) compare the HE immediately post-dose and (2) compare the HE from immediately post-dose with 24 hours post-dose. Hematoma volumes were transformed (natural logarithm) to improve residual distributions, including potential concerns of heteroscedasticity, but also to remain consistent with the prior results in reporting and that it yields a percent change interpretation that is relevant. To account for the repeated measurments within patients, a linear mixed effect model with random intercept is indicated. Since this study is a randomized trial, baseline ICH volume was incorporated as a covariate, reducing the timepoints of repeated measures to 2: immedialetly post-dose and 24 hours post-dose. However, the analysis is complicated by the varying onset to dosing intervals among the patients. Therefore, the time scale was shifted so that a time of zero corresponds to the immediate post-dose ICH volume measurement. The time from onset to immediate post-dose CT scan was included as a covariate.
