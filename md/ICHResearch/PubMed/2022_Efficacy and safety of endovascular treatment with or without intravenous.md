# Efficacy and safety of endovascular treatment with or without intravenous alteplase in acute anterior circulation large vessel occlusion stroke: a meta‑analysis of randomized controlled trials  

Jun Zhang1,2,3,4 · Cong Yuan1,2,3,4 · Xinyu Deng1,2,3,4 · Qiang Yuan1,2,3,4 · Meihua Wang1,2,3,4 · Pengfei $\mathsf{F}\mathbf{u}^{1,2,3,4}$ · Jiang Fang1,2,3,4 · Zhuoying $\mathsf{Du}^{1,2,3,4}\textcircled{\mathrm{(p)}}$ · Jin $\mathsf{H}\mathsf{u}^{1,2,3,4}$  

Received: 8 March 2022 / Accepted: 14 March 2022 / Published online: 22 March 2022   
$\circledcirc$ Fondazione Società Italiana di Neurologia 2022  

# Abstract  

Objective  The current meta-analysis aimed to investigate the efficacy and safety of direct endovascular treatment (EVT) and bridging therapy (EVT with prior intravenous thrombolysis (IVT)) in patients with acute anterior circulation large vessel occlusion (LVO) stroke.  

Methods  This meta-analysis followed PRISMA guidelines. Eligible RCTs were identified through a systemic search of electronic databases (PubMed, Ovid, Web of Science, and Cochrane Library) from the inception dates to January 10, 2022. The pooled analyses were performed using RevMan 5.3 software. The primary outcome was functional outcome on the modified Rankin Scale (mRS) (range 0 to 5) at 90 days. The secondary outcomes included successful reperfusion, intracranial hemorrhage, and mortality (mRS 6) within 90 days.  

Results  A total of 4 RCTs involving 1633 patients were finally included. Findings of pooled analyses indicated that neither the primary outcomes (no disability (mRS 0), no significant disability despite some symptoms (mRS 1), slight disability (mRS 2), moderate disability (mRS 3), moderately severe disability (mRS 4), severe disability (mRS 5), excellent outcome (mRS 0–1), functional independence outcome $(\mathrm{mRS}0{-}2)$ , and poor outcome (mRS 3–5)) nor the secondary outcomes (successful reperfusion, intracranial hemorrhage, and mortality) in the EVT groups were not statistically significant compared with the IVT plus EVT groups $\mathrm{\mathit{(P>0.05)}}$ ). In addition, the outcomes of sensitivity analysis implied that the findings of meta-analysis were credible.  

Conclusions  Among patients with acute ischemic stroke due to LVO of anterior circulation, EVT alone yielded efficacy and safety outcomes similar to IVT plus EVT.  

Keywords  Acute ischemic stroke $^{\ast}$ Endovascular thrombectomy $\cdot$ Intravenous thrombolysis $^{\ast}$ Meta-analysis  

# Introduction  

Endovascular treatment (EVT) for acute ischemic stroke has attracted great interest within the field of stroke since 2015, when several randomized controlled trials (RCTs)  

were published, providing evidence that EVT may improve outcome in selected patients [1–6]. To date, there remains controversy regarding efficacy and safety of intravenous thrombolysis (IVT) as adjunctive therapy to EVT in patients with acute large vessel occlusion (LVO) of the anterior circulation. Several meta-analyses and observational studies suggested that IVT prior to EVT may not provide an additional benefit for patients treated with EVT [7–10], while some studies showed better outcomes in patients treated with EVT plus IVT [11–16].  

In theory, IVT prior to EVT has both benefits and risks. IVT may occasionally induce successful reperfusion preceding EVT, avoiding the need for following endovascular procedure [17]. Furthermore, IVT may facilitate successful recanalization through fibrin degeneration, and reduce downstream microemboli, thereby improving collateral circulation [18–20]. Conversely, IVT may increase the risk of hemorrhage and incur thrombus fragmentation and subsequent clot migration, impairing distal perfusion [21–23]. Moreover, IVT limits the use of heparin and antiplatelets, especially in patients with tandem extra-cranial and intracranial occlusions. The controversy led to the conduction of several randomized controlled trials (RCTs), some of which have been recently published.  

In this study, we aimed to perform data pooled analysis of published RCTs to compare the efficacy and safety of direct EVT and bridging therapy (EVT with prior IVT) in patients with acute ischemic stroke attributable to LVO of anterior circulation.  

# Methods  

# Search strategy and selection criteria  

This systemic review and meta-analysis was performed under the guidance of the preferred reporting items for systematic reviews and meta-analyses (PRISMA) guidelines [24]. Databases (PubMed, Ovid, Web of Science, and Cochrane Library) were systematically searched. The terms “stroke” and “thrombolysis or intravenous alteplase” and “mechanical thrombectomy, or endovascular therapy, or endovascular treatment” were applied to identify RCTs from inception dates to January 10, 2022. Specific retrieval strategies were adjusted according to different databases. The literature search was independently conducted by J.Z. and C.Y. to ensure the accuracy. Any discrepancy was solved by consultation of an investigator.  

This meta-analysis enrolled patients with acute ischemic stroke (onset time window was less than $4.5\mathrm{~h~},$ ) due to the anterior circulation LVO who were eligible for intravenous alteplase and were randomly assigned to undergo either EVT alone or intravenous alteplase followed by EVT. For convenience, the patients treated with EVT alone were defined as experimental group and the patients receiving intravenous alteplase followed by EVT were defined as control group.  

# Quality assessment  

Quality assessment was independently conducted by two authors (C.Y. and Z.J.). The risk of bias in each included RCT was assessed with the Cochrane Handbook for Systematic Reviews of Interventions [25]. The following 7 domains were analyzed: random sequence generation, allocation concealment, blinding of participants and personnel, blinding of outcomes assessment, incomplete outcome data, selective outcome reporting, and other bias. Each studies was categorized as being based on low, high, or unclear risk of bias. We used the following methods: (1) low risk of bias (plausible bias unlikely to seriously alter the results) if all domains are at low risk of bias; (2) unclear risk of bias (plausible bias that raises some doubt about the results) if one or more domains have an unclear risk of bias; or (3) high risk of bias (plausible bias that seriously weakens confidence in the results) if one or more domains are at high risk of bias. If there was any conflict, a discussion involving 3 or more authors would take place to reach a consensus.  

# Data extraction  

Two authors (J.Z. and C.Y.) reviewed every eligible study and extracted the data including (1) information of the literature (title, author, publication date, etc.); (2) characteristics of the trials (sample size, country, inclusion criteria, time window, and thrombolytics); (3) baseline characteristics (demographic characteristics, medical history, clinical characteristics, site of large vessel occlusion, etc.); and (4) primary and secondary outcomes. The primary outcome was functional outcome on the modified Rankin Scale (mRS) (range, 0 to 5) at 90 days and we defined mRS 0–1, 0–2, and 3–5 as excellent, functional independence, and poor outcomes, respectively. The secondary outcomes included successful reperfusion which was defined as an extended Thrombolysis in Cerebral Infarction scale score of 2b, 2c, or 3, intracranial hemorrhage, and mortality (mRS 6) within 90 days. For studies with insufficient information, the reviewers contacted the primary authors, when possible, to acquire and verify the data. Disagreements were resolved by discussion with other authors.  

# Statistical analysis  

Event rate was pooled using the ratio and standard error values and was weighted using the inverse variance method. We performed to calculate $95\%$ confidence intervals (CIs) using the inverse variance statistical method. Dichotomous data were analyzed using risk ratios (RRs) with $95\%$ CIs. We calculated $95\%$ CIs performing the Mantel Haenszel statistical method. $I_{\l}$ -square $(I^{2})$ statistics and $Q$ tests were applied to assess the impact of study heterogeneity on the results of the meta-analysis. According to the Cochrane review guidelines [24, 25], if significant heterogeneity was present at $P<0.1$ or $I^{2}>50\%$ , a random-effect model was chosen; otherwise, a fixed-effect model was used. Moreover, sensitivity analysis was conducted by removing each study individually to evaluate the quality and consistency of the results. Visual inspection of the funnel plot was done to assess publication bias when the number of related studies was $\geq5$ .  

# Results  

# Study selection  

We initially identified 111 studies with the search strategy mentioned above, of which 4 duplicates were excluded. In the remaining 107 articles, 98 were excluded due to clear irrelevance after screening titles and abstracts. We read full text of 9 articles and excluded 5 of them because they were not RCTs or were moderately irrelevant. Finally, four RCTs met the criteria: DIRECT MT (Direct Intraarterial Thrombectomy to Revascularize Acute Ischemic Stroke Patients With LVO Efficiently in Chinese Tertiary Hospitals) [26], DEVT (Direct Endovascular Treatment Versus Standard Bridging Therapy for Patients With Acute Stroke With LVO in the Anterior Circulation) [27], SKIP (Direct Mechanical Thrombectomy in Acute LVO Stroke) [28], and MR CLEAN-NO IV (Multicenter Randomized Clinical Trial of Endovascular Treatment for Acute Ischemic Stroke in the Netherlands) [29]. The selection process is presented in Fig. 1.

# Baseline characteristics  

The four trials included 1633 individuals in all, comprising 817 $(50.0\%)$ assigned to experimental arm and 816 $(50.0\%)$ assigned to control arm [27–29]. The patients’ median age ranged from 69 to 76 years old. There was a preponderance of male patients in acute ischemic stroke. All included studies mainly reported the mRS at 90 days, successful reperfusion, and intracranial hemorrhage. Baseline characteristics of included studies are summarized in Table 1.  

# Quality assessment  

All included articles were of high quality, and the quality evaluation results are depicted in Fig. 2. All articles were low risk on random sequence generation, blinding of outcome assessment, incomplete outcome data, selective reporting, and other bias. However, all of the included studies designs did not considered allocation concealment and double-blinding could not be performed due to the principle of informed consent for surgical interventions.

# Discussions  

In this meta-analysis, we found no significant difference in mRS 0 to 6, successful reperfusion, and intracranial hemorrhage rate between direct EVT and EVT with prior IVT in patients with acute stroke caused by LVO of anterior circulation.  

Before emergence of the RCTs, there have been a fair number of observational studies, post hoc analyses, and meta-analyses with inconsistent results [7–16]. Several observational studies displayed no differences in the outcomes between two groups, while others reported improved outcomes in patients treated with IVT before EVT, including better functional outcomes, higher successful reperfusion rates, and reduced mortality [30]. Additionally, one comparative study showed significantly lower mortality in patients with internal carotid artery occlusion treated with EVT alone [9]. However, the result of observational study is confounded by selection bias. Because patients treated with EVT alone might have contraindications to IVT, which may be worsened the outcome. Of note, in all of 8 studies that excluded IVT-ineligible patients, efficacy and safety outcomes were similar [31–38]. Likewise, most post hoc analyses of previous RCTs failed to document any difference between two groups [30].  

Meta-analyses pooling data from studies which included patients both eligible and ineligible to IVT reported better functional outcome, lower mortality, and higher successful recanalization rates associated with IVT plus EVT [14–16], whereas comparable efficacy and safety outcomes were displayed in meta-analyses which only included IVTeligible patients [10, 39, 40]. Given the fact that these non-RCTs were confounded by selection bias and absence of randomization, further evidence of RCTs was required to resolve this controversy.  

There have been 4 RCTs published, which rendered different results in supporting (or disproving) the hypothesis that EVT alone is non-inferior to the combination of EVT and IVT [26–29]. DIRECT MT and DEVT both proved the non-inferiority, whereas SKIP failed to demonstrate non-inferiority, and CLEAN-NO IV showed EVT alone was neither superior nor noninferior to bridging therapy. Several reasons may explain the inconsistency.  

First, difference in intervention. Endovascular treatment modality across the trials was not exactly the same. In DIRECT MT, $96.2\%$ of the bridging therapy group utilized stent retrievers as first option, but the numbers fell to $77.8\%$ in CLEAN-NO IV, in which aspiration was the first approach in $20.9\%$ of the bridging therapy group. A post hoc analysis of the COMPASS trial demonstrated that prior IVT may have different influence on stent retriever first approach and aspiration first approach [41]. Besides, SKIP used a lower dose of $0.6~\mathrm{mg/kg}$ of alteplase and ENCHANTED failed to show the non-inferiority of lowdose alteplase to standard-dose alteplase [42]. Second, difference in selection criteria. For instance, DIRECT-MT and MR CLEAN-NO IV included patients with LVO of internal carotid artery (ICA), first segment of the middle cerebral artery (M1), or second segment of the middle cerebral artery (M2), whereas DEVT and SKIP only included patients with ICA or M1 and excluded M2 occlusions, which are known to have a higher response rate to IVT [43]. Besides, DIRECT-MT and MR CLEAN-NO IV included patients with National Institutes of Health Stroke Scale $({\mathrm{NIHSS}})\geq2$ , and SKIP included patients with ${\mathrm{NIHSS}}\geq6$ . Third, difference in study population. DIRECT-MT, DEVT and SKIP were conducted in Asian individuals, who were reported to have a significantly higher prevalence of intracranial atherosclerotic disease [44], which is less responsive to IVT [45]. Fourth, difference in workflow measures. For example, times from onset to alteplase and times from onset to groin puncture were shorter in MR CLEAN-NO IV, which may be attributable to the application of deferred consent and only including patients who presented directly to a hospital capable of EVT. Apart from the four points above, there are differences in many aspects. Hence, caution is required when interpreting the results of the trials.  

The methods used to identify non-inferiority margins also suggest cautious interpretation of results. The non-inferiority margins in all of the four trials were selected using the fixed-margin approach rather than the minimal clinically important difference. DIRECT-MT and MR CLEAN-NO IV pre-specified non-inferiority margins with a lower boundary of the common odds ratio of 0.8, while SKIP pre-specified an OR of 0.74, and in DEVT the non-inferiority margin $\mathrm{was}-10\%$ . The non-inferiority margins were too broad to draw a definitive conclusion that EVT alone is non-inferior to bridging therapy [46]. A meta-analysis of available data from RCTs demonstrated that EVT alone is statistically noninferior to IVT plus EVT but failed to meet most stringent non-inferiority margin [47]. Thus, it is more prudent to say that EVT alone is comparable to EVT plus IVT, rather than noninferior.  

There are several limitations to our meta-analysis. First, with partial data unavailable, we are incapable of performing further analyses. In fact, the efficacy and safety of IVT and EVT are influenced by concomitant disease, clot location, prehospital triage systems, and numerous other factors [48, 49]. Hence, it is important to figure out the treatment efficacy and safety on various patient subgroups as we advance towards patient-tailored therapy and precision medicine for patients’ greater benefit. Second, our results may not apply to the circumstances when other thrombolytic drugs would be used apart from intravenous alteplase. Tenecteplase was reported to be associated with a higher incidence of reperfusion and better functional outcome than alteplase before EVT in EXTEND-IA TNK [50], which might lead to different results. Additionally, some other thrombolytic drugs have been researched, including argatroban and eptifibatide [51]. Third, the trials were conducted in China, Japan, and European countries. Thus, the results may be inapplicable to patients in other countries and regions. Fourth, four trials only included stroke patients with occlusion of ICA, M1, and M2. Therefore, our results cannot be generalized to all LVO and posterior circulation infarction. Fifth, EVT was initiated before the termination of alteplase infusion in the vast majority of patients. Thus, our results could not apply to patients who are administered alteplase at one hospital and transferred to another hospital capable of EVT (“dripand-ship” patients), in whom IVT was reported to be more effective than in mothership patients [52].  

Data of SWIFT DIRECT (NCT03192332) and DIRECT SAFE (NCT03494920) trials are pending. A patient-level meta-analysis of more trials is expected to conclusively determine whether EVT alone is noninferior to EVT plus IVT in patients with anterior circulation ischemic stroke who are eligible for IVT and EVT, especially in specific subgroups.  

# Conclusions  

Our meta-analysis indicate that in acute ischemic stroke patients with LVO of anterior circulation who can be treated within $4.5\mathrm{h}$ after symptom onset, EVT alone is comparable with IVT plus EVT in efficacy and safety outcomes. However, more studies, ideally using RCTs designs, are needed in this area.
