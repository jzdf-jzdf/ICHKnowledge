# Abstract.  

BACKGROUND: Comprehensive geriatric assessment (CGA) has been used in inpatient, outpatient, and emergency patients in Western countries and is an important evaluation tool in medicine. In China, the application of CGA to multiple single diseases has achieved satisfactory intervention effects.  

OBJECTIVE: To explore the effect of CGA on postoperative quality of life (QoL), psychological state, neurological recovery, and self-efficacy in patients with cerebral hemorrhage.  

METHODS: In this randomized controlled trial, a total of 133 postoperative patients with cerebral hemorrhage who were treated and nursed in our hospital between March 2019 and March 2021 were randomly assigned to a control group (68 patients) and an observation group (65 patients). The control group was given a general comprehensive care intervention. The observation group was evaluated using an electronic medical record-based CGA system that assessed patient prognosis and was given individualized interventions based on the CGA findings. The postoperative QoL, psychological state, neurological recovery, and self-efficacy of the two groups were compared.  

RESULTS: After the intervention, self-decompression, self-decision-making, and positive attitudes of the observation group were higher than those of the control group. However, the National Institute of Health Stroke Scale score of the observation group was lower than that of the control group, the Self-rating anxiety scale and self-rating depression scale scores of the observation group were lower than those of the control group, and the social support score was significantly higher in the observation group than in the control group. After the intervention, the mental vitality, social interaction, emotional restriction, and mental status scores of the observation group were significantly higher than those of the control group.  

CONCLUSION: Comprehensive evaluation of patients with cerebral hemorrhage based on a CGA, targeting the individual factors that affect the prognosis of patients, and formulating and implementing individualized nursing intervention programs based on the CGA results can effectively relieve the symptoms of cerebral hemorrhage, reduce anxiety and depression, and improve the QoL of patients with cerebral hemorrhage.  

Keywords: Intracerebral hemorrhage, comprehensive geriatric assessment, quality of life, neurological function, self-efficacy  

# 1. Introduction  

Intracerebral hemorrhage refers to primary non-traumatic hemorrhage in the brain parenchyma. It is a serious condition, and the incidence is high among patients treated in neurology departments [1]. Cerebral hemorrhage is associated with high morbidity and mortality, the treatment is complicated, and the hospitalization time and the recovery period are long, which not only causes great physical discomfort to the patient but also brings mental and psychological stress and a high economic burden [2]. Studies in China and other countries have revealed many risk factors affecting the prognosis of patients, including physiological, psychological, dietary, family support, cognitive, and environmental factors [3]. In patients with cerebral hemorrhage, because the physiological functions have degenerated before the onset, the prognosis is worse, and the reasons for the problems affecting the prognosis of the patients are more complicated and result from the interaction of multiple factors [4]. Currently, the nursing care of patients is not standardized, and the selection of interventions depends primarily on the clinical experience of medical staff [5]. The method of evidence-based nursing can relieve conditions affecting the prognosis of patients, relieve pain, improve physical function, and promote recovery by identifying conditions that affect the prognosis of patients and taking corresponding measures based on an individualized assessment of factors that affect the prognosis of patients [6].  

Comprehensive geriatric assessment (CGA) refers to the use of multidimensional methods to comprehensively evaluate the individual’s physical health, functional status, mental health, social support, and surrounding environment and to formulate prevention and treatment plans for the purpose of protecting health and function and improving their quality of life (QoL), including both overall assessment and corresponding targeted interventions [7]. CGA has been used in inpatient, outpatient, and emergency patients in Western countries and is an important evaluation tool in medicine [8]. It is administered to hospitalized patients in the acute and subacute stages. Comprehensive multidisciplinary evaluation and intervention can rapidly improve various symptoms in patients and protect various bodily functions [9]. In China, the application of CGA to multiple single diseases has achieved satisfactory intervention effects [10,11]. Therefore, this study aimed to provide a reference for clinical nursing after cerebral hemorrhage by exploring the influence of CGA on the QoL, psychological state, neurological recovery, and self-efficacy of patients who had undergone surgery for cerebral hemorrhage.  

# 2. Materials and methods  

# 2.1. Study design and participants  

This study was a randomized controlled trial. Based on the incidence of the disease, the sample size was computed using the formula: $n=Z2(p*q)/d2$ . Take $Z=1.96$ , where p is the overall rate, $q=1-p$ . Let $p=0.8$ , $d$ is a fraction of $p$ . A total of 133 patients who were treated and nursed in our hospital between March 2019 and March 2021 were randomly divided into a control group ( $\begin{array}{r}{\dot{n}=68\dot{}}\end{array}$ ) and an observation group $(n=65$ ). Diagnostic criteria for cerebral hemorrhage: According to the Guidelines for Diagnosis and Treatment of Cerebral Hemorrhage in China [12], cerebral hemorrhage was diagnosed based on a history of chronic hypertension, sudden onset of the condition during activities or emotional agitation, markedly elevated blood pressure, and signs of increased intracranial pressure such as headache, nausea, and vomiting. Clinical manifestations include hemiplegia, aphasia, other focal neurological deficits, and signs of meningeal irritation, which may be accompanied by a disturbance of consciousness. The cerebral hemorrhage was confirmed using computed tomography (CT), magnetic resonance imaging (MRI), and other imaging studies. This study was approved by the Institutional Review Board of Liyuan Hospital, Tongji Medical College Huazhong University of Science and Technology (no. 2019A0145).  

# 2.2. Inclusion and exclusion criteria  

The inclusion criteria were: (1) no contraindications to minimally invasive surgery; (2) no previous episodes of cerebral hemorrhage; (3) clear level of consciousness and ability to speak; and (4) eligible patients or their family members were aware of the study and provided written informed consent. The exclusion criteria were: (1) a history of other diseases that affect the recovery of neurological function within 5 months of onset; (2) more than 3 days between onset and receiving treatment; (3) dysfunction of vital organs such as liver, lung, and kidney; (4) a history of severe autoimmune diseases, coagulation disorders, malignant tumors, or allergies; (5) confusion, disturbance of consciousness, or combined mental illness; (6) contraindications to related treatment and nursing, or severe postoperative complications; (7) pregnant or lactating women; and (8) inability to undergo regular follow-up after discharge from the hospital.  

# 2.3. Methods  

# 2.3.1. Holistic integrated intervention  

The control group were provided with an overall comprehensive intervention that included low-flow oxygen inhalation, ensuring that the patient’s airway was unobstructed, reducing the movement of the patient, paying close attention to any changes in the patient’s condition, and monitoring the changes in the patient’s blood pressure, consciousness, pupil, and other indicators once every 30 minutes. If the patient was comatose, CT was performed and the appropriate emergency care was provided. Nitroglycerin was used to control blood pressure, and intravenous mannitol infusion was used to reduce intracranial pressure. Intravenous mannitol infusion was also used to reduce the patient’s kidney load, the use of antibiotics was minimized, and acid-base balance was maintained. Detailed records of change of urine color were maintained, and routine urine examinations were conducted regularly. If proteinuria, hematuria, or other signs of renal dysfunction were found, corresponding intervention measures were taken immediately. Patients who were comatose have more secretions that may affect their expectoration. Therefore, to prevent lung infection, it was ensured that the patient’s airway was kept unobstructed, and sputum suction was provided to prevent excessive sputum accumulation. The sputum suction was increased or decreased, as appropriate, according to the patient’s condition, and a vibrating sputum excretion machine was used to promote sputum discharge. Samples were submitted for culture regularly, cultured bacteria underwent antibiotic susceptibility testing in the laboratory, and appropriate antibiotics were administered on time. The patient’s body temperature was closely monitored. Patients with fever were cooled by placing ice cubes in their armpits or around their neck or using a physical ice blanket. Vomiting and the passage of stools were carefully monitored, samples of vomit were examined, and hypoalbuminemia and potential electrolyte imbalances were corrected. Antidiuretic hormone therapy was administered, patients were turned over every 2 hours, and their body was scrubbed daily and checked for skin damage. For paralyzed patients, a suitable position was established to ensure normal blood circulation. After undergoing surgery, patients were treated with lipid emulsion, anti-inflammatory agents, and rehydration to ensure that their blood pressure remained at a normal level. Patients were provided with a varied, nutritious diet that included foods such as chicken, milk powder, fish soup, and vegetables; their oral cavity was moistened; close attention was paid to changes in the state of the surgical incision; a nursing intervention was performed daily to strengthen the perineum; the operation of the urinary catheter was monitored; the urine bag was replaced regularly, following strict aseptic procedures.  

# 2.3.2. Comprehensive geriatric assessment  

The observation group was evaluated using an electronic medical records-based CGA system to assess their prognosis, and an individualized nursing intervention was developed according to the existing and potential individual factors found in the evaluation that affected patient prognosis.  

# Dietary intervention  

An individualized diet plan was formulated based on swallowing ability, gastrointestinal function, serum albumin, protein and electrolyte levels, and the doctor’s orders. The next day’s diet was planned each night; daily dietary fiber intake was estimated according to the patient’s actual eating situation to ensure a daily intake of $25\mathrm{-}30~\mathrm{g}$ . For patients who were unable to eat orally, the food prescribed in the diet was liquidized and injected through the gastric tube to ensure that the patient had an adequate daily food intake. Multiple small meals were given at regular intervals. Family members were trained to prepare food in accordance with the formulated diet prescription, and patients were instructed to eat according to the time, food type, and amount specified in the diet prescription.; If the blood sugar were in permissible limits, the patient was allowed to drink a small amount of honey water and yogurt. A nutritionist was consulted as needed, and intravenous fluid intake or oral water intake was maintained at approximately 2000 mL/day during the acute phase and postoperatively, with the volume adjusted according to the 24-hour intake and output.  

# Cognitive function training  

Cognitive function training helps patients familiarize themselves with the environment and eliminate unfamiliarity; reminds or assists patients to eat, drink, defecate, rest, and exercise at a relatively fixed time every day, and gradually develop and adapt to regular daily activity habits; guides patients to focus on defecation events and the abdomen, by performing abdominal breathing and pressing the abdomen with their hands while holding their breath, and discouraging doing distracting things such as reading, chatting, and looking at mobile phones during defecation. Patients with aphasia can be provided with pictures of food, drinking water, and a toilet can help them to express their needs, and patients can be encouraged to express their needs with pictures, gestures, and expressions.  

# Self-care training  

Bedridden patients with muscle strength level II or above were encouraged to perform active exercises. They were provided with training to restore neurological control to muscles, improve limb muscle strength, and prepare for getting out of bed early [13]. Patients with stable conditions in the non-acute stage underwent training on activities of daily living, such as eating, dressing, walking, and toileting. The caregiver assisted in the completion of the action, then completed the action using the patient’s unaffected limb, and then gradually used the affected limb to assist the unaffected limb to complete the action, prepared the necessary auxiliary equipment, and paid attention to protecting the patient and avoiding falls and falling off the bed.  

# Psychological intervention  

According to the assessment results of the scale, patients were guided in the timely discovery of negative emotions; their harm was explained; patients were encouraged to pay attention to their emotional and psychological problems and express and vent their inner emotions and emotional changes, and to actively participate in the regulation of their psychological state. Patients were provided with timely affirmation and encouragement when their self-care methods were correct or when the nursing intervention achieved positive results so as to establish rehabilitation beliefs and obtain patients’ active cooperation. The patient’s social support system was used to provide guidance, comfort, and psychological support and maintain a good psychological state. The care ability of family members was evaluated, and an effort was made to understand the family members’ psychological dynamics and care difficulties and to provide appropriate psychological counseling to maintain a good spirit and improve their ability to provide patients with life care and spiritual comfort.  

# Family and social support  

Family members and friends were encouraged to care for and accompany the patient, eliminating the patient’s loneliness, enabling the patient to feel the care of family and friends, obtaining sufficient psychological support, and enhancing the patient’s sense of self-efficacy. After the patient’s vital signs stabilized and the hematoma was well controlled, appropriate massage and passive movement of the patient’s limbs and joints were performed to reduce the occurrence of complications such as deep vein thrombosis and pressure ulcers. Patients were instructed to perform passive movement on the limb joint bed, following the step-by-step principle, gradually increasing the training intensity, and gradually transitioning to bedside activities for 20 minutes per session, 2–3 times per day. Patients were encouraged to undergo training in activities of daily living such as self-dressing, toileting, grooming, and language function training to promote all aspects of their functional recovery.  

# Medication intervention  

The patient’s medication was reviewed to identify drugs that may have an adverse effect on their prognosis, such as dehydrating diuretics, calcium antagonists, antipyretic analgesics, sedative-hypnotics, psychiatric drugs, gastric mucosal protective agents, and antibiotics. The number of abdominal massages was increased in patients who were administered drugs that inhibited bowel movements. Urine volume was monitored in patients receiving dehydrating diuretics, and the positive and negative fluid balance was recorded. Patients who were administered antipyretic analgesics, calcium antagonists, and gastrointestinal mucosal protection and were encouraged to consume additional water, drinking yogurt or honey water in moderation. The nurse provided timely feedback to the doctor to enable the doctor to adjust the medication plan and reduce or stop the dose in time. Patients and family members were informed that they should not use medication to promote defecation without a prescription. The long-term use of laxatives, glycerin enemas, and other drugs was discouraged, and efforts were made to reduce the patient’s mental dependence on drugs.  

# 2.4. Observation indicators  

(1) The QoL score: The QoL score included four parts [14]: mental vitality, social interaction, emotional limitation, and mental status scores, each scored from 0 to 100. The higher the score, the better the patient’s QoL. The QoL score is the simplest and most widely used method to evaluate the QoL of patients. It is easy for patients to understand and accept and has good reliability and validity. The measured Cronbach’s alpha value is 0.962, indicating high sensitivity and convenient recording. (2) The self-efficacy scale was used to evaluate self-efficacy before and after the intervention in the two groups [15]. The scale had a total of 28 items, including self-decompression (10 items), self-decision (3 items), and positive attitude (15 items), and each item was scored on a scale of 1 to 5 for a total score out of 140 points, with a higher score representing better self-efficacy. (3) On the self-rating anxiety scale (SAS) [16], more than 50 points indicated anxiety; the higher the score, the more serious the anxiety. On the self-rating depression scale (SDS) [17], a score greater than 53 indicated depression, and a higher score represented more severe depression. The Social Support Rate Score (SSRS) comprised 10 items with a total score of 66 [18]. A higher score indicated a higher level of social support. These scales were not affected by factors such as age, sex, or economic status. The overall Cronbach’s alpha value measured before use was 0.924. (4) The neurological function of the patients in the two groups was evaluated using the National Institute of Health Stroke Scale (NIHSS) [19], with a scoring standard ranging from 0 to 35 points. Lower scores indicated better neurological recovery.

# 2.5. Statistical analysis  

Data analysis was performed using SPSS version 25.0 (IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA). The measurements were expressed as mean $\pm$ standard deviation (SD) and compared using the $t$ -test. The count data was expressed as a percentage $(\%)$ and compared using the $\chi^{2}$ test. Statistical significance was set at $P<$ 0.05.

The observation group had 17 patients with high school and below, 31 undergraduates, and 17 with master’s degrees and above. The control group had 18 patients with high school and below, 35 undergraduates, and 15 with master’s degrees and above. # 4. Discussion  

CGA is a systematic and comprehensive assessment of patients’ somatic function, psychology, cognition, self-management, social support, environment, and other multiple factors that may affect disease progression and prognosis, using multidisciplinary knowledge of clinical medicine, nursing, nutrition, psychology, and social sciences to identify existing and potential health problems, find relevant factors that contribute to these problems, and based on the CGA results, medical staff can assess and evaluate a person’s physical health, functional status, mental health, social support, and surroundings comprehensively, and to develop prevention and treatment plans aimed at protecting the person’s health and function to maximize the QoL [20]. It includes both holistic assessment of the patient and providing the corresponding targeted interventions, and the CGA mainly includes medical assessment, somatic functioning, psychosomatic, CGA syndrome, socioeconomic, and environmental factors [21].  

In this study, the self-stress reduction, self-determination, and positive attitudes of the observation group were higher than those of the comparison group after the intervention; however, the NIHSS scores of the observation group were lower than those of the comparison group, indicating that the neurological recovery and self-efficacy of patients with cerebral hemorrhage were improved by conducting a CGA of the patients. This indicates that systematic nursing interventions are more helpful in enhancing patients’ self-efficacy. This may be because, during the comprehensive assessment based on CGA, patients are given psychological interventions, family social support, health education, and other aspects of care, which can eliminate patients’ negative emotions in a timely manner so that patients can establish a correct perception of their disease and recovery, and thus treat their condition more positively and obtain a good sense of self-efficacy [22]. Self-efficacy refers to the degree of confidence in one’s ability to use the skills one possesses to accomplish a certain work behavior [23]. Patients’ awareness of the long process required for limb rehabilitation due to good family support and emotional communication allows them to consciously adjust their mindset and cope with the disease in a calm manner, which reduces the impact of the disease process on self-efficacy and leads to increased confidence and active functional exercise [24]. When functional rehabilitation of the limbs does not reach the expected goal, there is a decrease in self-efficacy and anxiety, leading to a decrease in the level of self-efficacy in patients [25].  

In this study, the SAS and SDS scores of patients in the observation group were lower than those in the comparison group after the intervention, whereas the social support scores were significantly higher than those in the comparison group, indicating that anxiety, depression, and social support of patients with cerebral hemorrhage were improved by applying a comprehensive assessment based on CGA. The analysis of the mechanism of action of applying the CGA comprehensive assessment and individualized intervention model to effectively alleviate anxiety and depression in patients with cerebral hemorrhage combined with prognosis affecting patients may be that in previous studies of patients with prognosis affecting patients, nursing staff also attached importance to the influence of psychological factors. However, assessing patients’ psychological status mostly relied on nurses’ experience and subjective judgment, which could more objectively and truly reflect the patients’ emotional problems and severity, allowing nursing staff to more accurately understand the patient’s psychological condition and provide a basis for psychological interventions [26]. Nursing staff communicate actively with patients, encourage them to confide, actively express and vent their inner emotions and psychological changes, preach disease knowledge, and provide timely feedback to patients when the treatment effect is satisfactory so that they can understand the treatment and prognosis [27]. Educating about the disease and providing feedback on the treatment effect can reduce their tension, anxiety, and fear due to the lack of knowledge about disease treatment and prognosis, help them gradually accept the reality of the disease, and build confidence that by actively cooperating with treatment and care, they can improve all body functions and achieve a high degree of recovery [27]. The attention of patients’ family members is overly focused on the patient’s condition, treatment, and prognosis. They do not have the time and energy to adjust to their own mental, emotional, and cognitive aspects and cannot fully adapt to their caregiving role [28]. After the nursing staff assessed the caregiving abilities of the family members and provided knowledge dissemination and psychological guidance, the family members gained knowledge about the causes of cerebral hemorrhage, treatment, care, and prognosis [29]. Their own tension and anxiety were relieved, and their ability to care for patients improved, allowing them to cooperate with medical staff in a more coordinated manner so that they could receive attention, companionship, and support from nursing staff and family members, helping them find spiritual dependence, reducing loneliness, and improving psychological security and comfort [30]. By fully mobilizing family members to accompany and help patients with physical function and self-care training, the role of family function in mediating the relationship between daily living ability and post-stroke anxiety, depression, and other adverse emotions, patients’ daily living ability is improved and their confidence in disease recovery and return to society is enhanced [31].  

The mental vitality score, social interaction score, emotional limitation score, and mental status of patients in the observation group were significantly higher than those in the comparison group after the intervention, indicating that the QoL of patients with cerebral hemorrhage was improved by performing a comprehensive CGA-based assessment. This may be because individualized cognitive function, physical function, and self-care training were developed based on the results of the CGA assessment of patients to improve their cognitive, activity, and daily living abilities; increase autonomy and independence in daily activities such as eating, bathing, toileting, and dressing; and increase the likelihood of patients working normally, handling family affairs, and participating in social activities [32]. Through the mediating role of motor self-efficacy, QoL is influenced by the ability of daily living and rehabilitation effects of stroke patients; the QoL of patients is improved when the ability of daily living is improved, and the effects of functional training are revealed [33]. Based on the caregiving ability assessment and the survey of knowledge about the prognosis of patients affected by cerebral hemorrhage, we understand the shortcomings and knowledge blind spots of family members’ caregiving ability and carry out health education and psychological care for family members so that they can understand and meet the health needs of patients in a timely manner and provide a high standard of family care and support [34]. Patients and family members with timely knowledge of cerebral hemorrhage disease, rehabilitation, and care can actively cooperate with treatment and nursing care and improve their compliance and cooperation with medical personnel, which, in turn, improves the effectiveness of disease treatment, accelerates neurological recovery, and allows patients to improve their QoL [35]. Several studies have shown that the QoL of patients with stroke is related to the years of illness, length of hospital stay, health assessment, emotional state, ability to perform daily activities, rehabilitation training, and living caregivers [36]. After the onset of cerebral hemorrhage, patients require family care and worry about not being able to return to society, creating a sense of loneliness and helplessness or even sadness and despair. Patients’ illnesses cause heavy financial and psychological burdens for their families, and families lack knowledge of cerebral hemorrhage disease and caregiving and are often overwhelmed when caring for patients; moreover, with the prolongation of the disease, family members’ attention to patients may decrease, and the lack of timely and correct care from family members also reduces the QoL of patients with cerebral hemorrhage [37]. Therefore, comprehensive measures should be taken to improve the QoL of patients with cerebral hemorrhage to alleviate somatic discomfort, improve negative psychological states, foster self-care ability, and improve family caregiving ability.  

CGA is a central and important component of comprehensive human care that uses specific tools to summarize the status of several domains that may affect the overall health and disease outcomes of patients, including the assessment of somatic, psychological, spiritual, nutritional, cognitive, social, medication, economic, and environmental conditions [38]. The above data illustrate that a comprehensive and integrated CGA assessment of patients with combined cerebral hemorrhage affects their prognosis, analysis of the assessment results, identification of individual factors that contribute to problems affecting the patient’s prognosis, and development of individualized nursing interventions to address the patient’s diet, activity, psychology, family care, and lack of knowledge [39]. It can effectively relieve patients’ prognostic symptoms, such as difficult defecation, abdominal pain, and abdominal distension; increase body comfort; reduce the occurrence of serious complications such as intracranial rebleeding and electrolyte disorders; improve patients’ nutritional status; enhance body resistance and healing ability; reduce anxiety, depression, and other adverse emotions; and allow patients to cope with the disease in a positive mental state and improve their QoL [40].  

This study has some limitations. First, the small sample size was a major limitation. Second, the study was a single-center study. Third, all participants were Chinese, so generalizability to other populations may be limited. Given these limitations, further large multicenter studies should be conducted to validate the results of this study.  

# 5. Conclusion  

Conducting a comprehensive CGA of patients with cerebral hemorrhage and developing and implementing individualized nursing intervention programs based on individual factors affecting patients’ prognosis alleviated symptoms effectively, reduced anxiety and depression, and improved the QoL of patients with cerebral hemorrhage.
