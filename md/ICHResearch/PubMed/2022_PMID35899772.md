# Early antiplatelet for minor stroke following thrombolysis (EAST): Rationale and design  

Xiao-Qiu Li\*, Yu Cui\* , Xin-Hong Wang and Hui-Sheng Chen  



# Abstract  

Background: Early neurological deterioration (END) occurs in about $10\%$ of patients after intravenous thrombolysis (IVT) and is related to poor outcome. In theory, early antiplatelet following IVT could reduce END by preventing reocclusion and stroke progression, but current guidelines recommend starting antiplatelet treatment at $24~\mathrm{h}$ after IVT due to concerns about hemorrhagic transformation. Given higher risk of hemorrhagic transformation in severe stroke, we hypothesized that minor stroke patients following IVT can safely benefit from early antiplatelet treatment.  

Aims: To explore the efficacy and safety of early antiplatelet in minor stroke patients after IVT.  

Sample size estimates: A maximum of 1022 patients are required to test the superiority hypothesis with $80\%$ power according to a two-side 0.05 level of significance, stratified by age, gender, history of stroke or transient ischemic attack, history of hypertension, history of diabetes mellitus, systolic blood pressure at admission, time from IVT to treatment, thrombolysis drug, stroke territory, and stroke etiology.  

Design: Early antiplatelet for minor stroke following thrombolysis is a prospective, double-blinded, multicenter, randomized and placebo-controlled trial. Minor stroke patients within 6 h following IVT are randomly assigned into experimental group and control group with the ratio of 1:1. The experimental group is orally administered with $300\mathsf{mg}$ clopidogrel and $100\mathsf{mg}$ aspirin, and control group with placebo. Subsequently, both groups received guideline-based antithrombotic treatment from $24~\mathsf{h}$ after IVT to 90 days.  

Outcome: The primary efficacy endpoint is excellent functional outcome, defined as the modified Rankin Scale 0–1 at 90 days after randomization, while primary safety endpoint is symptomatic intracerebral hemorrhage, defined as National Institutes of Health Stroke Scale score increase $\geq4$ caused by intracranial hemorrhage within $36~\mathsf{h}$ after randomization.  

Conclusions: The results of EAST will provide us powerful early antiplatelet evidence for minor stroke population following intravenous thrombolysis in clinical practice.

# Introduction and rationale  

Intravenous thrombolysis (IVT) with alteplase is proven to be effective in acute ischemic stroke (AIS) patients within $4.5\mathrm{~h~}$ of symptom onset.1,2 For AIS patients treated with IVT, antiplatelet treatment is recommended delayed until 24 h later.1,2 About $10\%$ of early neurological deterioration (END) were previously reported in the NINDS study,3 $73\%$ of which were related to re-occlusion of recanalized vessel. Previous studies have explored the safety and efficacy of standard-dose alteplase versus low-dose alteplase plus eptifibatide.4–6 The results showed that low-dose alteplase plus eptifibatide was safe for AIS. Another two studies also explored the safety and efficacy of tirofiban administrated at different time points following IVT.7,8 The results showed that intravenous tirofiban after standard-dose alteplase seemed safe and potentially beneficial. However, these studies were limited with non-standard dose of alteplase, or small sample size, or non-randomized design. The antiplatelet therapy in combination with rt-PA thrombolysis in ischemic stroke (ARTIS) was the first large sample-size randomized clinical trial to compare the effects of early addition of intravenous aspirin to alteplase with standard dose versus only alteplase with standard dose in ischemic stroke.9 But the trial was prematurely terminated because of an excess of sICH outweighing the benefit. The high risk of sICH may be attributed to unrestricted neurological deficit severity of subjects as well as earlier administration of aspirin.10  

In this context, we hypothesized that early antiplatelet may be safe and can improve clinical prognosis in minor stroke patients after IVT. Thus, this trial is designed to explore the hypothesis.  



# Methods  



# Design  

Early Antiplatelet for Minor Stroke following Thrombolysis (EAST) (NCT05193071) is a prospective, double-blind, and multicenter, randomized, placebo-controlled study in China aiming to estimate the efficacy and safety of early antiplatelet in minor stroke patients within 6 h after the end of IVT.  



# Study population  

In EAST trial, eligible participants are patients with minor stroke (National Institute of Health Stroke Scale, NIHSS 0–5) following IVT including minor stroke before and after IVT or minor stroke improved from non-minor stroke after IVT, judged by the investigator. They will be enrolled at approximately 30 sites in China between June 2022 and June 2024. The detailed inclusion/exclusion criteria are listed in Supplementary sTable 1.  



# Standard protocol approvals, registrations, and patient consents  

The EAST trail is listed on www.clinicaltrial.gov (NCT05193071). The protocol and data collection of the trial have or will been approved by the ethics committee of General Hospital of Northern Theater Command and all participating cites. All patients or their representatives will provide written informed consent before inclusion into the trial.  



# Randomization and intervention  

The eligible patients treated with intravenous alteplase $(0.9\mathrm{mg/kg}$ , the maximum dose: $90\mathrm{mg}$ ) or tenecteplase ( $0.25\mathrm{mg/kg}$ , the maximum dose: $25\mathrm{mg}$ ) will be assigned into experimental group and control group as a ratio of 1:1 using central and computerized random sequence generation stratified by center (Figure 1). The experimental group will be orally administered with $300\mathrm{mg}$ clopidogrel and $100\mathrm{mg}$ aspirin immediately after randomization. The control group will be orally administered with placebo immediately after randomization. Both groups subsequently received guideline-based antithrombotic treatment from $^{241}$ after IVT to 90 days.  



# Outcomes  

The primary endpoint is proportion of modified Rankin Scale (mRS) 0–1 at 90 days.  

The second endpoints are as follows: (1) proportion of mRS (0–2) at 90 days; (2) distribution of mRS at 90 days; (3) change in NIHSS compared with baseline at $^{24\textrm{h}}$ , 48 h, and 10 days; (4) incidence of END, defined as an increase of more than 2 points in NIHSS score, but not the result of cerebral hemorrhage, compared with baseline at $24\mathrm{~h~}$ ; and (5) occurrence of stroke or other vascular events at 90 days.  

The safety endpoints include proportion of sICH within $^{36\mathrm{~h~}}$ , incidence of any cerebral hemorrhage within $^{36\mathrm{~h~}}$ , and occurrence of all-cause death in 90 days. sICH is defined as any evidence of bleeding on the head CT scan associated with clinically significant neurological deterioration (NIHSS score $\geq4$ points increase) in the opinion of the clinical investigator or independent safety monitor.  



# Follow-up procedure  

Study visits will be performed at $^{24\textrm{h}}$ , $48\mathrm{~h~}$ , 10 days, and 90 days after randomization (Supplementary sTable 2). At baseline, demographic characteristics, routine laboratory tests, and neuroimaging will be collected and NIHSS and mRS will be evaluated. At $^{24\mathrm{h}}$ , a repeat non-contrast CT/MRI will be performed for both groups. NIHSS scoring will be repeated at $^{24\mathrm{~h~}}$ , $^{48\mathrm{~h~}}$ , and $10\pm2$ days, and mRS will be assessed at $90\pm7$ days. All clinical assessments including NIHSS and mRS were evaluated by certified assessors according to a standardized procedure manual in each study center. The primary endpoint (mRS at 90 days) will be mainly evaluated by face-to-face or, if a face-to-face interview was not possible, by telephone interview. Concomitant medications and adverse events (AEs) within 90 days after randomization will be recorded in detail by investigators and adjudicated by certified assessors. The cycle of this research is expected to be about 24 months.

# All AEs monitoring  

AE is any adverse medical event that occurs during the study. All information about AEs should be recorded on the AEs page of the case report, and whether the unexpected AE is associated with early antiplatelet treatment will be further adjudicated by principal investigator.  



# Data management and monitoring  

All data are obtained by MedSci (http://EAST.medsci.cn) that has the function of registering patient case report form. The enrolled patients will be randomly assigned into the experimental group and control group at a ratio of 1:1. The data will be downloaded from MedSci with a dedicated person for statistical analysis. An independent DSMC is established to ensure ongoing monitoring of data security, such as hemorrhagic events and other AEs. # Sample size determination  

According to previous study,11 the proportion of expected excellent functional outcome $(\mathrm{mRS~}0{-}1)$ at 90 days in control group is estimated to be about $86\%$ . The proportion in the experimental group is estimated to be a $6\%$ increase, compared with proportion in the control group. Using power $=80\%$ and $\upalpha{=}0.05$ to carry out the two-side test, the calculated sample size to test the superiority hypothesis is 920. In consideration of $10\%$ lost to follow-up, the total sample size is 1022. Therefore, this study included 1022 patients, with 511 patients in each group.  



# Statistical analysis  

Intention-to-treat (ITT) analysis will be used to analyze the therapeutic effects of the two groups and all the data will be analyzed with SPSS 23.0 Software. The mean $\pm$ standard deviation (SD) will be used if the data are normally distributed, and the median and quartile spacing (IQR) will be used if the data are non-normally distributed. Count data are expressed as $n$ $(\%)$ . Difference of the primary endpoint and secondary endpoints such as mRS (0–2) at 90 days, incidence of END, proportion of sICH within $^{36\mathrm{~h~}}$ , incidence of any cerebral hemorrhage within $^{36\mathrm{~h~}}$ , and occurrence of all-cause death in 90 days will be compared using binary logistic regression. Distribution of mRS at 90 days between two groups will be compared using the ordinal logistic regression. Change in NIHSS score between two groups will be compared using general linear model. Timeto-events of stroke recurrence and other vascular events will be compared using Cox regression. There is statistical significance if p-value $<0.05$ .  

The primary endpoint in EAST will further be stratified by age, gender, history of stroke or transient ischemic attack, history of hypertension, history of diabetes mellitus, systolic blood pressure at admission $\mathrm{\Lambda}^{\prime}{>}140\mathrm{mm}$ Hg $\mathrm{vs}\leqslant140\mathrm{mm}\mathrm{Hg}$ , time from the end of IVT to treatment $\mathrm{\Lambda}^{\prime}<2\mathrm{h}$ vs $\geqslant2\mathrm{~h~},$ ), thrombolysis drug (tenecteplase vs alteplase), stroke territory (anterior vs posterior circulation infarction), and stroke etiology (large-artery atherosclerosis, cardioembolism, small-artery occlusion, other determined cause, and undetermined cause). Differences of primary endpoint in above specific subgroups will be assessed by testing for interaction of the preset baseline variable with primary endpoint.

# Discussion  

At the time of the first submission of this article, the trial does not begin to recruit the patients. Recruitment will be continued until the complete sample size is achieved, which is expected to be in August 2023 at the latest.  

As an effective treatment for acute ischemic stroke, IVT is strongly recommended by guidelines, but it is mainly limited by strict time window, low reperfusion rate, and END. Considering that END is closely associated with vessel reocclusion, early antiplatelet treatment seems plausible, but antiplatelet is recommended to be administrated $^{24\textrm{h}}$ following IVT by current guidelines. Previous studies have investigated the effect of early antiplatelet (during or after IVT) in acute ischemic stroke, but there is no definite evidence due to the limitations of small sample size or the premature termination resulted from high risk of sICH. Thus, we conduct a prospective, double-blind, multicenter, randomized, placebo-controlled study aiming to explore the efficacy and safety of early antiplatelet in acute minor ischemic stroke patients after IVT.  

The EAST has several differences from two previous randomized clinical trials (Supplementary sTable 3).4,9 First, the difference in neurological deficits in targeted patients: non-minor stroke in CLEAR $(\mathrm{NIHSS}\geqslant5\$ )4 and ARTIS (unrestricted NIHSS)9 versus minor stroke in EAST (NIHSS: 0–5). Although it is still under debate whether IVT is effective and safe for ischemic stroke patients with mild neurological symptom, $40\%\sim50.5\%$ of this population received IVT in clinical practice.11,12 Given the condition, mild stroke patients who received intravenous thrombolysis were not ruled out in EAST trial. Furthermore, about one-third of patients with minor stroke harbor a large vessel occlusion and potentially develop a medium to large infarction,13 which will attribute to END in minor stroke.14 Thus, these patients maybe benefit from early antiplatelet after IVT. In addition, it is well known that higher baseline NIHSS score is a significant predictor of sICH after IVT.15 Based on these thoughts, we infer that minor stroke after IVT should be the best target of early antiplatelet. Second, the difference in time points of antiplatelet treatment: at the same time with alteplase treatment in CLEAR4 versus within $90\mathrm{min}$ after start of alteplase treatment in ARTIS9 versus within $^\textrm{\scriptsize6h}$ after the end of alteplase or tenecteplase in EAST. We choose to start antiplatelet treatment within $^\textrm{\scriptsize6h}$ after the end of IVT, based on two considerations: (1) re-occlusion or stroke progression mostly occurs in this time window and (2) earlier administration such as ARTIS study may increase the risk of sICH. Third, the difference in antithrombotic strategies: intravenous eptifibatide in CLEAR4 versus intravenous aspirin in ARTIS9 versus oral aspirin plus clopidogrel in EAST. In EAST trial, aspirin plus clopidogrel was chosen based on three reasons: (1) the dual antiplatelet has been demonstrated to be superior to aspirin alone for reducing the risk of minor stroke treated within $^{24\textrm{h}}$ after the onset of symptoms;16,17 (2) only one time of dual antiplatelet could avoid the possible hemorrhagic transformation given increased risk of long-term use; and (3) oral antiplatelet is easy and cheap. Finally, the difference in thrombolysis drug doses: low-dose alteplase (0.3 and $0.45\mathrm{mg/kg)}$ in CLEAR4 versus standard-dose alteplase $(0.9\mathrm{mg/kg)}$ in ARTIS9 and standard-dose alteplase $(0.9\mathrm{mg/kg})$ or tenecteplase $(0.25\mathrm{mg/kg)}$ in EAST. Given no noninferiority of low-dose versus standard-dose alteplase with respect to death and disability at 90 days,6,18 standard-dose alteplase or tenecteplase is used in this study.  



# Conclusion  

The results of EAST will provide us powerful early antiplatelet evidence for minor stroke population following intravenous thrombolysis in clinical practice.
