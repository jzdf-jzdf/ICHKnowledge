# ABSTRACT  

OBJECTIVES The aim of this study was to determine the safety of selective intracoronary hypothermia during primary percutaneous coronary intervention (PPCI) in patients with anterior ST-segment elevation myocardial infarction (STEMI).  

BACKGROUND Selective intracoronary hypothermia is a novel treatment designed to reduce myocardial reperfusion injury and is currently being investigated in the ongoing randomized controlled EURO-ICE (European Intracoronary Cooling Evaluation in Patients With ST-Elevation Myocardial Infarction) trial (NCT03447834). Data on the safety of such a procedure during PPCI are still limited.  

METHODS The first 50 patients with anterior STEMI treated with selective intracoronary hypothermia during PPCI were included in this analysis and compared for safety with the first 50 patients randomized to the control group undergoing standard PPCI. In-hospital mortality, occurrence of rhythm or conduction disturbances, stent thrombosis, onset of heart failure during the procedure, and subsequent hospital admission were assessed.  

RESULTS In-hospital mortality was $0\%$ . One patient in both groups developed cardiogenic shock. Atrial fibrillation occurred in 0 and 3 patients $\ensuremath{\left(P=0.24\right)},$ ), and ventricular fibrillation occurred in 5 and 3 patients $(P=0.72)$ in the intracoronary hypothermia group and control group, respectively. Stent thrombosis occurred in 2 patients in the intracoronary hypothermia group; 1 instance was intraprocedural, and the other occurred following interruption of dual-antiplatelet therapy consequent to an intracranial hemorrhage 6 days after enrollment. No stent thrombosis was observed in the control group $(P=0.50)\$ ).  

CONCLUSIONS Selective intracoronary hypothermia during PPCI in patients with anterior STEMI can be implemented within the routine of PPCI and seems to be safe. The final safety results will be reported at the end of the trial. (J Am Coll Cardiol Intv 2021;-:-–-) $\circleddash$ 2021 by the American College of Cardiology Foundation.

$\mathbf{v}\mathbf{\mathbb{F}}=$ ventricular fibrillation P rimary percutaneous coronary intervention (PPCI) has become the cornerstone of treatment in patients with acute myocardial infarction (AMI) and has significantly improved outcomes (1). However, in a number of patients, reperfusion injury may undo a considerable part of the recovery of the ischemic myocardium achieved by PPCI (2,3). Consequently, preventing myocardial reperfusion injury is seen as the next challenge in patients with AMI, especially in the case of ST-elevation myocardial infarction (STEMI). Therefore, the importance of endeavors to limit myocardial reperfusion injury, in order to decrease infarct size and further improve clinical outcomes, is undisputed (2).  

Selective intracoronary hypothermia is a novel therapy intended to reduce myocardial reperfusion injury and has shown beneficial effects in animal studies (4). In humans, however, such therapy is not trivial, and its feasibility has been investigated only recently in a small pilot study (SINTAMI [Safety and Feasibility of Intracoronary Hypothermia in Acute Myocardial Infarction]) (5). So far, no clinical outcome data are available. At present, the effectiveness of selective intracoronary hypothermia to decrease infarct size is being investigated in 200 patients in the prospective, randomized controlled EURO-ICE (European Intracoronary Cooling Evaluation in Patients With ST-Elevation Myocardial Infarction) trial (NCT03447834) (6).  

For such a new and experimental therapy, monitoring of safety is paramount. In the SINTAMI study, Otterspoor et al (5) investigated the safety of selective intracoronary hypothermia in 10 patients with STEMI. In 2 of 4 patients with inferior STEMI and an occluded right coronary artery, symptomatic atrioventricular conduction disturbances were observed, 1 of which necessitated treatment with a temporary pacemaker. In 6 patients with large anterior STEMI, no complications were observed. For this reason, only patients with anterior STEMI are being recruited in the EUROICE trial (6). Upon approval of the study by the Institutional Review Board, interim analysis for safety of the procedure was mandated after 50 patients had been treated with selective intracoronary hypothermia. Furthermore, an independent data and safety monitoring board (DSMB) oversees the trial. and interim analyses will be performed at the discretion of the DSMB (6).  

Accordingly, in the present study we evaluated prespecified procedural safety in the first 50 patients with anterior STEMI in the ongoing EURO-ICE trial, treated with selective intracoronary hypothermia during PPCI, in comparison with the first 50 patients randomized to the control group undergoing standard PPCI.  

As the investigators and authors of this paper do not have access to outcome data by design, this report is limited to the predefined safety parameters.  

# METHODS  

From the ongoing EURO-ICE trial, the first 50 patients with anterior STEMI, treated with selective intracoronary hypothermia during PPCI, were included in this safety analysis and compared with the first 50 patients randomized to the control group undergoing standard PPCI. EURO-ICE is a 1:1 randomized controlled European multicenter trial evaluating the effect of selective intracoronary hypothermia on infarct size, as measured using cardiovascular magnetic resonance imaging (6).  

The study was approved by the Institutional Review Boards of all participating centers.  

Patients 18 to 80 years of age with anterior STEMI and summed ST-segment deviation on the electrocardiogram of at least $5\mathrm{mm}$ were screened for eligibility. Cardiac catheterization was performed using femoral or radial access. Eligibility was established in case of an occlusion in the proximal or mid segment of the left anterior descending coronary artery (LAD) with TIMI (Thrombolysis In Myocardial Infarction) flow grade 0 or 1. Oral consent was obtained in the catheterization laboratory immediately after angiography of the left coronary artery. Thereafter, the patient was randomized to the intracoronary cooling protocol during PPCI or to standard PPCI. Written informed consent was obtained later during hospital admission.  

SELECTIVE INTRACORONARY HYPOTHERMIA: INSTRUMENTATION. A 6-F guiding catheter was positioned in the left coronary artery, and the occlusion in the LAD was crossed using a regular guidewire (Figure 1). Immediately thereafter, a properly sized over-the-wire balloon (OTWB) was advanced and inflated to 4 atm at the site of the occlusion to secure persistent occlusion of the LAD. A pressure/temperature sensing wire (PressureWire X, Abbott) was connected to the CoroFlow version 3.0 software platform (Coroventis) and introduced into the ostium of the left main coronary artery. After equalization of the pressures with automatic “zeroing” of body temperature, the PressureWire X was advanced alongside the OTWB into the distal LAD, for which purpose the balloon was deflated for a few seconds. The regular guidewire was then retracted from the central lumen of the OTWB so that this lumen could be used for infusion of saline.  

SELECTIVE INTRACORONARY HYPOTHERMIA: COOLING. Selective intracoronary hypothermia consisted of 2 phases, the “occlusion” phase (Figure 2A) and the “reperfusion” phase (Figure 2B; also see Figure 3).  

During the occlusion phase, the OTWB remained inflated to 4 atm. This prevented reperfusion (and with it reperfusion injury). During this phase, saline at room temperature $(\sim22^{\circ}\mathrm{C})$ was infused through the central lumen of the OTWB for a duration of 7 to 10 minutes. The intracoronary target temperature was ${}^{6\circ}\mathrm{C}$ to $8^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$ less than body temperature. Because of the temperature gradient between the coronary artery and myocardium at risk (MaR) in this phase (cold transfer by conduction), the temperature of the MaR is $31^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$ to $33^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$ (7).  

In the reperfusion phase, the OTWB was deflated and blood flow was (partially) restored. To compensate for the mixture of warm blood flow in the MaR, cold saline $(\sim3^{\circ}\mathrm{C}{\cdot}4^{\circ}\mathrm{C})$ was used for infusion during this phase. The switch from saline at ${}^{22^{\circ}\mathrm{C}}$ to saline at $3^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$ was made just before the deflation of the balloon. The coronary target temperature was $4^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$ to ${}^{6\mathrm{{}^{\circ}C}}$ less than body temperature, and the duration of this phase was 10 minutes. As there is no relevant temperature gradient in this phase between the coronary artery and the corresponding MaR (cold transfer by convection), this translates into a myocardial temperature of $31^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$ to $33^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$ (7).  

After the reperfusion phase was finished, the OTWB was retracted, and the PressureWire X could be used as a guidewire for placement of a stent. If the operator wished to use a regular guidewire to finish the procedure, this guidewire could be inserted and advanced into the distal LAD through the central lumen of the OTWB. Thereafter, the OTWB was retracted using standard techniques.  

As an alternative to the use of a regular wire and OTWB, it was also possible to start the procedure immediately with the PressureWire X to cross the occlusion and to advance a specific monorail ballooninfusion catheter (CoolCell, Hexacath) over the PressureWire X to the site of the occlusion and inflate it to 4 atm. No second wire was necessary in that case, making the technical performance of the procedure easier (6). The CoolCell catheter is not yet Conformité Européenne approved and is used at some of the participating centers as an investigational medical product. The CoolCell catheter was used in 15 of the 50 patients included in this analysis.

After the cooling procedure, stenting was performed, and the procedure was completed according to standard care. During the occlusion phase, opioids and/or benzodiazepines may be used if indicated to reduce chest pain. All other medical treatments, periprocedurally and at follow-up, are identical to normal standard of care.  

CONTROL GROUP: STANDARD PPCI. Patients in the control group underwent PPCI completely according to standard techniques and according to the routine in the participating centers.  

ASSESSMENT OF COMPLICATIONS AND SAFETY. During the procedure, clinical, hemodynamic, and electrocardiographic variables were obtained. Duration of infusion, coronary temperature, and total amount of saline used per phase were recorded in patients treated with selective intracoronary hypothermia. Special attention was paid to the occurrence of hypotension, rhythm or conduction disturbances, and the effect of the cooling procedure on systemic temperature. TIMI flow grade at the end of the procedure and intraprocedural stent thrombosis (IPST) were assessed.

During the subsequent hospital admission, the occurrence of rhythm disturbances, stent thrombosis (ST), onset of heart failure, including the intravenous administration of diuretic agents and/or inotropes or vasopressors, and mortality were reported by site investigators and compared between groups.  

STATISTICAL ANALYSIS. Statistical analyses are presented in a descriptive manner. Normally distributed data are reported as mean $\pm\mathtt{SD}$ , skewed distributions as median and interquartile range (IQR). Binary variables and outcomes are presented as absolute numbers and percentages and were compared using a chi-square or Fisher exact test to a $\textbf{2}\times\textbf{2}$ table of binary events per group. The MannWhitney $U$ test was used to compare medians across both groups. A paired, 2-tailed Student’s $t$ -test or Wilcoxon signed rank test was used to compare paired values as appropriate depending on their distribution. A $P$ value of $<0.05$ was considered to indicate statical significance. SPSS Statistics version 25 (IBM) was used for all statistical analyses.  

# RESULTS  

BASELINE CHARACTERISTICS. Patients were included between January 2019 and January 2021 at 6 European heart centers. Baseline characteristics are presented in Table 1. The median ages of the patients were 62.1 and 64.9 years $(P=0.73)$ ) and 45 and 41 patients were men $(P=0.25).$ ) in the intracoronary hypothermia group and control group, respectively. Median ST-segment deviation on the classifying electrocardiogram was 16 and $13\mathrm{mm}$ $(P=0.26)$ ) in the intracoronary hypothermia group and control group, respectively.  

Angiography revealed the culprit occlusion in the proximal LAD (syntax segment 6) in 30 and 26 patients and in the mid LAD (syntax segment 7) in 20 and 24 patients $(P=0.42)$ ) in the intracoronary hypothermia group and control group, respectively. TIMI flow grade pre-PCI was 0 in 42 and 45 patients and 1 in 8 and 5 patients $\primeP=0.37,$ ) in the hypothermia group and control group, respectively. Symptom onset–to–balloon time was slightly shorter in the hypothermia group compared with the control group, 2 hours 23 minutes and 2 hours 44 minutes, respectively $(P=0.03,$ ). Doorto-balloon time was slightly longer in the hypothermia group compared with the control group, 30 and 24 minutes, respectively $\phantom{0}^{\prime}P<0.001,$ ). This indicates that an additional 6 minutes was needed for the instrumentation required for the cooling procedure. In the intracoronary hypothermia group, symptom onset–to– balloon time and door-to-balloon time are taken up to the start of the first balloon inflation (ie, excluding the duration of the occlusion phase).  

SELECTIVE INTRACORONARY HYPOTHERMIA. Target temperature was achieved within 104 seconds (IQR 17-165 seconds) after start of infusion (Table 2). The duration of the occlusion phase was 8 minutes (IQR: 7-10 minutes). Median intracoronary temperature during this phase was $-6.47^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$ (IQR $-6.00^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$ to $-7.00^{\circ}\mathrm{C})$ compared with baseline, and the tympanically measured systemic temperature fell by $-0.20^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$ (IQR: $0.00^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$ to $-0.60^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$ ; $P=0.001_{\cdot}$ ). The total infused volume of saline at room temperature was $162.5\mathrm{mL}$ (IQR: 132.5 to $200.0\mathrm{mL}$ ) at a rate of $20\mathrm{mL/min}$ (IQR: 20 to $22\mathrm{mL}/\mathrm{min})$ . No change was observed in heart rate during the occlusion phase.

Heart rate did not change, but MAP increased slightly compared with baseline $\mathrm{91}\mathrm{mmHg}$ ; IQR: 70 to 110 mm Hg; $P=0.04$ ) (Figure 4).  

In the 15 patients in whom the CoolCell catheter was used, door-to-balloon time was shorter than in the 35 patients in whom the OTWB was used, 27 minutes (IQR: 23-30 minutes) versus 32 minutes (IQR: 26-36 minutes) $(P=0.03,$ ).  

SAFETY ENDPOINTS. All patients survived to hospital discharge (Table 3, Central Illustration). Ventricular fibrillation (VF) necessitating defibrillation occurred in 5 and 3 patients $(P=0.72)$ at some point during the procedure in the intracoronary hypothermia group and control group, respectively. In the intracoronary hypothermia group, 1 patient had VF before saline infusion began. In 2 patients VF occurred during the occlusion phase, and in 1 patient VF occurred during the reperfusion phase, immediately after balloon deflation (ie, directly after reperfusion). And 1 patient had VF 10 minutes after the hypothermia protocol was completed. In the control group, 2 patients had VF before reperfusion, and 1 patient had VF directly after reperfusion. In all but 1 patient in both groups, treatment with 1 direct current was sufficient to restore rhythm and circulation immediately. In 1 patient in each group, chest compressions were performed for a short duration of time in addition to direct-current shock therapy. No other resuscitation procedures were required. None of the patients in the intracoronary hypothermia group had atrial fibrillation, and 3 patients $(6\%)$ in the control group had atrial fibrillation $(P=0.24.$ ).  

There was no report of peripheral embolization, and none of the procedures were complicated by air emboli. Post-PCI TIMI flow grade 2 or 3 was observed in 50 and 47 patients $(P=0.08)$ in the intracoronary hypothermia group and control group, respectively. ST was observed in 2 and 0 patients $(P=0.50)$ in the intracoronary hypothermia group and control group, respectively. In the intracoronary hypothermia group, 1 patient had ST while still in the catheterization laboratory. In the second patient, ST occurred after 6 days.

# DISCUSSION  

The aim of the present study was to investigate the safety of selective intracoronary hypothermia during PPCI in patients with anterior STEMI, both periprocedurally and during the index admission. Our results do not raise specific concerns with respect to the safety of this novel procedure. Final safety results will be reported after the trial is completed.  

When evaluating the safety of any investigational treatment in patients with STEMI, whether interventional or related to drugs, the question always remains whether a particular complication is the result of the investigational therapy or the underlying disease (ie, anterior STEMI).  

In the intracoronary hypothermia group, no difference was observed in heart rate, but a slight increase in MAP during the cooling procedure compared with baseline was seen. The total volume of infused saline was $325.0\mathrm{mL}$ (IQR: 268.0-407.5 mL), which was infused in 18 minutes (IQR: 16-20 minutes). This fluid challenge, although much smaller than the total volume of saline used for systemic hypothermia (5), could be related to this increase in MAP. No patients had hypotension during the cooling procedure.  

Tympanically measured systemic temperature during the cooling procedure decreased minimally in comparison with baseline (occlusion phase, $-0.20^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$ $[P=0.001]$ ; reperfusion phase, $-0.10^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$ $[P=0.01]$ ).

In addition, pre-PCI TIMI flow grade 0 also increases the risk for VF, with an odds ratio of 1.65 $95\%$ confidence interval: 1.14-2.40) (8). This applies to the majority of the included patients in this safety study $(87\%)$ . Therefore, the incidence of VF in this study is not very high and in line with expectations. Remarkably, and in contrast to studies in AMI with systemic hypothermia (9), not a single case of atrial fibrillation was observed in the intracoronary hypothermia group, compared with 3 patients in the control group $(P=0.24)$ ).  

Definite ST occurred in 2 patients in the intracoronary hypothermia group and in none of the patients in the control group $(P~=~0.50)$ . Although studies have shown that hypothermia may promote platelet adhesion, activation, and ultimately aggregation, and may therefore increase the risk for ST, different circumstances under which hypothermia is achieved (systemic vs local, duration, depth) are indistinguishably associated with this prothrombotic phenomenon (10). We believe that selective intracoronary hypothermia does not increase the risk for ST, as is supported by this analysis. One patient had IPST. Although excluded from the official Academic Research Consortium definitions of ST, IPST is more prevalent in patients with STEMI and pre-PCI TIMI flow grade 0 or 1 (11), corresponding to the profile of all patients in this safety study. In 1 patient, ST occurred after 6 days because of interruption of dual-antiplatelet therapy after the patient had an intracranial hemorrhage for which urgent craniotomy and evacuation of hematoma surgery were required. The patient made a full neurologic recovery.  

Finally, none of the patients died during the procedure or the subsequent hospital admission.  

STUDY LIMITATIONS. The present report is an exploratory safety analysis and contains only predefined safety parameters, as mandated by the Institutional Review Board and DSMB. No outcome data were available yet to the investigators and authors of this paper. Furthermore, bias due to a small sample size cannot be excluded.  

Only patients with anterior STEMI and an occlusion of the proximal or mid LAD were investigated, and these data cannot be extrapolated to inferior STEMI, in which conduction disturbances are more common (5).  

Nevertheless, these results are encouraging, but more definite evidence regarding the safety of selective intracoronary hypothermia in patients with STEMI and effectiveness will be provided when the ongoing randomized controlled EURO-ICE study is completed.  

# CONCLUSIONS  

This first report on the safety of selective intracoronary hypothermia during PPCI in patients with anterior STEMI suggests that the procedure can be implemented safely during PPCI without increased risk for complications. The ongoing randomized controlled EURO-ICE study will demonstrate whether selective intracoronary hypothermia will reduce infarct size and improve clinical outcomes.
