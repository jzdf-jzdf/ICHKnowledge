# Adjuvant Tranexamic Acid for Reducing Postoperative Recurrence of Chronic Subdura Hematoma in the Elderly: A Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis  

Ibrahem Albalkhi1,2, Marwan Alaswad1, Tariq Saleh1, Abdulrahman Senjab1, Baraa Helal1, Jibran Ahmad Khan1 - BACKGROUND: Chronic subdural hematoma (CSDH) is a frequently encountered neurosurgical disease among the elderly. The mainstay treatment involves surgical evacuation, but recurrence rates of approximately $13\%$ pose complications. Adjuvant treatments, including tranexamic acid (TXA), have been explored, yet consensus on their efficacy and safety in elderly patients remains uncertain. The study aims to examine the role of TXA as adjunctive therapy in reducing CSDH recurrence and explore any potential association between TXA use and thrombotic events in this patient population.  

- METHODS: The systematic review and meta-analysis adhered to Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses guidelines and Cochrane Handbook standards, searching databases up to July 2023 for randomized controlled trials and propensity-matched cohorts evaluating adjuvant TXA. The primary outcome was CSDH recurrence, and the secondary outcome was thrombosis risk, measured as relative risks.  

- RESULTS: A total of 6 studies were included, comprising 1403 patients with CSDH who underwent surgical treatment. Four studies were randomized controlled trials, while the other 2 were propensity-matched cohorts. The overall pooled relative risk for CSDH recurrence in the TXA group compared to the control group was 0.41 $95\%$ confidence interval [0.29e0.59], $P<0.01$ ), indicating a significant reduction in recurrence with TXA treatment.  

- CONCLUSIONS: In conclusion, our study indicates that adjuvant TXA may help reduce CSDH recurrence in elderly patients undergoing surgical treatment. However, the study has limitations and there is a need for further research to validate these findings.  

# INTRODUCTION  

hronic subdural hematoma (CSDH) is one of the most frequently encountered neurosurgical diseases amongst the elderly,1,2 with an estimated annual incidence of $5^{-58}$   
cases per 100,000 people in the general population. Moreover,   
the incidence of CSDH is expected to increase due to population   
aging and the increased number of patients on blood thinners.5  

The mainstay treatment of CSDH is surgical evacuation of the hematoma by craniotomy, burr hole craniostomy, or twist-drill craniostomy.6 However; surgery is frequently complicated by recurrence,7 which not only necessitates reoperation, but also carries risks for decreased functional outcome and morbidity in patients undergoing reoperation.7 It is estimated that CSDH recurrence occurs in $\mathrm{I}3\%$ of patients who undergo surgical evacuation.  

Multiple drugs have been reported in the literature as nonsurgical or adjuvant treatment for CSDH, including tranexamic acid (TXA),9,10 dexamethasone,11 and statins.12 However, there is still no consensus on their efficacy and safety profile in the elderly.  

TXA is an antifibrinolytic agent that binds to plasminogen and blocks its interaction with fibrin, thus preventing the dissolution of the fibrin clot.13 To this date, no consensus has been reached on the role of TXA as adjunctive therapy after CSDH evacuation by surgery, and randomized clinical trials are ongoing to reveal the efficacy of TXA in CSDH.14 The aim of our study is to examine the role of TXA as adjunctive therapy in reducing the recurrence of CSDH and whether or not TXA predisposed patients to thrombotic events.

# MATERIALS AND METHODS  

# Protocol and Registration  

This systematic review and meta-analysis is formulated in accordance with the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses guidelines and the Cochrane Handbook for Systematic Reviews of Interventions. The protocol for this review has been registered (Registration ID: CRD42023442349) with the International prospective register of Systematic Reviews.  

# Search Strategy  

A comprehensive literature search, spanning from inception to July 2023, was performed across the following databases: PubMed, EMBASE, clinicaltrials.gov, Cochrane library, and Google Scholar. The following search terms, “TXA,” “recurrence,” and “CSDH ,” were combined using Boolean operators and truncation. There were no restrictions on publication dates or study designs. However, the search was limited to English-language publications or articles with available English translations.  

# Inclusion and Exclusion Criteria  

Two authors (BH and MA) independently screened the titles and abstracts of the identified studies. Full-text assessments followed for potentially eligible studies, along with manual screening of reference lists of these articles to identify other relevant studies. The lead author (IA) was involved to reach a consensus in case of disagreements regarding study selection.  

Included studies were randomized controlled trials or propensity-matched cohorts that administered adjuvant TXA and conducted a follow-up of at least 2 months posttreatment. Exclusions were made for conference data, duplicate records, studies deemed irrelevant to the research question, review papers, case reports, studies where TXA was used as the primary medical management, and study protocols without reported results.  

# Data Extraction  

Using a standardized data extraction form, data elements predetermined for their relevance were extracted from each included study, and entered into a structured dataset with the following parameters: study type, population size, gender, population age, recurrence rate, thrombosis rate, and follow-up. Two authors (BH and MA) independently conducted the data extraction to maintain the consistency of data.  

The primary outcome of this review was the recurrence of CSDH, measured in terms of relative risks and the secondary outcome was the risk of thrombosis, also quantified in terms of relative risks.  

# Quality Appraisal  

Two authors (BH and MA) independently assessed the risk of bias for each included study using the Cochrane’s ROB 2 Risk of Bias Tool. The overall quality of each study was categorized as good, fair, or poor based on the assessment. Any differences in ratings were resolved through discussion with a third independent author (IA), ensuring a consensus.  

All the studies had a low risk of bias except for 3 which had some concern. The studies performed well in all 5 domains (Supplementary Figure 1).  

# Statistical Analysis  

The statistical analysis was performed using R version 4.1.2 and the R studio program (RStudio, Inc., R Foundation for Statistical Computing, Vienna, Austria) using the default settings of the “meta” and “metabin” packages installed.15,16 The primary outcome, the relative risk of recurrence, and the secondary outcome, relative risk of thrombosis, were pooled using $95\%$ confidence intervals (CIs) and a random effects model. We conducted a subgroup analysis to explore the outcome based on the study type (randomized control trial and propensity-matched cohorts). Heterogeneity was assessed using the $\mathrm{I}^{2}$ test, and an $\mathrm{I}^{2}$ $<30\%$ was defined as low heterogeneity. $\tau^{2}$ was used to assess the between-study variance in our meta-analysis. All statistical tests were $^2$ -sided, and a $\mathrm{P}$ value $<0.05$ was considered statistically significant. Means are reported as mean $(95\%$ CI).  

Egger’s test was used to assess publication bias, and a P-value of less than 0.05 was considered significant.17 Furthermore, we used funnel plots to assess asymmetry.18 Notably, none of the tested endpoints exhibited publication bias according to Egger’s test. The funnel plot is illustrated in Supplementary Figure 2.  

# RESULTS  

# Search Results and Study Characteristics  

The initial search strategy yielded 412 articles across the 5 databases. The duplicate records $({\mathfrak{n}}={\mathfrak{n}})$ ) were then identified and removed. A conference paper $\bf(n\mathrm{~=~}\bf{I})$ was also identified and removed. The remaining 230 articles underwent title/abstract screening and were narrowed down to $64$ papers that underwent full-text screening. Studies that did not meet the inclusion criteria were excluded. Most of the excluded studies were unrelated to our study’s aim $(\mathfrak{n}=25)$ . Additionally, review papers $(\mathfrak{n}=\mathfrak{n})$ , case reports $({\mathfrak{n}}=\mathbf{\Gamma}_{\mathbf{I}0})$ , studies where TXA was used as the primary medical management $(\mathfrak{n}=6)$ ), and study protocols with no results $(\mathfrak{n}=6)$ were excluded. Finally, we included 6 studies in the systematic review.19-24 Figure 1 outlines the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses flow diagram of the study selection process.  

Four of the included studies were randomized control trials. $^{20-22,24}$ The remaining 2 studies were propensity-matched cohorts.19,23 All the included studies were recently published, encompassing the time frame from 2018 to 2023.  

# Baseline Characteristics  

Overall, our analysis included 1403 patients with CSDH who underwent surgical treatment. There were $654$ and 749 patients in the TXA and control groups, respectively. The mean age of the TXA group was 75.0 $(95\%$ CI $\left[71.0{-}7^{8.9}\right])$ , while the control group had a mean age of 74.9 $(95\%$ CI $[72.4^{-}77.5],$ ). The median follow-up ranged from 2 to 10 months.  

# Primary Outcome: Recurrence  

Among the included studies, 4 were randomized controlled trials, and the pooled relative risk for recurrence in this subgroup was $_{0.6\mathrm{I}}$ $(95\%$ CI $\scriptstyle\left[0.23-\mathrm{{I}}.62\right],$ ). Additionally, the remaining 2 studies utilized propensity-matched cohorts, yielding a pooled relative risk of $0.38$ $(95\%$ CI $\big[0.26-0.57\big],$ . Combining all 6 studies, the overall pooled relative risk demonstrated a significant reduction in the recurrence of CSDH in the TXA group compared to the control group, with a value of 0.41 $(95\%$ CI $\left[0.29^{-0.59}\right]$ , $\begin{array}{r}{\mathrm{P}<0.0\mathrm{I},}\end{array}$ ). Figure 2 illustrates the forest plot highlighting the pooled overall outcomes and outcomes based on the study type.

# DISCUSSION  

# Summary of Results  

There are 2 main results for this study: (1) the postoperative recurrence rate of CSDH in patients treated with surgical evacuation of the hematoma plus TXA as adjunctive therapy was $5.8\%$ , which is significantly lower than that of the control group at $\mathtt{I}3.6\%$ ; (2) the use of TXA was not associated with a higher risk of thrombosis $\mathrm{\Delta(P=o.}40\mathrm{)}$ ). Our study provides insights into the potential role of TXA as an adjuvant to surgical management of  

CSDH, while also underscoring the insignificant change in thrombosis rate with this therapy.  

# Recurrence  

The meta-analysis of surgically managed patients revealed an overall recurrence rate of $\mathtt{I}3.6\%$ , aligning closely with the findings of a large-scale study conducted in Japan involving over 60,000 participants, which reported a recurrence rate of $\mathbf{I}3.\mathbf{I}^{0}/\mathbf{0}$ .8 In a prospective study, Yamada et al. examined the efficacy of orally administered TXA in the prevention of CSDH recurrence postoperatively. They found that while the administration of TXA as an adjunct did not reduce the recurrence rate of CSDH, it did reduce the residual hematoma volume.22 Moreover, in another retrospective study on the role of TXA after surgical evacuation, the authors discovered a reduction in residual hematoma volume by $9\mathtt{I}.3\%$ in patients who received oral TXA, with none of the patients developing delayed recurrence or expansion of the CSDH.25 On the contrary, a multicenter prospective randomized controlled trial examined the effects TXA adjunct to surgery for the treatment of CSDH and showed no preventative effect of TXA. However, this randomized control trial had many limitations, including failing to recruit a large pool of participants and more than $30\%$ of the recruited participants being lost to follow-up. The nonsignificant results observed in these trials could potentially be attributed to the limited number of participants in the study arm, which may have impacted the statistical significance of the findings. Consequently, a more substantial relationship might have been established if the studies had a larger population size.26

Several factors can elevate the likelihood of CSDH recurrence following the primary surgical evacuation, including postoperative residual hematoma volume, the use of anticoagulants, greater preoperative midline shift, and lack of intraoperative irrigation.27  

In a retrospective study on 208 patients by Ridwan et al., they examined the factors that predict recurrence in CSDH. They concluded that imaging parameters are the most important predictors of recurrence and cure.28 Postoperative CSDH volume, along with the anatomy and composition of CSDH, proved to be the most important imaging parameters in predicting the recurrence and cure of CSDH.28 Future studies should consider reporting outcomes based on the anatomical distribution of CSDH. Various subtypes of CSDH can be differentiated via the Nakaguchi classification which can help in unifying the anatomical classification.29 Stanisic and Pripp proposed a grading system for the prediction of postoperative recurrence requiring reoperation, called the Oslo CSDH Grading System.30 This grading system was based on factors that can be objectively assessed at the time of first presentation and initial surgical treatment, including 3 main components: (1) density changes and appearance on computed tomography scan, (2) preoperative volume, and (3) postoperative volume.

Numerous other treatment modalities have also been proposed in the literature. Multiple studies, including clinical trials, have studied the use of dexamethasone, a glucocorticoid generally utilized for its anti-inflammatory properties. A randomized clinical trial conducted in the United Kingdom administered oral dexamethasone for 2 weeks and measured their outcomes on the modified Rankin scale. They discovered a lowered incidence of repeat operations, although with decreased positive outcomes and increased adverse events compared to placebo after 6 months.11 Middle meningeal artery embolization has also been proposed as a treatment option, proving to be an appropriate minimally invasive choice relative to surgical management.31,32 A systematic review and meta-analysis by Dian et al. described a $20\%$ reduction in recurrence, though this was concluded from limited data.31 Atorvastatin has also been referenced,12,33 however, concerns with the use of high doses arise due to potential drug interactions through CYP3A4.34 Hence TXA, due to its costeffectiveness, safety, and tolerability, emerges as a standout option.34,35 This makes TXA a prime candidate for preventing the recurrence of CSDH in elderly patients.  

# Thrombosis  

The secondary endpoint in our study was to examine if the administration of TXA as an adjunct therapy increases the risk of thrombotic events in patients with CSDH. Only 2 studies included in our review reported the occurrence of thrombosis in patients receiving TXA as an adjunct,19,20 however; our analysis did not reveal any association between TXA and the risk of thrombosis. Yamakawa et al. conducted a meta-analysis on the effects of TXA on thrombotic events in bleeding patients and found no evidence linking TXA to an increased risk of thrombotic events.36 Moreover, another meta-analysis by Mekary et al. concluded that TXA was not associated with an increased risk of thrombotic events.37  

# Limitations  

Although the results of our study all showed promising results for the role of TXA as an adjunctive therapy to surgical evaluation of CSDH, yet large, multicenter randomized control trials are required before any consensus could be reached regarding the role of TXA as adjunct in the management of CSDH. One notable limitation of the study is the relatively limited follow-up duration in some of the included studies. The duration of follow-up varied among the trials, and some studies had follow-up periods of less than 6 months. This restricted follow-up timeframe may not fully capture the long-term outcomes and potential late effects of adjuvant TXA treatment on CSDH recurrence. Furthermore, the significant weight given to 1 propensity-matched cohort study in the analysis may have influenced the overall results and introduced potential bias, impacting the generalizability of our findings. The influence of this study on the results remains a concern and should be considered when interpreting the implications of our study. Finally, the study’s results may be affected by various factors such as the pattern of CSDH, the patient’s previous history of CSDH, and existing comorbidities. However, it is important to note that a subgroup analysis was not feasible because the outcomes based on these factors were not adequately reported.  

# CONCLUSION  

In conclusion, our study suggests that adjuvant TXA may be beneficial in reducing CSDH recurrence in elderly patients undergoing surgical treatment. Sufficient literature is required to fully evaluate TXA’s potential therapeutic benefits and establish its use in clinical settings.
