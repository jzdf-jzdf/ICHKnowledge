# Cilostazol Administration for Subarachnoid Hemorrhage: A Meta-analysis of Randomized Controlled Trials  

Hongshen Chen, PhD,\* Wenqing Luo, PhD,† Xianfu Cai, PhD,\* and Jing Cai, PhD\*  

Introduction: The efficacy of cilostazol administration to treat subarachnoid hemorrhage remains controversial. We conduct a systematic review and meta-analysis to explore the influence of cilostazol administration on treatment efficacy for subarachnoid hemorrhage.  

Methods: We have searched PubMed, Embase, Web of science, EBSCO, and Cochrane Library databases through July 2020 for randomized controlled trials assessing the effect of cilostazol administration in patients with subarachnoid hemorrhage. This meta-analysis is performed using the random-effect model.  

Results: Four randomized controlled trials involving 405 patients were included in the meta-analysis. Overall, compared with control group for subarachnoid hemorrhage, cilostazol intervention can significantly reduce symptomatic vasospasm (odds ratio [OR], 0.35; $95\%$ confidence interval [CI], 0.21–0.60; $P=0.0001.$ ) and cerebral infarction (OR, 0.40; $95\%$ CI, 0.22–0.73; $P=0.003\$ ) and improve no or mild angiographic vasospasm (OR, 2.01; $95\%$ CI, 1.19–3.42; $P=0.01$ ) and an mRS score of 2 or less (OR, 2.70; $95\%$ CI, 1.09–6.71; $P=0.03$ ), but revealed no obvious influence on severe angiographic vasospasm (OR, 0.53; $95\%$ CI, $0.27{-}1.02$ $P=0.06,$ ). There were no increase in adverse events (OR, 1.17; $95\%$ CI, $0.54-2.52$ ; $P=0.69,$ ), hemorrhagic events (OR, 0.62; $95\%$ CI, 0.06–6.27; $P=0.69)$ , and cardiac events (OR, 2.14; $95\%$ CI, $0.44–10.27$ ; $P=0.34,$ after the cilostazol intervention than control intervention.  

Conclusions: Cilostazol treatment may be effective to treat subarachnoid hemorrhage in the terms of symptomatic vasospasm, cerebral infarction, no or mild angiographic vasospasm, and an mRS score of 2 or less.  

Key Words: cilostazol, subarachnoid hemorrhage, vasospasm, randomized controlled trials  

(Clin Neuropharm 2022;45: 111–116)  

S ubarachnoid hemorrhage is a life-threatening disea1s–e4  and mainly caused by the rupture of cerebral aneurysms. Delayed cerebral ischemia (DCI) caused by cerebral vasospasm has become one of the most important causes of mortality and morbidity in these patients. Many factors result in the incidence of DCI and include persistent luminal narrowing of the major cerebral arteries, microcirculatory disturbance, early brain injury, and cortical spreading depression. Many drugs and treatments have been develop to alleviate cerebral vasospasm and prevent DCI.8–11  

Cilostazol, a selective inhibitor of phospho-diesterase 3, were reported as an efficacious drug for the prevention of cerebral vasospasm in experimental studies.12,13 Cilostazol has been widely used for arteriosclerosis obliterans, the intermittent claudication, and the prevention of second ischemic stroke.14,15 The potential of cilostazol in preventing cerebral vasospasm relies on several mechanisms including antiplatelet and vasodilating effect, inhibition of reactive oxygen species, inhibition of apoptosis, antiinflammation, antilipid peroxidation, and induction of nitric oxide synthesis.13,16–19  

Several studies have explored the efficacy of cilostazol to prevent cerebral vasospasm in subarachnoid hemorrhage, but the results have been conflicting.20–22 With accumulating evidence, we therefore perform a systematic review and meta-analysis of RCTs to investigate the efficacy of cilostazol to prevent cerebral vasospasm in patients with subarachnoid hemorrhage.  

# MATERIALS AND METHODS  

Ethical approval and patient consent were not required because this was a systematic review and meta-analysis of previously published studies. The systematic review and meta-analysis were conducted and reported in adherence to PRISMA (Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses).23  

# Search Strategy and Study Selection  

Two investigators have independently searched the following databases (inception to July 2020): PubMed, Embase, Web of science, EBSCO, and Cochrane Library databases. The electronic search strategy was conducted using the following key words: cilostazol and subarachnoid hemorrhage. We also check the reference lists of the screened full-text studies to identify other potentially eligible trials.  

The inclusive selection criteria were as follows: (1) patients were diagnosed with subarachnoid hemorrhage; (2) intervention treatments were cilostazol administration versus placebo or usual care; and (3) study design was RCT.  

# Data Extraction and Outcome Measures  

We have extracted the following information: author, number of patients, age, male, hypertension, modified Rankin Scale (mRS) score, and detail methods in each group etc. Data have been extracted independently by 2 investigators, and discrepancies are resolved by consensus. We also contact the corresponding author to obtain the data when necessary.  

The primary outcome was symptomatic vasospasm. Secondary outcomes included no or mild angiographic vasospasm, severe angiographic vasospasm, cerebral infarction, an mRS score of 2 or less, adverse events, hemorrhagic events, and cardiac events.  

# Quality Assessment in Individual Studies  

Methodological quality of the included studies was independently evaluated using the modified Jadad scale.24 There were 3 items for Jadad scale: randomization (0–2 points), blinding (0–2 points), and dropouts and withdrawals (0–1 points). The score of Jadad Scale varied from 0 to 5 points. An article with Jadad score of 2 or less was considered to be of low quality. If the Jadad score of 3 or higher, the study was thought to have high quality.25

# Statistical Analysis  

We estimate the odds ratio (OR) with $95\%$ confidence intervals (CIs) for dichotomous outcomes (symptomatic vasospasm, no or mild angiographic vasospasm, severe angiographic vasospasm, cerebral infarction, an mRS score of 2 or less, adverse events, hemorrhagic events, and cardiac events). The random-effects model was used regardless of heterogeneity. Heterogeneity was reported using the $\scriptstyle{\vec{I}}^{2}$ statistic, and $\tilde{I^{2}}>50\%$ indicated significant heterogeneity.26 Whenever significant heterogeneity was present, we searched for potential sources of heterogeneity via omitting one study in turn for the meta-analysis or performing subgroup analysis. Publication bias was not evaluated because of the limited number $(<10)$ of included studies. All statistical analyses were performed using Review Manager Version 5.3 (the Cochrane Collaboration, Software Update, Oxford, United Kingdom).

# RESULTS  

# Literature Search, Study Characteristics, and Quality Assessment  

A detailed flowchart of the search and selection results was shown in Figure 1. Four hundred three potentially relevant articles were identified initially. Finally, 4 RCTs that met our inclusion criteria were included in the meta-analysis.20–22,27  

The baseline characteristics of the 4 eligible RCTs in the meta-analysis were summarized in Table 1. The 4 studies were published between 2011 and 2018, and the total sample size was 405. Cilostazol was administered at the dose of $100~\mathrm{mg}$ twice daily, and the treatment duration was 12 or 14 days.  

Among the 4 studies included here, 3 studies reported symptomatic vasospasm ,21,22,27 3 studies reported no or mild angiographic vasospasm, and severe angiographic vasospasm, $20\ –22\bigtriangledown_{4}$ studies reported cerebral infarction, $\not{2}0\not{p}\-\not{2}2,\dot{2}7}\:2$ studies reported an mRS score of 2 or less,22,27 and 2 studies reported adverse events, hemorrhagic events, and cardiac events.21,22 Jadad scores of the 4 included studies varied from 3 to 5, and all 4 studies were considered to be high-quality ones according to quality assessment.  

# Primary Outcome: Symptomatic Vasospasm  

These outcome data were analyzed with the random-effects model, and the pooled estimate of the 3 included RCTs suggested that compared with control group for subarachnoid hemorrhage, cilostazol demonstrates was associated with significantly reduced symptomatic vasospasm (OR, 0.35; $95\%$ CI, $0.21\substack{-0.60}$ ; $P=0.0001$ ) with no heterogeneity among the studies $(I^{2}=0\%$ , heterogeneity $P=0.49$ ; Fig. 2).  

# Sensitivity Analysis  

No heterogeneity is observed among the included studies for the primary outcomes, and thus, we do not perform sensitivity analysis via omitting one study in turn to detect the heterogeneity.  

# Secondary Outcomes  

Compared with control group for patients with subarachnoid hemorrhage, cilostazol intervention revealed substantial improvement in no or mild angiographic vasospasm (OR, 2.01; $95\%$ CI, 1.19–3.42; $P=0.01$ ; Fig. 3) but had no obvious impact on severe angiographic vasospasm (OR, 0.53; $95\%$ CI, 0.27–1.02; $P=0.06$ ; Fig. 4). In addition, cilostazol intervention benefited to decrease cerebral infarction (OR, $0.40;95\%$ CI, 0.22–0.73; $P{=}0.003$ ; Fig. 5) and improve an mRS score of 2 or less (OR, 2.70; $95\%$ CI, 1.09–6.71; $P=0.03$ ; Fig. 6). Adverse events (OR, 1.17; $95\%$ CI, 0.54–2.52; $P=0.69$ ; Fig. 7), hemorrhagic events (OR, 0.62; $95\%$ CI, 0.06–6.27; $P=0.69$ ; Fig. 8), and cardiac events (OR, 2.14; $95\%$ CI, 0.44–10.27; $P=0.34$ ; Fig. 9) were found to be similar between 2 groups.

# DISCUSSION  

The antiplatelet drug cilostazol was hypothesized to decrease angiographic vasospasm, DCI, and poor outcome in patients with subarachnoid hemorrhage.28 However, another RCT revealed that cilostazol did not reduce angiographic vasospasm but reduced DCI and poor outcomes in these patients.21 These indicated that cilostazol had pleiotropic effects on the reduction in DCI with regard to the relief of angiographic vasospasm, improvement of the microcirculation,27,29 suppression of spreading depolarization through a mechanism of microcirculatory vasodilatation, mediated by both cyclic adenosine monophosphate, and upregulation of endothelial nitric oxide synthesis.  

Several clinical studies investigated the efficacy of cilostazol on the prevention of cerebral vasospasm for subarachnoid hemorrhag e.22,27,30 Their results were not necessarily been consistent. Our meta-analysis included 4 RCTs and 405 patients. The main findings of this meta-analysis concluded that cilostazol was associated with significantly reduced symptomatic vasospasm and cerebral infarction and improved no or mild angiographic vasospasm and an mRS score of 2 or less as compared with control intervention in patients with subarachnoid hemorrhage. There were similar incidence of adverse events, hemorrhagic events, and cardiac events between 2 groups.  

Although there was no significant heterogeneity for primary outcome, several factors may produce some bias. First, basic management of subarachnoid hemorrhage were not standardized between groups, and they involved sodium ozagrel, calcium channel blockers, and/or statin. Second, the administration of cilostazol was initiated in different timing after subarachnoid hemorrhage, such as 48 hours, 96 hours, or no clear description after the onset of subarachnoid hemorrhage. Third, the treatment durations were 12 days, 14 days, or no clear description, which may affect the pooling results. Fourth, end-point assessment was not performed by double-blind evaluation in some studies, which led to some assessment bias.

This meta-analysis has several potential limitations that should be taken into account. First, our analysis is based on only 4 RCTs, and more RCTs with large samples should be conducted to confirm this issue. Next, the initiation time, duration, and methods of cilostazol intervention in included RCTs are different, which may have an influence on the pooling results. Finally, the analysis of some important outcomes, such mortality, cannot be performed in this meta-analysis because of the limited and included RCTs.  

# CONCLUSIONS  

Cilostazol intervention may provide additional benefits to the treatment of subarachnoid hemorrhage.
