# ABSTRACT  

Introduction  Intracerebral haemorrhage (ICH) stands as a leading global cause of death and disability, posing a significant challenge with limited treatment options, especially for chronic patients. Recent advances in stem cell therapies have opened new avenues for therapeutic potential. Our previous preclinical research has demonstrated that intracerebral transplantation of bone marrow stromal cells (BMSCs) combined with a recombinant human collagen type I scaffold enables higher cell survival and engraftment and holds promising potential. In this article, we present the protocol for a novel clinical trial, named ‘Research on Advanced Intervention using Novel Bone MarrOW stem cells for chronic intracerebral haemorrhage' (RAINBOW-Hx). Methods and analysis RAINBOW-Hx is a phase I/ll, open-label, uncontrolled study with the primary objective of assessing the safety and efficacy of intracerebral transplantation of autologous BMSCs combined with the scaffold (HUFF-01) in patients with chronic ICH. Eight patients, experiencing moderate to severe neurological deficits for 12 months or longer, will be enrolled. The haemorrhage location will be limited to the basal ganglia and thalamus. Approximately 50 mL of bone marrow will be extracted from the iliac bone of each patient, and BMSCs will be cultured using autologous platelet lysate. 3 days before transplantation, BMSCs will be combined with the scaffold to generate HUFF-01. Each patient will receive a 50 000 HUFF-01 dose, containing approximately 50 million BMSCs, through stereotactic transplantation into the haemorrhagic cavity. # INTRODUCTION  

Stroke, characterised by sudden occlusion (ischaemic stroke) or rupture (haemorrhagic stroke) of blood vessels, leading to significant brain damage, ranks as the second leading cause of global mortality and the primary cause of disability. Ischaemic stroke constitutes the majority of stroke cases $(75\%)$ compared with haemorrhagic stroke $(10\%)$ , yet the latter exhibits notably higher mortality $(\approx15\%)$ and disability rates $(\approx55\%)$ . While therapies like tissue plasminogen activator and thrombectomy have markedly improved the prognosis of ischaemic stroke,4 effective treatments for haemorrhagic stroke remain elusive.  

Although haematoma evacuation is frequently employed in the acute treatment of intracerebral haemorrhage (ICH), it is reported to be effective as a life-saving' measure rather than a means of achieving ‘functional improvement’.5– 7 Patients with ICH typically participate in rehabilitation following the acute phase, but their recovery often plateaus within the initial months.8 Thus, the functional recovery rate associated with ICH has shown little improvement over the past decades.3 9 Consequently, the development of innovative treatment strategies aimed at minimising or reversing neurological sequelae takes on paramount importance.  

Cell therapy holds significant promise for enhancing functional recovery in patients with neurological diseases. Stem cells exhibit a range of effects on neurological recovery, encompassing cell differentiation, immunomodulation, anti-inflammation and the stimulation of endogenous repair processes, including angiogenesis and neurogenesis.10 11 Prior clinical trials, with a primary focus on ischaemic stroke and traumatic brain injury, have yielded promising results.12–15 However, research on stem cell therapies for ICH is notably limited.16 Recently, we initiated a clinical trial for subacute ischaemic stroke, known as the Research on Advanced Intervention using Novel Bone MarrOW stem cells (RAINBOW) trial.17 Building on the techniques refined during this clinical trial, we have developed a product in which the mesenchymal stem cells (MSCs) are combined with a recombinant peptide (RCP) scaffold. Preclinical research has demonstrated that this product can maximise the therapeutic potential when transplanted into the haemorrhagic cavity of chronic ICH. Furthermore, we observed that transplanted cells were located in the vicinity of the haemorrhagic glial scar, indicating their potential to migrate or adapt within the hostile environment.18 Based on these circumstances and the development of our new product, we embarked on a phase I/Il, open-label, uncontrolled clinical trial, named the RAINBOW for chronic intracerebral haemorrhage (RAINBOW-­Hx).

# Study design  

This trial is a prospective, open-label, single-centre, uncontrolled and phase $\mathrm{I/II}$ study. The trial flow is illustrated in figure  1. The defined dose of the product is $5{\times}10^{4}$ HUFF-01, consisting of $5{\times}10^{8}$ MSCs. This study is conducted at the Hokkaido University Hospital, located in Sapporo, Japan. The key elements of the WHO trial registration data set are described in table 1, and the full description is described in the online supplemental file 2. The study will start patient entry on 17 January 2024. The end of the study is planned for February 2027.  

# Study population

After obtaining written consent from the patients, a detailed eligibility assessment will be performed to determine whether the patient fulfilled all the criteria (box 1).

In the cell processing centre (CPC), PCs were transferred into the bags for cryopreservation (F100, Nipro, Osaka, Japan), and they were stored in the freezer at $-150\circ\mathrm{C}$ . Ten culture dishes are prepared to obtain $5{\times}10^{5}$ HUFF-01. On the day of surgery, the supernatant is decanted and the HUFF-01 is gently resuspended in Artcereb (irrigation and perfusion solution used for cerebrospinal surgery; Otsuka Pharmaceutical Factory, Inc., Naruto, Japan) to a concentration of $2.5{\times}10^{4}/\mathrm{mL}$ for $2\mathrm{mL}$ , which consists of $5{\times}10^{7}$ BMSCs.  

# Characteristics of HUFF-01  

The phenotypes of BMSCs are assessed before coculturing with the RCP m-piece, confirming their positivity for CD44, CD90, CD105 and CD166 and negativity for CD45. The expression of markers is consistent with the mesenchymal stromal cell phenotype according to the position statement of the International Society for Cellular Therapy. 22 Four consecutive microbiological assessments were conducted to ascertain the absence of infection in the cell product. Sterility tests, mycoplasma tests and bacterial endotoxin tests were executed under Good Manufacturing Practice (GMP) standards for platelet lysate, aspirated bone marrow, the intermediate product of stem cells just before coculturing with the RCP m-piece and the final product. All results, except for the final product, were obtained before the shipment of the product. RCP m-piece is produced by the yeast Pichia pastoris under GMP conditions. To ensure the proper manufacturing of HUFF-01, the size of the HUFF-O1 will be assessed, with more than $90\%$ of its diameter falling within the range of $100{-}500\mathrm{mm}$ .  

# Intervention  

HUFF-Ol will be administered to the subject on the day when it is manufactured, without cryopreservation. HUFF-01 is transplanted into the haemorrhagic cavity using the MRI stereotactic technique (StealthStation Neuronavigation System, Medtronic, Dublin, Ireland) as previously described with minor modification.20 The subject will undergo one burr-hole craniotomy under local anaesthesia and sedation. Using a $1.5\mathrm{mm}$ outer diameter stereotactic cannula $\mathrm{{(1.1mm}}$ inner diameter, Injection probe H, Mizuho, Tokyo, Japan, Class I medical device), $2\mathrm{mL}$ of the product will be injected over a period of $5\mathrm{min}$ . The cannula will be removed $5\mathrm{min}$ after completing the injection.

# Safety  

Treatment-emergent adverse events (TEAEs） were defined as any event not present before the initiation of the bone marrow harvest procedure or any event already present that worsened in either intensity or frequency following exposure to the bone marrow harvest or surgical procedure. TEAEs were graded using the Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events (version 5.0-­ JCOG) as follows: (1) mild, (2) moderate, (3) severe, (4) life-threatening or (5) death. The relationship between TEAEs and procedures was evaluated as follows. (a) Unrelated; no temporal relationship to cell treatment or surgical procedure, the presence of a reasonable causal relationship to another drug, concurrent disease or circumstance and the TEAE. (b) Unlikely; a temporal relationship to cell treatment or surgical procedure, but no reasonable causal relationship between the cell treatment or surgical procedure and the TEAE. (c) Possibly; a reasonable causal relationship between the cell treatment or surgical procedure and the TEAE. Information related to the withdrawal of cell treatment or surgical procedure was lacking or unclear. (d) Probably; a reasonable causal relationship between the cell treatment or surgical procedure and the TEAE. The event responded to withdrawal of cell treatment or surgical procedure. (e) Definitely; a reasonable causal relationship between the cell treatment or surgical procedure and the TEAE. The event responded to the withdrawal of cell treatment or surgical procedure.  

An independent Data and Safety Monitoring Board (DSMB) was established to oversee the safety aspects of the study. Board members were selected in accordance with the Japanese guidelines of Good Clinical Practice (GCP). The DSMB convened 7 days after the transplantation procedure for the first, second, third, fifth and eighth patients. Recruitment of new patients was permitted after obtaining DSMB approval.  

# Sample size and statistical analysis  

Based on data from previously reported intracerebral stem cell transplantation studies, which have reported adverse event rates ranging from 13% to 33%,13 20 23–25 a total of eight subjects will suffice to validate the safety of HUFF-01. The planned transplantation for each subject consists of 50 000 HUFF-01, containing approximately $5{\times}10^{7}$ BMSCs. However, approval has been granted for the use of 20 000 HUFF-01 in the event of unexpectedly low cell culture. Continuous data will be presented as means and SD, while categorical data will be presented as absolute frequencies or relative percentages.

# DISCUSSION  

The RAINBOW-­Hx trial aims to assess the safety and efficacy of intracerebral transplantation of stem cells combined with a scaffold. To the best of our knowledge, this trial is pioneering, being the first clinical trial to investigate intracerebral transplantation of cell combination products for patients with chronic brain haemorrhage.16 Several innovative approaches have been implemented to optimise the safety and efficacy of the treatment.  

Subject screening: In this trial, patients with ICH for 12 months or longer since the onset of ICH will be included. Given that natural recovery after stroke is reported to be limited to 6 months from onset,26–28 our inclusion criterion ensures the enrolment of patients with stable neurological deficits. However, the placebo effect should not be underestimated as this study lacks a control group. The haemorrhagic location is restricted to the unilateral basal ganglia and thalamus, comprising the majority of haemorrhages (approximately $70\%$ ).29 30 This selection offers the advantage of a standardised transplantation procedure by interpreting the treatment results. To prevent ectopic cell migration and engraftment, patients with evident communication between the cavity and cerebral ventricle are excluded. Patients with moderate to severe hemiparesis are eligible, exhibiting an mRS of 3 or 4 and Brunnstrom stage III or IV. This inclusion criterion ensures the enrolment of patients facing daily life challenges and who can potentially derive significant therapeutic benefits from the intervention.  

Safety and efficacy of the product: In this study, BMSC and platelet lysate are obtained from the patient itself, facilitating reduced immune rejection. Our group has obtained the data that autologous BMSC was able to be tracked for as long as 5 years from the transplantation in the previous clinical trial, and continuous recovery was observed even after 6 months from cell transplantation.17 A non-human primate study demonstrated that histocompatibility complex matching (HMC)-­matched cells showed lower local immune response and better graft survival rates after brain transplantation compared with unmatched HMC allogeneic cells.31 Since autologous cells show fully matched HMC, consequently better cell survival and engraftment are expected. Our preclinical study also demonstrated that HUFF-01 showed better cell engraftment in the brain and other organs.18 32 33 The scaffold employed in this trial consists of the alpha-1 sequence of human collagen type I, featuring 12 RGD (Arg-Gly-Asp) motifs in a single molecule, thereby ensuring robust cell adhesion .32–34 Stem cells can bind to this product through integrin, providing a stabilising effect and fostering the production of higher amounts of trophic factors.18  

# Safety issue for intracerebral transplantation  

Intracerebral transplantation carries the potential risk of inducing additional brain haemorrhage, which is reported to occur in $5{-}15\%$ of patients during needle insertion.15 In this study, we employ a specially designed needle offering distinct advantages. First, the cannula’s rounded-tip configuration with the mouse on the lateral wall minimises the risk of vessel injury. Second, the open mouth of the needle is sectorally designed to prevent the occurrence of jet flow, which has the potential to cause additional brain damage. Needle clogging may occur due to the adhesive nature of the cell product, necessitating the application of higher pressure to pass the sample through the needle. The shape will prevent in case of unexpected clogging or narrowing of the internal lumen.
