# ORIGINAL RESEARCH  

# Efficacy and Safety of Intravenous Tenecteplase Versus Alteplase in Treating Acute Ischemic Stroke With Diabetes and Admission Hyperglycemia  

Huihui Liu $\circledcirc$ , MD, PhD\*; Aoming Jin $\circledcirc$ , PhD\*; Yuesong Pan $\circledcirc$ , PhD; Xia Meng $\circledcirc$ , MD, PhD; Hao Li, PhD; Zixiao Li $\circledcirc$ , MD, PhD; Yongjun Wang $\circledcirc$ , MD; Shuya Li $\circledcirc$ , MD, PhD  

BACKGROUND: The aim of this study was to investigate the efficacy and safety of tenecteplase versus alteplase in patients with acute ischemic stroke, considering their diabetes history and admission hyperglycemia status.  

METHODS AND RESULTS: This was a post hoc analysis of the TRACE-2 (Tenecteplase Reperfusion Therapy in Acute Ischemic Cerebrovascular Events-2) randomized clinical trial that enrolled patients in China between June 2021 and May 2022. Eligible patients with acute ischemic stroke for standard intravenous thrombolysis, but ineligible for endovascular thrombectomy, were randomly assigned (1:1) to tenecteplase or alteplase within 4.5 hours of symptom onset. Admission hyperglycemia was defined as plasma glucose $>7.8\mathrm{mmol/L}$ . The primary efficacy and safety outcome were excellent functional outcome at 90 days (modified Rankin Scale score of $0{-}1$ ) and symptomatic intracranial hemorrhage within 36 hours, respectively. The Cochran–Mantel–Haenszel $\chi^{2}$ test was used for the outcomes. Of the 1382 patients included, 369 $(26.7\%)$ had a history of diabetes, and 482 $(34.9\%)$ experienced admission hyperglycemia. The primary efficacy outcome, comparing tenecteplase to alteplase, was achieved in 93 $(56.7\%)$ versus 97 $(48.3\%)$ among patients with a history of diabetes $(P{=}0.11^{\cdot}$ ) and 335 $(64.6\%)$ versus 300 $(62.2\%)$ among those without diabetes $(P{=}0.45)$ , respectively. The primary efficacy outcome for tenecteplase versus alteplase was comparable among patients with and without admission hyperglycemia $(57.5\%$ versus $53.9\%$ , $P{=}0.44$ ; $65.4\%$ versus $60.4\%$ , $\scriptstyleP=0.12$ , respectively). No significant difference in the risk of symptomatic intracranial hemorrhage within 36 hours was observed between tenecteplase and alteplase, regardless of diabetes history or admission hyperglycemia.  

CONCLUSIONS: This study demonstrated that intravenous tenecteplase exhibits similar clinical outcomes compared with alteplase, irrespective of the patient’s glucose metabolism status.

# CLINICAL PERSPECTIVE  

# What Is New?  

The study provides new insights on the comparable efficacy of $0.25~\mathsf{mg}/\mathsf{kg}$ of intravenous tenecteplase and the standard dose of alteplase in achieving excellent functional outcomes in acute ischemic stroke patients, regardless of their glucose metabolism status. Notably, the benefits of tenecteplase treatment were consistent among both patients with diabetes and patients without diabetes, as well as those with or without admission hyperglycemia, when compared with alteplase.  

# What Are the Clinical Implications?  

Clinically, these findings suggest that tenecteplase could be considered as a viable firstchoice thrombolysis option for patients with acute ischemic stroke without the need to differentiate on the basis of glucose metabolism status.   
This has important implications for simplifying treatment protocols and potentially improving overall outcomes for patients with stroke by offering an effective alternative to alteplase that is equally safe and may be administered more swiftly.

Interestingly, while studies have not found an overall reduction in the benefits of tissue plasminogen activator in patients with diabetes,6 there have been observed reductions in the rates of tissue plasminogen activator administrations among patients with diabetes.7 Additionally, admission hyperglycemia is common in patients with acute ischemic stroke (AIS), affecting ${\approx}30\%$ to $40\%$ of patients, even without a prior diabetes diagnosis.8 Elevated blood glucose levels at stroke onset increase the risk of hemorrhagic transformation when treated with tissue plasminogen activator, leading to poor clinical outcomes, extended hospital stays, and higher mortality rates.9 Consequently, admission glucose levels have been used in prognostic models to identify patients at risk of sICH after alteplase treatment.10  

Currently, alteplase remains the sole approved fibrinolytic drug for AIS in China and involves a bolus dose followed by a 1-hour infusion. In contrast, tenecteplase, a genetically modified alteplase variant, possesses a longer half-life, increased fibrin specificity, and heightened resistance to plasminogen activator inhibitor 1. Notably, tenecteplase requires less preparation time and can be administered more rapidly as a bolus.11 The practical advantages, coupled with its promising safety profile, have generated significant interest in potentially replacing alteplase for AIS treatment.12 Multiple trials have explored the optimal tenecteplase dosage, indicating that a dose of 0.25 mg/kg (up to $25~\mathrm{mg},$ may lead to improved early neurological outcomes, higher reperfusion rates in patients undergoing thrombectomy, and potentially improved 90-day outcomes compared with alteplase.13–17 Moreover, a recent phase 3 clinical trial, AcT (Alteplase Compared to Tenecteplase), showed that tenecteplase $(0.25\mathrm{{mg}/kg})$ was noninferior to alteplase $(0.9\mathrm{mg/kg})$ in achieving excellent functional outcomes at 90 days.18 Subsequently, the TRACE-2 (Tenecteplase Reperfusion Therapy in Acute Ischemic  Cerebrovascular  Events-2) trial  conducted in China yielded similar findings.19 However, it remains unclear whether the noninferiority profile of tenecteplase to alteplase holds consistently among patients with disordered glucose metabolism, given that stroke frequently occurs alongside diabetes and admission hyperglycemia.  

Thus, we used data from the TRACE-2 trial to comprehensively evaluate the efficacy and safety of intravenous tenecteplase versus intravenous alteplase in patients with AIS, considering their diabetic status and admission glucose levels.  

# METHODS  

# Data Availability  

The data that support the findings of this study are available from the corresponding author upon reasonable request.  

# Study Design and Populations  

This study constitutes a subgroup analysis of the TRACE-2 trial. Comprehensive information regarding the rationale, design, methods, and major findings of the TRACE-2 trial has been previously documented.19,20 Information on the trial protocol and statistical analysis plan were previously published.19 In brief, the TRACE-2 trial was a phase 3, multicenter, prospective, openlabel, randomized, controlled, blinded-end-point, noninferiority trial. It was conducted at 53 centers in China from June 12, 2021, to May 29, 2022. The trial enrolled adult patients with AIS who were eligible for intravenous thrombolysis but ineligible for or refused endovascular thrombectomy within 4.5 hours of symptom onset. These patients had an initial modified Rankin Scale (mRS) score of no more than 1 at enrollment and presented with a disabling ischemic stroke with a National Institutes of Health Stroke Scale score ranging from 5 to 25. Eligibility for intravenous thrombolysis in this trial adhered to the guidelines of the Chinese Stroke Association.21 This trial was approved by the ethics committee of Beijing Tiantan Hospital and all participating centers. Written informed consent was obtained from all participants or their legal representatives. This trial was registered at ClinicalTrials.gov (NCT04797013).  

# Randomization and Treatment  

Eligible patients were randomly assigned in a 1:1 ratio to receive either intravenous tenecteplase $(0.25\mathrm{mg}/$ $\mathsf{kg}$ , with a maximum dose of $25~\mathrm{mg})$ or intravenous alteplase $(0.9\mathrm{{mg}/kg}$ , with a maximum dose of $90~\mathsf{mg}$ , administered as $10\%$ as a bolus and the remainder over 1 hour).  

# Data Collection  

Baseline data collection encompassed demographic characteristics, medical histories (including hypertension, diabetes, dyslipidemia, coronary heart disease, and arrhythmia), current smoking status, history of medication use (antiplatelet, anticoagulant, lipidlowering, antihypertensive, and hypoglycemic agents), prestroke mRS, admission glucose levels, and National Institutes of Health Stroke Scale scores. Trained neurologists at the participating hospitals collected this information following a standardized data collection protocol developed by the steering committee. Time metrics, including onset-to-needle and door-to-needle time, were also recorded. Additionally, subsequent endovascular thrombectomy, if required, after enrollment was documented.  

Diabetes was defined as self-reported diagnosed diabetes or the use of hypoglycemic agents, while admission hyperglycemia was defined as an admission serum glucose level ${>}7.8$ mmol/L.22 In addition to grouping patients on the basis of the presence or absence of diabetes or hyperglycemia, we further categorized them into 4 distinct groups: diabetes with admission hyperglycemia, diabetes without admission hyperglycemia, without diabetes but with admission hyperglycemia, and without either condition.  

# Outcomes  

The primary efficacy outcome was defined as the proportion of patients achieving an excellent functional outcome at 90 days, indicated by an mRS score of 0 to 1. Secondary efficacy outcomes included favorable functional outcomes at 90 days (mRS score of 0–2), the mRS score at 90 days, neurological improvement on the National Institutes of Health Stroke Scale (defined as a decrease of at least 4 points, a score of no more than 1 at 24 hours and at 7 days or discharge), and a Barthel Index score of at least 95 points at 90 days.  

The primary safety outcome was the rate of sICH within 36 hours of thrombolytic treatment administration, defined according to the criteria from European Cooperative Acute Stroke Study III.23 Other safety outcomes encompassed parenchymal hematoma, as defined by the criteria of the Safe Implementation of Thrombolysis in Stroke-­Monitoring study,24 any intracranial hemorrhage or other significant hemorrhagic events as defined by the Global Utilization of Streptokinase and Tissue Plasminogen Activator for Occluded Coronary Arteries criteria,25 and death from all causes within 90 days of disease onset. Serious adverse events were collected throughout the 90-day study period.  

# Statistical Analysis  

Efficacy analyses were conducted in the modified intention-to-treat population and in the per-protocol population. The modified intention-to-treat population included all participants who were randomly assigned and received their allocated thrombolytic treatment, while the per-protocol population consisted of participants who completed their assigned treatment without major protocol violations or missing data concerning primary efficacy end points. Baseline characteristics were compared between the groups. Continuous variables were expressed as median with interquartile range and analyzed using the nonparametric Mann– Whitney $U$ test. Categorical data were presented as proportion (percentage) and analyzed using $\chi^{2}$ tests or Fisher’s exact test, as appropriate. To compare efficacy and safety end points between 2 treatment groups, the Cochran–Mantel–Haenszel $\chi^{2}$ test was used, with adjustments made for the pooled-site effect (with a minimum of ${\ge}20$ patients in each stratum), and the $95\%$ CI for relative risk (RR) was calculated. For the ordinal 90-day mRS score, a common odds ratio with its $95\%$ CI was calculated using ordinal logistic regression, and proportional odds assumption was assessed by the $\chi^{2}$ score test. If the outcome was statistically significant, we further evaluated interactions between treatment and glucose metabolism status by including terms for treatment, subgroup, and treatment-by-subgroup interactions in the model. To compare outcomes between the diabetes and nondiabetes groups, adjusted odds ratios with $95\%$ CIs were calculated using logistic regression, accounting for potential confounders selected on the basis of univariate analysis that revealed statistically significant baseline differences. Two-sided $P$ values of 0.05 were considered statistically significant. All analyses were performed using SAS software, specifically version 9.4 (SAS Institute, Inc, Cary, NC).

# RESULTS  

A total of 1382 patients were included in the modified intention-to-treat analysis after excluding 52 patients with missing medical history or admission blood glucose data. Among them, 369 $(26.7\%)$ presenting had a history of diabetes, while 1013 did not (Figure S1). The median age of the patients was 66 years, and 431 $(31.2\%)$ were women. Detailed baseline characteristics of patients with and without a diabetes history as well as the 2 treatment groups, are shown in Table 1. Patients with a diabetes history had a higher body mass index and prestroke mRS score; a higher prevalence of hypertension, hyperlipidemia, and coronary heart disease; a higher percentage of previous antiplatelet, lipid-lowering, and antihypertensive therapy; and greater door-to-needle time compared with those without a diabetes history. However, the proportion of current smokers was lower in patients with stroke with a diabetes history than in those without. The baseline characteristics between the 2 lytic treatments were well-balanced in each subgroup, except for the lower weight, a higher proportion of lipid-lowering drugs, and shorter door-to-needle time in the tenecteplase group among patients with diabetes.  

Baseline characteristics varied considerably between patients with and without admission hyperglycemia (Table S1). Baseline characteristics between the 2 lytic treatments were well balanced in each of these subgroups, except for a shorter door-to-needle time in the tenecteplase group among patients with admission hyperglycemia. When further stratified by diabetes history and admission glycemia status, baseline characteristics between the 2 lytic treatments remained well balanced across the 4 subgroups, with the exception of the shorter door-to-needle time in the tenecteplase group among patients with both diabetes and admission hyperglycemia (Table S2).  

# Efficacy Outcomes  

The proportion of patients achieving the primary efficacy outcome of an excellent functional outcome at 90 days was significantly lower in patients with a diabetes history compared with those without $(52.1\%$ [190/365] versus $63.4\%$ [635/1001]; adjusted odds ratio, 0.67 $[95\%$ CI, $0.52–0.87]$ ; $P{=}0.03$ ; Table S3). In the modified intention-to-treat analysis, the proportion of excellent functional outcome at 90 days was 93 of 164 $(56.7\%)$ versus 97 of 201 $(48.3\%)$ in the tenecteplase and alteplase groups among patients with a diabetes history (RR, 1.18 $[95\%$ CI, 0.97–1.43]; $P{=}0.11$ ) and 335 of 519 $(64.6\%)$ versus 300 of 482 $(62.2\%)$ (RR, 1.04 $[95\%$ CI, 0.94–1.14]; $_{P=0.45}$ ) in those without, respectively (Table  2, Figure  [A] and [B]). No significant difference in treatment effect was observed for all secondary outcomes. The result of the per-protocol analysis was consistent with those of the modified intention-to-treat analysis  

Furthermore, in the modified intention-to-treat analysis, the proportion of an excellent functional outcome at 90 days was similar between the tenecteplase and alteplase groups in patients presenting with and without admission hyperglycemia (135/235 $[57.5\%]$ versus 130/241 $[53.9\%]$ ; RR, 1.07 $[95\%$ CI, 0.91–1.25]; $P{=}0.44$ ; and 293/448 $[65.4\%]$ versus 267/442 $[60.4\%]$ ; RR, 1.08 $[95\%$ CI, 0.98–1.20]; $\scriptstyleP=0.12$ , respectively; Table 3, Figure $[\mathrm{C}]$ and [D]). The per-protocol analysis results were also consistent with those of the modified intention-to-treat analysis. No significant differences in treatment effect were observed for any secondary outcomes. When further stratified by diabetes history and admission glycemia status, similar findings were observed in both the modified intention-to-treat and per-protocol analyses $\boldsymbol{\mathrm{\Delta}}P$ for interaction $_{1=0.20}$ and 0.11, respectively; Table S4).

# Safety Outcomes  

The risks of sICH within 36 hours, sICH with 90 days, and serious adverse events were all significantly increased in patients with a diabetes history compared with those without $\mathrm{\mathit{P}{=}0.046}$ , 0.02, and 0.009, respectively; Table S3). The rate of sICH within 36 hours between the tenecteplase and alteplase groups occurred in 7 $(4.2\%)$ versus 4 $(2.0\%)$ (RR, 2.12 $[95\%$ CI, 0.63–7.11]; $P{=}0.21$ ) among patients with a diabetes history, and 8 $(1.5\%)$ versus 8 $(1.6\%)$ (RR, 0.94 $[95\%$ CI, 0.36–2.49]; $P{=}0.90.$ ) among patients without, respectively (Table 2). Other safety outcomes were similar between the 2 treatments in patients with and without a diabetes history. Similar findings were observed in the primary and other safety outcomes between the 2 treatments in patients with and without admission hyperglycemia (Table 3).  

When further stratified by diabetes history and admission glycemia status, the primary safety outcome between the tenecteplase and alteplase groups occurred in 6 $(4.3\%)$ versus 4 $(2.5\%)$ among patients with both diabetes and admission hyperglycemia, 1 $(2.8\%)$ versus 0 among patients with diabetes but without admission hyperglycemia, 1 $(1.0\%)$ versus 1 $(1.2\%)$ among patients without diabetes but with admission hyperglycemia, and 7 $(1.7\%)$ in both groups among patients without either condition (Table S4).  

# DISCUSSION  

In this glucose metabolism subgroup analysis of the TRACE-2 trial, we observed that the proportion of patients achieving an excellent functional outcome was significantly lower in those with a diabetes history compared with those without. However, this proportion was similar between patients treated with $0.25\mathrm{mg/kg}$ of intravenous tenecteplase and those who received the standard dose of intravenous alteplase, regardless of the presence or absence of diabetes or admission hyperglycemia. Furthermore, the safety outcomes of the 2 thrombolytic treatments were consistent across all subgroups.  

Diabetes is generally associated with higher risks of unfavorable outcomes and sICH in patients with stroke after intravenous thrombolysis,26 although some studies have reported different findings.6,27 Our study on intravenous thrombolysis demonstrated that patients with stroke with a history of diabetes had a higher proportion of preexisting comorbidities, such as hypertension, hyperlipidemia, and coronary heart disease, compared with those without diabetes. Additionally, patients with diabetes and stroke exhibited decreased functional outcomes and an increased risk of sICH, even after adjusting for potential confounders, including these comorbidities. These unfavorable outcomes might be attributed to lower recanalization rates, signifying impaired fibrinolytic response and heightened coagulation due to elevated blood glucose.28–30 Furthermore, diabetes often coexists with hyperinsulinemia, leading to a hypercoagulable state and reduced fibrinolysis through increased PAI production.30,31

Recent clinical trials demonstrated that $0.25\mathsf{mg}/\mathsf{kg}$ of tenecteplase was noninferior and safe compared with alteplase for patients with AlS within a 4.5-hour window.15,18,19,32 However, it remained unclear whether the efficacy and safety profiles of the 2 thrombolytic agents were similar in patients with glucose metabolism disorders. Our subgroup analysis of the TRACE-ll study revealed that, among patients with a diabetes history, the door-to-needle time was significantly shorter in the tenecteplase group than in the alteplase group. Nonetheless, the efficacy and safety of $0.25~\mathsf{mg}/\mathsf{kg}$ of tenecteplase were comparable to the standard dose of alteplase among patients with a diabetes history. The potential explanation for this finding is unclear. It is noteworthy that the rate of excellent functional outcome was numerically higher with tenecteplase compared with alteplase in the diabetes subgroup. While these numbers did not reach statistical significance, a trend toward a higher frequency of excellent outcomes with tenecteplase was observed, particularly in the per-protocol analysis. The per-protocol analysis includes only those adhering to the study protocol, while the intention-to-treat analysis includes cases of protocol deviations, making intention-to-treat analysis generally more conservative. This might explain the observed trend in the perprotocol analysis. Further studies on diabetes patients with larger sample sizes are needed to clarify whether the rate of excellent functional outcome differs between these 2 lytic agents.  

Admission hyperglycemia after stroke is common, potentially reflecting stress hyperglycemia, impaired glucose tolerance, or unrecognized diabetes.33 Previous studies consistently linked admission hyperglycemia to worse outcomes and higher mortality rate following thrombolysis,26,34,35 not just in patients with stroke with diabetes but also patients with stroke without diabetes.36 These associations may partially result from decreased fibrinolytic activity of alteplase in the presence of hyperglycemia.10,37 Therefore, it was necessary to evaluate the efficacy and safety of the 2 lytic medications in patients with and without admission hyperglycemia. Interestingly, our study revealed that the door-to-needle time was significantly shorter in the tenecteplase group among patients with admission hyperglycemia. While efficacy and safety outcomes were similar between the 2 lytic medications in patients with and without admission hyperglycemia, it is worth noting that the point estimate favoring tenecteplase was greater in patients with diabetes than in those without, while it was slightly smaller in patients with admission hyperglycemia than in those without. This discrepancy may be due to the fact that some patients with diabetes presented with normoglycemia at admission, or the diagnosis of diabetes was unknown in patients with admission hyperglycemia. Future studies should use more precise assessments of glucose metabolism status to compare the efficacy of tenecteplase and alteplase in these populations. Regardless, these findings suggested that tenecteplase $(0.25\mathrm{\:mg/kg})$ could be a viable first-choice thrombolysis option for patients with varying glucose metabolism statuses due to its ease of administration and efficient workflow.  

# Limitations  

This substudy has several limitations. First, it is a post hoc design and analysis of the TRACE-2 trial. Second, $13.3\%$ of included patients had admission hyperglycemia without documented diabetes, raising the possibility of preexisting undiagnosed diabetes. However, the data for hemoglobin $\mathsf{A}_{1\mathsf{C}}$ were missing. This could introduce some measure bias in the identification of diabetes and the subsequent analysis of its effects. Further study should focus on collecting these data to improve the accuracy of diabetes classification. Third, we included only patients treated within 4.5 hours from stroke onset, limiting generalizability to those treated outside this window. Fourth, the TRACE-2 trial was conducted exclusively in China, and our findings may not be extrapolated to non-Chinese patients. Finally, patients with large-vessel occlusion eligible for endovascular thrombectomy were excluded, so the treatment effect in patients with varying glucose metabolism statuses and large-vessel occlusion presence was not assessed. Therefore, our findings might not be applicable to patients with large-vessel occlusion for whom thrombectomy is intended.  

# Conclusions  

In this subgroup analysis of the TRACE-2 trial, 0.25 mg/kg of tenecteplase demonstrated similar benefits and safety as the standard dose of $0.9\mathrm{{mg/kg}}$ of alteplase in patients with AIS with and without glucose metabolism disorders. These results suggest that tenecteplase is a reasonable alternative to alteplase in this patient subpopulation.
