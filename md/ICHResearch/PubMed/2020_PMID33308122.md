Abstract: Background: Shoulder-hand syndrome (SHS) refers to a syndrome causing sudden edema, shoulder pain and limited hand function. Qingpeng ointment, a kind of Tibetan medicine, can reduce swelling, relieve pain, tonify stagnation and clear the meridians, which is consistent with the pathological mechanism of SHS after stroke. Therefore, if clinical trials can be used to explore the effectiveness of Qingpeng ointment for the treatment of poststroke SHS and promote its application in clinical medicine, it may prove the specific significance for the treatment of poststroke SHS poststroke SHS.  

Objective: The aim of the study was to investigate the clinical efficacy and safety of Qingpeng ointment in the treatment of poststroke SHS and to provide an objective basis for a better therapeutic treatment for poststroke SHS  

Methods: A prospective, randomized, controlled study was conducted. This study recruited 120 patients with poststroke SHS who met the inclusion criteria. They were randomized into the treatment group and the control group, with 60 patients allocated to each group. The treatment group received routine medical treatment and rehabilitative care after using the Qingpeng ointment, while the patients in the control group received only routine treatment without the ointment. All patients received clinical assessment with the Visual Analogue Scale (VAS), measurement of the range of motion (ROM) of the upper-limb joints, the Fugl-Meyer Assessment of Upper Extremity (FMA-U) and the Modified Barthel Index Score (MBI) before and after the whole treatment.  

Results: After 4 weeks of treatment, the VAS scores of both the groups decreased significantly (P $<0.05)$ , and the difference between the two groups was statistically significant $\mathrm{~P~}<0.05)$ . No statistical significance was observed for the difference between the treatment group and control group in terms of the FMA-U and MBI scores and the forward bend, backward, outstretch, external rotation and pronation angles after treatment. The increases in the values of VAS, FMA-M and MBI in the treatment group were greater than those in the control group, and the difference was statistically significant $(\mathrm{P}<0.05)$ . The increases in the values of the forward bend, outreach and external rotation angles in the treatment group were greater than those in the control group, and the difference was statistically significant $(\mathrm{P}<0.05)$ .  

Conclusion: The treatment group showed better results than the control group in terms of the relief of pain symptoms, the improvement of motor function and the improvement of the activities of daily living for patients with shoulder-hand syndrome after cerebral hemorrhage. Qingpeng ointment was found to be effective and safe for treating poststroke SHS.

# 1. INTRODUCTION  

It is reported that the incidence of new strokes in China is approximately 379 per 100,000 persons per year; the incidence rate has increased by approximately $8.3\%$ in a decade, and the incident population also tends to be younger [1]. Stroke survivors may have various complications. Shoulder-hand syndrome (SHS) after stroke refers to the combination of sudden edema and joint pain (mainly in the shoulder) after cerebrovascular disease, resulting in limited hand function [2]. The main symptoms include pain and swelling of the affected shoulder joint. It usually starts within one to three months after stroke and is one of the most common chronic complications after a stroke. Studies have shown that the incidence of SHS after acute cerebrovascular disease is approximately $12.5\%$ to $70\%$ [3], while in China it is close to $30\%$ [4]. SHS is a hindrance factor for stroke rehabilitation. It was first reported by Stejnbroker in 1947. In 1994, the International Association for Pain Research classified poststroke SHS as complex local pain syndrome type I and indicated that this disease is closely related to the mediation of the sympathetic nerves [5]. SHS directly hinders the recovery of upper limb motor function, and only a small number of those patients can return to the original functional state, greatly affecting the healing process and recovery. Severe patients may develop deformed hands and completely lose hand function. Therefore, we must pay more attention to SHS.  

At present, most studies have concluded that SHS is a reflex sympathetic dystrophy (RSD) [6], which is often associated with prolonged shoulder subluxation [7-10], shoulder muscle spasm [11-14], wrist joint forced flexion, a "shoulder-hand pump" mechanism of injury [15], hand-side intravenous infusion [16], hand injury and other factors. The development process is divided into three phases in clinical practice [17]. Among them, phase I has the best therapeutic outcome, and phase II and III have a poor prognosis. It is suggested that if SHS can be prevented in advance, diagnosed early, and treated correctly in time, it can be effectively prevented, and the occurrence and development could be controlled. Recently, common treatments for poststroke SHS include rehabilitation training, rehabilitation physical factor therapy, closure therapy, nerve block, local surgery, etc [18]. Drug treatment is mostly based on antiinflammatory analgesics and peony. However, there is no specific drug for its treatment.  

In Chinese medicine, poststroke SHS conforms to the description of hemiplegia, partial wind, partial dryness and other diseases. Chinese medicine has unique efficacy in treating poststroke SHS. The main methods include the use of Chinese herbs, acupuncture, massage, Chinese medicine fumigation [19], Chinese medicine hot compress, Chinese medicine hand soaking [20], etc. Chinese herbs can replenish QI, nourish the blood, promote blood circulation, relieve pain, reduce dehumidification, clear away heat and remove phlegm.  

Tibetan medicine has a long history of external use and has great advantages in pain treatment. It also has unique effects on stroke in long-term clinical applications. Qingpeng ointment is a traditional, classic Tibetan prescription. It is composed of nine Tibetan medicines, including oxytropis, rhubarb, iron hammer, scorpion (nonnuclear), hairy scorpion, emblica, benzoin, glutinous vine, and fennel [21]; no obvious adverse reactions have been found in clinical applications since it became available. Qingpeng ointment is mainly used to treat arthritis, muscle swelling and pain, and in recent years, many studies have been conducted on the treatment of chronic eczema and other skin diseases. Qingpeng ointment has the effect of pain relief, smoothness of the band, and smooth meridian, and it was found that the use of Qingpeng ointment in patients with stroke-induced SHS may be effective, but still no relevant clinical trials have been performed. If the effectiveness and safety of Qingpeng ointment for the treatment of post-stroke SHS can be proved through reasonable clinical trials and popularized in clinical practice, this could be of concrete significance to the treatment of poststroke SHS.  



# 2. MATERIALS AND METHODS  

This study is a prospective, randomized controlled trial. The research unit was the Guangdong Provincial Hospital of Chinese Medicine, and the Department of Rehabilitation was responsible for subject recruitment, performance of interventions and assessments and statistical analysis. All cases met the criteria for intracerebral hemorrhage in the diagnosis of various types of cerebrovascular diseases as revised by the Fourth Annual Conference of Cerebrovascular Diseases of the Chinese Medical Association in 1995. Before enrollment, each qualified subject signed an informed consent form after being screened for eligibility. After that, they received baseline assessments before randomization. Eligible patients were divided into 2 groups in a 1:1 ratio by following a random number table (Fig. 1).

# 2.1.2. Intervention Program  



# 2.1.2.1. Basic Treatment  

Basic treatment comprised internal treatment and rehabilitation training. According to the 2014 Guidelines for the Prevention and Treatment of Cerebrovascular Diseases in China [22], internal treatment for secondary prevention of stroke was performed. Blood pressure, blood lipids, blood sugar control and other symptomatic treatments were performed according to the specific conditions of the patients. According to the 2011 China Stroke Rehabilitation Treatment Guidance [5], the corresponding rehabilitation treatment plan was administered.  



# 2.1.2.2. Treatment Plan for the Treatment Group  

Patients in this group received Qingpeng ointment (produced by Tibet Qizheng Tibetan Medicine Co., Ltd. Chinese medicine quasi-word: Z54020140) applied evenly on the shoulder joints, wrist joints and palms of the affected upper limbs. After pressing for 20 minutes, the patients underwent rehabilitation training after 10 minutes. Patients in the treatment group were asked to apply Qingpeng ointment evenly on the shoulder joints, wrist joints and palms of the affected upper limbs with pressing for 20 minutes and to perform rehabilitation training after 10 minutes twice a day for four weeks.  



# 2.1.2.3. Treatment Plan for the Control Group  

No intervention.  



# 2.1.2.4. Drug Distribution Rules  

All medicines were kept in the pharmacy department, and each patient's medication was registered according to the hospital medicine procedures.  



# 2.1.2.5. Drug Inventory  

Medical staff monitored the medication use of each patient to determine the patient's medication compliance and to decide whether the patient could continue to participate in the trial. Exit criteria for poor adherence included the calculation of the respective medication rate (formula: respective medication rate $=$ actual dose/application dose) $\times$ $100\%$ . If the respective medication rate was less than $80\%$ or larger than $120\%$ , the patients exited the trial.  



# 2.1.2.6. Drug Storage, Distribution and Recycling  

The research drug was uniformly stored and managed by the research unit, and the patients’ drugs were issued individually according to the hospital's drug issuance regulations.  



# 2.1.3. Outcomes  

All outcomes were determined before treatment and after a 4-week treatment to compare the efficacy (twice in total).  



# 2.1.3.1. Change in the Degree of Pain in the Affected Shoulder  

The VAS was used to test the degree of pain. It was scored from 0-10; $"0"$ indicated no pain, and $"10"$ indicated the most severe pain. The higher the score was, the greater was the pain. The patient selected the scores that best reflected their pain, so that the patient's pain level could be visually assessed. The method is sensitive, reliable, and easy to use, but the subjects need to have an understanding of abstract concepts. VAS is currently used most frequently in clinical practice. The absolute value of VAS should be used for each score, and the subjective bias should be minimized.  



# 2.1.3.2. Motor Function Change  

Swedish scholar Fugl Meyer developed a more quantitative and accurate evaluation method of motor function according to Brunnstrom, known as the FuglMeyer motion function scale [23]. We used the Simplified Fugl-Meyer motion function assessment method to assess the motor function of the affected side. The total score for the Fugl-Meyer motor function assessment scale is 100 points; there are 50 items with a total of 2 points each. According to the quality of submission completion, the evaluator gives scores of $"0","1"$ , or $2"$ . A total of 33 items are evaluated for the upper limbs, with a total score of 66 points. Seventeen items are evaluated for the lower limbs, with a total score of 34 points. The higher the score is, the better the motor function is. A total score of less than 50 indicates severe dyskinesia. A total score between 50-84 indicates obvious movement disorder. A total score between 85-95 indicates moderate dyskinesia. A total score between 96-100 indicates mild dyskinesia. The Fugl-Meyer evaluation method is sensitive, reliable, comprehensive and credible. It is the most widely used motor function assessment method in clinical and scientific research. However, it is cumbersome and time-consuming. In this study, we will use the upper extremity section of the FuglMeyer assessment (FMA-U).  



# 2.1.3.3. Activities of Daily Living (ADL) Changes  

The modified Barthel Index was used to assess the activities of daily living. In 1989, Shah et al. developed the modified Barthel Index (MBI) by improving the Barthel index. The MBI scale has high feasibility, credibility and sensitivity. It can be used not only to evaluate the functional status before and after rehabilitation but also to predict the rehabilitation effect. It is the most commonly used ADL evaluation scale in clinical practice. The MBI scale includes 11 items with a total score of 100. Evaluation is based on the actual situation of the patient. The sum of the scores of each item is the total score;  the lowest score is $"0"$ , and the highest score is $"100"$ . A score between 0 to 20 points indicates extremely severe dysfunction. A score between 25 to 45 points indicates severe dysfunction. A score between 50 to 70 points indicates moderate functional defects. A score between 75-95 points indicates mild functional defects. A score of 100 points indicates ADL self-care. The higher the total score is, the better the ADL is.  



# 2.1.3.4. Shoulder Joint Mobility Change  

The shoulder joint mobility was tested with a protractor. The movements of the shoulder joints are as follows:  

flexion: $70–90°$ ; stretch: $40^{\circ}$ ; outreach: $80–90°$ ; adduction: $40–60°$ ; pronation: $70–90°$ ; external rotation: $15^{\circ}$ ; and lift up: $160–180^{\circ}$ . The greater the number of degrees is, the better the shoulder joint mobility is.  



# 2.1.4. Sample Size  

This study is a randomized controlled clinical trial, and the sample size was calculated according to the Power Analysis of Sample Size (PASS). $\mathtt{a}{=}0.05$ and $1-\upbeta{=}0.80$ were set. According to the meta-analysis of the clinical effect of acupuncture on stroke, the required sample size was calculated according to the two-sample mean comparison formula, and the calculation result was 50 cases per group, totaling 100 cases. Considering that some uncontrollable factors may lead to the loss of the patients in the research process, an increase of $15\%$ (15 people) of the sample will result in a total of 115 patients in this study. To enhance the effectiveness and persuasiveness of the observation, the sample size was expanded to 120 cases.  



# 2.1.5. Recruitment  

To achieve our targeted sample size (see above), we posted recruitment posters in both the outpatient and inpatient departments in the Guangdong Provincial Hospital of Chinese Medicine. Inclusion criteria and contact information were shown on the poster.  



# 2.1.6. Randomization and Allocation Concealment  

The SPSS statistical software was used to generate the random coding table, and the nonsubject group was responsible for the production and preservation of the random number column. The whole process of the research is guaranteed to be hidden.  



# 2.1.7. Blinding (Masking)  

In this study, the assessors and the analysts were blinded to the grouping of subjects. Physicians from the Rehabilitation Department were responsible for the assessment of the clinical scales. They received the same training before the trial began. Statistical analysts were responsible for data analysis, and the analysts did not know the randomization status. Thus, the assessors and analysts did not know to which group each subject was assigned and whether or not they received Qingpeng ointment.  



# 2.1.8. Statistical Methods  

The statistical analysis software used was SPSS 23.0. Independent sample t test was used for quantitative pretreatment data conforming to the normal distribution, and for the data not conforming to the normal distribution, a rank sum test was used. Chi-square test was used for pretreatment counting data. For the efficacy comparison before and after treatment, the matching sample t test or rank sum test (in group) were used, and for the comparison of the efficacy between the two groups, the two independent samples t test of the rank sum test was used. In the statistical results, a $P$ lower than 0.05 was considered to be of statistical significance.

# 3. RESULTS  



# 3.1. Baseline Data  

A total of 84 males and 36 females were included in the two groups; patients were between 27 to 80 years old, with an average age of $54.30{\pm}11.55$ years, and had a disease course of 15-175 days, with an average course of $81.16{\pm}38.10$ days. Before treatment, the average VAS score was $3.96{\pm}1.52\$ , the average ROM scores were $39.53{\scriptstyle\pm54.54}$ , $10.99{\scriptstyle\pm17.99}$ , $34.28{\scriptstyle\pm53.01}$ , $14.77{\scriptstyle\pm24.43}$ , and $12.46{\pm}21.90$ , the average FMA-U score was $12.64{\pm}14.04$ , and the average daily living activity score was $44.75{\scriptstyle\pm23.13}$ .  



# 3.1.1. Baseline Comparison  

The results show that there were no statistically significant differences in gender, age and disease course between the two groups before treatment $(\mathrm{~P~}>0.05)$ . Therefore, the baseline general information in the two groups was consistent and comparable (Table 1).  

The age data in the two groups were in line with a normal distribution, and the independent sample t test was used. Gender data were tested by the chi-square test. The disease course of the two groups did not conform to the normal distribution, and the nonparametric Mann-Whitney test was used.  



# 3.1.2. Comparison of the VAS, FMA- $U_{i}$ , MBI and ROM of Shoulder Scores between the Two Groups Before Treatment  

There were no statistically significant differences in the VAS, FMA-U, MBI and ROM scores for the shoulder between the two groups before treatment $\mathrm{~\textit~{~(~P~}~}>0.05\mathrm{)~}$ . Therefore, the baseline of outcomes in the two groups was consistent and comparable (Table 2).  



# 3.2. Outcomes and Estimation  



# 3.2.1. Comparison of the VAS, FMA-U, MBI and ROM Scores

# 4. DISCUSSION  



# 4.1. Tibetan Medicine's Understanding of Qingpeng Ointment  

The compatibility of Qingpeng ointment can be roughly analyzed according to the theory of “Monarch, Minister, Assistant and Guide” in Chinese medicine. The monarch herb, Oxytropis, which has anti-inflammatory, swelling relief and pain relief properties, is the main ingredient of Qingpeng ointment.  

In the past, Tibetans often used Qingpeng ointment to treat various types of phlegm and blood stasis syndrome. The phlegm and blood stasis syndromes are similar to the diseases named "Huangshuir" and "Gangba" in Tibetan medicine. Modern clinical practice often uses Qingpeng ointment to treat muscle swelling and pain, fracture, joint degeneration, cervical spondylosis, frozen shoulder, gout, rheumatoid arthritis, rheumatoid arthritis, chronic eczema and dermatitis, psoriasis, chloasma, erysipelas, herpes zoster, and frostbite, and it has a good curative effect and high safety. According to modern pharmacological research, nine herbs in Qingpeng ointment are rich in various active ingredients. The monarch medicine is echidna bean, which has anti-inflammatory, anti-swelling, and pain relief properties and is the most important ingredient of Qingpeng ointment. Subrhubarb and wide ribvine are minister medicines. They can remove dampness and heat, with the ratchet beans inducing diuresis and reducing edema. As assistant medicines, hammer, benzoin and musk can enhance the efficacy of the monarch and minister medicines. Sanzi is the guide medicine and can reconcile all medicines. Combined with other drugs, Qingpeng ointment can promote blood circulation, remove blood stasis and collaterals, detoxify, cause detumescence, relieve pain, clear heat, remove wind and dampness, activate Qi and promote blood circulation and arthralgia.  



# 4.2. Possible Mechanism of Qingpeng Ointment in Treating Cerebral Hemorrhage Shoulder-Hand Syndrome  

There is no unified theory about the pathogenesis of shoulder-hand syndrome, but most of the direct causes are related to pain and inflammatory exudative edema; the blood vessels of the affected limb are easily dilated and paralyzed, which is a manifestation of nutritional disorders. Studies have shown that the body's pain and swelling are associated with the release of NOcGMP pathway and various inflammatory mediators [24]. Excessive NO will dilate blood vessels and increase exudation, promoting the release of inflammatory mediators PGE2, TNF- $\mathbf{\nabla}\cdot\mathbf{a}~$ , IL-1, IL2, and $\mathrm{IL}{-}1\upbeta$ , and synergizing with other cytokines and inflammatory cells to damage joints [25-26].  

The ingredients of Qingpeng ointment can be antiinflammatory, reduce inflammatory mediators, shrink blood vessels, reduce capillary permeability, have anti-edema properties, promote analgesia, improve spasm, and regulate immunity and other effects, which are conducive to the rehabilitation of shoulder-hand syndrome. Xu et al. [27] found that Qingpeng ointment can inhibit peripheral pain and central pain at the same time, and its analgesic mechanism may be inhibition of NOcGMP pathway and opioid mechanism. By inhibiting the NOcGMP pathway associated with pain, it can reduce inflammatory mediators and pain-causing substances, reducing the central pain sensitivity and the transmission of peripheral pain to the center, and thus achieving pain relief. The content of the opioid mechanism is through high plasma $\upbeta$ -EP levels to increase endogenous opioid levels. Most studies show that Qingpeng ointment resists inflammation and swelling by inhibiting inflammatory mediators. Li et al. [25] found that the inflammatory mediator affected by Qingpeng ointment is $\mathrm{IL}{-}1\upbeta$ and TNF- $\mathbf{\nabla}\cdot\mathbf{a}~$ in synovial joints. Qi et al. [26] believe that the inflammatory mediator in joints is prostaglandin E2 (PGE2), which can reduce joint inflammation. Xu et al. [27] found that SP, as the main neurotransmitter involved in peripheral pain, has an inflammatory effect and promotes the release of serum IL-1, while Qingpeng ointment can reduce SP levels in the blood. Li et al. [28] found that Qingpeng ointment can inhibit the release of inflammatory cells and histamine, reduce the infiltration of neutrophils and regulate the expression of inflammatory mediators IL-2, TNF- $\mathbf{\nabla}\cdot\mathbf{a}~$ and INF- $\upgamma$ in mice models. In addition, Li and Li [29] proved that Qingpeng ointment can reduce joint pain and synovial damage by inhibiting NO level, thereby achieving the effect of protecting articular cartilage. However, it is believed that the decrease in NO level inhibits the level of pro-inflammatory cytokines and has no significant effect on serum $\mathrm{IL-}1\upbeta$ levels [30].  

The mechanism of Qingpeng ointment for post-stroke shoulder-hand syndrome is probably achieved by relieving pain and improving hand function, and the basis of this improvement is that Qingpeng ointment can effectively relieve analgesia, anti-inflammatory osmosis and edema.  

As a chronic, progressive complication, the cause of post-stroke shoulder-hand syndrome is complex and unclear, and the clinical symptoms are various. It would seriously affect the prognosis of rehabilitation if it develops to the middle and late stages. Currently, there are no norms or guidelines for the diagnosis and treatment of poststroke shoulder-hand syndrome, and no rapid and effective treatment methods have been found clinically.  



# CONCLUSION  

After combined treatment of Qingpeng ointment and conventional basic treatment, the upper limb joint pain, shoulder joint mobility, upper limb motor function and daily life activity ability of patients with shoulder-hand syndrome after cerebral hemorrhage significantly improved compared with before. In the control group, cerebral hemorrhage patients with shoulder-hand syndrome had improved upper limb joint pain, upper extremity motor function, and activities of daily living activities after treatment. The angles of flexion, extension, and abduction of shoulder joint improved compared with before treatment, while shoulder external rotation and pronation did not obviously change, indicating that the control group’s improvement of overall shoulder mobility is not as good as that of the treatment group. On the basis of the same conventional treatment, the use of Qingpeng ointment can obviously effectively alleviate the pain symptoms of patients with shoulder hand syndrome after cerebral hemorrhage. In FMA-U, MBI score, and shoulder joint activity improvement, the difference between the two groups was not obvious, and so we carried out a statistical analysis of the mean difference. The results show that treatment group patients improved more than the control group patients. Therefore, we can conclude that Qingpeng ointment can effectively improve patients’ motor function and shoulder pain.
