# HIGHLIGHTS:  

This pilot study demonstrated low-dose intracoronary (IC) tenecteplase (TNK) compared to placebo (saline) as an adjunct to primary PCI (PPCI) for ST-elevation myocardial infarction (STEMI) was feasible and safe but did not improve percent stenosis of the culprit lesion.   
 However, a trend towards reduction in thrombus burden with less hyperemia (a marker of distal embolization) was demonstrated with IC TNK.  

# ABSTRACT  

Following primary percutaneous coronary intervention (PPCI) for ST segment elevation myocardial infarction (STEMI), microvascular perfusion is often impaired secondary to thrombotic embolization.  Intracoronary (IC) fibrinolytic administration may reduce thrombotic burden and distal embolization. The ICE-T-TIMI-49 study evaluated the feasibility and safety of low-dose IC tenecteplase (TNK) during PPCI.  The study randomized 40 PPCI patients to a volume matched bolus of IC TNK $(4\mathrm{mg})$ $\scriptstyle(\mathtt{n}{=}20.$ ) or IC saline placebo $\scriptstyle(\mathrm{n}=20,$ ) before and following PPCI.  The primary endpoint was percent diameter stenosis of the culprit lesion following first bolus. The primary endpoint did not differ between IC placebo (median $100\%$ , interquartile range [IQR] 83.0,100.0) and IC TNK (median $100\%$ stenosis, IQR 91.0,100.0; $\mathtt{p}{=}0.522,$ ). However, the proportion of patients with reduction in thrombus following first bolus tended to be greater with IC TNK (placebo: 1 $2.5\%$ versus IC TNK: $40.0\%$ , $\mathtt{p}{=}0.133$ ).  Following PPCI, the corrected Thrombolysis In Myocardial Infarction (TIMI) frame count (cTFC) was lower (faster) with placebo (16.0 frames [IQR 12.0,24.0] versus 24.0 frames [22.0,32.0], $\mathtt{p}{=}0.045$ ) due to a trend towards greater frequency of hyperemia (cTFC ${<}14$ ), a marker of distal embolization $50.0\%$ versus $8.3\%$ , $\mathrm{p}{=}0.056,$ ).  There was no difference in TIMI major bleeds and no ICH. In conclusion, treatment with low-dose IC TNK appears safe and well tolerated during PPCI. While IC TNK administration did not improve percent stenosis, a trend towards reduced thrombus burden was demonstrated with less hyperemia (a marker of distal embolization). Our findings provide support for a large randomized study.  

Key Words: ST-segment elevation myocardial infarction, percutaneous coronary intervention, intracoronary tenecteplase, TIMI frame count, TIMI myocardial perfusion grade  

While primary percutaneous coronary intervention (PPCI) in ST-elevation myocardial infarction (STEMI) restores normal epicardial flow, limitations remain, including persistently impaired myocardial perfusion which in part is due to thrombotic embolization . This has been associated with worsened myocardial salvage2 and increased mortality3–5.  Roughly one-quarter of patients will have impaired microvascular flow following reperfusion in STEMI . Moreover, distal embolization of intracoronary (IC) thrombus during mechanical intervention can lead to further infarct expansion .  PPCI in conjunction with localized fibrinolytic therapy may represent an optimal strategy to improve the burden of epicardial and microvascular thrombus. Low-dose IC fibrinolytic administration provides higher local epicardial concentration which may result in greater safety than parenteral administration .  The ICE-T TIMI-49 study evaluates the feasibility and safety of low-dose IC tenecteplase (TNK) administration as adjunctive therapy to PPCI.  

# METHODS  

ICE T-TIMI 49 was a randomized, open-label, multicenter, placebo-controlled pilot study designed to evaluate the feasibility and safety of a $4\mathrm{mg}$ ( $[2\mathrm{ml}$ bolus) IC TNK administered prior to and following PPCI with protocol mandated aspirin (full dose aspirin), clopidogrel $\mathrm{300mg}$ load followed by 75 mg per day), unfractionated heparin (70U/kg) and intravenous glycoprotein IIb/IIIa inhibition.  The study randomized patients in a 1:1 fashion to receive either adjunctive low-dose IC TNK or volume matched placebo before and after PPCI (Figure 1). The IC TNK dose selected is approximately $20\%$ of systemic dose which minimizes the risk of bleeding while providing higher concentrations ( $\mathord{\sim}20$ fold) at the targeted site.  

Initial diagnostic angiography was performed and TIMI grade 0 / 1 flow was confirmed in the culprit artery.  The wire and balloon assembly were then advanced just proximal to the lesion. The guidewire was then withdrawn, and the first bolus of study drug was then administered by injecting either TNK or volume matched saline placebo through the central lumen of the balloon just proximal to the occlusion. This was then followed by PCI and / or stenting of the culprit artery.  Following PCI, the balloon was advanced distal to the lesion.  The guidewire was then withdrawn, and a second volume matched bolus of study drug was then administered beyond the culprit lesion.  Following each bolus (pre and post PCI), the drug was allowed to act for two minutes.  This was then followed by angiography of the culprit artery to assess the angiographic outcomes.  

Patients who were 18 years of age or older were eligible for enrollment if they exhibited: 1) symptoms of ischemic chest pain for at least 20 minutes and <6 hours suggestive of acute myocardial infarction; 2) ST-elevation of at least $0.1\mathrm{mV}$ in two contiguous limb leads OR STelevation of at least $0.2\mathrm{mV}$ in two contiguous precordial leads; 3) an occluded infarct-related artery at the time of coronary angiography (TIMI Flow Grade 0 or 1) and 4) were intended to be treated with concomitant use of aspirin, clopidogrel, unfractionated heparin, and glycoprotein IIb/IIIa inhibition and were scheduled for PPCI with the intent to stent the infarct-related artery.  

Exclusion criteria included: a maximal systolic blood pressure less than $80\mathrm{mm}\mathrm{Hg}$ following initial fluid and/or inotrope resuscitation; uncontrolled hypertension (SBP $>180$ or DBP ${>}110$ mm Hg) at time of enrollment; cardiac arrest or arrhythmia requiring chest compressions or cardiopulmonary resuscitation; known pregnancy; known thrombocytopenia  (platelet count <100,000); known severe renal insufficiency (creatinine $>4.0\mathrm{mg/dL})$ ; active or recent gastrointestinal hemorrhage; major surgery less than one month prior; known coagulopathy, platelet disorder, thrombocytopenia, or known neoplasm; previous hemorrhagic cerebrovascular disorder or active intracranial pathology; current warfarin therapy; administration of fibrinolytic agent within 72 hours; known allergy or contraindication to fibrinolytics, aspirin, heparin, or clopidogrel; presence of left main coronary artery or ostial (ostium of the left anterior descending artery, left circumflex artery or right coronary artery) culprit lesion;  lesion in a non-native coronary artery; or the need for urgent coronary artery bypass grafting.  

The primary endpoint was percent diameter stenosis of the culprit lesion after administration of study drug (post first bolus) on diagnostic angiography prior to PPCI.  The secondary endpoints were percent diameter stenosis post PCI, flow velocity using the Corrected Thrombolysis In Myocardial Infarction (TIMI) Frame Count $\left(\mathrm{cTFC}\right)^{9}$ , hyperemic flow using cTFC ${<}14$ (marker of distal embolization) ,  patency using TIMI Flow Grade , microvascular obstruction using TIMI myocardial perfusion grade (TMPG) and thrombus burden using TIMI thrombus grade12.   For the primary endpoint, the single plane projection following the first administration of the study drug that identified the stenosis in its tightest severity was selected for quantitative angiographic analysis using a previously described and validated automated edge detection algorithm13. All measurements were performed in the angiographic core laboratory (TIMI angiographic core laboratory, TIMI Study Group, Brigham and Women’s Hospital, Boston, MA) and was blinded to the randomization assignment and clinical outcomes.  

The clinical endpoints included death, recurrent MI, severe heart failure, repeat hospitalization or cardiac arrhythmia. Safety endpoints included stroke, intracranial hemorrhage (ICH), transfusion, thrombocytopenia, or bleeding events. Major and minor bleeding was assessed using the TIMI criteria .  

The clinical and angiographic outcomes were evaluated between the two arms using Fisher’s exact test with two-sided table at 0.05 level of significance. Data were presented as the number and the percentage for categorical endpoints.  If continuous variables were normally distributed, they were presented as mean and standard deviation. Two sample t-tests were used to compare the means.  For continuous variables which were not normally distributed, medians and interquartile ranges were reported.  Wilcoxon rank-sum tests were used to compare the two arms. The PERFUSE Core Laboratory and Data Coordinating Center performed data entry and data management. Analysis of the data was performed by the TIMI Study Group.  

# RESULTS  

Study drug could not be administered in 4 patients and were excluded from the analysis. There were no differences between the control and treatment groups with respect to baseline characteristics (Table 1). All patients in both groups received aspirin and unfractionated heparin. Glycoprotein IIb/IIIa inhibitors pre-PCI were given in $84\%$ versus $94\%$ of patients in the IC TNK and placebo groups respectively. Clopidogrel was administered to $95\%$ versus $100\%$ respectively.  

As seen in Table 2, the post-study drug pre-PCI percent stenosis (primary endpoint) did not differ between IC placebo and IC TNK. In addition, no significant improvement in percent diameter stenosis from the baseline was noted between the two groups. The proportion of culprit artery patency following first bolus of study drug trended in favor of patients treated with IC TNK, however no significant difference in TIMI flow grade 2/3 was observed following first bolus of study drug. The proportion of patients who sustained an improvement (i.e. decrease) in thrombus grade tended to be greater among patients administered IC TNK.  

As seen in Table 3, following PPCI but before the second bolus of TNK, no differences in the culprit lesion diameter were noted between groups with majority of patients having TIMI2/3 flow. High rates of TMPG 2/3 were observed with placebo and IC TNK. Both groups had low corrected cTFC (i.e. fast flow), however the placebo group (saline) compared to IC TNK was significantly lower (i.e. faster flow). When looking at the cumulative distribution function, a significant difference was noted between groups $(\mathtt{p}{=}0.04)$ ) (Figure 2). As pre-specified, a strong trend towards increased hyperemic flow (cTFC $<14$ frames) (a marker of distal embolization) was observed in the placebo group compared to IC TNK ( $50\%$ versus $8\%$ , $\mathrm{p}{=}0.056,$ ). As seen in Table 4, following PCI and after the administration of the second bolus of study drug, there was no difference in any angiographic outcomes (Table 4).  

During the 30-day post-procedure follow-up period, no differences between IC placebo and IC TNK were noted in death ( $0\%$ vs $5\%$ , $\mathtt{p}{=}1.00$ ), repeat hospitalization $0\%$ vs. $5\%$ , $\mathtt{p}{=}1.00\$ ) or cardiac arrhythmias $13\%$ vs. $5\%$ , $\mathsf{p}{=}0.57,$ ). There were no cases of recurrent myocardial infarction or severe heart failure. With respect to non-CABG bleeding, there were no TIMI major bleeds in the study.  TIMI minor bleeding during the 30-day follow-up period was reported in one patient in the IC TNK group while none were reported in placebo group $(\mathrm{p}{=}1.00)$ ). TIMI minimal bleeds occurred in $20\%$ (4/20) patients treated with IC TNK vs $13\%$ (2/16) patients treated with IC placebo $(\mathtt{p}{=}0.67)$ . One IC TNK patient was transfused $\scriptstyle{\mathbf{p}=1.000,}$ ). No difference in thrombocytopenia was noted $19\%$ vs. $5\%$ , $\mathrm{p}{=}0.30,$ ). There were no strokes or intracranial hemorrhages in the two groups.  

# DISCUSSION  

Administering low-dose IC TNK as adjunctive therapy to aspirin, clopidogrel, unfractionated heparin and glycoprotein IIb/IIIa inhibition during PPCI appears feasible without excessive risk. Although IC TNK did not improve the primary efficacy endpoint, thrombus burden appeared less (trend) pre PCI with less hyperemia post PCI. There was numerically more TIMI minor and minimal bleeding following IC TNK, but no TIMI major bleeds were observed. Reassuringly, no major bleeds or ICH were seen in this study.  

A systemic pharmacologic approach to reducing thrombus burden has also been tested  using intravenous full-dose fibrinolytic therapy in patients receiving PPCI.  Termed facilitated PCI, the largest trial Assessment of Safety and Efficacy of a New Thrombolytic-4 (ASSENT-4) intended to randomize 4000 patients to full dose systemic TNK or placebo prior to PPCI. However, it was stopped prematurely due to an excessive risk of mortality in the TNK group. Of the 1667 patients randomized, there was an increased risk of death, heart failure or shock within 90-days and increased in-hospital ICH with systemic full dose TNK . A meta-analysis of all studies investigating a facilitated approach confirmed these findings .  

An IC route of administration results in higher local epicardial concentration - therefore requires a much lower dose.  This in turn minimizes the systemic depletion of fibrinogen which may reduce major bleeding complications. In a pilot study of forty-one patients, low-dose IC streptokinase following completion of PPCI was associated with improved metrics of microvascular function including coronary flow reserve (CFR), index of microvascular resistance (IMR), collateral flow index, coronary wedge pressure and diastolic deceleration time and TIMI frame count, however this was assessed at 2 days following PPCI using invasive coronary hemodynamics (pressure/temperature guidewire). Reassuringly, no TIMI major bleeds were reported17.  

To date, the largest IC study included 440 patients in a 1:1:1 randomization to $20\mathrm{mg}$ alteplase, $10~\mathrm{mg}$ alteplase and placebo. No difference in the primary outcome of microvascular obstruction (MVO) on cardiac magnetic resonance imaging (MRI) (done in days 2 through 7 from admission) was demonstrated . However, important to note is the safety of this approach with no differences in major bleeding or myocardial hemorrhage seen on cardiac MRI . The lack of benefit seen in this clinical trial may be explained by the following: (1) ambiguity with  

MVO on cardiac MRI; (2) short ischemic times [trend towards benefit with IC fibrinolysis in those with ischemic times ${\ge}4$ hours); (3) wide timeframe where cardiac MRI performed which could lead to a lack of precision (variability) in results.  

Critical to note is the strong trend towards hyperemia in the placebo group. While rapid blood flow has been associated with improved clinical outcomes, the largest study to date in systemic fibrinolysis patients demonstrated a U shaped relationship between the cTFC following PCI and clinical outcomes such that hyperemia (cTFC ${<}14$ frames [ultra-fast blood flow]) was associated with poorer clinical outcomes following PCI, particularly in the context of impaired myocardial perfusion . In this context, embolization of atherosclerotic and thrombotic debris into the microvasculature releases endogenous adenosine from surrounding affected areas leading to hyperem ia19–21. In turn, this can lead to left ventricular dysfunction while still maintaining a hyperemic response in coronary epicardial flow . In the current study, hyperemia (a marker of distal embolization) was reduced with IC TNK.  

This study has some limitations. As a small pilot study, it was not adequately powered to exclude a modest increase in bleeding events.  The local administration of drug was upstream of the clot and was not administered directly into thrombus (i.e. using a microporous balloon such as the ClearWay [Atrium Medical, Hudson, NH] catheter).  There may have been reflux of drug into the aorta and/or other coronary vessels. Study drug could not be administered to 4 patients in the study and was excluded from the analysis. Although TIMI 0/1 flow was required for randomization, a minority of patients had baseline flow, however similar in both groups. Incomplete myocardial assessments (due to technical acquisition) occurred. Only two minutes elapsed after study drug was administered before the angiogram was performed to assess efficacy, and this may not have been a sufficient time for clot to lyse. Given the small sample size of this study, it is not clear if the more rapid flow / hyperemia observed in the placebo arm is a chance finding, or if it is due to a greater frequency of distal embolization. Other serologic data (creatine kinase, complete blood count, glucose, etc.) were not collected.  Finally, the optimal dose of IC TNK for safety and efficacy remains to be confirmed.  

In conclusion, the ICE T-TIMI 49 study found low-dose IC TNK as adjunctive therapy to PPCI was feasible and safe in STEMI.  Although IC TNK did not improve the primary efficacy endpoint (the percent diameter stenosis of the culprit lesion following the first bolus of study drug), thrombus burden (which takes into account thrombus length as well as lumen encroachment) as well as hyperemia (a marker of distal embolization) tended to be improved. While encouraging, a large randomized trial powered for clinical outcomes needs to be designed to evaluate this approach.
