# Mechanical thrombectomy in low Alberta stroke program early CT score (ASPECTS) in hyperacute stroke-a systematic review and meta-analysis

Objective Major randomized controlled trials of mechanical thrombectomy (MT) for acute ischemic stroke (AIS) failed to include a substantial number of patients presenting with low baseline Alberta Stroke Program Early CT Score (ASPECTS:0–5). Patients experiencing hyperacute strokes (last known well $\leq6\mathfrak{h}$ ) can potentially benefit most from MT. We conducted a systematic review and meta-analysis to report presentation severity and radiographic and clinical outcomes for hyperacute stroke patients presenting with low-ASPECTS.  

Methods: Our comprehensive literature search of PubMed, Embase, and Cochrane databases up to August 31, 2022 included articles reporting patients presenting hyperacutely who underwent MT for anterior circulation large vessel occlusion AIS with an ASPECTS $\leq5$ on baseline imaging. Pooled averages were calculated for age and presenting National Institutes of Health Stroke Scale (NIHsS). Fixed- and random-effects meta-analyses for weighted estimation of overall rates were performed.  

Forest plots were generated for proportions and estimated overall outcome rates.  

Results: 18 studies (1958 patients) were included (mean $\mathsf{age}=64.1$ years; presenting $\mathsf{NIHSS}=18.4)$ . Final modified thrombolysis in cerebral infarction 2b-3 grade was achieved in $76.4\%$ , with symptomatic intracranial hemorrhage in $12.1\%$ . Good (modified Rankin Scale [mRS] 0–2) and ambulatory (mRS 0–3) 3-­month outcomes were achieved by 27.4 and $46.7\%$ . respectively; 90-day mortality was $26.4\%$ .  

Conclusion: MT in low-ASPECTS hyperacute stroke patients may result in ambulatory clinical outcomes with acceptable hemorrhage risk. Recanalization rates achieved were similar to those in patients presenting with ASPECTS $\geq6$ ; this did not fully translate to better clinical outcomes.  

Advances in knowledge: MT should be considered for hyperacute strokes with low presenting ASPECTS.  

# INTRODUCTION  

The major randomized controlled trials (RCTs) and other corroborating studies confirming the clinical efficacy of endovascular therapy (EVT) with mechanical thrombectomy (MT) widely excluded acute ischemic stroke (AIS) patients with low $(\le5)$ presenting Alberta Stroke Program Early CT Score (ASPECTS).1–4 Of the five major trials, only Multicenter Randomized Clinical Trial of Endovascular Treatment for Acute Ischemic Stroke in the Netherlands (MR CLEAN) included patients with low baseline ASPECTS (0–5) and large ischemic cores. Solitaire With the Intention for Thrombectomy as Primary Endovascular Treatment (SWIFT-PRIME), Extending the Time for Thrombolysis in Emergency Neurological DeficitsIntra Arterial (EXTEND-IA), Endovascular Treatment for Small Core and Anterior Circulation Proximal Occlusion With Emphasis on Minimizing CT to Recanalization Times (ESCAPE), and Randomized Trial of Revascularization With Solitaire FR Device Versus Best Medical Therapy in the Treatment of Acute Stroke due to Anterior Circulation Large Vessel Occlusion Presenting Within Eight Hours of Symptom Onset (REVASCAT), all specifically excluded patients with low baseline ASPECT and core extension assessed using non-contrast CT (NCCT) or diffusion-weighted imaging MRI (DWI-­MRI).1–3,5 A subanalysis from MR CLEAN revealed no benefit of MT in patients ( $n=28$ ) with a baseline ASPECTS of 0–4.4 However, pooled analysis in the Highly Effective Reperfusion Evaluated in Multiple Endovascular Stroke trials (HERMES) meta-analysis showed a non-significant trend towards an MT treatment benefit for this subset of patients.6 It is important to consider the role of futile recanalization in patients with low ASPECTS (0–5) when interpreting these study results, as this reflects the failure to improve functional outcomes after successful recanalization.7 Another key consideration in the application of these results to low ASPECTS stroke patients is the possible predictors of neurologic and functional outcomes among this subset of stroke patients, as defined by modified Rankin Scale (mRS) score. For example, it has been established that factors such as high $(>10)$ National Institutes of Health Stroke Scale (NIHSS) score at presentation, age ${>}70$ years, systolic hypertension, and hyperglycemia upon admission can serve as predictors for poor outcomes following recanalization.7,8  

Several small retrospective studies and a meta-analysis have reported a surprisingly high percentage of patients $(25-30\%)$ experiencing good functional outcomes at 90 days (d) $\mathrm{(mRS~}0{-}2$ score), despite a large ischemic core at presentation.9–11 Because the time since last known well (LKW) is a major determinant of good long-term functional outcome, one major criticism of those previous publications is the heterogeneity in terms of time since LKW. Previous studies reveal that excellent outcomes are achieved for patients presenting with hyperacute strokes, defined as LKW within 6 h (h).12,13 To our knowledge, no systematic review or meta-analysis exists that is designed to measure outcomes for hyperacute low ASPECTS AIS patients treated with MT. It is important to acknowledge previous studies by Diestro et al and Cagnazzo et al that attempted to report outcomes for low ASPECTS AIS patients treated acutely with MT; however, the hyperacute presentation of the population was not specified in the design of the inclusion criteria in these studies.14,15 Similarly, Safouris et al report the outcomes of EVT in low ASPECTS AIS patients; however, this study does not specifically address patients presenting hyperacutely.16 We aimed to design and conduct a systematic review and meta-analysis reporting immediate recanalization rates, functional and neurologic outcomes, and symptomatic intracerebral hemorrhage (sICH) rate for the subset of patients presenting hyperacutely with AIS and ASPECTS 0–5.  

# METHODS  

This study was performed in accordance with Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-­Analyses (PRISMA) guidelines.17  

# Search strategy  

We performed a comprehensive literature search of PubMed, Embase, and the Cochrane Library (Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews, Cochrane Central Register of Controlled Trials, and Cochrane Methodology) from their dates of inception up to August 31, 2022. The following keywords were used in combination with Boolean operators to increase search sensitivity and specificity: “endovascular,” “thrombectomy,” “ASPECTS,” and “stroke.” Full search strategies are listed in Supplementary Table 1.  

# Study selection  

Our search results were imported to Rayyan, a web- and mobilebased application for systematic reviews and meta-analyses, to facilitate abstract screening and study selection.18 We included articles that reported patients ${\ge}18$ years of age who underwent MT for anterior circulation AIS with an $\mathrm{ASPECTS}\leq5$ on baseline CT or MRI scan and constituted patients presenting within $^{6\mathrm{h}}$ of LKW (hyperacute). Additionally, the articles had to report at least one of the outcomes of interest, including final modified thrombolysis in cerebral infarction (mTICI) grade, post-procedural symptomatic intracranial hemorrhage (sICH), 90d modified mRS scores (main outcome of interest), and 90d mortality. Successful MT recanalization outcomes were defined as mTICI 2b-3 grade. Good outcomes were defined as mRS 0–2 score at 90d and ambulatory outcomes as $\mathrm{mRS~}0{-}3$ score at 90d. Articles with fewer than 10 patients, those not written in the English language, case reports, review articles, animal studies, cadaveric studies, editorials, and comments were excluded. When studies contained overlapping cohorts, only the most complete report was included for quantitative assessment. Studies that did not separate outcomes for MT and intravenous (i.v.) thrombolytic therapy, that reported overlapping data, with absence of interest outcomes, and those without extractable data were also excluded.  

# Data extraction and quality assessment  

Two of the authors separately recorded the following data from the eligible studies: first author, year of publication, total number of included patients, total number of patients with ASPECTS $\leq$ 5, age, sex, and comorbidities including diabetes mellitus (DM), hypertension (HTN), hyperlipidemia, atrial fibrillation, coronary artery disease, and smoking history. Pre-hospital $\mathrm{mRS}0{-}2$ score, presenting NIHSS score, and neuroimaging used to assess the ASPECTS were also recorded. In terms of procedural characteristics, i.v. tissue plasminogen activator (i.v.-tPA) use, location of intracranial occlusion, and number of patients who underwent MT were noted. For outcome assessment, the final mTICI 2b-3 grade, post-procedure sICH rate, $90\mathrm{d}\mathrm{mRS}0{-}2$ score, 90d $\mathrm{mRS~}0{-}3$ score, and 90d mortality were recorded. The risk of bias of the selected articles was evaluated using the NewcastleOttawa Scale (NOS), in which study quality is graded based on three major categories: study population selection, comparability between groups, and outcome.19,20  

# Statistical analysis  

All statistical analyses were conducted using the R v. 3.6.3 package meta (https:// cran.r- project.org). For categorical variables, frequency with percentages were reported, whereas continuous variables were reported as means with the respective $95\%$ confidence interval (CI). Fixed-effects and random-effects metaanalyses for weighted estimation of overall rates and respective $95\%$ CIs were performed for each variable extracted from the included studies. Heterogeneity across studies was quantified using $\mathrm{I}^{2}$ statistics, in which values ${>}50\%$ suggest significant heterogeneity. Forest plots were generated for the proportions and estimated overall rates. Additionally, we utilized Egger’s regression test to evaluate for publication bias. A $\boldsymbol{p}$ -value $<0.05$ was considered statistically significant.

# RESULTS  

# Study selection  

A total of 1980 studies were identified when completing the search of the three databases. Of these studies, 357 were removed because they were duplicates of other included studies. Of the remaining 1627 studies, 1611 studies did not meet the inclusion criteria and were therefore excluded. A total of 18 studies were included in the final analysis, 16 from the original searches9–11,21–33 and 2 through searching the citations of recently published literature.34,35 A visual summary including a detailed breakdown of exclusion reasons with the respective number of studies is provided in the PRISMA flow diagram in Figure 1.  

# Study characteristics  

The 18 included studies were published between the years of 2014 and 2022.9–11,21–35 Among these studies, a total of 1958 patients presenting hyperacutely with anterior circulation stroke caused by large vessel occlusion with an ASPECTS of 0–5 were identified and included in the final analysis.9–11,21–35 Most studies (16 of 18) had a cumulative NOS score ${\ge}7$ indicating high quality (Table 1). The pooled mean age according to the randomeffects model was 64.1 years $95\%$ CI 54.01–74.23; $\mathrm{I}^{2}98.9\%^{\cdot}$ ). Regarding the severity of patient presentation, the pooled mean  

NIHSS score at presentation was 18.4 ( $95\%$ CI: 17.4–19.4). The ASPECTS score was analyzed in 10 $(55.6\%)$ studies with NCCT ,10,11,21,24,26,28,30,32,33,35 5 studies $(27.7\%)$ using MRI,9,25,27,29,34 and 3 studies $(16.7\%)$ using MRI and a CT modality.22,23,31 All patients included from these studies presented to the emergency room within less than 6 h of LKW.  

Among the demographics and outcomes analyzed, each of the following was found to have potential publication bias through assessment with Egger’s $\boldsymbol{p}$ -value: age $(p<.001)$ , hyperlipidemia $\left(p=.011\right)$ , smoking history ( $\primep=.005)$ , presenting NIHSS score $(p<.001)$ , final mTICI 2b-3 $\left(p=.006\right)$ , postprocedural sICH $(p=.044)$ , good outcome $\mathrm{mRS~}0{-}2$ score at 90d) $(p<.001)$ , and ambulatory outcome $\mathrm{'mRS~}0{-}3$ score at 90d) $(p=0.003)$ (Table  2).9–11,21–35 All other variables, including sex, diabetes mellitus, hypertension, atrial fibrillation, i.v.-tPA used, and 90d-­mortality, were found to have insignificant bias present among the included studies (Table 1).9–11,21–35

# 90-day post-procedural outcomes  

mRS score was the main outcome used in our analysis, in which good and ambulatory outcomes were recorded. All 18 studies reported the number of patients with good 90d outcomes, which were found to occur in $27.4\%$ $95\%$ CI 18.7–40.1; $\Gamma^{2}97\%$ ) patients (Figure 3a).9–11,21–35 The outcomes were further investigated by 11 $(61.1\%)$ of the 18 included studies as ambulatory in which the total number of patients who achieved an mRS score of 0–3 at 90 days was reported, and the pooled rate was $46.7\%$ (95% CI 35.1; 62.2; I2 98%) (Figure  3b).9–11,26–31,34,35 Among 10 studies that reportedly determined ASPECTS 0–5 on baseline CT, the mean rate of $\mathrm{mRS}0{-}2$ scores at 90 days was $23.1\%$ .10,11,21,24,26,28,30,32,33,35 Mortality rates at 90 days were reported in 9 of these 10 studies, with a resulting mean rate of $25.3\%$ .10,11,21,24,26,28,32,33,35 Among five studies reporting a determination of ASPECTS on baseline MRI, the mean rates of $\mathrm{mRS~}0{-}2$ scores and mortality at 90 days were 35.4 and $37.2\%$ , respectively.9,25,27,29,34 Among three studies reporting ASPECTS based on both CT and MRI at baseline, the rates of $\mathrm{mRS~}0{-}2$ scores and mortality at 90 days were 21.7 and $29.0\%$ , respectively.22,23,31 Finally, 90-day mortality rates were presented by 17 $(94.4\%)$ studies; in a pooled analysis, the mortality rate was $26.4\%$ $95\%$ CI 20.4–34.0; I2 91%) (Figure 4).9–11,21–29,31–35  

# DISCUSSION  

In this study, we reported radiographic, functional, and clinical outcomes through a comprehensive systematic review and meta-analysis of low ASPECTS anterior circulation AIS patients who presented hyperacutely $(\mathrm{LKW}<6\mathrm{h}$ ) for revascularization with MT. Our systematic review showed a high rate of successful recanalization (TICI 2b-3 in $76.4\%$ ), and ambulatory outcome $(90\mathrm{d}\mathrm{mRS}0{-}3\$ score in $46.7\%$ ), with an acceptable rate of postprocedural sICH $(12.1\%)$ , and 90d mortality $(26.4\%)$ . These results provide valuable information with a large sample size regarding the safety and effectiveness of MT for large-core, low ASPECTS patients who presented with hyperacute stroke.  

EVT is an established treatment modality for AIS patients with good ASPECTS $\left(\ge6\right)$ , especially with improved operator experience and recent advancements in neurointerventional device technologies (including third-generation stent retrievers and large-diameter aspiration catheters).1-6,36 Due to the exclusion of low ASPECTS $(\le5)$ patients with large baseline ischemic core from most RCTs, efficacy of EVT for these patients could not be established and remains controversial.2,3,5 Patients with ASPECTS of ${<}7$ were excluded from REVASCAT, and those with ASPECTS of ${<}6$ were excluded from ESCAPE and SWIFTPRIME.2,3,5 Exclusion of these patients was logical, because the primary objective of early revascularization is to prevent infarct progression and hence minimize the size of the final infarct volume.2,3,5 Until recently, patients with a large ischemic core and minimal salvageable penumbra were expected to suffer long-term dependence or death.2.3,5 The MR CLEAN investigators performed a subanalysis on the effect of ASPECTS on intraarterial efficacy.4 Because of the inadequate sample size, these researchers were unable to determine definitively if endovascular treatments improved the functional neurologic outcome (mRS scores) of patients with major infarcts at 3 months. In the MR CLEAN low ASPECTS (0–4) cohort, fewer than $5\%$ of patients achieved functional independence $\mathrm{~mRS~}0{-}2$ at 90d), and an approximately $40\%$ were deceased by 3 months.4 These results highlight the initial experience with MT in patients with a large ischemic core and contrast sharply with those from a more recent study as well as our meta-analysis.4

The selection of patients for MT based on ASPECTS obtained through either NCCT or MRI remains problematic. In our meta-analysis, of the 18 included studies, 5 reported ASPECTS based upon MRI, 10 did so based upon NCCT, and 3 determined ASPECTS using MRI and an NCCT imaging modality. This heterogeneity in terms of imaging accuracy is a significant limitation not only for our study but also in the three most recent trials: Recovery by Endovascular Salvage for Cerebral Ultra-­Acute Embolism–Japan Large Ischemic Core Trial (RESCUE-Japan LIMIT), Study of Endovascular Therapy in Acute Anterior Circulation Large Vessel Occlusive Patients With a Large Infarct Core (ANGEL-­ASPECT), and A Randomized Controlled Trial to Optimize Patient’s Selection for Endovascular Treatment in Acute Ischemic Stroke (SELECT2).31,37–39 In the RESCUE-Japan LIMIT trial, $86\%$ of the enrolled patients underwent MRI-based ASPECTS assessment, whereas the remainder were evaluated through NCCT-­ASPECTS.31 This brings both logistical and methodological issues to the forefront. The first issue arises from the fact that hyperacute MRI might not be accessible at most stroke centers, thus potentially limiting treatment opportunities and real-­world application of study results. Secondly, there is evidence suggesting that a DWI-­ASPECTS is often at least one point lower than an NCCT-­ASPECTS for the same patient. Consequently, this could result in the enrollment of patients for whom the advantages of MT are already recognized (DWI-­ASPECTS 5 or NCCT-­ASPECTS 6), possibly skewing the results towards favoring EVT. In contrast, SELECT 2 depended on NCCT-­ASPECTS and CT perfusion core volume calculation, accepting patients with NCCT-­ASPECTS scores ranging from 3 to 5 or a CT perfusion core (relative cerebral blood flow ${<}30\%$ greater than $50\mathrm{ml}$ ).39 Regrettably, these two selection methods were applied independently, rather than being used to complement each other.

Through our systematic review and pooled averages, we demonstrated that EVT may be beneficial in hyperacute stroke patients $(\mathrm{LKW}\leq6\mathrm{h}$ ) presenting with low ASPECTS. The potential benefit of EVT in these patients may be explained by the limited correlation between infarct volumes and baseline ASPECTS.40 Despite the fact that ASPECTS has a good correlation with outcome metrics, ASPECTS is not a weighed or linear scale; and, as a result, there are significant variances in regional eloquence and tissue volumes in the 10 scored areas.41 In one study included in our systematic review and meta-analysis, Bouslama et al demonstrated that depending on the size of the infarct, outcomes within the same ASPECTS category differ significantly.21 Patients who had an ASPECTS score of ${\le}5$ and an infarct volume of ${\le}70\mathrm{ml}$ achieved functional independence in approximately $40\%$ of MT cases vs $18.8\%$ who underwent medical therapy (adjusted $\mathrm{OR}~=~0.12$ , $95\%$ CI 0.016–0.912, $p=0.04)$ .21 In another study, Broocks et al showed that low ASPECTS patients (≤5) who have a good collateral score tend to have better ambulatory outcomes $\mathrm{~mRS~}0{-}3$ scores) than those with a poor collateral score.42 These results are similar to our analysis, whereby hyperacute stroke patients who underwent MT had a good outcome ( $\mathrm{~mRS~}0{-}2$ scores) in $27.4\%$ of cases but an ambulatory outcome $\mathrm{(mRS~}0-3$ score) in $46.7\%$ of cases. These results indicate the need to assess and select patients with a low ASPECTS score more closely and to not withhold EVT from the entire low ASPECTS population.  

Our study also highlights the problem with futile recanalization. In our analysis, although excellent immediate revascularization indicated by an mTICI 2b-3 grade was achieved in $76.4\%$ of cases, this did not fully translate to a good clinical outcomes 90d post-procedure. Neuroimaging and clinical characteristics must be considered while determining eligibility for MT.43,44 Individuals with salvageable tissue who could benefit from recanalization may be identified based on the presence of ischemic core and penumbral volume on neuroimaging.4 3,44 However, in up to $50\%$ of patients, effective recanalization cannot yield better results $\mathrm{'mRS}0{-}2$ or 0–3), which is referred to as futile recanalization.43,44 Therefore, it is crucial to consider clinical criteria when choosing individuals who have enough brain reserve to recover from ischemic damage.  

To date, the most compelling evidence in favor of endovascular intervention has been provided by the results of the RESCUE-Japan LIMIT, as well as the more recent results of the ANGEL-­ASPECT and SELECT2 RCTs.31,37,38 In the results from RESCUE-Japan LIMIT, patients randomized to the EVT arm had significantly better outcomes $\mathrm{'mRS}0{-}3$ scores) at 90 days when compared with those in the medical management arm $31.0\%$ vs $12.7\%$ ; relative risk $=2.4$ ; $95\%$ CI (1.4–4.4); $p=0.002,$ ). Patients in the endovascular arm also had significantly better improvement in the NIHSS score of 8 points or higher at $^{48\mathrm{h}}$ than those in the medically managed group $(31.0\%$ vs $8.8\%$ ; relative risk $=3.51$ ; $95\%\mathrm{CI}=1.76{-}7.00)$ .  

The results of the ANGEL-­ASPECT and SELECT2 RCTs were similar to those demonstrated in RESCUE-Japan LIMIT. In published findings from ANGEL-­ASPECT, patients randomized to the EVT arm showed a shift towards better outcomes (defined as $\mathrm{mRS}0{-}2$ scores at 90 days) than those in the medicalmanagement group (generalized odds ratio, 1.37; $95\%\mathrm{CI}$ (1.11–1.69); $p=0.004)$ .38 In that study, higher rates of both symptomatic and any intracranial hemorrhage were observed in the EVT group than the medical-­management group $(6.1~\nus~2.7$ and $49.1~\nus17.3\%$ , respectively).38 The results of the SELECT2 trial demonstrated a shift towards better outcomes (lower mRS scores at 90 days) in the thrombectomy treatment group than the medical management group (generalized odds ratio, 1.51; $95\%\mathrm{CI}$ (1.20–1.89; $p<0.001,$ ).45 That trial also revealed higher rates of functional independence in patients treated with thrombectomy relative to the medical management group (relative risk, 2.97; $95\%\mathrm{CI}$ , 1.60–5.51).45 Both trials were stopped early following the demonstration of efficacy in interim analyses.45,46 It is important to note that only patients with an ASPECTS of 3-5 were included in both RESCUE-Japan LIMIT and ANGELASPECT, with exclusion of all patients who presented with an ASPECTS of 0–2. This allowed for the identification and exclusion of patients who are likely to have poor neurologic outcomes because of a large infarcted core. This same inclusion criteria is not reported by the SELECT2 trial.39 The highly encouraging results from RESCUE-Japan LIMIT, ANGEL-ASPECT, and SELECT2 have provided much needed Level I evidence, which highlights an expected paradigm shift in selecting and treating eligible large-core patients with available endovascular interventions.  

The following three RCTs are also being conducted currently to assess the safety and efficacy of EVT for low ASPECTS large-core patients: Thrombectomy for Emergent Salvage of Large Anterior Circulation Ischemic Stroke, Efficacy and Safety of Thrombectomy in Stroke With Extended Lesion and Extended Time Window: A Randomized, Controlled Trial, and Large Stroke Therapy Evaluation.39,45,46 These trials are expected to provide further evidence in terms of patient selection and functional and neurologic outcomes.  

Our study has several limitations. The topic of hyperacute strokes with low ASPECTS is fairly recent, and studies specifically designed to examine outcomes among this patient population are sparsely discussed in the literature. A uniform analysis was difficult because the methodology and key outcomes of the studies varied. Furthermore, our study may be biased due to absence of an agreed-upon protocol. An inherent limitation of the study is the varying stroke protocols implemented at different centers with some relying more on CT perfusion and collateral circulation scoring to guide EVT decision-­making and treating low ASPECTS patients who would otherwise not be treated if the decision solely relied on head NCCT. However, our sound methods and bias assessment help address some of the limitation. Despite these shortcomings, we present the largest systematic review and meta-analysis demonstrating key findings in terms of clinical outcomes and safety profile of MT in low ASPECTS patients presenting within $^{6\mathrm{h}}$ of stroke onset.  

# CONCLUSION  

Endovascular recanalization in patients with hyperacute anterior circulation stroke presenting with low ASPECTS score (≤5) may result in ambulatory clinical outcomes ( $\mathrm{~mRS~}0{-}3$ scores at 3 months) with an acceptable risk of hemorrhage for this unique cohort. Although a high proportion of patients achieve successful recanalization, this benefit is not fully translated to better clinical outcomes.
