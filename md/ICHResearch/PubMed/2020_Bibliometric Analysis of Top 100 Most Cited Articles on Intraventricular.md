# Bibliometric Analysis of Top 100 Most Cited Articles on Intraventricular Hemorrhage  

Turki Elarjani1, Othman T. Almutairi2, Modhi A. Alhussinan4, Nada Alnefaie5, Gmaan Alzhrani2, Mohammed Bafaquh2, Abdulrahman Y. Alturki2,3  

- BACKGROUND: Intraventricular hemorrhage (IVH) is a rare cause of intracranial bleeding across all age groups, with dismal sequelae in most of the affected population. The reported data on IVH are numerous, with multiple levels of evidence. We performed a citation-based analysis to identify the most-cited reports on IVH.  

- METHODS: A thorough search of the Scopus database was conducted using “intraventricular hemorrhage” as the search keyword. The 100 most cited studies were stratified in descending order. The reports were reviewed in-depth and categorized accordingly. Bibliometric parameters of interest were obtained for analysis.  

- RESULTS: The most-cited studies had been published between 1927 and 2017. Most $(n=60)$ had been published between 1980 and 2000. The most-cited studies had received a total of 16,512 citations, with an average of 174 citations per report. Studies on pathogenesis were the most prolific, with 23 articles included. A total of 13 randomized controlled trials were identified. The top contributing country was the United States, with 67 reports. The leading institution was the Washington University School of Medicine in St. Louis, Missouri, with 11 studies. Pediatrics was the most active journal, with 20 studies.  

- CONCLUSION: The present collection of highly cited studies can aid in the understanding of chronological trends and could serve as an efficient guide to delineate the reports involved in the evidence-based practice of the management of IVH.  

# INTRODUCTION  

ntraventricular hemorrhage (IVH) is defined as bleeding within the ventricles. IVH can be divided according to the etiology and age group as adult and neonatal types. Adult IVH can result from primary causes $(3\%$ of cases) or, more often, secondary causes, with spontaneous nontraumatic intracerebral hemorrhage (ICH) the etiology for almost one half of the cases.1-5 The reported mortality rates of adults with IVH in prospective observational studies have ranged from $42\%$ to $78\bar{\%}.^{2,\bar{4},6,7}$ In contrast, neonatal IVH will be caused by bleeding within the germinal matrix. The incidence of neonatal IVH has been $\sim38\%$ in newborn infants with a very low birth weight (i.e., <1500 g).8 Adult IVH management is mainly concerned with limiting the extent of bleeding, preventing the progression of hydrocephalus, and controlling the intracranial pressure.5,9,10 However, neonatal IVH management has been primarily preventive, including supportive measures directed toward cerebral blood flow fluctuations and early detection and management of complications, such as hydrocephalus.11-13

# METHODS  

The use of the Scopus database was selected to perform a titlespecific search with no time restrictions in June 2020 using “intraventricular hemorrhage” as the search keyword. The outcome of the data search was stratified according to each article’s citation count (CC), and the top 100 most-cited studies were selected for further analysis. The parameters of interest were obtained from the selected studies, which included the following: article title, authors, affiliation of the first author, journal of publication, country of origin, year of publication, and CC according to the Scopus database.  

An in-depth review of the top 100 articles to identify the study categories was performed. The top 100 studies were categorized according to the age group (adult vs. pediatric). Additional subcategorization of each age group led to the clustering of the studies into the following categories: clinical, diagnosis, epidemiology, etiology, experimental, neuropathology, pathogenesis, prevention, and prognosis. The pertinent bibliometric parameters of interest were obtained: article citation per year (CY), authors’ Hindex, Journal’s Source Normalized Impact Per Paper, and Journal’s SCImago Journal Rank.  

# RESULTS  

The bibliometric-based search identified 2113 studies discussing IVH, which had been reported from 1927 to 2020 (Figure 1). The 100 most-cited studies of IVH had received a total of 16,512 citations, with an average of 174.15 citations per study and an overall $5{\cdot}4\%$ rate of self-citations for all authors (Supplementary Table 1). Studies on IVH were very prevalent in the 1980s and 2000s, in which $\sim6\0$ studies had been reported from highly cited studies on IVH. An overview of the publication trends showed that most of the reports (approximately two thirds) had focused on pathogenesis, prognosis, and etiology (Figures 2 and 3). Almost $7\mathrm{{r}^{0}/\mathrm{{0}}}$ of the studies had addressed neonatal IVH and $29\%$ , adult IVH.  

A detailed analysis showed that 13 randomized controlled trials (RCTs) had been reported on IVH, with their CCs ranging from 83 to 281 per clinical trial (Table 1). The study with the highest CC on IVH was reported in $\mathrm{\Pi}^{1987}$ by Papile et al.,32 in the Journal of Pediatrics entitled “incidence and evolution of subependymal and intraventricular hemorrhage: a study of infants with birth weights less than $\mathrm{\bar{1}},500~\mathrm{gm}_{;}$ ,” with a 3818 CC and 90.9 CY.

# DISCUSSION  

The chronological trends in IVH studies had focused primarily on the neonatal population for the etiology, risk factors, radiological identification using ultrasonography, and outcomes. Later, more studies had focused on the association of cerebral blood flow velocity and the elevated IVH risk in those with respiratory distress syndrome (RDS). Clinical trials had tested indomethacin and phenobarbital for IVH prevention. In the 1990s, more studies had investigated the role of tissue plasminogen activator (t-PA) and urokinase in treating IVH. More studies were interested in investigating the time course of IVH development from the germinal matrix and associated patent ductus arteriosus as a risk factor. At the beginning of the 21st century, the number of RCTs on IVH was at its peak. A significant emphasis was found for the use of an intraventricular injection of t-PA and delayed cord clamping for management. More RCTs had focused on the natural history, especially if hydrocephalus is present.

The highest cited study (epidemiology category; neonatal IVH study) in the top 100 list, with a $3^{8\mathrm{I}8}$ CC and $_{90.9}\mathrm{CY}$ , was the 1978 study entitled “incidence and evolution of subependymal and intraventricular hemorrhage: a study of infants with birth weights less than $\mathrm{~\tt~{~I~}~},500\mathrm{~gm}^{,}$ by Papile et al.,32 in the Journal of Pediatrics. Their study was the first to introduce the IVH grading system using computed tomography (CT) and concluded that neonates with grade IIIeIV IVH were prone to develop hydrocephalus.32  

The second highest cited study (prognosis category; neonatal IVH article) and the first in the prognosis category, with a 402 CC and 10.86 CY, was the 1983 study entitled “relationship of cerebral intraventricular hemorrhage and early childhood neurologic handicaps” by Papile et al.,33 in the Journal of Pediatrics. They studied the outcomes stratified by the IVH grade and reported that neonates with grade IeII IVH had better outcomes in terms of developmental and neuromotor handicaps $\left(\mathtt{I}^{0}/\mathfrak{o}$ and $\mathrm{{\bf~I}}\mathrm{{\bf~I}}^{0}/\mathrm{{\bf~O}}$ respectively) compared with the outcomes for those with grade IIIeIV IVH $(36\%$ and $76\%$ , respectively).33 In addition, the same investigators had reported the top 2 most-cited articles, which are considered landmark publications in neonatal IVH because they introduced the grading system, which is still used today.

# Pathogenesis Category  

The reports in the pathogenesis category (peak publications, 2000e2014) had studied the relationship of venous stasis, changes in mean blood pressure and autoregulation, metabolic acidosis, and hypoxia leading to IVH. Subsequently, the maternale placental association with IVH and the pathophysiology of patent ductus arteriosus and IVH were studied. Highly cited publications on neonatal IVH populated this category.

The top-cited study in the pathology category (ranked third overall; neonatal IVH article), with a 339 CC and 9.16 CY, was the 1983 study entitled “fluctuating cerebral blood-flow velocity in respiratory-distress syndrome: relation to the development of intraventricular hemorrhage” by Perlman et al.,34 in the New England Journal of Medicine. They had studied the relationship of the cerebral blood flow velocity alterations through Doppler examination of the anterior cerebral artery in infants weighing $<\mathbf{I}500~\mathbf{g}$ with RDS.34 They concluded that RDS causes a severe alteration in the cerebral blood flow velocity, which could result in IVH.  

Another study (ranked 13th overall; neonatal IVH article), with a 195 CC and 8.13 CY, was the 1996 report entitled “early ductal shunting and intraventricular hemorrhage in ventilated preterm infants” by Evans et al.,35 in the Archives of Disease in Childhood: Fetal and Neonatal Edition. Their results showed that infants with more significant patent ductus arteriosus, lower right ventricular output, and inadequate antenatal steroid administration were more prone to develop IVH.35  

# Prognosis Category  

The studies in the prognosis category (peak publications, 2000e 2015) had focused solely on mortality, neurodevelopmental deficits, and hydrocephalus as the outcomes of IVH. The highest cited articles in this category had targeted the adult age group. The highest-ranking RCT in this category (ranked 18th overall; adult IVH article), with a 182 CC and $\scriptstyle{\mathbf{I}3}\mathbf{CY}$ , was the 2006 study entitled “intraventricular hemorrhage and hydrocephalus after spontaneous intracerebral hemorrhage: results from the STICH trial” by Bhattathiri et al.,2 in Acta Neurochirurgica Supplementum. The presence of IVH will worsen the outcomes of patients with ICH $\begin{array}{rlr}{\left(\mathrm{P}}&{{}<}&{0.0000\mathrm{I}\right)}\end{array}$ ). Hydrocephalus will result in further worse outcomes in patients with ICH and IVH $\left(\mathrm{P}=0.03\right)$ ). However, prompt surgical management of IVH and hydrocephalus in patients with ICH yielded a favorable outcome, although the difference was not statistically significant.  

The second most cited RCT (ranked 19th overall; adult IVH article), with a 174 CC and 12.43 CY, was the 2006 study entitled “dynamics of intraventricular hemorrhage in patients with spontaneous intracerebral hemorrhage: risk factors, clinical impact, and effect of hemostatic therapy with recombinant activated factor VII” by Steiner et al.,4 in Neurosurgery. IVH growth was less in the patients who had received recombinant factor VII versus placebo $[\mathrm{IO}^{\mathrm{0}}/\mathrm{0}$ vs. $\mathrm{\mathbf{I}}7^{\mathrm{\mathbf{0}}}/\mathrm{\mathbf{0}}$ , respectively). Additionally, they reported that the predictors of IVH growth were a larger ICH size at presentation, the presence of IVH with clinical symptoms, a mean arterial pressure ${>}\mathrm{I}60\mathrm{mm}\mathrm{Hg}$ , treatment with recombinant factor VII, and a shorter time from clinical presentation to IVH $\left(\mathrm{P}<0.037\right.$ ).4  

# Etiology Category  

Studies assessing the etiological factors of IVH had evaluated the risk factors in neonatal IVH in the later years, emphasizing white matter disease and hypercapnia as risk factors. The peak publication years were $_{19}82\mathrm{-r}9^{8}3$ and 2002e2006. The highest cited article in this category (ranked eighth overall; neonatal IVH article), with a 253 CC and $6.66\mathrm{CY}$ , was the 1982 study entitled “risk factors in the development of intraventricular hemorrhage in the preterm neonate” by Levene et al.,36 in Archives of Disease in Childhood. They studied 32 risk factors correlating with IVH. Only hypercapnia (due to RDS) and acidosis were significant factors in causing IVH $[8\mathrm{r}^{0}/\mathrm{o}$ risk).36 Their study is considered a landmark publication because it connected RDS to IVH.36  

# Management Category  

The management category had the highest number of RCTs (6 trials), with the peak publication numbers in 2000e2017. Most studies and trials investigated the utility of t-PA in the management of IVH. The highest cited RCT in the management category (ranked ninth overall; neonatal IVH article), with a 250 CC and 17.86 CY, was the 2006 study entitled “delayed cord clamping in very preterm infants reduces the incidence of intraventricular hemorrhage and late-onset sepsis: a randomized, controlled trial” by Mercer et al., in Pediatrics. Their trial showed a protective effect of delayed cord clamping to treat IVH in deficient bodyweight neonates compared with early cord clamping.22 The second highest RCT in the management category (ranked 25th overall; adult IVH article), with a 153 CC and 7.65 CY, was the 2000 study entitled “treatment of intraventricular hemorrhage with urokinase: effects on 30-day survival” by Naff et al.,23 in Stroke. The injection of intraventricular urokinase led to an observed 30-day mortality of $25\%$ (the expected 30-day mortality rate was $58\%$ ).  

The third highest ranked RCT (ranked 45th overall; adult IVH article), with a 129 CC and $_{43}\mathrm{CY}$ , was the 2017 study entitled “thrombolytic removal of intraventricular hemorrhage in treatment of severe stroke: results of the randomised, multicentre, multiregion, placebo-controlled CLEAR III trial” by Hanley et al.,25 in Lancet. No difference in the primary outcome (functional status) was found in the alteplase group compared with the normal saline-injected group.25 The fourth highest-ranked RCT (ranked 50th overall; adult IVH article), with a 123 CC and 13.66 CY, was the 2011 study entitled “low-dose recombinant t-PA enhances clot resolution in brain hemorrhage: the intraventricular hemorrhage thrombolysis trial” by Naff et al.,26 in Stroke. The patient group that had received recombinant t-PA did not have a statistically significant survival and functional outcome advantage. However, clot resolution was greater in the recombinant t-PA group $\mathrm{'}\mathrm{I}8\%$ vs. $8\%$ of placebo group; $\begin{array}{r}{\mathrm{P}<0.00\mathrm{I},}\end{array}$ ).26  

The last RCT in the management category (ranked 88th overall; neonatal IVH article) with an $^{87}$ CC and 6.7 CY was the 2007 study entitled “randomized clinical trial of prevention of hydrocephalus after intraventricular hemorrhage in preterm infants: brainwashing versus tapping fluid” by Whitelaw et al.,30 in Pediatrics. All patients had hydrocephalus; however, the investigators found no statistically significance differences in drainage, irrigation, and injection of fibrinolytic therapy in terms of reducing shunt surgery and mortality compared with the standard treatment of tapping the reservoir. In contrast, the treatment group had a greater risk of further IVH.30  

# Clinical Category  

The studies in the category (peak publication years, 1981e1989) had broadly focused on neonatal and adult IVH, incorporating the various risk factors, diagnostic methods, management, and outcomes in most studies. They served as review studies. The highest cited report in the clinical category (ranked sixth overall; adult IVH article), with a 293 CC and 7.71 CY, was the 1982 study entitled “computed tomographic diagnosis of intraventricular hemorrhage: etiology and prognosis” by Graeb et al.,37 in Radiology. They summarized the different causes of IVH, including aneurysm rupture and hypertensive hemorrhage. In addition, they concluded that IVH mortality can reach as high as $50\%$ .37  

# Epidemiology Category  

The epidemiology category (diverse peak publications, $\scriptstyle{\mathrm{I97}}^{\mathrm{8-r_{\mathrm{97}}}\mathrm{9},}$ 1983e1989, $\scriptstyle{\ttIgg3-Igg9})$ included studies that had evaluated the incidence of neonatal IVH and the incidence of the associated hydrocephalus. Within the next 2 decades, investigators were interested in studying the chronology of the incidence rates in neonatal IVH and in comparing the incidence between different countries. The second highest cited article in this category (neonatal IVH article), with a 143 CC and $4\cdot^{6\ttI}$ CY, was the 1989 study entitled “intraventricular hemorrhage in preterm infants: declining incidence in the 1980s” by Philip et al.,21 in Pediatrics. The investigators demonstrated a decrease in true IVH from $22\%$ in 1980e1981 to $7\%$ in 1986e1987.21  

# Diagnosis Category  

The reports included in the diagnosis category (peak publications, $\scriptstyle{\mathrm{~I~}974^{-}{\mathrm{~I~}}979}$ , 1999) had initially studied the CT appearance of IVH, followed by a Doppler examination through the fontanelles. Some later studies had focused on the blood levels of S100 and prediction. The highest cited article in the diagnosis category (ranked 17th overall; neonatal IVH article), with a 180 CC and 4.39 CY, was the 1979 study entitled “noninvasive diagnosis of neonatal asphyxia and intraventricular hemorrhage by Doppler ultrasound” by Bada et al.,38 in the Journal of Pediatrics. The pulsatility index was measured and found to be lower than normal in infants with asphyxia due to vasodilation. In contrast, the pulsatility index was higher in infants with intracerebraleintraventricular hemorrhage, indicating an increase in blood flow owing to low vascular resistance.38  

# Prevention Category  

The prevention category (peak publications, 1981e1988) included 5 RCTs on indomethacin administration in preterm neonates and steroid administration in pregnant women to prevent IVH. The highest cited RCT (ranked seventh overall; neonatal IVH article), with a 281 CC and 10.8 CY, was the 1994 study entitled “low-dose indomethacin and prevention of intraventricular hemorrhage: a multicenter randomized trial” by Ment et al.,39 in Pediatrics. They concluded that neonates who had received indomethacin had a low incidence of IVH $\left(\mathrm{P}=0.03\right.$ ) and lower severity of IVH $(\mathrm{{P}=}$ 0.01).  

The second highest cited RCT (ranked 29th overall; neonatal IVH article), with a $_{\ttI4}6$ CC and $5.84$ CY, was the 1995 study entitled “antenatal steroids, delivery mode, and intraventricular hemorrhage in preterm infants” by Ment et al.24 in the American Journal of Obstetrics and Gynecology. Neonates who had been delivered via cesarean section and had received indomethacin were less likely to develop early IVH $\mathrm{\Delta(P=o.o3}$ and $\mathrm{~P~}<0.000\mathrm{I}$ respectively) compared with neonates delivered vaginally who had not received indomethacin $\left(\mathrm{P}=0.02\right)\$ ).24 Another RCT (ranked 60th overall; neonatal IVH article), with a 107 CC and 2.74 CY, was the 1981 study entitled “prevention of intraventricular haemorrhage in preterm infants by phenobarbitone: a controlled trial” by Donn et al., in Lancet. Intravenous phenobarbitone injection decreased the incidence of IVH to $\mathbf{I}3.3\%$ compared with the control group with an incidence of $46.7\%$ .29  

# Experimental Category  

The studies included in the experimental category (diverse publication years, 1980s, 1990s, 2000s) had primarily tested animal models and simulated IVH to test the effects of blood pressure and cerebrospinal fluid circulation. The highest cited article (ranked 66th overall), with a 102 CC and 4.43 CY, was the 1997 study entitled “ventricular dilatation in experimental intraventricular hemorrhage in pigs: characterization of cerebrospinal fluid dynamics and the effects of fibrinolytic treatment” by Mayfrank et al.,40 in Stroke. They showed that the ventricular size had increased after IVH $\left(\mathrm{P}<0.00\mathrm{I}\right)$ ) and that the ventricular size had a linear positive correlation with the clot size $\left(\mathrm{P}~=~0.014\right.,$ ). In addition, the cerebrospinal fluid output resistance increased progressively $\mathit{\check{P}}<0.00\mathtt{I}\$ .40

# Study Limitations  

The bibliometric-based inherent limitations were also present in our study, resulting in the risk of exaggerated results owing to citation accumulation during a specific year. However, we minimized this risk with the use of the CY. Also, author self-citation was minimal in our analysis.  

# Neuropathology Category  

The neuropathology category (peak publications, 1987e1989) included 2 studies related to germinal matrix pathology. The highest cited article (ranked 15th overall; neonatal IVH article), with a 190 CC and 6.13 CY, was the $\scriptstyle{\mathrm{I}9}^{89}$ study entitled “intraventricular hemorrhage in the premature infant—current concepts: part $\mathrm{I}^{\mathfrak{N}}$ by Volpe et al.,41 in the Annals of Neurology. The investigators divided the pathophysiological findings into 3 types: 1) intravascular, with perturbations in cerebral blood flow and increased cerebral venous pressure; 2) vascular, with a relationship found between germinal matrix vessel wall vulnerability and ischemia; and 3) extravascular, related to glial and mesenchymal support of the germinal matrix.41  

# CONCLUSION  

The results of the present study have shown that IVH studies had an initial trend of identifying the etiology and risk factors, mainly in neonatal IVH. Subsequently, the association between neonatal IVH and RDS was extensively studied. Later, more clinical trials had studied the utility of intraventricular thrombolysis injections in adults. The results from the present analysis can serve as an introductory guide to adult and neonatal IVH for multiple clinical specialties, ranging from pediatrics to neurosurgery. In addition, we have identified areas in need of proper investigation for future projects.
