# Safety of Adjunctive Intraarterial Tenecteplase Following Mechanical Thrombectomy: The ALLY Pilot Trial  

Syed F. Zaidi , MD\*; Alicia C. Castonguay , PhD\*; Osama O. Zaidat , MD, MS; Ashutosh P. Jadhav , MD, PhD; Sunil A. Sheth , MD; Diogo C. Haussen , MD; Thanh N. Nguyen , MD; Richard E. Burgess, MD, PhD; Hisham S. Alhajala , MD; Khaled Gharaibeh, MD; Hisham Salahuddin , MD; Rahul Rao , MD; Marion J. Oliver, MD; Mouhammad A. Jumaa , MD  

BACKGROUND: Recent studies suggest that the use of adjunctive intraarterial alteplase after mechanical thrombectomy (MT) may improve outcomes; however, there are limited data on the use of intraarterial tenecteplase, a newer-generation lytic, in this acute ischemic stroke patient population. Here, we evaluate the use of intraarterial tenecteplase in the ALLY pilot study (Adjunctive Intraarterial Tenecteplase Following Mechanical Thrombectomy).  

METHODS: ALLY was a prospective, single-center, nonrandomized pilot study assessing the feasibility and safety of intraarterial tenecteplase up to $4.5~\mathrm{mg}$ in acute ischemic stroke-large vessel occlusion MT patients with incomplete recanalization. The primary safety end point was any intracranial hemorrhage and neurological worsening by ${\ge}4$ points on the National Institutes of Health Stroke Scale within 24 hours of treatment with intraarterial tenecteplase. A post hoc analysis was performed with a control cohort of MT patients (ALLY MT) not receiving intraarterial tenecteplase.  

RESULTS: From April 2022 to July 2023, 218 MTs were performed at ProMedica Hospital (Toledo, OH), of which 20 patients were enrolled in ALLY. The mean age was $66.1\pm13.8$ years, with $35\%$ women. Median baseline National Institutes of Health Stroke Scale scores and Alberta Stroke Program Early CT Scores were 13 (interquartile range, 9–18.8) and 10 (interquartile range, 9–10), respectively. IV thrombolysis was administered in $55\%$ . Most patients presented with middle cerebral artery occlusion $(90\%)$ ). Post-MT modified Treatment in Cerebral Ischemia grade was 2b and $_{2\mathsf{C}}$ in 11 and 9 patients, respectively. Final modified Treatment in Cerebral Ischemia 2b, 2c, and 3 was achieved in $55\%$ (11/20), $35\%$ (7/20), and $10\%$ (2/20), respectively. Any intracranial hemorrhage was observed in 11 patients; however, only 1 patient had symptomatic intracranial hemorrhage. A favorable functional outcome (modified Rankin Scale score, 0–2) at 90 days was achieved in $50\%$ . No difference in intracranial hemorrhage rates was observed between the ALLY and ALLY MT cohorts.  

CONCLUSIONS: The use of adjunctive intraarterial tenecteplase up to $4.5\mathrm{mg}$ in patients with acute ischemic stroke with incomplete reperfusion post-MT is feasible and was not associated with increased rates of hemorrhage. Larger, randomized studies are needed to assess the safety and efficacy of intraarterial tenecteplase in this population.

echanical thrombectomy (MT) is an established treatment for acute ischemic stroke (AIS) due to large vessel occlusion.1–5 The success of MT in preventing death and disability is highly dependent on the degree of reperfusion.6 However, results from randomized pivotal trials show that only one-third of the treated patients achieve complete reperfusion. Most MT procedures result in incomplete or near-complete reperfusion, which can contribute to a higher risk of strokerelated death and disability.7  

More typically, following MT with reperfusion less than modified Treatment in Cerebral Ischemia (mTICI) grade 3, residual distal clots may not be amenable to mechanical clot retrieval. In such settings, pharmacological thrombolysis may play a role in augmenting the degree of post-MT reperfusion. This synergy between the pharmacological agents, mainly intraarterial alteplase, and MT is supported by recent observational studies.8–11 The randomized CHOICE trial (Chemical Optimization of Cerebral Embolectomy) showed a higher rate of excellent functional outcomes among patients treated with postMT intraarterial alteplase compared with placebo.12  

Tenecteplase is a third-generation fibrinolytic agent that has been genetically modified from alteplase to confer a higher affinity for fibrin, reduced binding to plasminogen inhibitor-1, and a longer half-life. These improved pharmacodynamic and pharmacokinetic properties clinically translate to a lower dose and shorter infusion times. Several clinical trials have investigated the intravenous use of tenecteplase in patients with AIS, demonstrating its efficacy in thrombolysis, with some trials showing noninferiority compared with alteplase.13–17 However, it has yet to be systematically studied as an intraarterial adjunct in the setting of MT, which may require a lower dosage of tenecteplase due to its localized administration.  

The ALLY (Adjunctive Intraarterial Tenecteplase Following Mechanical Thrombectomy) pilot study assessed the feasibility and safety of adjunctive intraarterial tenecteplase in patients with AIS with anterior large vessel occlusion following incomplete reperfusion after MT.  

# METHODS  

# Trial Design  

The authors declare that all supporting data are available within the article (and its online supplementary files). The ALLY pilot trial was an investigator-initiated, prospective, phase 2b, nonrandomized, single-center trial of acute ischemic stroke patients treated with standard of care MT that achieved incomplete recanalization (mTICI 2b or 2c). All patients received postMT adjunctive intraarterial tenecteplase (up to $4.5\mathrm{mg}$ ) and were followed up to 3 months post-intraarterial tenecteplase treatment. Adverse event monitoring and adjudication were performed by an independent medical safety monitor. All imaging data points were independently adjudicated by the imaging core laboratory.  

The study protocol was approved by the local site institutional review board and performed at ProMedica Hospital (Toledo, OH). Written informed consent could be obtained up until 10 minutes after the patient had achieved mTICI 2b or 2c reperfusion. Patients were considered enrolled in ALLY if: (1) the patient or legally authorized representative had signed the informed consent form, (2) all eligibility criteria were met, and (3) the patient had been treated with adjunctive intraarterial tenecteplase. The trial was registered on clinicaltrials. gov (URL: https://www.clinicaltrials.gov; Unique identifier: NCT05172934). The study sponsor had no role in the design, analysis, or writing of the article.  

# Eligibility  

Patients were considered for the ALLY trial if the following key inclusion criteria were met: (1) aged 18 to 85 years, (2) anterior circulation stroke due to intracranial internal carotid artery, middle cerebral artery (MCA) M1, or M2 occlusions treated with MT within 24 hours of stroke symptoms, (3) ${\le}5$ device passes, and (4) mTICI grade 2b or 2c with persistent occlusion(s) in terminal branches deemed not amenable to MT. Patients who had received intravenous thrombolysis before MT were permitted. For patients receiving thrombolysis, a repeat scan was not required before enrolling in the ALLY trial. A complete list of inclusion and exclusion criteria is in Table S1.  

The baseline scan requirement for patients treated within 6 hours of presentation was a noncontrast head CT scan with Alberta Stroke Program Early CT Score ${\ge}6$ ; for patients treated between 6 and 24 hours, a favorable CT or MR perfusion scan defined as baseline ischemic core volume of ${<}70\mathrm{mL}$ , mismatch ratio of ${>}1.8$ , and mismatch volume $>15~\mathsf{mL}$ was required.  

# Intervention  

MT was performed per the enrolling center’s standard of care protocol. All enrolled patients received up to three $1.5\mathrm{mg}$ boluses of adjunctive intraarterial tenecteplase (minimum dose of $1.5\mathrm{mg}$ , maximum dose of $4.5\mathrm{mg}$ ). The concentration of intraarterial tenecteplase was $0.5\mathrm{mg/mL}$ , administered as a $3\mathrm{mL}$ bolus over 1 minute. After administration of the intraarterial tenecteplase dose, an angiographic run was performed from the guide catheter after 5 minutes. Another bolus was recommended if the angiographic run showed persistent occlusion(s) with less than mTICI 3 recanalization. Repeat intraarterial tenecteplase dosing was at the discretion of the operator. For single distal occlusions, the microcatheter was placed as proximal to the distal occlusion as possible for intraarterial tenecteplase administration. For multiple distal occlusions, the microcatheter was placed distal to the M1 segment, at the level of the MCA bifurcation. However, in complex anatomy or multiple distal occlusions, the microcatheter could be placed in the distal M1 segment at the level of the MCA bifurcation. The intraarterial tenecteplase administration protocol was designed to allow for flexibility in administration of the tenecteplase to observe the revascularization after each incremental dose.  

# Study Outcomes  

The primary safety end point was any intracranial hemorrhage defined as neurological worsening of at least 4 points on the National Institutes of Health Stroke Scale, according to the second ECASS II (European-Australasian Acute Stroke Study)18 criteria within 24 hours of treatment with intraarterial tenecteplase.  

The secondary end points were: (1) improved reperfusion (mTICI 2c/3) adjudicated by the core laboratory at the end of intraarterial treatment, (2) asymptomatic intracranial hemorrhage, (3) final angiographic reperfusion grade at the end of the procedure, and (4) 90-day ordinal modified Rankin Scale (mRS).  

A favorable mRS was defined as mRS 0 to 2, and an excellent mRS was defined as 0 to 1 at 90 days. Mortality at 90 days and baseline mRS as a categorized variable will also be reported.  

# Post Hoc Analysis  

A cohort of patients with AIS that underwent MT (but were not enrolled in the ALLY trial) during the trial period was identified from the enrolling center’s MT database. Patients were included in the ALLY MT cohort if the following inclusion criteria were met: (1) age 18 to 85 years, (2) internal carotid artery, M1, or M2 occlusion, (3) achieved Thrombolysis in Cerebral Infarction [TICI] 2b or TICI 2c at the end of the MT procedure, and (4) Alberta Stroke Program Early CT Score ${\ge}6$ . Baseline characteristics and clinical and procedural outcomes were compared between the ALLY and ALLY MT cohorts.  

# Statistical Analysis  

For the ALLY subjects, standard descriptive statistics, including mean, SD, and median with interquartile range, are used for continuous variables, and frequency distributions are presented for categorical variables. Comparisons were made with the control subjects using the Fisher Exact test for the continuous and $\chi^{2}$ for categorical variables.  

# RESULTS  

The ALLY trial was conducted between April 2022 and July 2023. During the 15-month trial period, 218 MTs were performed at the study site. Of these, 107 resulted in TICI 2b and 2c recanalization (Figure 1). Twenty consecutive patients met the study criteria and were enrolled after obtaining informed consent.

# Clinical Outcomes  

Of the 20 ALLY subjects, $50\%$ (10/20) attained favorable functional outcomes (mRS, 0–2) at 90 days, with $20\%$ achieving excellent functional outcomes (mRS, $0{-}1$ ). The 90-day mortality rate was $15\%$ (3/20) in the ALLY cohort (Table 2).  

# Post Hoc Analysis  

A retrospective cohort of 61 post-MT TICI 2b and 2c patients (ALLY MT) treated during the study period was compared with ALLY study patients. Baseline characteristics were comparable between the cohorts; however, IV thrombolysis use was higher in the ALLY patients $55\%$ versus $21.3\%$ , $\widetilde{P{=}0.01}$ ; Table 1). No difference in any intracranial hemorrhage or symptomatic intracranial hemorrhage (sICH) was observed between the cohorts (Table 2). The rates of good functional outcomes (mRS, 0–2) and mortality were similar between the groups: $50\%$ versus $37.7\%$ $(P=0.4)$ ) and mortality ( $15\%$ versus $22.6\%$ , $\mathtt{P=}0.5$ ), respectively.

# DISCUSSION  

In the phase 2b ALLY pilot study, the use of adjunctive intraarterial tenecteplase up to $4.5~\mathrm{mg}$ was feasible and safe after incomplete reperfusion for occlusions of the anterior circulation (internal carotid artery, MCA). To our knowledge, this is the first prospective study to demonstrate the feasibility and safety of adjunctive intraarterial tenecteplase after MT in this AIS population.  

The use of intraarterial tPA (tissue-type plasminogen activator) following incomplete reperfusion after MT has been reported in several retrospective stud$\mathrm{i}\in{\mathsf{S}}^{8-11}$ and, most recently, in an RCT.12 The CHOICE trial, which investigated the use of intraarterial tPA, randomized patients with AIS with successful revascularization (expanded Thrombolysis in Cerebral Infarction [eTICI] 2b50 or higher) of proximal large vessel occlusion after MT to either intraarterial alteplase or placebo.12 Although the study was terminated early due to lack of placebo availability and low enrollment rates caused by the COVID-19 pandemic, the CHOICE trial demonstrated that the use of intraarterial alteplase compared with placebo resulted in higher rates of excellent neurological outcomes (mRS, $0{-}1$ ) at 90 days ${'59.0\%}$ versus $40.4\%$ ; $P{=}0.047$ ). No improvement in angiographic eTICI score $\scriptstyle\left(8.5\%\right)$ versus $7.7\%$ ; $\widetilde{P{=}0.91}$ ) or difference in infarct volume at 48 hours ${'}7.7\mathrm{mL}$ [3.7–29.3] versus $12.7~\mathrm{mL}$ [3.1–35.5]; $\mathsf{P{=}0.79},$ ) was observed between the alteplase and placebo cohorts. Low rates of sICH were also noted in both treatment groups $(0\%$ versus $3.8\%$ ). Similarly, ALLY patients demonstrated low rates of sICH $(5\%$ ; 1/20); however, rates of excellent functional outcomes were lower $(20\%)$ 4/20) in the ALLY trial than in the CHOICE trial intraarterial alteplase patients. Due to the limitations of the CHOICE trial (including small sample size), CHOICE 2 (URL: https://www.clinicaltrials. gov; Unique identifier: NCT05797792) aims to randomize 440 patients to either a 15-minute infusion with intraarterial alteplase or standard of care, which may provide additional evidence to support the use of intraarterial alteplase as an adjunctive treatment after MT with complete or near-complete reperfusion.  

Few studies have prospectively investigated the safety and efficacy of adjunctive intraarterial tenecteplase. The investigator-initiated, multicenter, ATTENTION-intraarterial trial (Intra-Arterial TNK Following Endovascular Thrombectomy in Patients With Large Vessel Occlusion of Posterior Circulation) randomized AIS patients with posterior circulation occlusions (vertebral, basilar, P1 segment of the posterior cerebral artery) achieving eTICI 2b-3 after MT to either intraarterial tenecteplase $0.0625~\mathsf{mg}/\mathsf{kg}$ or standard of care.19 ATTENTION-intraarterial enrolled 208 patients across 31 centers in China and found no significant difference in 90-day excellent functional outcomes $(\mathsf{m}\mathsf{R}\mathsf{S},0-1\$ ) between the intraarterial tenecteplase and control $(34.6\%$ versus $26\%$ ) cohorts in a preliminary report. Mortality and sICH rates were also comparable between the 2 groups.20 The ALLY pilot study showed similar results of safety and efficacy when compared with our retrospective control AIS population with anterior circulation occlusion; however, larger, randomized studies are needed to understand the clinical benefit and risk of adjunct intraarterial tenecteplase in this population.  

Tenecteplase, a third-generation fibrinolytic, has numerous pharmacokinetic advantages over alteplase, including a longer half-life. In practice, this allows for bolus treatment without prolonged administration and concomitant risk of catheter-related injury or catheterrelated spasm in the intracranial circulation. In the ALLY study, we sought to pilot an intraarterial administration protocol, which allows for a standardized tenecteplase dose infused over 1 minute (up to 3 doses, 4.5 mg maximum). In all 20 ALLY patients, the infusion protocol was successfully administered, and no complications with the infusion regimen were observed during the trial. In contrast, ATTENTION-intraarterial utilized a bolus infusion regimen of $0.0625~\mathrm{mg/kg}$ over 15 seconds, with a maximum dose of $6.25~\mathrm{mg}$ .19,20 The optimal infusion protocol and dosage for adjunctive intraarterial tenecteplase are still unknown; however, the ALLY phase 2b trial provides supportive evidence of the feasibility and safety of a standardized intraarterial tenecteplase infusion protocol.  

Several studies are examining at the role of MT for primary medium-vessel occlusions.21–23 With the advent of microcatheter and stent-retriever technology, operators are becoming more familiar with mid-sized vessels as potential targets for MT. However, despite these developments, access to smaller distal vessels for MT aspiration or stent-retriever passes remains technically challenging and raises significant safety concerns.24 Even with optimal technique or device selection, arterial tortuosity and perforator injury are anatomic limitations with instrumentation.25 This is even more relevant in cases with multiple distal branch occlusions requiring repeat MT passes. A safe and efficacious pharmacological agent is an option in these instances, and safety data from ALLY suggest that intraarterial tenecteplase holds promise as an alternative therapy.  

Our pragmatic study design, with an enrollment window of up to 24 hours and no exclusion for pre-MT IV thrombolytic treatment, highlights its generalizability to current interventional practice. As $55\%$ of ALLY patients received pre-IV lytic therapy, no trend toward increased sICH was observed in this pilot study, which further highlights the safety of intraarterial tenecteplase.  

The ALLY trial provides important data to inform the design of a phase III study. Ideally, a phase III design, similar to that of the CHOICE and CHOICE 2 trials or the Safety and Efficacy of Intraarterial TECHNO trial (Tenecteplase for Noncomplete Reperfusion of Intracranial Occlusions), would include randomization to a placebo control group or best medical management. Similar to CHOICE 2 and TECHNO, the primary outcome for a phase III study would most likely examine microvascular reperfusion after intraarterial tenecteplase infusion. As both CHOICE 2 and TECHNO are ongoing, the results of these phase III RCTs will provide critical data and insight into the safety and efficacy of intraarterial lytic for AIS MT patients after incomplete revascularization.  

# Limitations  

Our study has several limitations. First, as this was a singlecenter pilot study, selection bias as well as the small sample size could limit the interpretation of these results. Second, as the optimal dose for intraarterial tenecteplase is unknown, this pilot study only assessed safety up to this maximum dose. Finally, this study did not include a prospective control arm, which limits the interpretation of study results.  

# Conclusions  

To our knowledge, this is the first prospective pilot study to assess the safety of intraarterial tenecteplase as an adjunctive therapy in patients with AIS with anterior circulation occlusion and incomplete revascularization after MT. Our results demonstrate the feasibility and safety of a standardized intraarterial tenecteplase infusion protocol up to 4.5 mg. Larger, randomized trials are needed to assess the safety and efficacy of intraarterial tenecteplase in this AIS population.
