Background: Pembrolizumab previously demonstrated robust antitumor activity and manageable safety in a phase Ib study of patients with heavily pretreated, programmed death ligand 1 (PD-L1)-positive, recurrent or metastatic nasopharyngeal carcinoma (NPC). The phase III KEYNOTE-122 study was conducted to further evaluate pembrolizumab versus chemotherapy in patients with platinum-pretreated, recurrent and/or metastatic NPC. Final analysis results are presented.  

Patients and methods: KEYNOTE-122 was an open-label, randomized study conducted at 29 sites, globally. Participants with platinum-pretreated recurrent and/or metastatic NPC were randomly assigned $(1:1)$ to pembrolizumab or chemotherapy with capecitabine, gemcitabine, or docetaxel. Randomization was stratified by liver metastasis (present versus absent). The primary endpoint was overall survival (OS), analyzed in the intention-to-treat population using the stratified log-rank test (superiority threshold, one-sided $P=0.0187$ ). Safety was assessed in the as-treated population.  

Results: Between 5 May 2016 and 28 May 2018, 233 participants were randomly assigned to treatment (pembrolizumab, $n=117$ ; chemotherapy, $n=116\$ ); Most participants $(86.7\%)$ received study treatment in the second-line or later setting. Median time from randomization to data cut-off (30 November 2020) was 45.1 months (interquartile range, 39.0-48.8 months). Median OS was 17.2 months $[95\%$ confidence interval (CI) 11.7-22.9 months] with pembrolizumab and 15.3 months $95\%$ CI 10.9-18.1 months) with chemotherapy [hazard ratio, 0.90 ( $95\%$ CI 0.67-1.19; $P=0.2262_{i}$ ]. Grade 3-5 treatment-related adverse events occurred in 12 of 116 participants $(10.3\%)$ with pembrolizumab and 49 of 112 participants $(43.8\%)$ with chemotherapy. Three treatment-related deaths occurred: 1 participant $\left(0.9\%\right)$ with pembrolizumab (pneumonitis) and 2 $\left(1.8\%\right)$ with chemotherapy (pneumonia, intracranial hemorrhage).  

Conclusion: Pembrolizumab did not significantly improve OS compared with chemotherapy in participants with platinum-pretreated recurrent and/or metastatic NPC but did have manageable safety and a lower incidence of treatment-related adverse events.  

Key words: nasopharyngeal cancer, programmed death-1 (PD-1), immunotherapy, pembrolizumab  



# INTRODUCTION  

Nasopharyngeal carcinoma (NPC) represents a unique type of head and neck cancer with distinct geographic distribution.1,2 Of $\sim133$ 354 new cases of NPC and 80 008 associated deaths in 2020, most occurred in east and southeast Asia.2  

The most widely recommended first-line systemic therapy for unresectable recurrent or metastatic (R/M) NPC is cisplatin plus gemcitabine, which is generally associated with limited long-term survival.4-6 Early data suggest a potential benefit with capecitabine maintenance in metastatic NPC that has responded to induction chemotherapy7; however, there is currently no standard second-line treatment of advanced NPC.4 Novel therapies that extend survival are needed.8  

Infection with the EpsteineBarr virus (EBV) is believed to play a causative role in NPC development. In a preclinical study, increased expression of programmed death ligand 1 (PD-L1) was observed in EBV-positive NPC cells, resulting in suppressed immunity and tumor surveillance.9 Targeting the programmed death 1 (PD-1)/PD-L1 pathway may, therefore, be a promising strategy to restore anti-EBV tumor antigen-specific T-cell immunity in NPC.10 This is supported clinically, with several trials demonstrating efficacy of PD-1 inhibitors in NPC.11-16  

The PD-1 inhibitor pembrolizumab had antitumor activity in patients with heavily pretreated R/M NPC in the phase Ib KEYNOTE-028 study.11 We describe the results of the protocol-specified final analysis of KEYNOTE-122 (NCT02611960), which was conducted to evaluate pembrolizumab versus chemotherapy in platinum-pretreated R/M NPC.  



# METHODS  



# Study design and participants  

The open-label, phase III KEYNOTE-122 trial was conducted at 29 sites in Canada; Hong Kong, China; Taiwan; Malaysia; Philippines; Singapore; South Korea; Thailand; and the United States. Participants were randomly assigned at 27 sites (Supplementary Table S1, available at https://doi.org/ 10.1016/j.annonc.2022.12.007). Participants were 18 years or older and had histologically confirmed, nonkeratinizing differentiated [World Health Organization (WHO) type II] or undifferentiated (WHO type III) EBV-positive (per EBVencoded small RNA in situ hybridization assay), R/M NPC; an Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group performance status of 0 or 1; measurable disease per RECIST, version 1.1; and archival tissue available for biomarker analysis. Patients must have received at least one prior platinum-based chemotherapy (additional detail in Supplementary Material, available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.annonc.2022.12.007).  

The study protocol and amendments were approved by the appropriate institutional review boards and ethics review committees at each institution. The study was conducted in accordance with the protocol, Good Clinical Practice guidelines, and the Declaration of Helsinki. All participants provided written informed consent. The study had an external data monitoring committee that made recommendations regarding safety, efficacy, and continued ethical integrity (additional detail in Supplementary Material, available at https://doi.org/10. 1016/j.annonc.2022.12.007).  



# Randomization and masking  

Participants were randomly assigned $(1:1)$ using a central interactive voice response and integrated web response system (Almac Clinical Technologies, Souderton, PA) to pembrolizumab or investigator’s choice of chemotherapy (capecitabine, gemcitabine, or docetaxel). Randomization was stratified by liver metastasis (presence versus absence). Participants were assigned in blocks of two per stratum.  



# Procedures  

Participants received pembrolizumab $200~\mathsf{mg}$ intravenously every 3 weeks or capecitabine $1000\mathrm{mg/m}^{2}$ (or $1250\:\mathsf{mg/m}^{2}$ from cycle 2 per tolerability and local practices) orally twice daily on days 1-14 per 3-week cycle, gemcitabine $1250\mathrm{mg/m}^{2}$ intravenously on days 1 and 8 per 3-week cycle, or docetaxel $75~\mathrm{mg/m}^{2}$ intravenously on day 1 per 3-week cycle. Pembrolizumab continued for up to 35 cycles; chemotherapy beyond 35 cycles was allowed if tolerated. Treatment continued until confirmed radiographic disease progression, intolerable toxicity, investigator decision, or treatment completion. In the pembrolizumab group, participants with a complete response (CR) could discontinue treatment if they had received at least eight pembrolizumab cycles, including at least two cycles after initial CR.  

Imaging was carried out every 6 weeks in year 1 and every 9 weeks thereafter. Adverse events (AEs) were monitored up to 30 days after the last dose of study treatment (90 days for serious AEs and events of clinical interest) or until new anticancer therapy was initiated. AEs were graded per the National Cancer Institute Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events, version 4.0. Survival status was monitored until death, withdrawal, or study end.  

PD-L1 expression was assessed centrally using the PD-L1 IHC 22C3 pharmDx (Agilent Technologies; Carpinteria, CA) and represented by combined positive score [CPS; number of PD-L1-staining cells (tumor cells, lymphocytes, macrophages) divided by total viable tumor cells, multiplied by 100].  



# Outcomes  

The primary endpoint was overall survival (OS). Secondary endpoints were progression-free survival (PFS), objective response rate (ORR), duration of response (DOR), and 6- and 12-month PFS rates, all per RECIST $\mathsf{v}1.1^{17}$ by blinded independent central review; 12- and 24-month OS rates; and safety (additional detail in Supplementary Material, available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.annonc.2022.12.007).  



# Statistical analysis  

The protocol specified an interim and final analysis. The external data monitoring committee recommended continuing the study after reviewing the interim analysis.  

Enrollment of approximately 230 participants provided $92.9\%$ power to detect a hazard ratio (HR) for death of 0.6 or better for comparison of OS with pembrolizumab versus chemotherapy, with 184 deaths and a one-sided a of 0.0187 at final analysis (additional detail in Supplementary Material, available at https://doi.org/10.1016/j.annonc. 2022.12.007). Statistical analyses were conducted using SAS, version 9.4.  

OS, PFS, and ORR were assessed in all randomly assigned participants [intention-to-treat (ITT) population]. DOR was analyzed in participants with confirmed CR or partial response (PR). Safety was assessed in all participants who received at least one dose of treatment (as-treated population).  

OS, PFS, and DOR were estimated using the Kaplane Meier method. Between-group differences in OS and PFS were tested using the stratified log-rank test. HRs and associated $95\%$ confidence intervals (CIs) were calculated using a stratified Cox proportional hazard model with the Efron method of handling ties. Prespecified subgroup analysis of OS by age ( ${<}65$ versus ${\geq}65$ years), liver metastasis (present versus absent), PD-L1 CPS ${<}10$ versus ${\geq}10)$ , and type of comparator (pembrolizumab versus capecitabine, gemcitabine, or docetaxel) were prespecified. Post hoc exploratory analysis of OS by region, PD-L1 $\mathsf{CPS}\ge1$ and $\mathsf{CPS}\geq20$ , and subsequent anticancer therapy were also conducted. The study is ongoing but not recruiting.  



# RESULTS  

Between 5 May 2016 and 28 May 28 2018, 287 participants were screened, and 233 were assigned to treatment (pembrolizumab, $n=117.$ ; chemotherapy, $n=116$ ) (Figure 1); 116 participants in the pembrolizumab group and 112 in the chemotherapy group received treatment. Baseline demographics and disease characteristics were balanced between treatment groups (Table 1). Most participants $(86.7\%)$ received study treatment as second-line or later therapy. More participants in the chemotherapy group had received three or more prior lines of therapy (pembrolizumab, $15.4\%$ chemotherapy $25.9\%,$ . Median time from randomization to data cut-off (30 November 2020) was 45.1 months [interquartile range (IQR), 39.0-48.8 months]. Eleven participants $(9.4\%)$ in the pembrolizumab group completed treatment and 105 $(89.7\%)$ discontinued (Figure 1). In the chemotherapy group, 110 participants $(94.8\%)$ discontinued treatment and two $(1.7\%)$ were ongoing (Figure 1). The most common reason for discontinuation was progressive disease [pembrolizumab, $n=82\:(70.1\%)$ ; chemotherapy, $n=62\:(53.4\%)]$ .  

At final analysis, 95 participants $(81.2\%)$ had died in the pembrolizumab group and 97 $(83.6\%)$ had died in the chemotherapy group. Median OS was 17.2 months $95\%$ CI 11.7-22.9 months) with pembrolizumab and 15.3 months ( $95\%$ CI 10.9-18.1 months) with chemotherapy; HR was 0.90 $95\%$ CI 0.67-1.19; $P=0.2262_{,}$ , which did not meet the significance threshold (0.0187). OS rates at 24 months were $40.2\%$ ( $95\%$ CI $31.3\%$ to $48.9\%$ and $32.2\%$ ( $95\%$ CI $23.9\%$ to $40.8\%)$ ), respectively (Figure 2).

# DISCUSSION  

In the final analysis of KEYNOTE-122, pembrolizumab did not significantly improve OS versus chemotherapy in participants with platinum-pretreated recurrent and/or metastatic NPC. Pembrolizumab did not improve PFS or ORR versus chemotherapy but did have manageable safety with a substantially lower incidence of treatment-related AEs. Although there was no significant difference in efficacy between arms, the improved safety profile of pembrolizumab suggests it could potentially be considered where reduced toxicity is preferred; however, it should be noted that KEYNOTE-122 was not a non-inferiority study, and therefore equivalence was not tested.  

Subgroup analyses in participants with PD-L1-positive disease showed no efficacy benefit with pembrolizumab in participants with PD-L1 $\mathtt{CPS}\ge1,\ge10,$ or ${\geq}20$ . A phase II study of nivolumab-pretreated advanced NPC similarly found no significant association between PD-L1 expression and survival or response; however, more participants with PD-L1-positive disease responded to nivolumab. Surprisingly, in the current analysis, an OS benefit was observed with pembrolizumab in participants with PD-L1 CPS ${<}10$ . One factor that may have contributed was the longer treatment duration with pembrolizumab versus chemotherapy in this subgroup (median, 4.6 versus 2.8 months). However, it should also be considered that tissue samples were archival and may not have reflected PD-L1 expression at enrollment and that the study was not powered to compare outcomes by PD-L1 status. The predictive and prognostic value of PD-L1 expression in NPC remains to be determined.18,19  

Analysis of participants who received subsequent anticancer therapy showed numerically longer OS in the pembrolizumab group. This could be the result of carryover effect, where benefit is seen beyond treatment cessation, or may indicate participants who received pembrolizumab benefited from subsequent chemotherapy. The post hoc analysis of OS in which participants who received subsequent immunotherapy were censored provided results similar to the overall population.

Several studies have examined PD-1 inhibitor monotherapy in R/M NPC.11-14,20-22 T he only randomized trial is a phase II study in platinum-refractory R/M NPC in which spartalizumab monotherapy did not improve PFS versus chemotherapy [1.9 versus 6.6 months; HR, 1.36 $95\%$ CI 0.87-2.12)].21  

Given the limited benefit of anti-PD-1 monotherapy in NPC, combining PD-1 inhibitors with other agents may improve efficacy. Promising results have been reported for both camrelizumab and toripalimab plus chemotherapy as first-line treatment of R/M NPC. In the phase III CAPTAIN1st study, adding camrelizumab to cisplatin and gemcitabine significantly prolonged PFS [9.7 versus 6.9 months; HR, 0.54 $95\%$ CI 0.39-0.76); $P=0.0002]$ . Similarly, in the phase III JUPITER-02 study, toripalimab plus cisplatin and gemcitabine significantly improved PFS versus placebo plus gemcitabine and cisplatin [11.7 versus 8.0 months; HR, 0.52 $(95\%\mathrm{Cl}0.36–0.74)$ ; $P=0.0003]$ .16 An improvement in PFS was also observed across PD-L1 subgroups.16 These results led to inclusion of cisplatin and gemcitabine plus a PD-1 inhibitor in guidelines as a recommended regimen for the first-line treatment of advanced NPC.6 Notably, in both trials, patients received treatment for six cycles, followed by maintenance with PD-1 inhibitors or placebo.15,16 The PFS curves in these trials overlapped in the initial months and started to separate during the maintenance phase, suggesting that anti-PD-1 maintenance contributed to efficacy. OS data were immature in both trials.15,16 In contrast to CAPTAIN-1st and JUPITER-02 in which chemotherapy was limited to six cycles, the extended exposure to chemotherapy in KEYNOTE-122 may have contributed to longer OS in the chemotherapy group, as NPC is a chemotherapysensitive cancer.23 CAPTAIN-1st and JUPITER-02 were also conducted in the first-line setting, whereas participants in KEYNOTE-122 were generally heavily pretreated, which may have contributed to comparatively poorer outcomes.15,16  

Various combination regimens involving anti-PD-1/L1 inhibitors for R/M NPC are being investigated in the second-line setting, including avelumab plus axitinib (NCT04562441), sintilimab plus bevacizumab (NCT04872582), and tabelecleucel plus pembrolizumab for EBV-positive NPC.24 The encouraging results observed with PD-1 inhibitors plus chemotherapy in the first-line setting may also be applicable as induction or adjuvant therapy, which is under investigation.  

In this study, the KaplaneMeier curves for PFS did not demonstrate a crossover phenomenon or a tail effect. Both are often observed with immunotherapy, reflecting a subgroup who derive long-term benefit. A slight crossover in curves, but no tail effect was observed for OS. Limitations of KEYNOTE-122 include the broad inclusion criteria, which resulted in a population with varying prior treatment exposures. The choice of chemotherapy was also at the investigator’s discretion and likely reflected a range of factors, including participant characteristics and prior treatment. Subgroup analysis suggested OS benefit in participants from Hong Kong, China, or Taiwan; however, differences in baseline characteristics and prior therapies may have influenced the results. Subgroup analyses by region were also post hoc and exploratory, and the study was not powered to show significance in subgroups.  

Overall, our findings showed that pembrolizumab did not significantly improve outcomes versus investigator’s choice of capecitabine, gemcitabine, or docetaxel in participants with platinum-pretreated recurrent and/or metastatic NPC but did have manageable safety and a lower incidence of treatment-related AEs.
