# Efficacy and Safety of a Dedicated Device for Cerebral Venous Thrombectomy: A Pilot Randomized Clinical Trial  

Yi Xu , MD\*; Yan Wu, MD\*; Miaowen Jiang, PhD\*; Baoying Song, MD; Chuanhui Li , MD; Chuanjie Wu , MD; Jiangang Duan , PhD; Ran Meng , MD; Chen Zhou , MD; Sijie Li, MD; Feng Yan , MD; Jian Chen, MD; Ming Li, PhD; Xunming Ji , MD, PhD  

BACKGROUND: Lack of a dedicated thrombectomy device for cerebral venous thrombosis hinders the recanalization ability of endovascular treatment (EVT). Novel NiTi-braided stent retriever (Venous-TD) is a dedicated venous sinus thrombectomy device. This study aims to demonstrate the safety and efficacy of Venous-TD.  

METHODS: In this pilot, prospective, randomized, single-blind, parallel-group control, single-center clinical study, patients with cerebral venous thrombosis from Beijing Xuanwu Hospital were included. Randomization was performed to EVT with either the Venous-TD or Angioguard with Sterling balloon (control group). The primary efficacy outcome was the proportion of immediate complete recanalization during EVT. Secondary outcomes included the proportion of functional independence and moderate to severe residential headache at 180 days after EVT. Safety outcomes included peri-procedural complications, all-cause mortality, and symptomatic intracranial hemorrhage after EVT.  

RESULTS: A total of 61 patients were enrolled and randomized. Thirty-one patients were randomized to the Venous-TD group, and 30 were randomized to the control group. The median (interquartile range) age was 28 (21–45) in the Venous-TD group and 34 (24–43) in the control group. The proportion of patients with a National Institutes of Health Stroke Scale score ${>}8$ on admission was 8 $(25.8\%)$ in the Venous-TD group and 11 $(36.7\%)$ in the control group. During EVT, Venous-TD significantly improved the proportion of complete recanalization compared with Angioguard (23 $[\dot{7}6.7\%]$ versus 6 $\left[20.0\%\right]$ ; relative risk, 3.833 $[95\%$ CI, 1.825–8.054]). The proportions of long-term functional independence at 180 days in the Venous-TD group and the control group were not significantly different. The proportion of patients with severe residual headache at 180 days in the Venous-TD group was significantly lower than that in the control group ( $3[9.7\%]$ versus 10 $[35.7\%]$ ; relative risk, 0.271 $[95\%$ CI, 0.083–0.886]). Safety outcomes showed no statistically significant difference between the 2 groups.  

CONCLUSIONS: This trial indicated that Venous-TD did not increase complications in EVT of cerebral venous thrombosis and can significantly increase the proportion of complete recanalization. A multicenter phase III randomized control trial assessing efficacy and safety of Venous-TD is warranted.

erebral venous thrombosis (CVT) is an important cause of stroke in young adults with increased incidence over the past decade.1,2 An association between persistent venous occlusion and worse functional outcome was reported, indicating the important role of recanalization therapy in CVT.3,4 Subacute thrombus, which is defined as the thrombus treated after 7 days of onset, is difficult to achieve complete recanalization.5 Unfortunately, $50\%$ to $70\%$ of patients with CVT present subacute, nonspecific symptoms when onset, which lead to the delay of diagnosis.6,7 As a result, ${\approx}50\%$ of the patients treated only by anticoagulation and $60\%$ of the patients treated by endovascular treatment (EVT) fail to achieve complete recanalization, which may relate to the fact that $20\%$ of the patients with CVT retain their disability or die.6–8  

The lack of an effective and dedicated thrombectomy device for CVT may hinder the recanalization ability of EVT because the characteristics of intracranial venous thrombosis, especially subacute thrombosis, and the anatomic structure of intracranial veins are different from those of arteries or peripheral veins.9–13 In 2020, the first and only large randomized control trial on EVT for CVT, the TOACT trial (Thrombolysis or Anticoagulation for Cerebral Venous Thrombosis), showed no difference in clinical outcome between those allocated to anticoagulation only and adjunctive EVT. According to the study, there was high heterogeneity in the device.14 Therefore, we recently developed a kind of venous sinus thrombectomy device, Venous-TD, which is tailored to the cerebral venous system and especially suitable for thrombus in subacute stage. To evaluate the efficacy and safety of Venous-TD in a clinical setting, a pilot, prospective, randomized, singleblind, parallel-group control, single-center clinical study was performed. In this study, we compared the recanalization rate and long-term outcome of EVT treated with Venous-TD and the Angioguard with a Sterling balloon.  

# METHODS  

# Patients  

The authors declare that all supporting data are available within the article and its Supplemental Material. This study followed the CONSORT (Consolidated Standards of Reporting Trials) 2010 pilot study extension reporting guideline.15 The Ethics Committee Board of Xuanwu Hospital has approved this clinical study (No. [2021]228). We enrolled patients from the neurosurgery department in Xuanwu Hospital from 2022 to 2023. Written informed consent was obtained from all patient participants or their legal representatives before enrollment. The trial was designed to enroll 60 patients with the following eligibility criteria: (1) adults aged 18 to 85 years; (2) intracranial venous sinus thrombosis in acute or subacute stage $(\ge28$ days from onset to diagnosis) confirmed by clinical and imaging examination; and (3) meeting the indications for EVT.16,17 The key exclusion criteria were as follows: (1) isolated cortical venous thrombosis without venous sinus thrombosis or isolated cavernous sinus thrombosis; (2) recurrent intracranial venous sinus thrombosis; (3) pregnancy; and (4) contraindications of anticoagulation or thrombolysis. The precise inclusion and exclusion criteria are shown in the Supplemental Material.  

# Randomization and Blinding  

In this pilot, prospective, randomized, single-blind, parallelgroup control, single-center clinical study, patients with CVT from Beijing Xuanwu Hospital were included. Participants were randomized 1:1 to receive either EVT with Venous-TD or Angioguard with Sterling balloon (see the trial protocol in Supplemental Material for details). As this trial is a randomized controlled clinical study of medical instruments, the instruments used for the control group and the Venous-TD group in the trial are different, so it is difficult to double-blind patients and surgeons. The researchers involved in the study were not allowed to disclose their group information to the enrolled patients to avoid bias selection of patients. Assessors of the functional outcomes were trained research nurses or neurologists who were blinded to treatment randomization.  

# Devices  

In this study, we used Venous-TD (VT-7-25-1250/VT-10-25- 1250, HOH Medical), which is a newly developed stent retriever designed specifically for removing blood clots from the venous sinus (Figure 1A; the picture of the device in kind is shown in Figure S1). In the control group, we used an Angioguard (6 mm, Cordis) with a Sterling balloon dilatation catheter (Boston Scientific Corporation). The reasons for the device selection in the control group are as follows: no products specifically applied in the intravascular treatment of venous sinus thrombectomy have been approved by the regulatory authorities; the clinically applied devices were all arterial thrombectomy devices, which were used off-label, and their efficacy and safety have not been proven. Among them, balloon thrombectomy and catheter suction were the most used and have a better recanalization effect and fewer complications than other existing devices.18,19  

# Interventions  

The enrolled patients underwent EVT at times but no later than 24 hours after randomization. The intervention group received thrombectomy with Venous-TD, whereas the control group received thrombectomy with Angioguard with Sterling balloon. The procedures were performed under general anesthesia. A cerebral angiography was performed to confirm the existence and location of the thrombus. An 8F vascular sheath was introduced into the internal jugular vein, and systematic heparinization was performed.

For the Venous-TD group, (1) the diameter adjuster of the bolt mesh was adjusted to reduce the diameter of the bolt mesh to the minimum, and the locking knob on the handle was tightened; (2) the bolt extraction net was inserted into the end of the 3-way valve connected with the catheter, the push rod was pushed slowly forward, the real-time position of the bolt extraction net was observed through the image, and the knob at the end of the 3-way valve was tightened to fix the push rod; (3) the locking knob was loosened, the diameter regulator of the thrombus mesh was adjusted to fit the diameter of the blood vessels through image observation, and the locking knob was locked; and (4) the push and pull lever of the knob was loosened at the end of the 3-way valve so that the thrombus extraction network moves from the far end to the near end.  

For the control group, a spiral, shapeable wire (Terumo Inc, Somerset, New Jersey) was used to dislodge the thrombus. An Angioguard distal protection device was inserted into the sinus and was used to cut the thrombus. The specific steps are the same as in a previous study of our group.19  

For both groups, salvaged blood was collected via an aspiration tube and transferred into a closed, rigid collection reservoir. The salvaged blood underwent a washing process and was then retransfused to the patient via a peripheral route (Figure 1B through 1D). For local thrombolysis, 100 to $1500\times10^{3}$ IU of urokinase was administered into the cerebral venous sinus at a rate of $4\times10^{3}$ IU/min by a guiding catheter. In our study, before and after thrombectomy, cerebral angiography from the artery was performed to confirm the recanalization of the sinus. The intracranial circulation time (ICT) was calculated as the time from the first appearance of the contrast agent in the arterial system to the complete clearing from the venous system, with the possible exception of circumscribed venous stasis that was not cleared by the end of a prolonged angiography series $(>20$ seconds in our research).20,21  

All patients received low molecular weight heparin by subcutaneous injection every 12 hours (for body weight ${<}50~\mathsf{kg}$ : $0.4~\mathrm{mL}$ ; 50–70 kg: 0.6 mL; ${>}70$ kg: $0.8mL$ ) before EVT. All patients had to commence (or continue) low molecular weight heparin at a therapeutic dose after EVT was completed. After the clinical condition was stabilized, all patients received anticoagulation for a minimum of 3 months if a transient risk factor was present or for 6 to 12 months in all other cases.  

# Outcomes  

The primary outcome measures were the proportion of immediate complete recanalization. The 3-grade classification scheme considers different grades for patients with complete recanalization, partial recanalization, and no recanalization. Complete recanalization was defined as blood flow without any interruption, partial recanalization was defined as small interruptions of continuous blood flow and narrowing of the venous lumen, and no recanalization was defined as interrupted blood flow.  

The secondary outcome measures were as follows: (1) the proportion of immediate recanalization (including complete and partial); (2) the operation duration. The operation duration refers to the time from entry of thrombectomy device to vascular recanalization or the end time of surgery for patients with no vascular recanalization; (3) ICT before and after thrombectomy was calculated as the time from the first appearance of the contrast agent in the arterial system to the complete clearing from the venous system, with the possible exception of circumscribed venous stasis that was not cleared by the end of a prolonged angiography series ${\scriptstyle>}20$ s in our research); (4) the proportion of cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) opening pressure $\geq180\mathrm{mmH}_{2}0$ pre-procedure and 7 days after EVT or at discharge post-procedure by lumbar puncture; (5) functional independence (modified Rankin Scale [mRS] score of $_{0-2}$ ; up to 7, 30, 90, and 180 days post-procedure); (6) recurrence of CVT (up to 180 days post-procedure). The recurrence rate of CVT was confirmed by clinical manifestations and imaging examinations; (7) long-term complete recanalization (at 180 days post-procedure, $\pm2$ weeks). Long-term complete recanalization confirmed by imaging examinations; and (8) the occurrence of moderate or severe residential headache (up to 30, 90, and 180 days post-procedure). Residential headache was defined as a headache related to CVT or a history of chronic headache before CVT occurred $\geq10$ days in 1 month before the last follow-up visit, which was further classified for severity as mild, moderate (disturbing ongoing activities), or severe (requiring bed rest or hospital admission) using a well-designed questionnaire based on previous studies.6  

The safety outcome measures were as follows: (1) procedure-associated complications, including new intracranial hemorrhage, aggravation of primary intracranial hemorrhage, and puncture point hematoma/pseudoaneurysm (time frame: intraoperative); (2) all-cause mortality (within 180 days post-procedure); and (3) symptomatic intracranial hemorrhage within 180 days after EVT (within 180 days post-procedure). Symptomatic intracranial hemorrhage was confirmed by clinical manifestations and imaging examinations. Test termination criteria are shown in the Supplemental Material.  

# Statistical Analysis  

For the description of quantitative indicators, the mean, SD, median, minimum, maximum, and interquartile range (IQR) were calculated, and classification indicators were used to describe the number of cases and percentage of each group. For comparison between 2 groups, an intention-to-treat approach was used. Comparisons of quantitative indicators were expressed as relative risk ratios. The precision of effect estimates was described with $95\%$ CIs. $\chi^{2}$ test or Fisher exact test was used for classification indicators, and Wilcoxon rank-sum test was used for rank data. Univariate and multivariate logistic regression analyses were performed, and odds ratios and $95\%$ CIs were calculated to identify the risk factors for severe headache at 180 days. SPSS 22.0 for Windows (IBM Corp) and R, version 4.3.0, were used to analyze all data.  

# RESULTS  

A total of 61 patients were enrolled and randomized from March 2022 to January 2023. Of these patients, 31 $(50.8\%)$ were randomized to the Venous-TD group, and 30 $(49.2\%)$ were randomized to the control group (Figure 2). One patient in the intervention group did not receive EVT because of the improved situation shortly after randomization. Ninety-day follow-up data were available for 60 patients $(98.4\%)$ ), and 180-day data were available for 59 patients $(96.7\%)$ ). All 61 patients were included in the final analysis.

For the long-term condition of recanalization, utilizing Venous-TD in EVT can significantly improve the proportion of complete recanalization at 180 days after EVT compared with Angioguard (26 $\left[83.9\%\right]$ versus 10 $[33.3\%]$ , relative risk ratio, 2.516 $[95\%$ CI, 1.482–4.271]). The results also revealed that Venous-TD reduced the incidence of moderate to severe residential headache at 180 days compared with Angioguard (3 [9.7] versus 10 [35.7], relative risk ratio, 0.271 $[95\%$ CI, 0.083–0.886]), although the proportion of moderate to severe residential headache at 30 days or 90 days remained not significantly different between the 2 groups.

Safety outcomes are shown in Table 3. During EVT, new intracranial hemorrhage occurred in 1 case in the Venous-TD group and 2 in the control group, which showed no significant difference (1 $[3.3\%]$ versus 2 $\left[6.7\%\right]$ ; $P{=}0.492,$ ). There were no significant differences between the occurrence of newly occurring intracranial hemorrhage in the Venous-TD group and the control group during EVT (1 $[3.3\%]$ versus 2 $[6.7\%]$ ; $_{P=0.492}$ ). Two and 3 patients experienced puncture point hematoma or pseudoaneurysm in the Venous-TD group and the control group, respectively. Two and 3 patients had aggravated cerebral edema in the Venous-TD group and the control group, respectively, which also showed no significant difference. No patients died during EVT or within 180 days in the Venous-TD group, whereas in the control group, 1 patient died because of septic shock within 30 days after EVT, and 1 patient died because of reoccurred thrombosis and cerebral hemorrhage within 90 days after EVT.  

To identify the influencing factors for moderate and severe headache at 180 days in patients treated by EVT, all variables at baseline, immediate complete recanalization, operation duration, CSF opening pressure ${>}180$ $\mathsf{mmH}_{2}\mathsf{O}$ at 7 days after EVT, recurrence of CVT at 180 days, complete recanalization at 180 days, and intracranial hemorrhage during or after EVT were included in the univariate logistic regression analysis. Three variables, including operation duration, CSF opening pressure $>180\mathrm{mmH}_{_{2}}\mathrm{O}$ 7 days after EVT, and complete recanalization at 180 days, were found to be significantly related to moderate and severe headache at 180 days (Table S2). Then, a multivariate logistic regression analysis including the 3 variables mentioned above was performed. The results showed that a CSF opening pressure $>180\mathrm{mmH}_{2}0$ at 7 days was an independent risk factor for moderate and severe headache at 180 days (odds ratio, 7.24 $[95\%$ CI, $1.23\substack{-42.64}]$ ; $_{P=0.029}$ ), whereas complete recanalization at 180 days was an independent protective factor for moderate and severe headache at 180 days (odds ratio, 0.04 $[95\%$ CI, $0.00-$ 0.43]; $_{\mathrm{-}0.008}$ ; Table 4).  

# DISCUSSION  

According to our study, Venous-TD could be a safe and relatively efficient device. Regarding safety outcomes, all complications showed no significant difference from the control group. A systematic review in 2022 showed that the rate of development or worsening of ICH was around $10\%$ in a previous study, and $12\%$ to $16\%$ of patients died within 1 year, which was also higher than the Venous-TD group in our study.

For efficiency outcomes, a higher immediate complete recanalization rate $(76.7\%)$ and a higher longterm complete recanalization rate $(83.9\%)$ could be obtained by the Venous-TD group than by the control group in this study. A previous systematic review reported that the rate of complete recanalization was $69\%$ in EVT (immediately to 3 months after EVT), which was slightly lower than that in Venous-TD. However, in 2013, a study performed by Li et al19 showed that the rate of immediate complete recanalization of EVT by Angioguard was $87\%$ , which is higher than our result. We hypothesized that this difference was because of differences in the number of involved sinus veins in the patients. In the study by Li et al, the proportion of patients with ${\ge}3$ veins involved was $54\%$ , whereas in our study, it was $87.1\%$ . The relatively high load of thrombus may make the operation difficult. In addition, the operation duration in the Venous-TD group was shorter than in the control group. To our knowledge, no studies have analyzed the operation duration explicitly. A shorter operation duration further represents a better efficacy of Venous-TD and a less chance of complications.  

In our study, there was no significant difference in the ICT right after thrombectomy between 2 groups. To our knowledge, there are no studies on the changes in ICT before and after thrombectomy of CVT at present.  

We hypothesize that one of the reasons is that CVT will cause cerebral edema, ischemia, and often intracerebral hemorrhage.23 Thrombectomy may achieve recanalization, but the damages cannot be recovered immediately and will still affect cerebral microcirculation. In our study, the ΔICT in the Venous-TD group was significantly higher than that in the control group, which may indirectly indicate that Venous-TD has better efficacy.  

In this study, the proportion of mRS scores of 0 to 2 in the Venous-TD group at the 180-day follow-up was $87.1\%$ . There was high heterogeneity in long-term functional outcomes in previous studies because of different baselines. In the TO-ACT trial, in patients with EVT with standard care, the proportion of mRS scores 0 to 2 at 6 months was $82\%$ . A meta-analysis published in 2019 did not detect differences in outcomes stratified by treatment approach. Despite poor baseline characteristics, $70\%$ to $80\%$ of the patients eventually achieved mRS scores of 0 to 2 in the follow-up.8 Venous-TD did not significantly improve mRS scores compared with the control group in our study or in previous studies.  

Although previous studies have shown that the prognosis of CVT is mostly good, its main evaluation tools, such as the mRS score and the National Institutes of Health Stroke Scale score, cannot accurately and comprehensively demonstrate the damage caused by cerebral venous return obstruction. Studies have shown that up to $25\%$ of the patients who survived acute-stage CVT have chronic sequelae, among which headache is the most common chronic sequelae, often associated with chronic high cranial pressure caused by the lack of recanalization of venous sinuses.24–28 Some chronic headaches do not affect motor function or the ability to live but seriously reduce the quality of life.18 In our study, the severity and frequency of headache were significantly reduced during the follow-up period in the Venous-TD group. According to our logistic regression analysis, the lack of recanalization may lead to persistent venous hypertension or intracranial hypertension, which may be the main cause of residual headache after CVT.24 Further studies are expected to identify the exact relationship between headache and recanalization and persistent venous hypertension in CVT.

This trial has several limitations. First, this study is a pilot trial performed in a single center, which leads to inevitable bias. A multicenter study conducted by our group evaluating this new device is ongoing. Second, patients treated with EVT by Angioguard were set as the control group. However, EVT is a nonproven therapy, a group that is treated by full-dose anticoagulation according to international guidelines is needed for more convincing results in future studies. The primary outcome in our study was recanalization, which is an anatomic outcome rather than functional because this study mainly focuses on the efficiency of the device. Further study setting functional outcome as the primary outcome is needed. Third, the small sample size leads to important imbalances between the 2 treatment arms including age, onset-to-treatment time, focal deficits, ICH, and so on. Studies with large sample size are needed to reduce these imbalances. Fourth, in our study, the site of the thrombus was slightly different between the 2 groups, which may have influenced the results.29 Further research is needed to explore the optimal indications for EVT in CVT. Fifth, for the outcomes during the operation, the assessors were the surgeons who were not blinded to the device, which is one of the main limitations of our trial. Future research should set up bystanders during the operation who are blinded to the intervention as the assessors. Sixth, early reocclusion after EVT is an important issue in EVT for CVT. Early control venography is needed in further investigations.

# CONCLUSIONS  

This pilot, prospective, randomized, single-blind, parallelgroup control, single-center clinical study demonstrated that EVT performed by Venous-TD can significantly increase the rate of immediate and long-term complete recanalization, shorten the operation duration, and reduce the long-term severity and frequency of headache compared with EVT performed by the Angioguard with Sterling balloon. A multicenter phase III randomized control trial assessing the efficacy and safety of VenousTD is warranted.
