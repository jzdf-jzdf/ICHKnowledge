# Clot removAl with or without decompRessive craniectomy under ICP monitoring for supratentorial IntraCerebral Hemorrhage (CARICH): a randomized controlled trial  

Chao Zhang, MD, Shuixian Zhang, MS, Yi Yin, MD, Long Wang, MS, Lan Li, MS, Chuan Lan, MS, Jiantao Shi, MD, Zhouyang Jiang, MS, Hongfei Ge, MD, Xuegang Li, MS, Zaiyong Ao, MS, Shengli Hu, MD, Jingyu Chen, MD, Hua Feng, MD, PhD\*, Rong Hu, MD, PhD\*  

Background: Decompressive craniectomy (DC), a surgery to remove part of the skull and open the dura mater, maybe an effective treatment for controlling intracranial hypertension. It remains great interest to elucidate whether DC is beneficial to intracerebral hemorrhage (ICH) patients who warrant clot removal (CR) to prevent intracranial hypertension.  

Methods: The trial was a prospective, pragmatic, controlled trial involving adult patients with ICH who were undergoing removal of hematoma. ICH patients were randomly assigned at a 1:1 ratioto undergo CR with or without DC under the monitoring of intracranial pressure. The primary outcome was the proportion of unfavorable functional outcome (modified Rankin Scale 3–6) at 3 months. Secondary outcomes included the mortality at 3 months and the occurrence of reoperation.  

Results: A total of 102 patients were assigned to the CR with DC group and 102 to the CR group. Median hematoma volume was $54.0\ensuremath{\mathrm{m}}$ (range $30{-}80\mathrm{m}!$ ) and median preoperative Glasgow Coma Scale was 10 (range 5–15). At 3 months, 94 patients $(92.2\%)$ in CR with DC group and 83 patients $(81.4\%)$ in the CR group had unfavorable functional outcome $(P=0.023)$ ). Fourteen patients $(13.7\%)$ in the CR with DC group died versus five patients $(4.9\%)$ in the CR group $\ensuremath{\langleP=0.030\rangle}$ . The number of patients with reoperation was similar between the CR with DC group and CR group (5.9 vs. $3.9\%$ ; $P=0.5\cdot$ 17). Postoperative intracranial pressure values were not significantly different between two groups and the mean values were less than $20\mathrm{mmHg}$ .  

Conclusions: CR without DC decreased the rate of modified Rankin Scale score of 3–6 and mortality in patients with ICH, compared with CR with DC.  

Keywords: clot removal, decompressive craniectomy, intracerebral hemorrhage, intracranial pressure, outcome  

# Introduction  

Intracerebral hemorrhage (ICH) is the most fatal and refractory subtype of stroke, with mortality more than $50\%$ 1 year after ICH onset[1]. The mass effect of hematoma and following brain edema can result in the intracranial hypertension . Uncontrolled intracranial hypertension will lead to brain herniation and

death[2]. A 2019 systematic review and meta-analysis showed that the prevalence and mortality of intracranial hypertension were high after $\mathrm{ICH}^{[3]}$ .  

Decompressive craniectomy (DC), a surgery to remove part of the skull and open the underlying dura mater, maybe an effective method for reducing intracranial pressure[4–6]. Several studies have documented significant reduction of intracranial pressure following DC with secondary increased cerebral blood perfusion and cerebral oxygen supply resulting in decreasing morbidity and mortality[4,7,8]. However, severe traumatic brain injury and space-occupying hemispheric infarctions were the most common indication for a $\mathrm{DC}^{[5,9,10]}$ . The efficacy of DC for improving functional outcomes remains uncertain in ICH patients[11]. In most cases, DC is considered as a lifesaving procedure for ICH patients who warrant clot removal (CR) to prevent intracranial hypertension[11]. However, lacking of strong randomized evidence, DC is often performed based on clinician judgment and increase subsequent operation for reconstructing the skull (termed cranioplasty) that has risks. It is unclear whether the benefits of DC would be greater[11].  

To avoid pre-emptive DC during CR procedure in ICH patients meeting surgical indications, we conducted a prospective randomized controlled study to compare the outcomes of CR with or without DC in adult patients with ICH.  

# Material and methods  

# Study design and patients  

The trial was a prospective, pragmatic, controlled trial involving adult patients with ICH who were undergoing removal of hematoma. When the intraparenchymal hematoma volume exceeded $30\mathrm{ml}$ or the patient’s consciousness deteriorated, a surgery for CR was performed[12,13]. This trial was registered with ClinicalTrials.gov. This report is compliant with the CONSORT guideline[14]. Ethics approval was obtained from the Ethics Committee of our center. Written informed consent was obtained from patients or their approved surrogate.  

Eligible patients were aged 18–80 years with a supratentorial ICH with a stable volume of $30{-}80\mathrm{ml}$ , with a Glasgow Coma Scale (GCS) score of 5 or higher, and within $^{72\mathrm{~h~}}$ after stroke onset. The exclusion criteria included the ICH being secondary to aneurysm, vascular malformation, anticoagulant-associated pathogenesis, tumor, head trauma, or that a patient has severe renal, hepatic or respiratory failure or was unlikely to adhere to the treatment.  

# Randomization and treatment  

A computer-generated number sequence was used and a trialgroup assignment ratio of 1:1. Patients were randomly allocated to the CR with DC or CR groups under the monitoring of ICP. Hematoma was measured with the 3D-slicer software. Patients who were assigned to CR group had CR with ICP monitoring. The bone flap replaced and fixed to the surrounding skull with an appropriate fixation system before scalp closure, if removing a large bone flap. Patients who were assigned to $\mathrm{CR}+\mathrm{DC}$ group had CR with ICP monitoring, and their dura left open or there was a nonconstricting duraplasty before scalp closure, and their bone flap was left out. The type of the incision, method used to close the dura in the $\mathrm{CR}+$ DC group, and method for scalp closure were left to the discretion of the surgeons. After hematoma removal and careful observation without bleeding, the ICP monitoring device (Codman, Johnson and Johnson Medical Ltd.) was inserted into the cavity of hematoma, if the hematoma extended into the ventricle, the ICP would be inserted the ventricle along the extending corridor. According to the American Heart Association and the Chinese guidelines, ICP management was administered by treating physicians. Specified interventions included osmotic therapy, hyperventilation, analgesia, sedation, and surgical management[15]. The goals of ICP management were to ‘sustain ICP below $20\mathrm{mmHg}$ and to improve the patient’s level of consciousness’[15,16]. The protocol specified that placement of an ICP had to be followed by a computed tomography scan of the brain to monitor any new bleeding or rebleeding or aggravated brain edema[15,16]. According to the American Heart Association and the Chinese guidelines for the treatment of spontaneous ICH, the treatment protocol would be individually decided according to the result of repeat computed tomography and the condition of patients[11].  

# Outcomes  

The primary outcome was the proportion of unfavorable functional outcome measured by modified Rankin scale (mRS) at 3 months. Unfavorable outcome was defined as mRS grade 3–6 at 3 months. Secondary outcomes were all-cause mortality at 3 months after stroke and the proportion of reoperation. Exploratory analyses of the primary outcome at 3 months postICH were logistic regression on severity subgroup analyses; crosssectional mRS score (0–2 vs. 3–6) for hematoma subgroups, including for extension to ventricles, ICH location and ICH volume; cross-sectional mRS score (0–2 vs. 3–6) for demographic subgroups, including for age and sex; and analysis of cross-sectional mRS score (0–2 vs. 3–6) that time from stroke to treatment and GCS score. Safety outcomes were all-cause mortality at 30 days, bacterial brain infection at 30 days, and symptomatic bleeding after surgery.  

# Statistical analysis  

On the basis of the assumption that $90.7\%$ of patients would have an mRS score of 3–6 in the CR $^+$ DC group versus $74.0\%$ of patients in the CR group[17], we estimated that 200 patients (approximately 100 patients in each treatment group) would provide $81\%$ power at $\upalpha$ level 0.05 to detect an average effect size of $16.7\%$ . Outcome analyses were performed in the modified intention-to-treat (mITT) population, which included all randomly assigned patients except patients without consent and follow-up. Continuous data were presented as mean (SD) or median (interquartile ranges [IQRs]) and analyzed by t tests or Mann–Whitney $U$ tests, respectively. Categorical data were presented as counts (percentages) and analyzed by $\chi^{2}$ tests. Binary logistic regression was used in the subgroup analyses. Data analyses was performed using SPSS software for Windows (version 23.0, Inc.). A $P<0.05$ was considered statistically significant.  

# Results  

From May 2014 to January 2022, 1161 patients were screened for eligibility, and 204 were enrolled. A total of 204 patients were randomly assigned to the two study groups: 102 patients received CR and 102 underwent $\mathrm{CR}+\mathrm{DC}$ . Seventeen patients who were assigned to the CR group but underwent DC because of intracranial hypertension in the operation. Twenty-two patients who were assigned to the $\mathrm{CR}~+~\mathrm{DC}$ group but underwent CR, the main reason for nonadherence was normal intracranial pressure in the operation. Two hundred four patients $'n=102$ in the CR group; $n=102$ in the CR $+\mathrm{DC}$ group) received treatment and were included in the mITT analysis set (Fig. 1). The characteristics of the ICH patients at baseline were similar in the two groups (Table 1). The mean age was $54\pm11$ years, and 146 $(71.6\%)$ patients were male. The GCS score on admission was $10\pm3$ , range from 5 to 15. The mean hematoma was $54.0\pm14.6\mathrm{~m}$ , range from 30 to $80~\mathrm{ml}$ , and 157 $(77.0\%)$ patients were deep hematoma. The surgery time was $3.6\pm1.0\mathrm{h}$ .

# Discussion  

In this study, by comparison to CR with DC, CR without DC resulted in a statistically significant reduction in the rate of modified Rankin Scale score of 3–6 (92.2 vs. $81.4\%$ ). The rate of and mortality at 90 days was also lower in the CR group than CR with DC group (4.9 vs. $13.7\%$ ). The two groups did not differ in either the rate of reoperation or postoperative complications. The rate of intracranial hypertension in CR group was not higher than that in CR with DC group (46.0 vs. $39.6\%$ ).  

DC has been shown to be valuable for ICP reduction in many diseases associated with intracranial hypertension[5,6,9,10,18], especially in severe $\mathrm{TBI}^{[5,6,9]}$ . However, whether DC-induced ICP decrease translates into improved outcome remains controversial[18]. Actually, some studies have shown adding DC to hematoma evacuation resulted in increased survival at discharge with good functional outcome[19–23]. However, other investigations demonstrated that surgical CR without DC also bring benefits to ICH patients[24,25]. The efficacy of DC for improving functional outcomes is uncertain[11]. In this prospective randomized controlled study, we demonstrate DC cannot confer significant benefit for ICH patients undergoing surgical CR. This finding has practical implications that pre-emptive DC should be avoided during CR procedure in ICH patients meeting surgical indications, which help prevent subsequent possible cranioplasty. The mRS distribution at 3 months were as follows: death, $13.7\%$ in the DC $\mathrm{~;~+~CR~}$ group and $4.9\%$ in the CR group; vegetative state, 8.8 versus $4.9\%$ ; severe disability, 43.1 versus $48.0\%$ ; moderate disability, 26.5 versus $23.5\%$ ; mild disability, 5.8 versus $10.8\%$ ; and good recovery 2.0 versus $7.8\%$ . The rate of moderate disability or better $(\mathrm{mRS}0-3)$ was slightly higher in the CR group than that in the $\mathrm{CR}+\mathrm{DC}$ group (42.2 vs. $34.3\%$ , $P{=}0.249;$ ). However, the rates of moderate disability or better in the study at 3 months were lower than those in other studies at 12 months[26,27]. Functional outcome may be improvement at 12 months, because the recovery trajectory of ICH survivors may continue up to 1 year[27].

Safety is the most matters for surgery. The rate of intracranial hypertension is high in ICH patients[11]. Our study found that postoperative ICP trend maintained within the acceptable stable range after surgery in both CR and CR $^+$ DC groups. The proportion of patients with intracranial hypertension (ICP $\geq20\mathrm{mmHg}$ ) in CR group was similar to that in the $\mathrm{CR}+\mathrm{DC}$ group (46.0 vs. $39.6\%$ , $P=0.387$ ). Moreover, the events of ICP more than $30\mathrm{mmHg}$ were also no difference between the two groups $P=0.692^{\circ},$ ). Intracranial hypertension decreased after the treatment of mannitol for most ICH patients, only 10 $(4.9\%)$ of 204 patients performed reoperation in our study. There was no difference in reoperation at 1 months after surgery between the two groups $3.9\%$ for CR group, $5.9\%$ for $\mathrm{CR}~+~\mathrm{DC}$ group; $P=0.517_{,}$ , supporting the safety of the procedure. CR with or without DC may also have had other harmful complications, including ischemic stroke, intracranial infection, and hydrocephalus. However, the rates of most complications were similar in two groups, which were lower than those in previous studies[28].  

# Limitations  

The study still has some limitations. First, the primary outcome was assessed by telephone interviews instead of face-to-face interview with validated clinical examination, and may not reflect findings on clinical examination in the trial. Second, the primary and secondary outcomes were evaluated at 3 months. It may be short to evaluate the outcome of ICH patients. It is more valuable if providing mRS at long-term follow-up. Third, the study was a randomized, controlled, prospective trial in a single center. Fourth, the ICP data were available for 5 days, and some factors causing ICP fluctuation are lacking, such as sedated, ventilated, and the treatment of mannitol. Finally, the trial did not show the effect of other surgical techniques, such as external ventricular drainage, surgical approach, endoscopy, and neurosurgery, which may have a role for the outcome.  

# Conclusion  

In summary, CR without DC decreased the rate of modified Rankin Scale score of 3–6 and mortality in patients with ICH, compared with CR with DC. The study also showed that CR without DC was an effective therapeutic strategy for controlling intracranial hypertension in ICH patients.
