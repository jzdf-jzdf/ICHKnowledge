# TAGE Score for Symptomatic Intracranial Hemorrhage Prediction After Successful Endovascular Treatment in Acute Ischemic Stroke  

Paul Janvier , MD; Basile Kerleroux , MD; Guillaume Turc, MD, PhD; Marco Pasi, MD, PhD; Wassim Farhat, MD; Nicolas Bricout, MD; Joseph Benzakoun, MD, PhD; Laurence Legrand , MD; Frédéric Clarençon , MD, PhD; Serge Bracard, MD, PhD; Catherine Oppenheim, MD, PhD; Grégoire Boulouis, MD, PhD; Hilde Henon, MD, PhD; Olivier Naggara , MD, PhD\*; Wagih Ben Hassen, MD, PhD\*  

BACKGROUND: Determine if early venous filling (EVF) after complete successful recanalization with mechanical thrombectomy in acute ischemic stroke is an independent predictor of symptomatic intracranial hemorrhage (sICH) and integrate EVF into a risk score for sICH prediction.  

METHODS: Consecutive patients with anterior acute ischemic stroke treated by mechanical thrombectomy issued from patients enrolled in the THRACE trial (Thrombectomie des Artères Cérébrales) and from 2 prospective registries were included and divided into a derivation (Center I; $\scriptstyle{\mathsf{n}}=402.$ ) and validation cohorts (THRACE and center 2; $\scriptstyle\ensuremath{\mathsf{n}}=507$ ). EVF was evaluated by 2 blinded readers. sICH was defined according to the modified European cooperative acute stroke study II. Clinical and radiological data were analyzed in the derivation cohort (C1) to identify independent predictors of sICH and construct a predictive score test on the validation cohort (THRACE + C2).  

RESULTS: Symptomatic ICH rate was similar between the two cohorts $.9.9\%$ and $8.9\%$ respectively, $P=0.9$ ). Time from onsetto-successful recanalization ${>}270$ minutes (odds ratio [OR], 7.8 $[95\%$ CI, 2.5–24]), Alberta Stroke Program Early CT Score $\leq5$ [OR, 2.49 $95\%$ CI, 1.8–8.1) or 6–7 [OR, 1.15 $95\%$ CI, 1.03–4.46)]), glucose blood level ${>}7$ mmol/L (OR, 2.92 $[95\%$ CI, 1.26–6.7]), and EVF presence (OR, 11.9 $[95\%$ CI, 3.8–37.5]) were independent predictors of sICH and constituted the Time–Alberta Stroke Program Early CT–Glycemia–EVF score. Time–Alberta Stroke Program Early CT–Glycemia–EVF score was associated with an increased risk of sICH in the derivation cohort (OR increase per unit, 1.99 $[95\%$ CI, $1.53\substack{-2.59}$ ; $P\mathrm{<}0.001$ ) with area under the curve, 0.832 $[95\%$ CI, 0.767–0.898]. The score had good performance in the validation cohort (area under the curve, 0.801 $[95\%$ CI, 0.69–0.91]).  

CONCLUSIONS: Time–Alberta Stroke Program Early CT–Glycemia–EVF score is a simple tool with readily available clinical variables with good performances for sICH prediction after mechanical thrombectomy.

Early venous filling (EVF) is a potential imaging biomarker of luxury perfusion previously reported as a strong predictor of sICH after mechanical thrombectomy (MT).9,10 EVF can be detected after successful recanalization on digital subtraction angiography; consisting of an early opacification at the arterial phase of a cortical or deep vein.10 This early filling is potentially linked to local capillary dysfunction in the infarcted area leading to focal hyperemia (also called luxury perfusion) and blood-brain barrier disruption,11,12 which contributes to the development of reperfusion hemorrhage.9,10,13 No predictive score has yet integrated EVF for sICH prediction. The aim of our study was to determine in a large successfully recanalized population if EVF was an independent predictor of sICH and derive a score for sICH prediction with an external validation on a population issued from a prospective registry and THRACE trial (Thrombectomie des Artères Cérébrales).14  



# METHODS  



# Data Sharing Statement  

All data can be made available upon reasonable request to the corresponding author and after approval by the local ethics committee.  



# Participants  

Between January 2015 and December 2020, data were retrospectively extracted from two prospective registries including consecutive acute ischemic stroke with large vessel occlusion treated by MT. Derivation cohort was issued from a prospective registry from a first comprehensive stroke center (center 1: Centre Hospitalier Sainte-Anne, Paris, France). Validation cohort was issued from a prospective registry from a second comprehensive stroke center (center 2: Centre HospitaloUniversitaire de Lille, France) and from the randomized therapeutic THRACE trial.14  

Inclusion criteria for all cases were age $\geq18$ years, anterior circulation large vessel occlusion treated by MT±intravenous thrombolysis (IV tPA [intravenous tissue-type plasminogen activator]) with successful recanalization defined by a final expanded Treatment in Cerebral Ischemia (eTICI) ${}^{\geq}2\mathsf{B}$ ; postreperfusion digital subtracted angiography of sufficient quality for EVF assessment. Exclusion criteria were: no available control imaging (computed tomography [CT] scan or magnetic resonance imaging) or National Institutes of Health Stroke Scale evaluation at least 24 hours after MT. Baseline characteristics of excluded patients did not differ from those included in the study population (Table S1).  



# Standard Protocol Approvals, Registrations, and Patient Consents  

Data was extracted from prospective registries held after approval of national ethic committee (CNIL, MR004). Written informed consent was obtained from all patients or their legal representatives in the THRACE trial after approval of the Comité de Protection des Personnes III Nord Est Ethics Committee. This article was prepared according to the Transparent Reporting of a multivariable prediction model for individual prognosis or diagnosis statement.15  



# Clinical and Radiological Outcomes Assessment  

Stroke severity was assessed at admission by trained vascular neurologists with the National Institutes of Health Stroke Scale score (range, 0–42, with higher scores indicating greater stroke severity). Clinical and biological variables such as age, gender, National Institutes of Health Stroke Scale, blood pressure, dyslipidemia, current smoking status, diabetes, prestroke modified Rankin Scale, serum glucose level before treatment, administration of IV tPA were extracted.  



# Symptomatic Intracranial Hemorrhage  

Hemorrhagic transformation was evaluated on the 24-hour control imaging (CT-scan or magnetic resonance imaging) and classified into 4 following subtypes according to ECASS (European Cooperative Acute Stroke Study)16 classification: hemorrhagic infarction 1: hemorrhagic infarction small petechial hemorrhages; hemorrhagic infarction 2: hemorrhagic infarction with confluent petechial hemorrhages; parenchymal hematoma 1: parenchymal hematoma ${<}30\%$ of the infarcted area with minor mass effect attributable to the hematoma; parenchymal hematoma 2: parenchymal hematoma ${>}30\%$ of the infarcted area with substantial mass effect attributable to the hematoma. The primary end point was sICH defined by modified ECASS-II16: any intracranial hemorrhage on imaging control performed 24 hours after MT, associated with neurological deterioration (increase of ${\ge}4$ points on National Institutes of Health Stroke Scale from baseline).  



# EVF Identification  

All digital subtraction angiography were reviewed by 2 independent experienced interventional neuroradiologists (7- [W.B.H.] and 15-years’ experience [O.N.]) blinded to all patients’ clinical outcome and other imaging data. Readers provided a consensus evaluation regarding thrombus location on the pretreatment angiogram and assessed final eTICI and EVF on post-treatment angiogram. EVF presence was assessed on the post recanalization angiogram, defined by an early opacification of a cerebral vein concomitant to the arterial or capillary phase as previously described.10 Inter-reader concordance for EVF assessment was compared, with a substantial interobserver agreement (Cohen $\mathrm{\bfK}{=}0.72$ ).  



# Statistical Analysis  



# Score Derivation  

Continuous variables with a normal distribution were described as mean±SD and non-normally distributed variables were described as median and interquartile range. Univariable analysis was used to compare demographic, clinical, and radiological factors between patients with and without sICH. Multivariable logistic regression was then performed with factors associated with sICH with $R<0.1$ in the univariate analysis. Continuous variables such as time from onset-to-successful reperfusion, Alberta Stroke Program Early CT Score (ASPECTS) and glycemia were categorized using previously defined cutoffs.7 The scoring system was constructed by assigning points to the variables according to the value of the multivariable model regression coefficients: 1 point for regression coefficient ${>}1$ and ${\le}2$ , 2 points for variables with a regression coefficient $>2$ and ${\le}3$ and 3 points for variables with a regression coefficient ${>}3$ . Area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (ie, C statistics) was calculated as a measure of predictive ability of the model. The calibration of the score for sICH prediction was assessed with Hosmer-Lemeshow test and a graphical plot. External validation was performed on population issued from center 2 and THRACE trial.

# Predictors of sICH  

sICH was present in 40 patients $(9.9\%)$ in the derivation cohort. In univariate analysis, factors associated with sICH compared with no-sICH group were EVF presence $(72.5\%$ versus $32.3\%$ , respectively, $P<10^{-4}$ ), admission glucose level (mean 7.74 versus 6.74 mmol/L, respectively; $_{P=0.03.}$ ), ASPECTS (median, 5 versus 7, respectively; $_{P=0.003}$ ), onset-to-imaging (median, 161 versus 109 minutes; $P\mathrm{=}0.001$ ), onset-to-groin puncture (median, 270 versus 204 minutes; $_{P=0.02}$ ), onset-to-successful recanalization (median, 319 versus 247 minutes; $P{=}0.004$ ; Table 2). In the binary logistic regression model, independent predictor of sICH included: EVF presence (odds ratio [OR], 11.9 $[95\%$ CI, 3.8–37.5]; $P<10^{-3},$ ), admission glucose level ${>}7$ mmol/L (OR, 2.92 $[95\%$ CI, 1.26–6.7]; $P{<}0.03,$ ), ASPECTS ${\le}5$ (OR, 2.49 $[95\%$ CI, 1.8–8.1]; $P{=}0.014$ ), 6 to 7 (OR, 1.15 $[95\%$ CI, 1.03-4,4]; $_{P=0.046}$ ), and onsetto-successful recanalization ${\ge}270$ minutes (OR, 7.8 $[95\%$ CI, 2.5–24]; $P\leftarrow10^{-3}$ ; Table 3). The onset-to-groin puncture time ${\ge}270$ minutes was not associated with poor outcome after multivariate adjustment.

# DISCUSSION  

We identified in this study independent predictors of sICH in patients achieving successful recanalization after MT for acute ischemic stroke with large vessel occlusion. High delay from onset-to-successful recanalization, low ASPECTS, admission hyperglycemia, and presence of an EVF after MT were strong predictors associated with sICH and constituted the TAGE score. This new score successfully predicted the occurrence of sICH in two large heterogeneous cohorts of successfully recanalized patients.  

Time from stroke onset-to-recanalization was a strong independent predictor of sICH in the derivation cohort, consistent with previous studies highlighting the higher rates of sICH and a detrimental effect on functional outcome with increased time-to-treatment.17–20 The time-dependent effect of reperfusion therapies in acute stroke emphasizes the need for time-saving policies21 to reduce door to treatment timings.  

Hyperglycemia and ASPECTS are also well-known predictors of sICH incorporated in the TAGE score. Hyperglycemia has a deleterious effect on blood-brain barrier during ischemic stroke22,23 leading to increased infarct growth and higher risk of sICH.24,25 Low ASPECTS is correlated to higher infarct burden and increased sICH rates explaining its routine use for patients’ selection for reperfusion therapies.26  

The TAGE score is the first pragmatic tool for early and reliable prediction of sICH incorporation in the EVF finding. This angiographic sign can be easily assessed on digital subtraction angiography after successful recanalization with mechanical thrombectomy with reproducible results.9,10,13,27 The presence of EVF is probably linked to focal hyperemia in the infarct territory, due to blood-brain barrier disruption and microcapillary dysfunction, leading to HT and unfavorable outcome.28 EVF was a powerful predictor of sICH in the derivation cohort, consistent with previous studies, and its addition significantly enhanced the model prediction ability and allowed a good prediction performance of the

TAGE score on the derivation and external validation cohorts. The other novelty of the TAGE score compared with previously available scores, is the target population consisting only of successfully recanalized $(\mathsf{e}\mathsf{T}|\mathsf{C}|\geq2\mathsf{B})$ patients. We chose to focus on this population since EVF cannot be reliably assessed when the angiogram is partially masked by persistent arterial occlusions. Nevertheless, successful recanalization is achieved in ${>}70\%$ of patients with proximal occlusion treated with modern MT techniques.29 It seemed more relevant to develop the score on patients with successful recanalization, for whom an sICH would have a meaningful impact on functional outcome, rather than incorporate patients with unsuccessful MT for whom functional outcome is already heavily burdened by persistent arterial occlusion.  

The strengths of our study included a relatively large sample size of successfully treated patients in the derivation and external validation cohorts and the use of simple variables that can be quickly obtained in real-world conditions of acute stroke treatment. Nevertheless, several limitations should be noted. Data were issued from prospective registries and THRACE trial, with heterogeneous selection criteria. Presence of EVF can only be diagnosed once the therapeutic decision has been made and the reperfusion therapy performed, which greatly limits its impact on the therapeutic decision. Although the purpose of this kind of predictive score is not to select patients for a particular treatment, but to alert the clinician to an increased risk of sICH, imposing a more severe control of blood pressure or glycemia. Accurate and early identification of patients at high risk of symptomatic hemorrhagic transformation allows for targeted use of post-procedure monitoring and could be useful for patient selection in future intervention studies designed to avoid this outcome. Another limitation is the lack of patients’ hemodynamic data such as arterial pressure before and after MT that could contribute to sICH.30–32  

In conclusion, the TAGE score is a simple and accurate tool for sICH prediction, using time-to-successful reperfusion, ASPECTS, level-blood-glucose and EVF presence, all easily available at an early phase of acute ischemic stroke management. Larger studies are warranted to estimate the efficiency and reproducibility of this score.
