INTERACT2 Study

BACKGROUND AND PURPOSE: We determined associations of physiological abnormalities (systolic blood pressure, glucose, and body temperature) and warfarin use with outcomes in spontaneous intracerebral hemorrhage.  

METHODS: Post hoc analyses of INTERACT2 (Intensive Blood Pressure Reduction in Acute Cerebral Hemorrhage Trial) comparing systolic blood pressure control $(<140$ versus ${<}180\mathrm{mm}\mathsf{Hg},$ ) in 2839 hypertensive patients with intracerebral hemorrhage (onset ${<}6$ hours). Multivariable logistic regression defined associations of baseline scores assigned as 0 to 6 per $10\mathrm{mm}\mathsf{Hg}$ systolic blood pressure increase (range, $150{-}220\mathsf{mm}\mathsf{Hg},$ ) and 0 or 1 for serum glucose $(\le6.5$ versus ${>}6.5$ mmol/L), body temperature $(\le37.5^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$ versus ${>}37.5^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$ , and warfarin use (no versus yes) and death or major disability (modified Rankin Scale scores 3–6 at 90 days).  

RESULTS: Baseline score distribution was 0 $(7.7\%)$ , 1 ( $15.6\%\mathrm{`}$ , 2 ( $19.0\%\cdot$ , 3 $19.1\%)$ , 4 $15.2\%)$ , 5 $(11.6\%)$ , 6 $(8.9\%)$ , and 7 $(2.9\%)$ . After adjustment for baseline neurological severity and potential confounders, significant linear associations were evident for increasing (per point) score and death or major disability (odds ratio, 1.12 $[95\%$ CI, 1.07–1.17]), death (odds ratio, 1.15 $[95\%$ CI, 1.07–1.23]), and major disability (odds ratio, 1.10 $[95\%$ CI, 1.05–1.15]).  

CONCLUSIONS: Combination of abnormal physiological parameters and warfarin use is associated with poor outcomes in intracerebral hemorrhage. Effects of their early control is under investigation in INTERACT3 (Intensive Care Bundle With Blood Pressure Reduction in Acute Cerebral Hemorrhage Trial).

# METHODS  

INTERACT2 was an international, multicenter, prospective, randomized, open, blinded end point trial of 2839 patients with spontaneous ICH $(<6$ hours) and elevated SBP ( $150-$ $220\mathrm{mm}\mathsf{Hg}$ ) randomized to intensive (target ${<}140\mathrm{mm}\mathsf{Hg},$ ) or guideline-recommended (SBP ${<}180\mathrm{mm}\mathsf{Hg}$ ) blood pressure lowering.7 Demographic, history, and clinical data, including neurological severity on the National Institutes of Health Stroke Scale, were collected at baseline. Deidentified participant data used in these analyses can be shared by formal request to the corresponding author. Clinical outcomes were death or major disability (defined by modified Rankin Scale scores 3–6), death, and major disability (modified Rankin Scale scores 3–5) at 90 days. Ethics committee approvals and informed consents (patient or legal representative) were obtained at all participating hospitals.  

Scores were assigned for abnormal baseline levels of defined variables according to published odds ratios (ORs) for poor outcome: SBP increase (per $10\mathrm{mm}\mathsf{Hg}$ , ${>}140$ mm Hg; OR, 2.69 $[95\%$ CI, 1.13–6.4]),8 hyperglycemia (OR, 1.36 $[95\%$ CI, $1.08\mathrm{-}1.71]$ ),4 pyrexia (OR, 1.4 $[95\%$ CI, $1.01\substack{-2.0}]),^{5}$ and prior antithrombotic use (OR, 1.38 $[95\%$ CI, 0.93–2.04]).6 Baseline scores were calculated as 0 to 6 per $10\mathrm{mm}\mathsf{Hg}$ SBP increase (range, $150{-}220\mathrm{mm}\mathsf{Hg},$ ) and 0 or 1 for blood glucose $_{,\leq6.5}$ versus ${>}6.5$ mmol/L), body temperature $(\le37.5^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$ versus ${>}37.5^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$ ), and prior warfarin use (no versus yes).  

Multivariable logistic regression was used to determine associations of baseline scores and clinical outcomes, with adjustment for age, region, sex, hematoma volume, intraventricular extension, National Institutes of Health Stroke Scale score, hematoma location (deep versus other), and randomized treatment. Data are presented with OR and $95\%$ CI, with 2-sided $R{<}0.05$ being statistically significant. All analyses involved SAS, version 9.3 (SAS Institute, Cary, NC).

# DISCUSSION  

Secondary analysis of INTERACT2 shows a combination of abnormal baseline variables, SBP, blood glucose, and body temperature, and prior warfarin use significantly increases the odds of poor outcomes in acute ICH.  

Physiological variables are often elevated, overlapping, and readily modifiable in acute ICH. The most common, elevated SBP is associated with hyperglycemia and prior antithrombotic use9 and predicts hematoma growth, perihematomal edema, neurological deterioration, and poor functional outcome.8 Hyperglycemia can occur independently of diabetes and as with pyrexia, predicts poor outcome,3–5 while antithrombotic use, especially warfarin, is associated with greater hematoma growth and poor outcome.6 As well as the influence of comorbid chronic hypertension and diabetes, hyperglycemia and pyrexia can alter cerebral metabolism and increase oxidative stress and inflammatory cytokines.10

We acknowledge that our modeling was limited in using single baseline measures, which can change naturally or in response to treatment over time, while data supporting a benefit of intensive blood pressure lowering are not as robust as hoped.7 Moreover, there are limited data showing that rapid replenishment of vitamin K–dependent coagulation factors reduces hematoma growth and improve outcomes,11,12 and there have been no trials of glycemic control or pyrexia treatment in ICH. However, a before-and-after evaluation of a care bundle involving anticoagulation reversal, intensive SBP lowering, and rapid access to neurosurgery and critical care showed improved survival for ICH patients admitted to hospitals in Greater Manchester, United Kingdom, apparently mediated by a reduction in early do-not-resuscitate and increased critical care management.13 Collectively, therefore, there is good rationale for the ongoing INTERACT3 (Intensive Care Bundle With Blood Pressure Reduction in Acute Cerebral  

Hemorrhage Trial), which uses an international, multicenter, open, blinded outcome assessed, steppedwedge cluster randomized design to determine whether an intervention of a rapid goal-directed early intensive blood pressure lowering, hyperglycemia control, pyrexia treatment, and anticoagulation reversal, compared with standard of care, improves functional outcome from acute ICH (https://www.clinicaltrials.gov; unique identifier: NCT03209258).  

Although our large dataset had systematic measures for a range of variables in patients with broad characteristics from different health settings, our study is limited in being post hoc and vulnerable to selection bias by the exclusion of patients with a poor prognosis or large hematoma volume, and data on the control of anticoagulation were absent.  

The finding that a combination of abnormal physiological variables and warfarin use has strong associations with poor outcomes in ICH provides a rationale for INTERACT3—a large-scale randomized evaluation of a care bundle that may form a widely applied management protocol to optimize recovery from this serious illness.
