# Abstract  

Background. For patients with acute large vessel occlusion (ALVO) in the anterior circulation who are able to undergo mechanical thrombectomy (MTB) within 4.5 hours, the need for intravenous thrombolysis prior to the intervention remains unclear.  

Methods. Patients who were eligible for intravenous thrombolysis, who presented with ALVO in the anterior circulation, and who started MTB within 4.5 hours were matched at a 1:1 ratio to a thrombectomy alone group or to a bridging therapy group. Patients in the bridging therapy group were administered intravenous alteplase at a standard dose of $0.9\mathrm{mg/kg}$ . We evaluated the safety and efficacy of the thrombectomy alone group compared with the bridging therapy group.  

Results. From December 2020 to September 2021, 60 patients were recruited in the study and completed the trial. The baseline parameters of patients were similar between the two groups. At the 90-day follow-up, 18 patients $(60\%)$ ) in the thrombectomy alone group versus 18 patients $(60\%)$ in the bridging therapy group achieved functional independence (odds ratio [OR]: 1, $95\%$ confidence interval [CI], 0.36–2.81). The successful recanalization (Thrombolysis in Cerebral Infarction [TICI] grade 2b to 3) rates on final angiography were $90\%$ and $86.7\%$ , respectively (OR, 0.72, $95\%$ CI, 0.15–3.55). No significant differences were found between the two groups in the occurrence of symptomatic intracranial hemorrhage or 90-day mortality.  

Conclusion. Our preliminary results did not show the superiority of thrombectomy alone versus standard bridging therapy in patients with ALVO in the anterior circulation who undergo MTB within 4.5 hours. A larger sample size and other randomized controlled trials remain necessary to validate these results. Clin Ter 2022; 173 (2):107-114 doi: 10.7417/CT.2022.2403  

Key words: thrombectomy alone, bridging therapy, large vessel occlusion, acute ischemic stroke  

# Introduction  

Acute stroke is currently the second leading cause of mortality worldwide, after coronary artery disease (1). Stroke survivors may become disabled, requiring temporary or lifelong support, resulting in a heavy economic burden associated with treatment and rehabilitation. Ischemic stroke accounts for the majority of stroke events, accounting for up to $85\%$ of acute stroke occurrences (2). The incidence of this disease is projected to increase in the future due to a growing aging population worldwide (3). Therefore, a better understanding of the risk factors is necessary to improve stroke prevention, combined with the development of effective treatment methods, is necessary to reduce the burdens associated with this disease.  

Intravenous thrombolysis (IVT) was the sole treatment option available for acute ischemic stroke for nearly 20 years, since 1995 (4). Starting in 2015, a new era of stroke treatment began, with the consistent demonstration of the safety and efficacy of mechanical thrombectomy (MTB) for patients with large vessel occlusion (LVO) (5–9). Since then, MTB has become the standard of care for acute ischemic stroke (AIS) patients with LVO who can be treated within 4.5 hours, regardless of previous IVT use (10).  

According to current reputable guidelines, IVT using alteplase is recommended in parallel with MTB for eligible patients with AIS due to LVO in the anterior circulation who are treated within 4.5 hours (11,12). However, IVT followed by MTB, known as bridging therapy, is currently controversial. The use of IVT is believed to increase the potential for earlier recanalization due to the rapidity of administration and the softening of the thrombus, which may facilitate subsequent MTB (13–15). By contrast, IVT has also been associated with some disadvantages. First, the administration of the IVT procedure may delay MTB initiation in patients treated with bridging therapy. Second, the thrombus can become partially lysed by IVT and migrated to distal branches, which can complicate the MTB procedure (16,17). Third, IVT may increase the risk of symptomatic intracranial hemorrhage (ICH) after revascularization (18). Finally, in patients with LVO related to atherosclerosis, which is a common etiology among the Asian population, the use of IVT before MTB may increase the risks of symptomatic ICH when additional antithrombotics are required to perform acute stenting (19). A series of clinical trials have been and are currently being performed to address the question of whether IVT is necessary before MTB for the treatment of acute LVO (ALVO) (20–23). Our experimental intervention trial was conducted to test the hypothesis that the safety and efficacy of MTB alone, when performed within 4.5 hours of ALVO, were superior to standard bridging therapy.  

# Methods  

Study design and patient selection  

This study was conducted using a nonrandomized controlled, matched pairs design. Subjects were included if they 1) signed informed consent; 2) were aged ${\ge}18$ years; 3) presented with clinical manifestations of AIS, with National Institutes of Health Stroke Scale (NIHSS) scores ranging from 6 to 24 points; 4) presented with occlusion of the internal carotid artery (ICA) or the M1 or M2 segment of the middle cerebral artery (MCA) on computed tomography angiography (CTA) or magnetic resonance angiography (MRA); 5) presented with an Alberta Stroke Program Early Computed Tomography (CT) Score (ASPECTS) $\geq7$ ; 6) satisfied indications for IVT and MTB; and 7) underwent groin puncture within 4.5 hours of stroke onset. Subjects were excluded if 1) they presented with any contraindication to IVT or 2) were diagnosed with a severely disabling condition (modified Rankin Scale $[\mathrm{mRS}]>2$ ).  

Eligible patients are allocated in pairs to the thrombectomy alone group or the bridging therapy group, with pairs matched according to age ( $K^{70}$ years or ${\geq}70$ years), NIHSS score ( $_{<14}$ or ${\ge}14$ ), and occlusion site (M1 or other sites).  

The sample size was calculated based on the assumption that $60\%$ of patients in the thrombectomy alone group would achieve functional independence outcomes at the 90-day follow-up. This reference proportion for the bridging therapy group, obtained from the MR CLEAN trial, was $32.6\%$ (5). Based on a level of significance of $5\%$ , a power of $80\%$ , and with adjustment for a $15\%$ attrition rate, a total of 120 patients were estimated to be necessary to observe a difference between thrombectomy alone and the bridging therapy groups, with 60 patients in each group.  

Patient treatment  

Immediately following CT or MRI to determine eligibility for IVT and MTB, the patients were assigned to one of two treatment groups. The benefits and risks of treatment were explained in detail to family members, and a signed consent form was obtained before treatment initiation.  

Patients who were assigned to the bridging therapy group immediately received a standard dose of $0.9~\mathrm{mg}$ alteplase per kg of body weight (maximum dose of $90~\mathrm{mg}$ ).  

All patients were then transferred to the catheterization laboratory without delay to undergo the MTB procedure. The type of anesthesia (conscious sedation or general anesthesia) was determined according to the clinical status of the patient and the preference of each interventionist. Thrombectomy was performed using a second-generation stent retriever (Solitaire Platinum stent, Medtronic, Irvine, CA, USA; Trevor stent, Stryker Neurovascular, Mountain View, CA, USA), aspiration tubing (Sofia Plus catheter, MicroVention, CA, USA; Jet 7 catheter, Alameda, CA, USA; React catheter, Medtronic, Irvine, CA, USA), or a combined technique, at the discretion of the treating physician. Balloon angioplasty and extracranial or intracranial stenting could be performed as rescue therapy in LVO related to atherosclerosis, which required antiplatelets in some cases. The Thrombolysis in Cerebral Infarction (TICI) classification was used to grade the initial and final angiograms to evaluate reperfusion outcomes. The procedure was completed using an Angio-Seal 8F (Terumo) device or manual compression to accomplish femoral artery closure.  

Patient follow-up  

From 18 to 30 hours after the intervention, patients were re-evaluated for all clinical and radiological parameters. Clinical assessments included NIHSS score and hematoma at the site of groin puncture. Imaging assessments included ASPECTS scale, vessel recanalization, ICH on multimodal CT or MRI, and femoral artery pseudoaneurysm on Doppler ultrasound or CTA, if suspected.  

On day 90, the functional outcome (mRS) was assessed via telephone or at a planned visit. Severe adverse events affecting the final clinical outcome were recorded.  

# Outcomes  

The primary outcome was the percentage of functional independence, as defined by $\mathrm{mRS}\leq2$ at 90 days. The secondary efficacy outcomes were the overall distribution of mRS at 90 days (ordinal shift analysis) and the percentage of successful revascularization (TICI grade 2b to 3) at the end of the procedure. Safety outcomes were the percentage of symptomatic ICH and the percentage of death at 90 days.  

Statistical analysis  

The baseline characteristics of patients treated with and without IVT were compared using the Chi-square or Fisher’s exact tests for categorical variables and Student’s t-test or the Mann–Whitney U test for continuous variables.  

The good clinical outcome $(\mathrm{mRS}\leq2$ ) rate at 90 days and the successful revascularization (TICI grade 2b or 3) rate was compared using the Chi-square test. The mortality rate at 90 days and the symptomatic ICH rate was compared using Fisher’s exact test. For the overall distribution of mRS (ordinal shift analysis), the common odds ratio (OR) was obtained from generalized linear mixed models. All statistical tests were performed as one-sided, with $\mathrm{p}<0.05$ indicating a significant difference. Data were processed and analyzed using SPSS 16.0 software (SPSS Inc, Chicago, USA).

# Results  

From December 25, 2020, to September 17, 2021, a total of 163 patients were screened for study inclusion, and 75 qualified patients were recruited to participate in the study at Bach Mai Hospital in Vietnam. Among the recruited participants, 60 patients completed the trial, including 30 assigned to the thrombectomy alone group and 30 assigned to the bridging therapy group (Fig. 1).  

Baseline characteristics  

The baseline parameters of the patients were similar between the two groups (Table 1). The median age of the 60 patients was 64.5 years (interquartile range (IQR), 59–76.25 years), 39 patients $(65\%)$ were men, and the median NIHSS score was 12 (IQR, 10–15). The median ASPECTS value was 7 (IQR, 7–9), and the occlusion site was ICA in 22 cases $(36.7\%)$ , M1 in 33 cases $(55\%)$ , and M2 in 5 cases $(8.3\%)$ . According to the TOAST classification for stroke etiology, cardioembolism was the most common cause in our cohort $(51.7\%)$ ; large-artery atherosclerosis ranked second, accounting for $38.3\%$ .
