# Decompressive craniectomy and expansive duraplasty with evacuation of hypertensive intracerebral hematoma, a randomized controlled trial  

Wael Mohamed Mohamed Moussa1 $\cdot$ Wael Khedr1  

Received: 5 January 2016 /Revised: 22 April 2016 / Accepted: 5 May 2016   
$\copyright$ Springer-Verlag Berlin Heidelberg 2016  

Abstract Hypertensive intracerebral hemorrhage (ICH) has high morbidity and mortality rates. Decompressive craniectomy (DC) is generally used for the treatment of cases associated with refractory increased intracranial pressure (ICP). In this study, we investigated the beneficial effects of adding DC and expansive duraplasty (ED) to hematoma evacuation in patients who underwent surgery for large hypertensive ICH. A prospective randomized controlled clinical trial where 40 patients diagnosed having large hypertensive ICH was randomly allocated to either group A or B, each comprised 20 patients. Group A patients, the treatment group, were submitted to hematoma evacuation together with DC and ED, whereas group B patients, the control group, were submitted only to hematoma evacuation. Twenty-three $(57.5~\%)$ of the patients were males, with an overall age range of 34–79 years (mean 59.3 years). Preoperative Glasgow Coma Scale (GCS) scores in group A ranged from 4 to 13 (mean 7.1), while in group B it ranged from 4 to 12 (mean 6.8). Postoperative hydrocephalus occurred in 3 $(15\%)$ patients in group A and in 4 $(20\%)$ patients in group B, whereas meningitis occurred in one patient $(5\%)$ in group A. The mortality rate was 2 $(10\%)$ patients in group A as compared to 5 $(25\%)$ patients in group B $(p{=}0.407)$ . High admission GCS $(p=0.0032)$ , younger age $(p=0.0023)$ , smaller hematoma volume $(p{=}0.044)$ , subcortical hematoma location $(p=0.041)$ ), absent or minimal preoperative $(p{=}0.0068)$ , and postoperative $(p=0.0031)$ ) midline shift as well as absent intraventricular extension of the hematoma $(p=0.036)$ contributed significantly to a better outcome. Selected patients’ subgroups who benefited from adding DC and ED to ICH evacuation were age category of 30 to less than 50 $(p=0.0015\$ ) and from 50 to less than 70 $(p=0.00619)$ as well as immediate preoperative GCS from 6 to 8 $(p=0.000436)$ and from 9 to 12 $(p=0.00774)$ . At 6 months’ follow-up, 14 ( $70\%$ patients of group A had favorable outcome as compared to 4 $(20\%)$ patients of group B $(p=0.0015$ ). Adding DC with ED to evacuation of a large hypertensive hemispheric ICH might improve the outcome in selected group of patients.

# Introduction  

Strokes can be either ischemic or hemorrhagic [1–3]. Hypertensive intracranial hemorrhage (ICH) accounts for $10-$ $20\%$ of stroke etiology with a 30-day mortality rate of $30-$ $50\%$ [4–7]. The International Surgical Trial in Intracerebral Hemorrhage (STICH) study, a landmark trial of over $1000\mathrm{ICH}$ patients, showed that emergency surgical hematoma evacuation via craniotomy within $^{72\mathrm{~h~}}$ of onset failed to improve the outcome compared with a policy of initial medical management [8–14]. Patients presenting with large ICH almost always experience a dismal outcome [4, 15–21].  

The rationale for adding decompressive craniectomy (DC) in surgery for cases experiencing refractory increased intracranial pressure (ICP), which beneficial therapeutic outcome can be further enhanced through adding expansive duraplasty (ED) to the procedure, is to allow for the edematous brain tissue to herniate outwards, avoiding compression of vital brain structures thus acting as a possible life-saving intervention [22–29]. Several studies have documented significant reduction of ICP following DC and ED with secondary increased cerebral blood perfusion and cerebral oxygen supply resulting in decreasing morbidity and mortality [30–36].  

Although documenting success in improving the outcome in other etiologies presenting with increased ICP, DC is much less commonly applied in patients with hypertensive ICH despite the fact that several studies have shown overall improved outcome when this procedure was employed [7, 37–42]. That is why we conducted a prospective randomized controlled trial aiming at finding a validated therapeutic potential of adding both DC and ED to the conventional ICH evacuation as well as analyzing the factors governing the outcome.  

# Materials and methods  

This study was conducted in Alexandria main University Hospital during the period from February 2013 to April 2015.  

Study design A prospective open label randomized controlled clinical trial.  

# Inclusion criteria  

Patients aged from 18 to 80 years with confirmed large hemispheric hypertensive ICH that had a measured hematoma volume of at least $60~\mathrm{ml}$ on the computerized tomography (CT) brain done immediately before surgery. In the event of presenting with an initial Glasgow Coma Scale (GCS) of 8 or less by these patients, immediate surgical intervention was implemented and patients were included in the study (Fig 1). However, if these patients had an initial GCS of more than 8, inclusion in the study was allowed only if surgery was resorted to, which was in case of neurological deterioration or failure to demonstrate signs of neurological improvement despite maximal medical therapy including head elevation, dehydration, hyperventilation and sedation.

# Exclusion criteria  

Patients diagnosed with associated major comorbidity, e.g., cancer, long-term uncontrolled diabetes mellitus, severe dementia, renal, hepatic or respiratory failure.  

# Patients’ allocation  

One hundred twenty-one patients were diagnosed with hypertensive ICH during the study period, 63 of them satisfied the inclusion and exclusion criteria. Patients’ families were offered joining the study starting from the first patient, and it took 58 patients to have 40 eligible patients whose families agreed to participate in the study. Patients from 59 till 63 were admitted to the hospital during the study period and though eligible for inclusion, they were not offered joining the study because the required number for the study has been already obtained. Selected patients were randomly assigned to either group A (the treatment group) where patients were submitted to evacuation of ICH along with DC and ED or to group B (the control group), where patients were submitted only to hematoma evacuation. Simple randomization with blocks of two was done using computer-generated random numbers to provide two groups with equal number of participants.

# Preoperatively  

Complete history-taking as well as general and neurological examination was done. Plain CT scanning of the brain was obtained on admission and repeated immediately before surgery in case of a delay of surgical intervention. Hematoma volume was calculated from the CT brain done immediately before surgery using the ABC/2 formula where A and B are the perpendicular maximal diameters of the lesion and C is the total length in the vertical plane. Surgery was indicated when the patient satisfied both inclusion and exclusion criteria.  

# Intraoperatively  

Both groups were submitted to microscopic evacuation of ICH as complete as possible through a craniotomy flap. In group A, a large frontotemporoparietal craniotomy flap was made extending medially till $1{-}3\mathrm{cm}$ from the midline, laterally including subtemporal decompression and posteriorly coinciding with the posterior ear line. Pericranial graft was harvested from the wound site seizing at least one third of the exposed dura and was used to augment the dural opening that was done in a stellate fashion spanning the entire craniotomy flap and was ultimately closed water-tight. The free craniotomy flap was implanted subcutaneously in the anterior abdominal wall. Group B patients were submitted to a craniotomy flap that was large enough to access the hematoma and was positioned and fixed in place after hematoma evacuation and water-tight dural closure. Patients enrolled in group A who demonstrated satisfactory and progressive postoperative neurological improvement with GCS of more than 8 had their free bone flap replaced and fixed in place starting from postoperative day (POD) 10.  

# Postoperatively  

Assessment of GCS was carried out every $^{2\textrm{h}}$ . Immediate postoperative CT brain was done and was repeated on POD 3, 7, and 14; unless the patient showed signs of deterioration, in which case immediate CT brain was obtained. The size of bone flap was calculated from postoperative lateral scout films as an ellipse with the longest axis being 2a and the $90^{\circ}$ short axis being 2b using the formula: $\pi\times\mathbf{a}\times\mathbf{b}$ [43]. The patient’s outcome was assessed using the Glasgow Outcome Scale (GOS) and the modified Rankin scale (mRS) at discharge, at 3 and 6 months after discharge. GOS scores were stratified as favorable outcome showing good recovery, moderate disability outcome, poor outcome including patients with severe disability, vegetative state or death. The mRS was denoted a good outcome if it valued $<=3$ . Patients were also categorized as having a good or a poor prognosis using the median prognostic score computed by the following equation which has previously been used in the STICH trial as a prognostication tool following ICH [13]:  

Prognostic score

# Conclusions  

In large hypertensive hemispheric ICH, adding DC with ED to hematoma evacuation is safe and contributes strongly to a better outcome as compared to only ICH evacuation in selected group of patients. Generally speaking, patient’s age less than 50 years, GCS of more than 8 immediately before surgery, subcortical location of the hematoma, smaller hematoma volume, absent intraventricular extension of the hematoma, and absent or minimal preoperative as well as postoperative midline shift were favorable prognostic factors for postoperative patient outcome. A therapeutic advantage of adding DC and ED to ICH evacuation was specifically found in younger age groups as well as when the immediate preoperative GCS was in the range of 6–12. Further studies with larger patient’s cohort are needed to confirm or refute our findings. A prospective randomized controlled clinical trial comparing the results of conservative treatment, DC alone, and DC associated with ED with or without hematoma evacuation will give further insight for the justification of invading the brain tissue to evacuate the hematoma.
