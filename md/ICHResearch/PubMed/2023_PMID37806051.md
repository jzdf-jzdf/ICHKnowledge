Objective: Acute basilar artery occlusion (BAO) is a devastating neurologic event. As endovascular thrombectomy (EVT) became more prevalent for anterior circulation strokes, investigations were conducted in the posterior circulation. Its success in improving outcomes compared to standard medical therapy (SMT) after BAO has been debated.  

Methods: We conducted a systematic review and meta-analysis of all randomized controlled trials (RCTs) and observational cohort studies evaluating EVT compared to SMT in acute BAO. We queried PubMed, Embase, and Cochrane for studies. Primary outcome was good functional outcome at 90 days (modified Rankin scale $({\mathrm{mRS}})\leq$ 3). We analyzed studies for risk of bias (ROB) and calculated pooled risk ratios (RRs), odds ratios (ORs), and mean differences (MDs) with $95\%$ confidence intervals $(95\%\mathrm{CI})$ using the random effects model for our primary outcome and secondary positive outcomes and harms.  

Results: We identified four RCTs (991 patients randomized) and three cohort studies (1030 patients treated intrial) that fit inclusion criteria. Three RCTs had low ROB, one had serious ROB. One cohort study had high ROB, one had moderate ROB, and one had low ROB. EVT was statistically significantly more associated with good functional outcome than SMT in RCTs $\scriptstyle\mathrm{RR}=1.54$ , $95\%\mathrm{CI}{=}1.16{-}2.04$ , $\mathbf{p}=0.003\$ ) and trended towards significance in cohort studies $\mathrm{{RR}}{=}2.64\$ , $95\%\mathrm{CI}{=}0.87{-}8.00$ , $\mathbf{p}=0.09.$ . # 1. Introduction  

From 2014–2015, seven randomized controlled trials (RCTs) were published that established a new stroke treatment paradigm worldwide. Endovascular thrombectomy (EVT) rapidly became the standard of care for ischemic stroke due to anterior circulation large vessel occlusion [1–7]. Posterior circulation occlusions were, however, excluded from these trials. Though posterior circulation strokes have a variable pre­ sentation and neurologic compromise, basilar artery occlusion (BAO) is associated with the most severe presentation and a $45\%$ mortality rate [8].  

BAO has been studied in recent years. After the publication of various prospective and retrospective cohort studies, two randomized controlled trials (RCTs) were conducted and published in 2020 (Liu et al., “Basilar  

Artery Occlusion Endovascular Intervention Versus Standard Medical Treatment for Vertebrobasilar Artery Occlusion: an Open-label, Rand­ omised Controlled Trial”, the “BEST” Trial) and 2021 (Langezaal et al., “(Endovascular Therapy for Stroke Due to Basilar-Artery Occlusion”, the “BASICS” Trial) [9,10]. Neither of these two studies found that endo­ vascular thrombectomy improves rates of a functional outcome, defined as a modified Rankin score (mRS) of 0–3, nor mortality at three months. Further, there were identified flaws in the BEST and BASICS method­ ology including poor recruitment, sizeable amounts of patients treated outside study protocols, and high crossover rates [9,10]. The findings of BEST and BASICS also contrast several previously published cohort studies showing benefits of thrombectomy [11–13]. With these factors in mind, despite thenegative results in BEST and BASICS, researchers and clinicians believed EVT may still improve functional outcomes compared to standard medical treatment (SMT).  

In this landscape, two other RCTs, ATTENTION (Trial of Endovas­ cular Treatment of Acute Basilar-Artery Occlusion) and BAOCHE (Trial of Thrombectomy $6{-}24\mathrm{~h~}$ after Stroke Due to Basilar-Artery Occlusion), recently published their results that EVT improves functional benefit after BAO [14,15]. We seek to analyze all published cohort studies and RCTs to establish the genuine relationship between EVT and BAO involving benefits and harms.  



# 2. Methods  



# 2.1. Search strategy  

This meta-analysis and systematic review was conducted according to the required guidelines: Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses (PRISMA) [16]. PubMed, Embase, and the Cochrane Library were searched in October 2022 with no language re­ striction, using the following search terms: Thrombosis, thrombus, blood clot, occlusion, ischemia, ischemic, cerebrovascular accident, basilar, vertebrobasilar, thrombectomy, embolectomy, endovascular, percutaneous (see supplemental methods for complete search strategy). Each publication included for analysis had its reference list manually searched for further possible relevant sources to identify any additional publications. Two authors (HG and CW) independently evaluated the eligibility of the studies for analysis. Any discrepancies were addressed by the corresponding author (EP).  

Inclusion and exclusion criteria were based on our question formu­ lated using the PICO methodology:  

Population: Acute ischemic stroke secondary to basilar artery occlusion.  

Intervention: EVT with standard medical treatment (SMT).  

Comparison: SMT only.  

Primary Outcome: Good neurologic outcome at 90 days, defined as mRS) of 0–3.  

Secondary Outcomes: Mean mRS at 90 days; excellent neurologic outcome at 90 days, defined as mRS 0–2; mRS 0–4; mortality at 90 days; symptomatic intracranial hemorrhage (sICH); parenchymal hematoma type 1 (PH1); PH2; heart failure; pneumonia; gastrointestinal hemor­ rhage; acute kidney injury; subarachnoid hemorrhage.  

To be included, all studies had to have all aspects of PIC listed above, and at least the primary outcome defined above and be either an RCT or cohort study (prospective or retrospective). Exclusion criteria consisted of a) non-English language; b) non-full length manuscripts; c) nonhuman studies; d) overlapping datasets; e) the majority of the EVT group must have had at least an attempt made at thrombectomy (that is to say, the 2009 prospective BASICS study was not included as only $38\%$ of patients underwent attempted thrombectomy) [8].  



# 2.2. Data extraction and risk of bias assessment  

Two authors (HG and CW) independently extracted demographic information, cohort characteristics, and the outcome information listed above. Definitions of sICH varied mildly between trials; we extracted data according to each study’s definition in line with Malik et al [17].  

Two authors (HG and CW) independently assessed the risk of bias and quality of each RCT using the Cochrane “Risk-of-bias tool for ran­ domized trials” (RoB) and cohort studies using the “Risk of Bias in Nonrandomized studies – of interventions” (ROBINS-I) tool [18,19]. Any discrepancies were addressed by EP.  



# 2.3. Statistical analysis  

We pooled the categorical outcomes data from the RCTs using the Mantel Haenszel random effects model to report risk ratio (RR) and $95\%$ confidence intervals $95\%{\mathrm{CIs}})$ and odds ratio (OR) and $95\%\mathrm{CIs}$ for the cohort study data. We pooled the continuous outcomes data (mRS at 3 months) using the inverse variance random effects model to report mean difference (MD) and $95\%\mathrm{CIs}$ . We assessed the heterogeneity by the $\mathrm{I}^{2}$ statistic. We used Revman software by Cochrane for meta-analysis.  



# 3. Results  



# 3.1. Search results and included studies  

Our searches resulted in a library of 4074 potentially eligible articles. After removing duplicates, 3124 articles remained. These articles were screened for inclusion using the inclusion/exclusion criteria listed above. This yielded a group of four RCTs (BEST, BASICS, ATTENTION, BAOCHE) [9,10,14,15], and three cohort studies (Dias et al., Yoshimoto et al., and Zi et al.) [11,12,20]. A flow diagram of the study selection process is presented in Fig. 1. Of note, because Yoshimoto et al. did not publish their data in a way that they could be assimilated meaningfully for the outcomes listed above, its stratified arms (low national institute of health stroke scale (NIHSS) and high NIHSS) were maintained as separate studies for analysis).

It is worth describing some key differences between the RCTs (as outlined in Table 1). BASICS enrolled patients over the longest time (eight years) and started before the landmark stent retriever trials. BEST enrolled patients over three years, ATTENTION over two years, and BAOCHE over six years. Symptom onset to inclusion criteria were expanded in more recent studies. In the initial BASICS and BEST trials, criteria were $_{0-6\textrm{h}}$ and $_{0-8\mathrm{~h~}}$ , respectively, compared to $_{0-12\mathrm{~h~}}$ for ATTENTION and $6{-}24\mathrm{~h~}$ for BOACHE. Crossover in each group was relatively low in all studies (less than $5\%$ in each study arm) except BEST, where $21.5\%$ of SMT patients crossed to EVT. Median NIHSS at presentation and IV thrombolysis administered in each arm for each study were relatively consistent. Another important difference between the trials is their success in obtaining “successful” reperfusion, typically defined as modified Thrombolysis in Cerebral Infarction (mTICI) of 2b3. BEST had the lowest rate of successful reperfusion at $41\%$ , then BA­ SICS at $68\%$ , followed by BAOCHE at $81\%$ , and ATTENTION at $91\%$ [9, 10,14,15]. Definition of sICH was using the SITS-MOST criteria in the BEST, ATTENTION, and BAOCHE trials; BASICS used the Heidelberg Bleeding Classification instead [9,10,14,15].  

We studied three observational cohort analyses as outlined in Table 2. The biggest of which was from the BASILAR group [12]. This study was from January 2014-May 2019 and analyzed patients with acute BAO within $24\mathrm{~h~}$ of estimated occlusion time. Eight hundred twenty-nine patients were included; 647 underwent EVT and SMT, and 182 received only SMT [12]. Yoshimoto et al. presented the next largest cohort study from the RESCUE-2 registry between October 2014 and September 2016 [11]. This study found 182 BAOs, ultimately 177 were fit for inclusion. These 177 were further stratified into mild strokes (baseline NIHSS 0–9; $\mathtt{n}=40$ ) and severe (NIHSS 10–40; $\mathbf{n}=137.$ , with 18 mild stroke patients undergoing EVT (versus 21 SMT) and 60 severe stroke patients undergoing EVT (versus 17 SMT) [11]. Lastly, Dias et al. studied 34 patients between August 2004 and December 2015 who underwent EVT or SMT for BAO [20]. In this study, 19 patients under­ went EVT, and 15 underwent SMT. Of note, of the EVT group, three patients $(16\%)$ did not have thrombectomy as part of their EVT (intra-arterial thrombolysis or angioplasty) [20].

# 3.4. Risk of bias assessment  

Our meta-analysis included seven studies (four RCTs and three observational studies). A risk of bias assessment per the criteria outlined above in the Methods section was performed. A risk of bias assessment for the RCTs is presented in Fig. 4AC. Tao et al. did not report the details of randomization sequence generation methods [15]. Jovin et al. re­ ported that some patients underwent thrombectomy outside the trial, and consecutive enrollment did not occur [14]. Liu et al. concealed the allocation using sealed envelopes, and a third of the qualifying patients declined trial participation, potentially leading to selection bias in this RCT [10]. The risk of performance, detection, attrition, and reporting bias was low among all included RCTs (Fig. 4AC).  

As for other biases, the RCT by Liu et al. also was found to have deviations from the intended intervention because of the trial context, and these deviations were likely to have affected the trial outcome and were not balanced between groups.10 Similarly, the same study featured failures in implementing the assigned intervention that could have affected the outcome. While, an appropriate analysis was used to esti­ mate this effect, it still resulted in a serious bias in the deviations from the intended interventions.10 Also, in this RCT, the small sample size resulting from the early termination of the trial and the high rate of crossovers reduced the power of intended subgroup analyses [10]. Jovin et al. reported that a limited proportion of patients received intravenous thrombolysis. Less than two-thirds of the patients who arrived at the referring hospital or endovascular center within $4.5\mathrm{h}$ after onset received intravenous thrombolysis. It is also possible that the low pro­ portion of patients with thrombolytic administration might have been related to the requirement that patients initially pay for the thrombo­ lytic drug [14]. Langezaal et al. reported that $29.2\%$ of eligible patients were treated outside of the trial and that $79.0\%$ of these received endovascular therapy. This factor may have introduced bias in the enrolled population. In addition, $5\%$ of the patients in the medical treatment group crossed over to the endovascular group. Also, because recruitment was lower than anticipated, this trial was underpowered for some analyses, including the planned subgroup analyses [9]. Tao et al. also reported that most patients in China are required to pay in advance for intravenous thrombolysis. Although similarly present in the two trial groups, this disincentive may have led to the relatively low use of intravenous thrombolysis and contributed to a poorer outcome in the control group [15]. We concluded that overall the methodological quality of these studies was moderate to high, leading to a cumulative low risk of bias. However, we noted that the BEST trial had a serious overall risk of bias, generally favoring the SMT arm. .  

Of the three observational studies included, a similar risk of bias assessment was performed and is presented in Fig. 4BD. In the domain of confounding, one study (Yoshimoto et al.) had the potential for con­ founding and did not appear to use an appropriate analysis method to control for all of the domains and thus featured a serious risk of bias, the other two studies $(66\%)$ featured moderate risk of confounding [11]. In the domain of bias due to missing data, one study $(33\%)$ featured a moderate risk of bias as there were patients excluded due to missing data, and that information was not published in the article (Dias et al.), the remaining two articles $(66\%)$ featured low bias in this domain [20]. # 4. Discussion  

Our study synthesizes the significant prior studies analyzing the benefits and risks associated with modern endovascular thrombectomy for basilar artery occlusion. It supports the benefit of EVT. Our results show that EVT in BAO increases rates of good functional outcomes, re­ duces disability, and improves mortality despite having an increased risk in periprocedural symptomatic ICH.  

These results align with the two newly published RCTs (ATTENTION and BAOCHE). We believe that the BASICS and BEST trials results differed for fundamental reasons. First, half of BASICS took place before stent retrievers had become the standard of care for EVT. Further, pro­ viders did not have consistently high rates of mTICI 2b-3 reperfusion [9]. Their intention-to-treat analysis of mTICI 2b-3 reperfusion was $41\%$ $(>25\%$ lower than the next lowest RCT). This would naturally lower the success rates of reperfusion with EVT and thus underestimate any effect size. This is evidenced by a recent trial by Feil et al. that showed that a successful reperfusion was associated with an OR of 4.92 for good functional outcomes in EVT for BAO [21]. Second, BEST had a significant crossover rate from SMT to EVT $(>20\%$ ) [10]. This would work to overestimate the effect size of SMT. While the cohort studies also had no significantly associated results, the heterogeneity in this analysis was quite high $(69\%)$ , likely due to the Yoshimoto et al. study. Their study may suggest that if NIHSS is mild, there is little-to-no improvement in good functional outcomes portended by EVT [11].

Further, two recent large registry-related trials were recently pub­ lished that further provide evidence for the benefit of EVT in patients with BAO. Feil et al. recently published a 3.5-year cohort from Germany who all received EVT for BAO. Of the 640 patients included, $34.8\%$ had a good functional outcome $(\mathrm{mRS}0-3)$ [21]. This is in comparison to our study, wherein in the RCT studies, EVT was associated with $45\%$ of participants achieving $\mathrm{mRS}0{-}3$ and SMT with $30\%$ . Dicpinigaitis et al. recently published an account of the National Inpatient Sample from 2015 to 2019, analyzing 3950 patients (1425 of which had EVT) and using discharge disposition to home without services surrogate for favorable functional outcome. They found that before and after pro­ pensity score adjustment, EVT was independently associated with favorable functional outcomes (adjusted $\mathrm{OR}=1.25\$ , 95CI 1.07–1.46, $\mathbf{p}=0.004)$ ), though not in-hospital mortality nor sICH [22]. Interest­ ingly, in agreement with the Yoshimoto et al. study, Dicpinigaitis et al. also found the effect size to be higher with NIHSS scores $>20$ [11,22].  

It appears to be a common finding between studies that while sICH may be associated with EVT, mortality is more likely with SMT. We believe this is because mortality is measured at 90 days. Though the deficits of sICH are more severe in the immediate period, subacute to chronic deficits due to BAOs have a greater impact on long-term mortality.  

This is not the first systematic review and meta-analysis to address this question. However, it is the most comprehensive, including all the RCTs and cohort studies to date, while also benefitting from publication in full of all studies included [17,23–26]. Because we were able to analyze full manuscripts, we could better provide complete accounts of the bias and results of these studies. Further, by including RCTs and cohort studies, we compiled a complete account of the studies on the subject in question.  

Despite these published studies, some questions remain. There was no consistency in similarity nor in differences for stroke onset to inclu­ sion or intervention. The RCTs varied from 0 to 6 h (BASICS), $_{0-8\mathrm{~h~}}$ (BEST), $_{0-12\mathrm{h}}$ (ATTENTION, and $6{-}24\mathrm{~h~}$ (BAOCHE).9,10,14,15 Though BEST and BASICS did not find a significant improvement in functional outcome at three months, we do not think it would be correct to say that EVT for BAO is only helpful if $^{8-24\mathrm{h}}$ after symptom onset. Meanwhile, is $24\mathrm{~h~}$ the maximum time within which EVT is warranted? Two recent RCTs showed that patients with large established “core” cerebral in­ farctions had better improvement with EVT than SMT. Perhaps future studies will also show benefits for EVT after the 24-hour mark for supratentorial or infratentorial strokes [27,28]. Further, though some studies show that $\mathrm{NIHSS}>20$ is associated with the greater benefit of EVT, is there a cut-off of NIHSS that should be used? [11,22] Another question these trials did not completely answer was if bridging throm­ bolytics improved neurologic outcome. A systematic review in 2022 attempted to address this. It showed that bridging thrombolytics in BAO improved mortality rates and in patients with large artery atheroscle­ rosis and also improved neurologic outcome at 90 days [29]. This sys­ tematic review however was comprised of only three multicenter retrospective studies though per the Joanna Briggs Institute Checklist, each were classified as “good” studies. As our systematic review was not designed to address this question, it remains as an important question requiring further research.  



# 4.1. Limitations  

Our study had several limitations. First, in several analyses, the level of heterogeneity was high. Specifically, our primary outcome in the RCTs $(59\%)$ and the cohort studies $(69\%)$ was high. For the cohort studies it is apparent why that is the case. However in the RCTs, it ap­ pears that the results of each study were more homogeneous than the $\mathrm{I}^{2}$ value would suggest, likely due to the overall low number of studies. Another limitation is the difference in definitions between studies. For example, the definition of hemorrhage was not uniform between studies, nor was the length of time permitted between symptom-onset and in­ clusion. Further, some studies had a high crossover rate, limiting their generalizability and statistical application. Lastly, it is worth mentioning the inherent publication bias in this and other systematic reviews pub­ lished on the subject. This may have caused us to develop incorrect false conclusions or may have otherwise explained certain results.  



# 5. Conclusion  

Our research synthesis provides strong evidence that modern endo­ vascular thrombectomy for basilar artery occlusion is associated with greater rates of good functional outcomes, lower rates of disability, and lower rates of death in the long term despite higher rates of periproce­ dural symptomatic intracranial hemorrhage. Moreover, recent large registry-related trials further support this benefit. It remains to be seen at what point after symptom onset and at what extent of patient deficits endovascular thrombectomy loses efficacy.
