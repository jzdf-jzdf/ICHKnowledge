# ABSTR ACT  

# BACKGROUND  

The effect of embolization of the middle meningeal artery in patients with subacute or chronic subdural hematoma is uncertain.  

# METHODS  

We performed a multicenter, open-label, randomized trial in China, involving patients with symptomatic nonacute subdural hematoma with mass effect. Patients were assigned to undergo burr-hole drainage or receive nonsurgical treatment at the surgeon’s discretion, and patients in each group were then randomly assigned, in a 1:1 ratio, to undergo middle meningeal artery embolization with liquid embolic material or to receive usual care. Patients whose condition warranted craniotomy were excluded. The primary outcome was symptomatic recurrence or progression of subdural hematoma within 90 days after randomization. Secondary outcomes included clinical and imaging outcomes. The main safety outcome was any serious adverse event (including death).  

# RESULTS  

The analysis included 722 patients, of whom 360 were assigned to the embolization group and 362 to the usual-care group. Burr-hole drainage was performed in $78.3\%$ of the enrolled patients; among the patients who underwent burr-hole drainage, the procedure occurred after embolization in $99.6\%$ . Symptomatic recurrence or progression of subdural hematoma within 90 days occurred in 24 patients $(6.7\%)$ in the embolization group and in 36 $(9.9\%)$ in the usual-care group (between-group difference, $-3.3$ percentage points; $95\%$ confidence interval, $-7.4$ to 0.8; $\mathrm{P}{=}0.10,$ ). The incidence of serious adverse events was lower in the embolization group than in the usual-care group $\left(6.7\%\right)$ vs. $11.6\%$ , $\mathrm{P}{=}0.02,$ ).  

# CONCLUSIONS  

Among patients with symptomatic nonacute subdural hematoma (of whom $78\%$ underwent burr-hole drainage), middle meningeal artery embolization resulted in a 90-day incidence of symptomatic recurrence or progression similar to that with usual care but was associated with a lower incidence of serious adverse events. hronic subdural hematoma is a common neurologic disorder that predominantly occurs in older persons. Its incidence is predicted to increase owing to an aging population and increasing use of antithrombotic agents.1 Surgical evacuation, through burr-hole drainage or open craniotomy, and supportive medical care are the standard treatments for symptomatic chronic subdural hematoma. However, the incidence of hematoma recurrence or progression remains high.2-5 One explanation is fragility of the neovasculature underlying the subdural membrane encapsulating the hematoma, which is supplied by vessels from the middle meningeal artery.6-9  

Endovascular embolization of the middle meningeal artery has emerged as a promising treatment method to reduce hematoma growth and recurrence of chronic subdural hematoma.10 A meta-analysis indicates a large potential effect of middle meningeal artery embolization,11 but there is limited evidence from multicenter, randomized, controlled trials. We conducted the Managing Non-acute Subdural Hematoma Using Liquid Materials: a Chinese Randomized Trial of Middle Meningeal Artery Treatment (MAGIC-MT) to determine whether the addition of middle meningeal artery embolization with the use of liquid materials to best-practice usual care would lower the incidence of hematoma progression or recurrence among patients with nonacute (chronic or subacute) subdural hematoma in China.  

# Methods  

# Trial Design  

MAGIC-MT was an investigator-initiated, multicenter, randomized, controlled trial with a PROBE (prospective, randomized, open-label, blinded endpoint) design.12 Written informed consent was obtained from all the patients or their legal representatives. The trial was designed by an international steering committee and monitored by an independent data and safety monitoring board. An independent contract research organization (Clinical Consultancy Research Center) and site-management organizations (Shanghai Luoke Medical Information Consulting and Beijing Shiyan Medical Technology) were involved in monitoring to ensure adherence to high standards of quality. Details are outlined in the Supplementary Appendix, which is available with the full text of this article at NEJM.org.  

The protocol (available at NEJM.org) was approved by all relevant local ethics committees and research boards.13 The final protocol and statistical analysis plan were completed on September 21, 2022, and December 2, 2023, respectively. The embolic material of the Onyx liquid embolic system (Medtronic) was provided free to patients by the investigators. All the investigators vouch for the completeness and accuracy of the data, adherence of the trial to the protocol, and the reporting of adverse events. The first draft of the manuscript was jointly written by the first three authors. The trial sponsors (Shanghai Shenkang Hospital Development Center, Changhai Hospital, Shanghai Municipal Health Commission, and Covidien/Medtronic) had no involvement in the trial design or conduct, protocol review, or manuscript preparation. Our trial was conducted in accordance with International Council for Harmonisation guidelines.  

# Patients and Centers  

The trial was conducted at 31 academic tertiary care centers in 12 provinces in China (Fig. S1 in the Supplementary Appendix). Personnel at all the sites were experienced in the conduct of research involving patients with neurotrauma or cerebrovascular disease and had undertaken both burrhole drainage and middle meningeal artery embolization in 50 or more cases of nonacute subdural hematoma in the previous year. On-site and online training were provided before and during the conduct of the trial to ensure adherence to the protocol, assessments, and standards of care.  

Patients were eligible for inclusion in the trial if they were 18 years of age or older with a symptomatic nonacute subdural hematoma producing mass effect and were previously independent in their everyday activities as defined by a score of 0, 1, or 2 on the modified Rankin scale (range, 0 to 6, with higher scores indicating greater disability and a score of 6 indicating death), according to the treating physician. Patients were excluded if they had bilateral subdural hematoma with unknown source of symptoms (hematoma on the left side, right side, or both sides) or if their condition warranted a craniotomy or emergency hematoma evacuation. # Randomization and Procedures  

Patients were first divided into two groups: those whose condition warranted burr-hole drainage of the subdural collection and those whose condition did not warrant drainage. The patients in each group were then randomly assigned, in a 1:1 ratio, to undergo middle meningeal artery embolization or to receive usual care. The decision for a patient to undergo burr-hole drainage was at the discretion of the treating physician according to clinical and imaging findings, but there were nonbinding guidelines in the protocol to assist with this decision. The trial-group assignments were known to the treating physician and the patient. The members of the outcomes assessment committee who adjudicated the clinical outcomes and the members of the clinical events committee who adjudicated the safety outcomes were unaware of the trial-group assignments. However, owing to the visibility of the embolization material on imaging, the staff of the core laboratory were aware of the trial-group assignments.  

Patients who were assigned to the embolization group underwent cerebral angiography and ipsilateral middle meningeal artery embolization of the subdural hematoma under general anesthesia or monitored local anesthesia. Briefly, access to the femoral or radial artery was obtained, and digital subtraction angiography was performed with the use of a standard 5-French diagnostic catheter. A guiding catheter was placed in the proximal external carotid artery. A microcatheter was advanced under roadmap guidance into the main trunk of the middle meningeal artery, and superselective angiography was performed to identify any potentially unsafe collateral vessels, such as the ophthalmic and petrous branches, before embolization. The middle meningeal artery was embolized with Onyx. Successful embolization was defined as selective embolization of both the frontal and parietal branches or embolization of the main middle meningeal artery trunk. According to the protocol, middle meningeal artery embolization was to be performed before burr-hole drainage.  

Patients who were assigned to the usual-care group were treated according to the current standard of care — that is, burr-hole drainage as needed, best medical management, or both. Standard burr-hole drainage was performed while the patient was under general anesthesia or monitored local anesthesia, and use of either a single bur hole or multiple burr holes was acceptable. After the hematoma was evacuated with ample irrigation, a subdural drain was placed into the subdural space and subsequently removed within several days on the basis of imaging and clinical indications. Usual care involved the use of atorvastatin, dexamethasone, and other drugs as needed in patients without a contraindication.  

# Trial Outcomes  

The primary outcome was symptomatic recurrence or progression of subdural hematoma within 90 days after randomization (with a window of $\pm14$ days). Symptomatic was defined as involving one or more of the following neurologic symptoms and signs: headache, cognitive impairment, dysarthria or dysphasia, ataxia or unsteadiness, paresis, sensory loss, and seizures. Recurrence of subdural hematoma was defined as a maximum hematoma thickness exceeding $10~\mathrm{mm}$ or reoperation during followup after previous burr-hole drainage. Progression of subdural hematoma was defined as an increase in the maximum hematoma thickness by more than $3\mathrm{mm}$ from baseline or surgical rescue in patients who were receiving usual care. Secondary outcomes included late symptomatic recurrence or progression of the subdural hematoma at 1 year; successful embolization of the target vessels on the basis of digital subtraction angiography; change in hematoma thickness from baseline to the repeat scan at 90 days; change in hematoma volume from baseline to 90 days; change in midline shift from baseline to 90 days; change in the modified Rankin scale score between 90 days and 1 year; a modified Rankin scale score of 0, 1, or 2 at 90 days and 1 year; a modified Rankin scale score of 0 to 3 at 90 days and 1 year; health-related quality of life on the EuroQol Group 5-Dimension 5-Level SelfReport Questionnaire (EQ-5D-5L) at 90 days and 1 year; and hospital length of stay, rehospitalization, discharge destination, and hospital expenses. # Statistical Analysis  

The reported incidence of hematoma recurrence or progression with usual care ranges from 9.3 to $27.5\%$ .3,4,14-20 Thus, we assumed an incidence of $12\%$ in the usual-care group. The reported incidence of hematoma recurrence or progression with middle meningeal artery embolization ranges from 1.4 to $8.9\%$ .12-16,21,22 Thus, we assumed an incidence of $5\%$ in the embolization group. The trial was powered to assess superiority, with a power of $90\%$ and two-sided alpha of 0.05 and allowance for $8\%$ loss to follow-up. The estimated sample size was 722 patients. Five additional patients were enrolled to compensate for those who withdrew consent.  

Two formal interim analyses were conducted: after the first 240 patients and after 480 patients had completed primary-outcome assessments. These analyses were conducted by an independent statistician. Given the conservative Haybittle–Peto stopping rule used by the data and safety monitoring board and the negligible amount of type I error (0.002) expended in two interim analyses of the emerging data, a P value of less than $5\%$ remained to indicate statistical significance. The data and safety monitoring board reviewed the unblinded data for efficacy, safety, and quality of trial conduct. The investigators were unaware of the trial-group assignments with respect to the results of the interim analyses. Baseline data are presented according to trial-group assignment, with the use of descriptive statistics as appropriate. Missing baseline data were not imputed. Analyses for primary, secondary, and safety outcomes were based on the intention-to-treat population. Analyses in the per-protocol population and safety population were also provided.  

The Cochran–Mantel–Haenszel chi-square test was used for formal hypothesis testing of the primary outcome between trial groups, with stratification for burr-hole drainage surgery. The betweengroup difference and $95\%$ confidence interval were calculated with the use of the Miettinen– Nurminen method. A generalized linear mixedeffects model with a log-link function was used to estimate the relative risk and $95\%$ confidence interval for the trial groups, with sex, age, burrhole drainage surgery, any use of antithrombotic therapy, and baseline hematoma volume as covariates and with trial site as a random effect. Prespecified subgroup analyses of the primary outcome were performed with the use of the same methods. Subgroups were defined according to age, sex, burr-hole drainage stratum, smoking status, known head trauma, use of antithrombotic medication, statin use, midline shift, baseline hematoma thickness, hematoma volume, and bilateral subdural hematoma.  

A Markov chain Monte Carlo method would be used for the multiple-imputation procedure under the missing-at-random assumption if the proportion of patients with missing primaryoutcome data was greater than or equal to $5\%$ . Otherwise, the missing-completely-at-random assumption would be adopted and patients with missing primary-outcome data would be regarded as not having had an event in the primary analysis. The missing-completely-at-random assumption was checked by sensitivity analyses in patients who underwent computed tomography after the predefined time window. The regression model included trial group, sex, age, body-mass index, baseline modified Rankin scale score, and baseline maximum hematoma thickness.  

Binary secondary efficacy outcomes were analyzed with the use of the same methods. Ordinal logistic regression was used for the shift in the modified Rankin scale score, and linear mixed-effects models were used for the change in maximum hematoma thickness and hematoma volume from baseline, with covariates and random effects identical to those used in the primary-outcome analysis. Secondary-outcome analyses are exploratory and based on a completecase analysis method with the assumption of the missing-completely-at-random mechanism in the main analysis. A chained equation method was used for the imputation of missing secondaryoutcome data. No multiplicity adjustment was planned for secondary-outcome analysis and subgroup analysis. Therefore, no formal statistical conclusions could be drawn from these results. All the analyses were performed with the use of SAS software, version 9.4 (SAS Institute).  

# Results  

# Patients  

From March 2021 through May 2023, a total of 1343 patients were assessed for eligibility at 31 sites. A total of 727 patients underwent randomization: 365 patients were assigned to undergo middle meningeal artery embolization (embolization group), and 362 were assigned to receive usual care. After 5 patients in the embolization group withdrew consent, 722 trial patients were included in the intention-to-treat population (Fig.  1, Fig. S2, and Table S1). Of the patients who were excluded before randomization, 154 declined to participate, 100 had bilateral subdural hematoma with unknown source of symptoms, 60 had recurrent subdural hematoma, 59 had no neurologic symptoms, 58 had a modified Rankin scale score of more than 2 before symptom onset, 37 had a serious or fatal coexisting disease or a life expectancy of less than 1 year, 29 were unable to receive embolization before burrhole drainage, 25 had an indication for craniotomy or emergency subdural hematoma evacuation, 11 met laboratory exclusion criteria, and 83 had other reasons (Fig. 1).  

The characteristics of the patients at baseline were similar in the embolization and usual-care groups (Table 1 and Table S2). The median age of the patients was 69 years (interquartile range, 61 to 74), and 596 patients $(82.5\%)$ were men. Head trauma was confirmed in 379 patients $(52.5\%)$ , and 53 patients $(7.3\%)$ were taking antithrombotic medication at the time of diagnosis. Hematoma imaging characteristics, including midline shift, maximum hematoma thickness, and hematoma volume, were similar in the two groups. Two patients in the embolization group and 15 patients in the usual-care group had missing data on the mean change in subdural hematoma thickness and the mean change in midline shift, and 4 patients in the embolization group and 16 patients in the usual-care group had missing data on the mean change in subdural hematoma volume (Table 2).  

# Interventions  

A total of 353 patients in the embolization group and 3 patients in the usual-care group received endovascular procedures, and 347 patients in the embolization group and 3 in the usual-care group successfully underwent middle meningeal artery embolization (Fig. 1). Successful middle meningeal artery embolization was achieved in $98.3\%$ of the patients in the embolization group. A total of 31 patients with major protocol violations $(4.3\%)$ were excluded from per-protocol analyses: 6 did not undergo embolization, 10 crossed over to the alternative treatment, and 15 declined to undergo follow-up computed tomography. A total of 565 patients $(78.3\%)$ underwent burrhole drainage, and the use of burr-hole drainage was well balanced between the two groups, with the procedure performed in 281 patients in the embolization group and 284 in the usual-care group (Table 1). A total of 273 of 274 patients who underwent both procedures $(99.6\%)$ underwent drainage after embolization. A total of 707 patients $(97.9\%)$ received statins or glucocorticoids.  

# Primary Outcome  

There were no missing data for the primary outcome. For the primary-outcome visit at 90 days after randomization (with a window of $\pm14$ days), the median follow-up time was 91 days (interquartile range, 86 to 96) in the embolization group and 91 days (interquartile range, 85 to 97) in the usual-care group. In the intention-to-treat analysis, symptomatic recurrence or progression of subdural hematoma was reported in 24 of 360 patients $(6.7\%)$ in the embolization group and in 36 of 362 patients $(9.9\%)$ in the usual-care group; the between-group difference was $-3.3$ percentage points $(95\%$ confidence interval [CI], $-7.4$ to 0.8; $\mathrm{P}{=}0.10,$ ) (Table 2). In the embolization group, 17 patients $(4.7\%)$ had symptomatic hematoma recurrence, and 7 $(1.9\%)$ had symptomatic hematoma progression. In the usual-care group, 19 patients $(5.2\%)$ had symptomatic hematoma recurrence, and 17 $(4.7\%)$ had symptomatic hematoma progression. # Secondary Outcomes  

The adjusted common odds ratio for a shift in the modified Rankin scale score at 90 days comparing the embolization group with the usual-care group was 1.10 $95\%$ CI, 0.82 to 1.46) (Fig. S4). Other secondary outcomes are shown in Table 2, including the EQ-5D-5L score at 90 days, mean length of hospital stay, rehospitalization, destination after discharge, and changes in the hematoma on imaging.  

# Safety  

Serious adverse events during the 90-day followup period occurred less often in the embolization group than in the usual-care group $(6.7\%$ vs. $11.6\%$ ; relative risk, 0.58; $95\%$ CI, 0.34 to 0.98) (Table  3). Mortality within 90 days was $0.6\%$ in the embolization group and $2.2\%$ in the usual-care group (relative risk, 0.27; $95\%$ CI, 0.06 to 1.25). Details of serious adverse events are shown in Table S7. Embolization-related complications occurred in 3 patients $(0.8\%)$ in the embolization group (1 patient had facial-nerve paralysis, and 2 patients had an allergic reaction to the contrast agent). The percentage of patients with burr-hole drainage–related complications was $2.5\%$ in the embolization group and $1.1\%$ in the usual-care group (relative risk, 2.26; $95\%$ CI, 0.69 to 7.40). The incidence of adverse events was similar in the two groups, and details of adverse events are shown in Table S8.

# Discussion  

In our trial involving patients with symptomatic nonacute subdural hematoma in China, most of whom underwent burr-hole drainage, the incidence of hematoma recurrence or progression within 90 days was similar among those who underwent middle meningeal artery embolization and those who received usual care. Previous nonrandomized studies suggested that middle meningeal artery embolization may reduce the risk of hematoma growth and recurrence by devascularizing the hematoma membranes. The reported incidence of recurrence or progression with middle meningeal artery embolization ranges from 1.4 to $8.9\%$ , as compared with 9.3 to $27.5\%$ with usual care.3,4,14-22 The results of our trial did not show a significantly lower incidence of hematoma growth or recurrence with embolization than with usual care but did show a significantly lower incidence of serious adverse events, for reasons that are unclear. Several additional multicenter, randomized, controlled trials are currently under way that may provide additional data on the effects of middle meningeal artery embolization.23  

Among patients undergoing burr-hole drainage, several studies have shown a lower incidence of recurrence or repeat surgery with middle meningeal artery embolization than with surgery alone.17,24 Further trials are needed to assess the effect of middle meningeal artery embolization in patients with and those without burr-hole drainage.  

Our trial has limitations. First, the decision to perform repeat or rescue burr-hole drainage was left to the discretion of the physicians at each participating site, and treating physicians and core laboratory members were aware of the trial-group assignments, which introduces the

Second, the majority of patients with symptomatic subdural hematoma in this trial were treated surgically, which may not be representative of conservatively treated patients. Third, only Onyx liquid embolic agent was used in this trial. Although liquid embolic agents show superior occlusion efficacy to solid embolic materials and precise embolization because of deeper penetration and permanence, solid embolic materials may be safer because they are less likely to enter dangerous anastomoses.25-28 The efficacy and safety profile of different embolic agents for middle meningeal artery embolization requires further investigation. Fourth, variation in different factors such as the imaging classification of subdural hematoma, drain position, number of burr holes, and timing of embolization, which were not standardized or analyzed, could have influenced the recurrence of subdural hematoma.19,29-31  

In this trial involving patients with symptomatic subacute or chronic subdural hematoma (most of whom underwent burr-hole drainage), the addition of middle meningeal artery embolization to usual care resulted in an incidence of hematoma recurrence or progression similar to that with usual care alone. However, middle meningeal artery embolization resulted in a lower incidence of serious adverse events, including death.
