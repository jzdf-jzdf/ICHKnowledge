# Efficacy and safety of neuroendoscopy surgery versus craniotomy for supratentorial intracerebral hemorrhage: an updated meta-analysis of randomized controlled trials

# Abstract  

Neuroendoscopy (NE) surgery emerged as a promising technique for the treatment of spontaneous intracerebral hemorrhage (ICH). A previous meta-analysis of randomized controlled trials (RCTs) analyzed the efficacy and safety of NE compared to craniotomy, but NE did not present a significant improvement in functional outcomes. However, a new study provided an opportunity to update the current knowledge. We searched PubMed, Embase, and Cochrane Central Register of Controlled Trials for RCTs reporting NE evacuation of spontaneous supratentorial ICH compared to craniotomy. The efficacy outcomes of interest were favorable functional outcome, functional disability, hematoma evacuation rate, and residual hematoma volume. The safety outcomes of interest were rebleeding, infection, and mortality. Seven RCTs were included containing 879 patients. The NE approach presented a significantly higher rate of favorable functional outcome compared with craniotomy (RR: 1.42; $95\%$ CI 1.17, 1.73; $p<0.001)$ . The evacuation rate was higher in patients who underwent the NE approach (MD: -8.36; $95\%$ CI -12.66, -4.07; $p<0.001$ ). NE did not show a benefit in improving the mortality rate (RR: 0.81, $95\%$ CI 0.54, 1.22; $p{=}0.32,$ . NE was associated with more favorable functional outcomes and lower rates of functional disabilities compared to craniotomy. Also, NE was superior regarding evacuation rate, while presenting a reduction in residual hematoma volume. NE might be associated with lower infection rates. Mortality was not improved by NE surgery. Larger, higher-quality randomized studies are needed to adequately evaluate the efficacy and safety of NE compared to craniotomy.

# Introduction  

Hypertensive intracerebral hemorrhage (ICH) is a type of spontaneous ICH, that accounts for approximately 10 to $15\%$ of all strokes [7, 19]. The strongest risk factor associated with ICH in most populations that have been studied was hypertension, while there are other risk factors, such as cerebral amyloid angiopathy, and anticoagulation [15, 16]. Transtentorial herniation stands as the most serious consequence arising from the combined impact of an intracerebral clot and surrounding edema [16].  

Numerous randomized controlled trials (RCTs) and meta-analyses have investigated the efficacy of surgery in treating supratentorial ICH [10, 11, 20, 22].  Although surgery is the common treatment of hypertensive cerebral hemorrhage, the prognosis is not the same in the variety of surgical procedures [8, 26]. The advance of minimal techniques provided other alternatives to craniotomy, which is known as the traditional surgical option. Neuroendoscopy (NE) surgery is one of these techniques that objectives to achieve rapid clot removal with better access and less surrounding brain injury [13].  

A previous meta-analysis of RCTs analyzed the efficacy and safety of NE compared to craniotomy, while did not show a benefit regarding the functional outcomes [5]. Furthermore, new studies have provided an opportunity to update the data on the topic [9, 14]. To our knowledge, the present systematic review and meta-analysis arises with the largest randomized sample about the use of NE compared to craniotomy for ICH.  

# Materials and methods  

# Eligibility criteria  

We restricted inclusion in this meta-analysis to RCTs reporting NE evacuation of spontaneous supratentorial ICH compared to craniotomy published in the English language. The selected patients were 18 years old or older, presenting with lobar, basal ganglia, internal capsule, or thalamus hemorrhage, accompanied or not by intraventricular hemorrhage. The included studies should have reported at least one of the following outcomes of interest: (1) functional outcomes; (2) complications; (3) mortality; (4) hematoma evacuation rate; and (5) residual hematoma volume. We excluded nonrandomized studies, studies missing data on surgical intervention, and studies reporting ICH secondary to traumatic lesions, brain tumors, and vascular malformations.  

# Search strategy and data extraction  

We systematically searched PubMed, Embase, and Cochrane Central Register of Controlled Trials from inception to April 2024 with the following search strategy: (hematoma OR haematoma OR haemorrhage OR hemorrhage) AND (intracerebral OR intracranial OR “basal ganglia” OR hypertensive) AND (endoscopic OR endoscopy OR neuroendoscopy OR neuroendoscopic) AND (randomized OR randomised OR randomly OR random). Two authors (G.A.M. and G.M.) independently extracted the data from individual studies following predefined search criteria. Disagreements were resolved by consensus between the authors.  

# Endpoints  

The efficacy outcomes of interest were favorable functional outcome, functional disability, hematoma evacuation rate, and residual hematoma volume. Favorable functional outcome definition varies among studies. Thus, we defined favorable functional outcomes as a modified Rankin Scale (mRS) of 0–3 points, Activity of Daily Living (ADL) of 1–3 points, Glasgow Outcome Scale (GOS) of 4–5 Points, and a Glasgow Prognosis Scale GRADE IV and V. Only the RCT by Gui et al. considered ADL of 4–5 points as “favorable prognosis” [4]. Functional disability was defined as mRS of 4–5 points, Activity of Daily Living (ADL) of 4–5 points, Glasgow Outcome Scale (GOS) of 2–3 Points, and a Glasgow Prognosis Scale GRADE II and III. The safety outcomes of interest were rebleeding, infection, and mortality. We considered infection as neurological infection, pneumonia, wound infection, and urinary tract infection.  

# Quality assessment  

The risk of bias and quality assessment in randomized studies were evaluated with version 2 of the Cochrane Risk of Bias assessment tool (RoB 2) [17]. Two independent authors (G.A.M. and B.A.) completed the risk of bias assessment. Disagreements were resolved through a consensus after discussing the reasons for the discrepancy.  

# Statistical analysis  

This systematic review and meta-analysis followed the Cochrane Collaboration Handbook of Systematic Review of Interventions and the Preferred Reporting Items for the Systematic Reviews and Meta-analysis (PRISMA) statement guidelines [6, 12]. The protocol was preregistered in the International Prospective Register of Systematic Reviews (PROSPERO; CRD42024512702).  

Review Manager 5.4.1 (Nordic Cochrane Centre, The Cochrane Collaboration) was used for all statistical analyses. A risk ratio (RR) with $95\%$ confidence intervals was used to report binary outcome treatment effects. Mean Difference (MD) with $95\%$ confidence intervals were used for continuous outcomes. The $\mathfrak{p}$ values inferior to 0.05 were considered as a statistically significant result for effect size. Heterogeneity was assessed with the Cochran Q test and I² statistics. The p values inferior to 0.10 and $\mathrm{I}^{2}\ge25\%$ were considered significant for heterogeneity. We used DerSimonian and Laird random-effects model. Sensitivity analyses, employing the leave-one-out approach, were conducted to verify the robustness of the findings. Publication bias was investigated with funnel plot analysis. The funnel plots are illustrated in Supplementary Fig. S1.  

# Results  

# Study selection and characteristics  

A total of 1249 potentially relevant publications were systematically identified. After removing duplicate records and screening titles and abstracts, ten articles were selected for full-text reading. Ultimately, seven studies comprising 879 patients were included (Fig.  1) [2, 3, 7, 9, 14, 24, 25]. A total of 430 $(48.9\%)$ patients underwent NE surgical evacuation, while 449 $(51.0\%)$ underwent craniotomy. The mean age of the NE group was $57.3\pm17.8$ years, while the mean age of the craniotomy group was $57.9\pm12.7$ years. The basal ganglia was the most commonly reported hemorrhage site. Detailed characteristics of the included studies are presented in Table 1.  

# Pooled analysis of all studies  

# Efficacy outcomes  

Six studies reported data for a favorable functional outcome [3, 7, 9, 14, 24, 25], comprising 448 events: 400 patients in the NE group $(48.8\%)$ and 419 patients in the craniotomy group $(51.1\%)$ . The NE approach was significantly superior to craniotomy in improving functional outcomes (RR: 1.42; $95\%$ CI 1.17, 1.72; $p<0.001$ ; $\mathrm{I}^{2}=57\%$ ; Fig. 2). Following our leave-one-out sensitivity analysis, NE maintained the superiority compared to craniotomy, with a low heterogeneity in the results (Supplementary Fig. S2).  

Functional disability was reported by five studies [3, 7, 9, 14, 25], comprising 236 events: 379 patients in the NE group $(49.3\%)$ and 389 patients in the craniotomy group $(50.6\%)$ . There was a benefit in the NE intervention compared to the traditional technique (RR: 0.61; $95\%$ CI 0.45,  

0.83; $p<0.001$ ; $\mathrm{I}^{2}=42\%$ ; Fig. 3). After the leave-one-out, the NE continued to present benefits in comparison with craniotomy, with a low heterogeneity in the results (Supplementary Fig. S3).  

The residual hematoma volume was significantly reduced (MD: -2.39, $95\%$ CI -4.52, -0.25; $p{=}0.03$ ; $\mathrm{I}^{2}=68\%$ ; Fig. 4), while the hematoma evacuation rate was higher in patients who underwent the NE approach (MD: -8.36; $95\%$ CI -12.66, -4.07; $p<0.001$ ; $\mathrm{I}^{2}=94\%$ ; Fig.  4). Even after the leave-one-out approach, the heterogeneity remained significantly high in the hematoma evacuation rate endpoint (Supplementary Fig. S4)  

# Safety outcomes  

NE surgery approach was not associated with a reduction in infection rate (RR: 0.48, $95\%$ CI 0.23, 1.04; $p{=}0.06$ ; $\mathrm{I}^{2}=69\%$ ; Fig.  5). In the leave-one-out, NE presented significantly less infection than craniotomy, with a low heterogeneity in the results Supplementary Fig. S5). Regarding rebleeding, there was no difference between the techniques (RR: 0.61, $95\%$ CI 0.21, 1.82; $p{=}0.38$ ; $\mathrm{I}^{2}=0\%$ ; Fig. 5). The mortality rate was reported by all studies [2, 3, 7, 9, 14, 24, 25], with a total of 35 events in the NE group $(8.1\%)$ , and 49 $(10.9\%)$ in the craniotomy group. The NE did not show benefits in improving mortality rate (RR: 0.81, $95\%$ CI 0.54, 1.22; $p{=}0.32$ ; $\mathrm{I}^{2}=0\%$ ; Fig. 5).  

# Quality assessment  

Six RCTs raised some concerns of risk of bias [2, 3, 7, 9, 24, 25], mostly due to the randomization process, deviation from intended intervention and in the selection of reported results. The other RCT was considered as low risk of bias (Fig. 6) [13].  

# Discussion  

NE evacuation of ICH versus craniotomy has been studied by several researchers and here we performed the most updated meta-analysis regarding its efficacy and safety. In this meta-analysis of seven studies and 879 patients [2, 3, 7, 9, 14, 24, 25], the main findings include: (1) NE was superior to craniotomy in improving functional outcomes; (2) patients who underwent craniotomy had more functional disability; (3) NE was not associated with less infections, rebleeding or mortality than the craniotomy group; and (4) evacuation rate in the NE group was significantly higher than the craniotomy group, with less residual hematoma volume.

Hypertensive ICH is a condition with high morbidity and mortality, inflicting major concerns regarding the most effective management. Auer et al. first proposed NE evacuation with favorable results in survival rate compared to medicamentous treatment, but only in patients below 60 years old and with a preoperative favorable state of consciousness [1]. Since then, NE evolved and emerged as a promising surgical technique for ICH management, due to its lower complication rates, better protection of the surrounding brain tissue, and high evacuation rate [22, 24]. NE also has shown superiority in prior meta-analyses when compared with conservative treatment and other surgical modalities [4, 6, 21].  

In a previous meta-analysis of RCTs by Hallenberger et al., NE showed no improvement in functional outcomes compared to craniotomy [5]. A recent RCT by Noiphithak et al. showed that patients selected for NE surgical evacuation achieved more favorable outcomes $(\mathrm{mRS~}0{-}3)$ at 180-day follow-up compared to the craniotomy group [14]. Another recent RCT by Lv et al. observed no significant improvement in functional outcomes between neuroendoscopy and craniotomy groups 6 months after surgery [9]. In our meta-analysis, NE presented a significantly higher rate of favorable functional outcomes compared to craniotomy $(64.5\%$ versus $45.3\%$ ). In addition, craniotomy was associated with more functional disability in our pooled analysis, indicating that the more invasive and traumatic approach could lead to a poor functional outcome. Our sensitivity analyses substantially reduced the heterogeneity of both functional endpoints, improving the generalizability of these findings.

Although there is support in the literature in favor of NE surgery compared to other MIS techniques [18, 21], our study showed different results than the most recent metaanalysis of the existing RCTs comparing NE surgical evacuation versus craniotomy [5]. NE surgery demonstrated a superior evacuation rate compared to craniotomy. In addition, the residual hematoma volume was significantly decreased after NE surgery in comparison to craniotomy. These results corroborate the improvement in neurological function in patients assigned for NE evacuation since this surgical approach allows less brain tissue injury during hematoma drainage, shorter operation time, and faster recovery [22, 23]. Besides the consistent finding of improvement in hematoma evacuation rate, it is crucial to acknowledge the high heterogeneity of the analyses and the possible impact on the results’ confiability. Of note, the current RCTs available did not investigate the application of NE drainage in patients with large ICH $\left(>70~\mathrm{ml}\right)$ , with or without intraventricular hemorrhage, and its efficacy in this situation remains controversial.  

NE is less invasive and a lower rate of complications related to the surgical procedure is expected [22, 24]. In prior meta-analyses, NE reduced complications rate compared to craniotomy [27]. In this meta-analysis, the MIS technique was not associated with less complication. However, in our sensitivity analysis, infection was significantly reduced in the NE group in comparison with the craniotomy. Moreover, NE was not able to show an improvement in mortality compared to craniotomy. Thus, NE might be associated with lower infection rates, but further large randomized studies are needed to properly evaluate the NE surgery safety profile.

This study has some limitations that should be considered: (1) despite adding new RCTs to the analysis, there are several small sample-size studies included in this meta-analysis; (2) we combine different measurements in functional outcomes analyses, warranting careful interpretation of the results (3) the heterogeneity noted is considerable, and the sources were not found in the hematoma evacuation rate endpoint; and (4) we limited our screening process to studies only published in English language, which could lead to some bias in the selection of the included studies.  

# Conclusion  

NE was associated with more favorable functional outcomes and lower rates of functional disabilities compared to craniotomy. Also, NE was superior regarding evacuation rate, while presenting a reduction in residual hematoma volume. NE might be associated with lower infection rates. Mortality was not improved by NE surgery. Larger, higher-quality randomized studies are needed to adequately evaluate the efficacy and safety of NE compared to craniotomy.
