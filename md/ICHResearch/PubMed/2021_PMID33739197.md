# Safety and efficacy of remote ischemic conditioning for the treatment of intracerebral hemorrhage: A proof-ofconcept randomized controlled trial  

Wenbo Zhao $1,2,3,\textcircled{10},$ , Fang Jiang1, Sijie Li3, Guiyou Liu2, Chuanjie $\mathbf{\Delta}\mathbf{w}_{\mathbf{u}}\mathbf{\Xi}^{\mathsf{I}}$ , Yuang Wang1, Changhong Ren2,3, Jing Zhang1, Fei Gu4, Quanzhong Zhang5, Xinjing Gao6, Zongen Gao7, Haiqing Song1, Qingfeng Ma1, Yuchuan Ding8 and Xunming Ji2,9 ; for the RICH-1 Investigators  



# Abstract  

Background: Remote ischemic conditioning can promote hematoma resolution, attenuate brain edema, and improve neurological recovery in animal models of intracerebral hemorrhage.  

Aims: This study aimed to evaluate the safety and preliminary efficacy of remote ischemic conditioning in patients with intracerebral hemorrhage.  

Methods: In this multicenter, randomized, controlled trial, 40 subjects with supratentorial intracerebral hemorrhage presenting within $24-48\ h$ of onset were randomly assigned to receive medical therapy plus remote ischemic conditioning for consecutive seven days or medical therapy alone. The primary safety outcome was neurological deterioration within seven days of enrollment, and the primary efficacy outcome was the changes of hematoma volume on CT images. Other outcomes included hematoma resolution rate at 7 days ([hematoma volume at 7 days  hematoma volume at baseline]/hematoma volume at baseline), perihematomal edema (PHE), and functional outcome at 90 days.  

Results: The mean age was $59.3\pm11.7$ years and hematoma volume was $13.9\pm4.5\mathrm{mL}$ . No subjects experienced neurological deterioration within seven days of enrollment, and no subject died or experienced remote ischemic conditioning-associated adverse events during the study period. At baseline, the hematoma volumes were $14.19\pm5.07\mathsf{mL}$ in the control group and $13.55\pm3.99\mathsf{mL}$ in the remote ischemic conditioning group, and they were $8.54\pm3.99\mathsf{mL}$ and $6.95\pm2.71\mathrm{mL}$ at seven days after enrollment, respectively, which is not a significant difference $(p>0.05$ each). The hematoma resolution rate in the remote ischemic conditioning group $(49.25\pm9.17\%)$ was significantly higher than in the control group ( $41.92\pm9.14\%$ ; MD, $7.3\%$ ; $95\%$ CI, $1.51{-}|3.16\%$ ; $\pmb{\mathscr{p}}{=}0.015$ ). The absolute PHE volume was $17.27\pm8.34\mathsf{mL}$ in the control group and $12.92\pm7.30\mathsf{mL}$ in the remote ischemic conditioning group at seven days after enrollment, which is not a significant between-group difference $(p=0.087)$ , but the relative PHE in the remote ischemic conditioning group $(1.77\pm0.39)$ was significantly lower than in the control group $(2.02\pm0.27$ ; MD, 0.25; $95\%$ CI, 0.39–0.47; $\pmb{\triangleright}=0.023$ ). At 90-day follow-up, 13 subjects $(65\%)$ in the remote ischemic conditioning group and 12 subjects $(60\%)$ in the control group achieved favorable functional outcomes (modified Rankin Scale score $\leq3.$ ), which is not a significant between-group difference $(p=0.744)$ .  

Conclusions: Repeated daily remote ischemic conditioning for consecutive seven days was safe and well tolerated in patients with intracerebral hemorrhage, and it may be able to improve hematoma resolution rate and reduce relative PHE. However, the effects of remote ischemic conditioning on the absolute hematoma and PHE volume and functional outcomes in this patient population need further investigations.

# Introduction  

Intracerebral hemorrhage (ICH), which accounts for more than $20\%$ of strokes in some countries like China, is the least treatable type of stroke; with an associated 30-day mortality is as high as $50\%$ , and only $20\%$ of survivors regain functional independence.1 The unfavorable outcomes of ICH mainly result from hematoma in the acute phase and subsequent perihematomal edema (PHE). Therefore, hematoma clearance and alleviation of PHE are considered to be pivotal therapeutic targets for ICH.2 Currently, surgical hematoma evacuation with invasive and minimally invasive strategies has been investigated for the treatment of ICH, but conclusive demonstrations of clinical benefit has remained largely elusive.3 In addition, many therapies that target PHE have been investigated and demonstrated promising preclinical results, but their clinical efficacy remain to be proven.4  

Remote ischemic conditioning (RIC) is a protective systemic strategy by which one or more cycles of brief limb ischemia confer protection in distant organs.5,6 RIC has been shown to have neuroprotective effects and its underlying mechanisms include neurovascular protection, anti-inflammatory effects, neuronal protection against excitotoxicity, circulating inflammasome activation, and transcriptional regulation of neuroprotective pathways. Recently, animal studies have shown that RIC may promote hematoma resolution via increasing anti-inflammatory macrophage activation8 and attenuate cerebral edema by affecting local factors in the brain, such as the $\mathrm{p}44/42$ mitogen-activated protein kinases, heme oxygenase-1, transferrin, and transferrin receptor. However, it remains unknown whether RIC is safe and effective in promoting hematoma resolution and reducing PHE in patients with ICH.  



# Aims  



# Methods  

To translate these preclinical data, we conducted the Remote Ischemic Conditioning for the treatment of intracerebral Hemorrhage (RICH-1) trial to evaluate the safety of RIC in patients with ICH and to determine the preliminary efficacy of RIC in promoting hematoma resolution and attenuating PHE.  

The study design and subjects, randomization masking, and intervention procedures have been reported in the published protocol.10  



# Imaging  

A non-contrast head CT was performed at baseline, on days 3 and 7 after randomization, and when necessary. CT scans were performed with 64-row CT scanners using standard local protocols, and each CT image with $5{\cdot}\mathrm{mm}$ slice thickness was used to measure the hematoma and PHE volumes using ImageJ 1.52 a (National Institutes of Health, USA). All volume measurements were calculated using a semi-automated process by tracing the perimeters of appropriate high- and low-attenuating zones and calculating the lesion areas for each slice multiplied by slice thickness to yield lesion volumes. All images were analyzed by two independent physicians who were blinded to the treatment assignment, and any disagreement was resolved by reaching a consensus between them.  



# Outcomes assessment  

Safety assessment. The primary safety outcome was neurological deterioration defined as an increase of four or more points in the National Institutes of Health Stroke Scale (NIHSS) or a decrease of 2 or more points in the Glasgow Coma Scale (GCS) score within seven days of enrollment without a clear explanation. Other safety outcomes included any death and any adverse event that prolonged hospitalization or required medical treatment during the study period.  

Efficacy assessment. The primary efficacy outcome was the changes of hematoma volume on CT images. In addition to the absolute values of hematoma volume, hematoma resolution rate at seven days was also assessed, and it (percentage) was calculated with the following formula: (hematoma volume at 7 days hematoma volume at baseline)/hematoma volume at baseline.  

Secondary efficacy outcomes included the change of PHE volume on CT images, and both the absolute PHE volumes and relative PHE volumes were evaluated. The absolute PHE volumes were calculated by subtracting the hematoma volume from the combined hematoma plus edema volume, and the relative PHE was calculated as a unitless ratio by dividing the absolute PHE volume by the hematoma volume.11 Hematoma expansion within three days of enrollment was also evaluated. Hematoma expansion was defined as an increase in the volume of intraparenchymal hemorrhage of $\geq33\%$ as determined by image analysis on the reinspected CT scan as compared with the baseline CT scan.12 The cutoff for hematoma expansion was defined according to the standard set in the study by Brott et al.,12 which was the change in size associated with significant neurological deterioration. The modified Rankin scale (mRS) scores at 90 days was assessed, with mRS score of 0–3 indicating good functional outcomes. The change in the NIHSS score and RIC-associated adverse events was also assessed.  

All outcomes were assessed by two independent investigators who were blinded to the treatment assignment. Any disagreement was resolved by reaching a consensus between them, or if no consensus could be reached, another observer made the final decision.  

Statistical analyses. No previous clinical trials have been conducted to investigate RIC in patients with ICH, and no parameters were available when estimating the sample size. The sample size was determined according to previous publications that reported that 15 patients per group are adequate for determining the feasibility of RIC and whether a larger multicenter trial should be conducted.13,14 To ensure that the main purpose of this study would be achieved, 20 subjects in each group were recruited.  

All statistical comparisons were made between the two groups. Categorical variables are presented as counts and percentages and analyzed using the $\chi^{2}$ test or continuity correction (when the expected value was less than 5) where appropriate. Continuous variables are presented as means and standard deviations (SDs) or medians and ranges and compared with the analysis of the independent-samples $t$ -test or Mann–Whitney U test. For the comparisons of the NIHSS score, GCS score, hematoma volume, and PHE volume, repeated measures analysis of variance was also used for analysis.  

No subjects were lost to follow-up at 90 days, but two subjects in the control group did not undergo head CT on day 3, while one subject in the RIC group did not undergo head CT examination on day 7. Missing imaging values were analyzed through a multiple imputation procedure with a missing-at-random assumption using a regression-switching approach. The imputation procedure was performed using the adjacent characteristics of subjects, a predictive meanmatching method for continuous variables for the intervention and control groups, and logistic regression models for categorical variables.  



# Results  

Between July 2019 and February 2020, a total of 40 consecutive subjects with ICH were recruited for this study (Figure 1). The mean age was $59.3\pm11.7$ years, and 23 subjects $(57.5\%)$ were male. The baseline characteristics were well balanced in both groups (Table 1). No subjects were lost to the 90-day follow up.  

At baseline, the median NIHSS score was 9.5 (range: 1–28) points, and the median GCS score was 15 (range: 10–15) points. Most hematomas [36 of 40 $(90\%)]$ were located in the deep white matter. The median time from onset to RIC treatment was 35.4 (range: 25.6–44.9) hours. Demographic and baseline data were wellbalanced between the two groups.  



# Safety outcomes  

None of the subjects experienced neurological deterioration within seven days of the enrollment, and none of the subjects died during the study period. Five subjects $(25\%)$ in the RIC group and six subjects $(30\%)$ in the control group experienced pulmonary infection $(p=0.72)$ , while two subjects $(10\%)$ in the RIC group and two subjects $(10\%)$ in the control group experienced deep vein thrombosis in the lower limbs $(p=1.00)$ , and no significant difference was noted (Table 2). In the RIC group, no subjects experienced transient erythema from repeated pressure cuff applications, and no adverse event was attributed to RIC procedures.  



# Hematoma volume  

Data of hematoma volume are summarized in Table 3. At baseline, the mean hematoma volumes were $14.19\pm$ $5.07\mathrm{mL}$ in the control group and $13.55\pm3.99\mathrm{mL}$ in the RIC group. Three days after enrollment, the mean hematoma volumes were $13.5\pm4.77\mathrm{mL}$ in the control group and $12.19\pm3.71\mathrm{mL}$ in the RIC group, while seven days after enrollment, they were $8.54\pm3.99\mathrm{mL}$ and $6.95\pm2.71\mathrm{mL}$ , respectively. The reduction in hematoma volume from baseline to day 7 was $5.65\pm$ $1.41\mathrm{mL}$ in the control group and $6.60\pm2.07\mathrm{mL}$ in the RIC group, which appears to be higher in the RIC group by $14.4\%$ . None of the aforementioned between-group differences were statistically significant $\mathrm{\mathit{\Omega}}_{\mathrm{\mathit{p}}>0.05}$ each); however, the hematoma resolution rate was $49.25\pm9.17\%$ in the RIC group, which was significantly higher than that of the control group $41.92\pm9.14\%$ , mean difference $7.3\%$ , $95\%$ confidence interval, $1.51{-}13.16\%$ , $p=0.015$ ).

# Perihematomal edema  

Values of absolute and relative PHE are also summarized in Table 3. The absolute PHE volume showed no significant between-group differences at baseline  

$16.80\pm9.84\mathrm{mL}$ vs. $14.50\pm6.80\mathrm{mL}$ , $p=0.396)$ , on day 3 $(20.31\pm8.31\mathrm{mL}$ vs. $16.99\pm8.84\mathrm{mL}$ , $p=0.229,$ , or on day 7 $17.27\pm8.34\mathrm{mL}$ vs. $12.92\pm7.30\mathrm{mL}$ , $p{=}0.087)$ . The mean relative PHE at baseline were $1.11\pm0.26$ in the control group and $1.05\pm0.23$ in the RIC group, and they were $1.49\pm0.30$ and $1.33\pm0.32$ on day 3, respectively. No significant between-group differences were detected ( $\mathrm{\mathit{p}}{}>0.05$ each). On day 7 after enrollment, the relative PHE was $2.02\pm0.27$ in the control group, which was significantly higher than that in the RIC group $[1.77\pm0.39$ ; mean difference 0.25; $95\%$ confidence interval, 0.39–0.47; $p=0.023]$ .  



# Clinical outcomes  

Changes in the NIHSS and GCS scores are summarized in Table 2. The median NIHSS scores at baseline were 9.5 (range: 1–28) points in the control group and 9.5 (range: 3–23) points in the RIC group $(p=0.509)$ at baseline, and they were 6 (0–21) points and 5 (0–19) points, respectively, on day 14 or on the day of discharge $(p=0.335)$ . The median GCS scores at baseline were 15 (range: 10–15) points in the control group and 15 (range: 10–15) points in the RIC group $(p=0.924)$ , and they were 15 (11–15) points and 15 (15–15) points, respectively, on day 14 or on the day of discharge $(p=0.317)$ .  

Distribution of the mRS scores on day 90 is illustrated in Figure 2. The median mRS scores (range) were 3 (0–5) in the control group and 2 (0–5) in the RIC group $\zetap=0.611,$ ). The proportions of subjects that achieved excellent outcomes (mRS scores of 0–1) were $25\%$ in the control group and $35\%$ in the RIC group $\overset{\cdot}{\boldsymbol{p}}=0.490,$ ), and functional independence (mRS scores of 0–2) was achieved in $45\%$ and $55\%$ , respectively $\overset{\cdot}{p}=0.527,$ ). Furthermore, favorable clinical outcome (mRS scores of 0–3) was achieved in 12 subjects $(60\%)$ in the control group and 13 subjects $(65\%)$ in the  

RIC group, and no significant difference was detected $(p=0.744)$ .  



# Discussion  

In this study, we found that repeated daily RIC for consecutive seven days was safe and well tolerated in patients with ICH who presented within $24-48\mathrm{h}$ of symptom onset, it did not result in any clinical and radiological adverse events; the hematoma resolution rate was significantly improved whereas the relative PHE was significantly reduced in those receiving RIC, but this study was not sufficiently powered to detect significant between-group differences in the hematoma volume, absolute PHE volume, and functional outcomes.  

In patients with ICH, hematoma volume is independently associated with the severity and prognosis, and therefore, hematoma clearance has been considered one of the most pivotal therapeutic targets for ICH.2,15,16 In a preclinical study, repeated RIC for $^{5-}$ 7 days has been found to promote hematoma resolution and the recovery of neurological function.8 To the best of our knowledge, the present clinical study firstly showed that in patients with ICH, repeated RIC for consecutive seven days could significantly improve the hematoma resolution rate. Compared with the changes of hematoma volume, hematoma resolution rate is calculated taking into account both the changes of hematoma volume and baseline hematoma volume, which can control the impacts of hematoma volume at start.17 Although the present study was not sufficiently powered to demonstrate the effects of RIC on the absolute hematoma resolution volume in patients with ICH, the higher hematoma resolution rate may also indicate the effects of RIC on the promotion of hematoma resolution in patients with ICH. Moreover, the reduction in hematoma volume from baseline to day 7 is much larger in patients treated with RIC than those not receiving RIC by $14.4\%$ . In the study protocol, we have defined that if the between-group difference in the change of hematoma volume at day 7 is not less than $12\%$ in favor of RIC, further trial that investigated the effects of RIC in patient with ICH will be moving forward. Currently, therefore, a multicenter, phase III randomized, sham controlled trial that evaluated the effects of RIC on the clinical outcomes in patients with ICH is now underway (RICH-2 study, http:// www.clinicaltrials.gov. Unique identifier: NCT04657133).

The proportions of subjects that achieved excellent outcomes (mRS scores of 0–1) were 25% in the control group and 35% in the RIC group, and functional independence (mRS scores of 0–2) was achieved in 45% and 55%, respectively. In previous clinical trials, PHE volume has been found to be associated with poor prognosis in patients with ICH, and the absolute PHE volume is associated with the size of a hematoma.18,19 To control the hematoma volume, the relative PHE, which is defined as PHE volume/ICH volume, was used to evaluate edema after ICH, and a larger relative PHE has been demonstrated to be associated with worse functional outcomes.11 In this study, the results showed that the relative PHE was smaller on day 7 in those received RIC, and these results were consistent with those of a previous animal study9 that revealed that RIC may reduce cerebral edema after ICH.  

For this study, we recruited patients with supratentorial ICH whose hematoma volumes were $10{-}30\mathrm{mL}$ on baseline CT imaging, and this group of patients did not need to undergo surgical interventions according to current guidelines and exhibited a favorable prognosis.15 This may be the main reason why most of them achieved a relatively favorable clinical outcomes, and no subjects died at the 3-month follow-up. Furthermore, in this study, no patients experienced hematoma extension, which is a common complication of ICH that causes poor clinical outcomes.20 The main reason may be that most hematoma expansion occurs within 24 h of onset,20,21 whereas in this study, we recruited subjects after the first $24\mathrm{{h}}$ of ICH onset (within $24-48\mathrm{h}$ of onset). Therefore, in future studies conducted to investigate hematoma resolution effects, recruiting patients with ICH after the first $24\mathrm{{h}}$ of symptom onset might be a better option.  

This study has several limitations. First, this study is of open-label design, the inherent limitations of this type of study design may cause certain biases, even though this study was conducted with raters blinded. In addition, the number of patients recruited in the study is still small, which preclude the power of this study in detecting efficacy of RIC on clinical outcomes. Finally, this study revealed that RIC may promote hematoma resolution and reduce relative PHE, but these effects of RIC were not reflected in the functional outcomes, and its underlying mechanisms remain unclear, which need further investigation. Nevertheless, the results of this study demonstrated that RIC seems to be safe and potentially effective in patients with ICH and therefore is an excellent candidate for further investigation. Actually, in Hougaard’s trial that was published in 2014,22 RIC has been investigated in patients with ICH, but related results were not reported. Currently, the RESIST trial (clinicaltrials.gov. Unique identifier: NCT03481777) and REMOTE-CAT trial (clinicaltrials.gov. Unique identifier: NCT03375762) investigating RIC in suspected stroke patients at the pre-hospital stage also enroll patients with ICH.  



# Conclusions  

The results of this study suggested that repeated daily RIC for seven consecutive days is safe and can improve the hematoma resolute rate and reduce the relative PHE in patients with ICH presenting within $24-48\mathrm{h}$ after symptom onset. Given the current lack of effective medical therapies for ICH, larger studies investigating the potential clinical efficacy of RIC and its effects on the absolute hematoma and PHE volume in this patient population are needed.
