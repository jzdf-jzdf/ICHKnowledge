# Abstract  

Background: Patients who suffer intracerebral hemorrhage (ICH) are at very high risk of recurrent ICH and other serious cardiovascular events. A single-pill combination (SPC) of blood pressure (BP) lowering drugs offers a potentially powerful but simple strategy to optimize secondary prevention.  

Objectives: The Triple Therapy Prevention of Recurrent Intracerebral Disease Events Trial (TRIDENT) aims to determine the effects of a novel SPC “Triple Pill,' three generic antihypertensive drugs with demonstrated eficacy and complementary mechanisms of action at half standard dose (telmisartan $20\mathsf{m}\mathsf{g},$ amlodipine $2.5\mathsf{mg},$ and indapamide $1.25\mathsf{mg})$ , with placebo for the prevention of recurrent stroke, cardiovascular events, and cognitive impairment after ICH.  

Design: An international, double-blind, placebo-controlled, randomized trial in adults with ICH and mild-moderate hypertension (systolic BP: $130\mathrm{-}160\mathrm{mm}\mathsf{H}g)$ , who are not taking any Triple Pill component drug at greater than half-dose. A total of 1500 randomized patients provide $90\%$ power to detect a hazard ratio of 0.5, over an average follow-up of 3 years, according to a total primary event rate (any stroke) of $12\%$ in the control arm and other assumptions. Secondary outcomes include recurrent ICH, cardiovascular events, and safety.  

Results: Recruitment started 28 September 2017. Up to 31 October 2021, 821 patients were randomized at 54 active sites in 10 countries. Triple Pill adherence after 30 months is $86\%$ . The required sample size should be achieved by 2024.  

Conclusion: Low-dose Triple Pill BP lowering could improve long-term outcome from ICH.

# Background and rationale  

Intracerebral hemorrhage (ICH) affects several million people worldwide each year,1 but accounts for greater disability-adjusted life years than ischemic stroke due to its poorer prognosis, fewer proven treatments, and very high risk of recurrent ICH, other cardiovascular events, and cognitive impairment/dementia.2 Randomized controlled trials (RCTs) have shown stronger proportional benefits from BP lowering therapy in preventing recurrent ICH than ischemic stroke,3 and for more intensive BP lowering.4 However, clinicians consider intensive BP lowering may cause falls and increase the risk of dementia in older people, concerns reinforced by J-shaped relationships between BP and cardiovascular events seen in some epidemiological studies and post hoc secondary analysis of RCTs.5 Some hypertension management guideline committees have taken a conservative view in recommending higher systolic BP (SBP) targets in the elderly, and for monotherapy being the preferred approach in them; if combination therapy is considered, it should be initiated at low doses and titrated slowly with careful monitoring to avoid adverse events.6 Clinicians may also have misgivings over BP lowering therapy in patients where hypertension is not considered an important cause, such as in “lobar” ICH, where about half of the cases are presumed to be caused by cerebral amyloid angiopathy.7 Yet, studies have shown that hypertension is associated with both “deep” and “superficial” ICH,8 and of similar benefits of BP lowering by location of ICH.9  

Single-pill combination (SPC) BP lowering can simplify therapy to improve adherence and tolerability, reduce pill burden, lower medical costs and resource consumption, reduce physician inertia, and achieve better BP control compared to conventional multiple-pill therapy for hypertension.10–12 Since risk reductions are proportional to magnitude of BP lowering, a more intensive SPC approach may be appropriate for the very “high-risk,” ICH patient group.  

The Triple Therapy Prevention of Recurrent Intracerebral Disease Events Trial (TRIDENT) aims to determine the effectiveness of a novel “Triple Pill” SPC of generic antihypertensive agents at half-dose (telmisartan $20\mathrm{mg}$ , amlodipine $2.5\mathrm{mg}$ , and indapamide $1.25\mathrm{mg}$ ) on recurrent stroke and other outcomes. The components of the Triple Pill were chosen on the basis of demonstrated tolerability, efficacy in reducing BP, major cardiovascular outcomes and mortality, and in being the most common combination regimes recommended in hypertension management guidelines.6,10–12 We outline the study protocol, its modifications, and progress to date.  



# Methods  



# Design  

TRIDENT is a double-blind, placebo-controlled, RCT at ${\sim}60$ hospitals in Australia, Brazil, Georgia, Malaysia, Nigeria, Singapore, Sri Lanka, Taiwan, the Netherlands, the United Kingdom, Switzerland, and Vietnam. It is registered with ClinicalTrials.gov (NCT02699645) and Australian New Zealand Clinical Trials Registry (ANZCTR) (ACTRN12616000327482). The double-blind period is preceded by a $2\pm1$ week, single-blind active run-in phase on the Triple Pill, to ensure the randomization of patients adherent to the treatment and follow-up schedule (Figure 1). Participating hospitals require approval from relevant regulatory authorities and ethics committees, and all patients provide written consent. An initial vanguard phase to assess procedures, adherence, and BP reduction in 1000 patients at 30–50 hospitals was dropped in September 2021 due to changes to recruitment timelines.

# Patient population  



# Eligibility criteria  

1.	 Adults $(\geqslant18$ years) with a history of brain imaging confirmed primary ICH; the time window criterion of “12 months since symptom onset” was removed in the protocol in September 2021 as data have emerged of no substantial differences in the longterm risks of major cardiovascular events after ICH, and to provide greater flexibility in recruitment13,14;   
2. Clinically stable, according to the attending clinician;   
3.	 Mean resting seated SBP (two measures, 5 min apart) in the range $130{-}160\mathrm{mmHg}$ ;   
4.	 Clinician uncertainty over benefits and risks of intensive BP lowering;   
5. Geographical proximity to allow timely follow-up of assessments;   
6.	 No BP lowering medication at greater than half standard dose;   
7.	 Provide consent.  



# Exclusion criteria  

1.	 Taking an angiotensin-converting enzyme inhibitor (ACE-I) that cannot be switched to an alternative  

(telmisartan 20 or $40\mathrm{mg}$ amlodipine 2.5 or $5\mathrm{mg}$ , and indapamide $1.25\mathrm{mg}$ );   
2.	 Contraindication to any component of the Triple Pill;   
3.	 Unlikely/unable to complete study procedures and/ or follow-up;   
4.	 Females pregnant or breastfeeding, or child-bearing capability without adequate birth control;   
5. Significant hyperkalemia and/or hyponatremia, according to local criteria;   
6.	 Estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR) of $<30\mathrm{mL/min/1.73m^{2}}$ ;   
7.	 Hepatic impairment (alanine aminotransferase or aspartate aminotransferase ${>}3\times$ upper limit of normal);   
8.	 Unsuitable for another reason (e.g. severe disability, memory, or behavioral disorder).  



# Randomization  

After successful completion of the run-in phase and confirmation of eligibility, patients are randomized via a secure web-based system at The George Institute for Global Health (TGI) Australia, using a sequence stratified by country of recruitment, age ( $\mathord{\left.\begin{array}{l}{<65}\end{array}\right.}$ vs. $\mathord{\geqslant}65$ years), and baseline SBP (mean of two, $<140$ vs. ${\geq}140\mathrm{mmHg}$ ).  



# Interventions  



# Data management and quality assurance  

All patients receive investigational medical product (IMP), one SPC Triple Pill (telmisartan $20\mathrm{mg}$ , amlodipine $2.5\mathrm{mg}$ , and indapamide $1.25\mathrm{mg}$ , or matched placebos) per day. If the attending clinician wishes to increase BP lowering treatment, open label treatment can be added, subject to specific algorithms to avoid potential adverse drug interaction or dose excess. Strategies can be employed, without the need to unblind, for patients with potential IMP-related side-effects of sufficient severity to warrant a change of treatment, which include: de-challenge and if appropriate, re-challenge; step down or discontinuation of non-study BP lowering therapy; and if indicated, permanently discontinuation of IMP and start appropriate open-label therapy. Due to limited availability of indapamide $1.25\mathrm{mg}$ and high costs to purchase, manufacture, and label the original IMP (an over-encapsulation of existing generic monotherapies and matching placebos), this SPC is no longer viable beyond 2021. However, TGI recently created the “profit-with-purpose” social enterprise George Health Enterprises Pty Ltd (GHE), for commercial development of affordable, scalable treatments and technologies for improved management of cardiovascular disease through the business George Medicines Pty Ltd (GM). GM has developed a bespoke SPC of the same combination of BP lowering agents called GMRx2, available in three dose versions, manufactured under contract by Piramal Pharma Solutions at their US Food and Drug Agency and EU good manufacturing practice-approved production facilities in India. With studies confirming comparative dissolution properties as the TRIDENT over-encapsulated IMP, GMRx2 dose version 2 (telmisartan $20\mathrm{mg}$ , amlodipine $2.5\mathrm{mg}$ , and indapamide $1.25\mathrm{mg};$ or placebo) is a suitable replacement IMP from April 2022.  



# Data collection and follow-up  

Patients are enrolled as soon as practicable after an ICH, and follow-up is between 1 and 7 years, with key information collected at randomization (baseline) and follow-up at 6 weeks, 6 months, and 6 monthly thereafter, until study end (details outlined in Supplemental Appendix). All evaluations are face-to-face or telephone interview, with the patient (or suitable surrogate), ideally in a hospital outpatient clinic but if necessary, at home. As well as the ascertainment of outcome events, measurements taken include resting seated BP, physical function (simplified modified Rankin scale), cognitive function (Montreal Cognitive Assessment, and where feasible Brief Memory and Executive Test), and health-related quality of life (HRQoL, EQ-5D-3L). Blood tests are at 6 weeks and end of study. Medication can be couriered to the home of participants if they are unable to visit a hospital/clinic. The protocol was modified in 2020 to allow for remote patient assessments and home delivery of IMP due to restrictions imposed by COVID-19.  

Investigators enter data into electronic case report forms (CRF) with unique username/password to a TGI database. Research staff access online reports on study status, CRF completion, and serious adverse event (SAE) reports to assist monitoring data quality, and perform remote and onsite data verification of recruitment, data quality (timeliness, completeness, consistency), and response to queries, safety, and outcome events.  



# Outcomes  

The primary outcome is any stroke, according to central blinded adjudication of event details. Secondary outcomes include recurrent ICH; ischemic stroke; major cardiovascular events (non-fatal myocardial infarction, non-fatal stroke (ischemic, hemorrhagic, or uncertain cause), or death from a vascular cause); HRQoL; physical function/disability; cognitive impairment and dementia; depression, separately on the EQ-5D-3L; all-cause mortality; SBP change; safety; and tolerability.  



# Economic evaluation  

An Australia-specific within-trial economic evaluation of the Triple Pill will be conducted from a healthcare payer’s perspective to inform any future regulatory submission, comparing incremental costs (including costs of drugs and administration) and differences in health service use (based on hospital admissions) in Australian patients versus the incremental effectiveness observed in the entire study in terms of quality-adjusted life years (QALYs). The incremental cost-effectiveness ratio will be calculated and benchmarked against the prevailing cost-effectiveness threshold (currently US\$50,000perQALYgained), withone-wayandprobabilisticsensitivityanalysisforstructural/parameteruncertainty.
