Introduction: The efficacy of cilostazol administration to treat subarachnoid hemorrhage remains controversial. We conduct a systematic review and meta-analysis to explore the influence of cilostazol administration on treatment efficacy for subarachnoid hemorrhage.  

Methods: We have searched PubMed, EMbase, Web of science, EBSCO, and Cochrane library databases through July 2020 for randomized controlled trials (RCTs) assessing the effect of cilostazol administration in patients with subarachnoid hemorrhage. This meta-analysis is performed using the random-effect model.  

Results: Four RCTs involving 405 patients were included in the meta-analysis. Overall, compared with control group for subarachnoid hemorrhage, cilostazol intervention can significantly reduce symptomatic vasospasm $\mathrm{\Delta}Q\mathrm{R}=0.35$ ; $95\%{\mathrm{CI}}=0.21$ to 0.60; $\mathrm{P}=0.0001$ ) and cerebral infarction $\mathrm{OR}=0.40$ ; $95\%\mathrm{CI}=0.22$ to 0.73; $\mathrm{P}=0.003\$ ), as well as improve no or mild angiographic vasospasm $\mathrm{\Delta}\mathrm{\cdot}\mathrm{OR}=2.01\$ ; $95\%\mathrm{CI}=1.19$ to 3.42; $\mathrm{P}=0.01$ ) and mRS score $\leq2$ $\mathrm{OR}=2.70\$ ; $95\%\mathrm{CI}=1.09$ to 6.71; $\mathrm{P}=0.03$ ), but revealed no obvious influence on severe angiographic vasospasm $\mathrm{\Delta}^{\mathrm{\prime}}0\mathrm{R}=0.53\$ ; $95\%$ $\mathrm{~\'~C~I~}=0.27$ to 1.02; $\mathrm{P}=0.06;$ . There were no increase in adverse events $\mathrm{{\dot{QR}}}=1.17$ ; $95\%$ $\mathrm{~C~I~}=~0.54$ to 2.52; $\mathrm{~P~}=0.69\$ , hemorrhagic events $\mathrm{\nabla}{\mathrm{OR}}=0.62\$ ; $95\%$ $\mathrm{CI}=0.06$ to 6.27; $\mathrm{~P~}=~0.69$ and cardiac events $\mathrm{\Delta}\mathrm{OR}=2.14\$ ; $95\%~\mathrm{CI}=0.44$ to 10.27; $\mathrm{P}=0.34,$ ) after the cilostazol intervention than control intervention.  

Conclusions: Cilostazol treatment may be effective to treat subarachnoid hemorrhage in the terms of symptomatic vasospasm, cerebral infarction, no or mild angiographic vasospasm and mRS score $\leq2$ .

# 1. Introduction  

Subarachnoid hemorrhage is a life-threatening disease and mainly caused by the rupture of cerebral aneurysms [1–4]. Delayed cerebral ischemia (DCI) caused by cerebral vasospasm have become one of the most important causes of mortality and morbidity in these patients. Many factors result in the incidence of DCI and include persistent luminal narrowing of the major cerebral arteries, microcirculatory disturbance, early brain injury, and cortical spreading depression [5–7]. Many drugs and treatments have been develop to alleviate cerebral vasospasm and prevent DCI [8–11].  

Cilostazol, a selective inhibitor of phospho-diesterase 3 (PDE3), were reported as an efficacious drug for the prevention of cerebral vasospasm in experimental studies [12,13]. Cilostazol has been widely used for arteriosclerosis obliterans, the intermittent claudication, and the prevention of second ischemic stroke [14,15]. The potential of cilostazol in preventing cerebral vasospasm relies on several mechanisms including anti-platelet and vasodilating effect, inhibition of reactive oxygen species (ROS), inhibition of apoptosis, anti-inflammation, anti-lipid peroxidation, and induction of nitric oxide (NO) synthesis [13,16–19].  

Several studies have explored the efficacy of cilostazol to prevent cerebral vasospasm in subarachnoid hemorrhage, but the results have been conflicting [20–22]. With accumulating evidence, we therefore perform a systematic review and metaanalysis of RCTs to investigate the efficacy of cilostazol to prevent cerebral vasospasm in patients with subarachnoid hemorrhage.

# 2.1. Search strategy and study selection  

Two investigators have independently searched the following databases (inception to July 2020): PubMed, EMbase, Web of science, EBSCO, and Cochrane library databases. The electronic search strategy was conducted using the following keywords: cilostazol, and subarachnoid hemorrhage. We also check the reference lists of the screened full-text studies to identify other potentially eligible trials.  

The inclusive selection criteria were as follows: (i) patients were diagnosed with subarachnoid hemorrhage; (ii) intervention treatments were cilostazol administration versus placebo or usual care; (iii) study design was RCT.  

# 2.2. Data extraction and outcome measures  

We have extracted the following information: author, number of patients, age, male, hypertension, modified Rankin Scale (mRS) score, and detail methods in each group etc. Data have been extracted independently by two investigators, and discrepancies are resolved by consensus. We also contact the corresponding author to obtain the data when necessary.  

The primary outcome was symptomatic vasospasm. Secondary outcomes included no or mild angiographic vasospasm, severe angiographic vasospasm, cerebral infarction, mRS score $\leq~2$ adverse events, hemorrhagic events and cardiac events.  

# 2.3. Quality assessment in individual studies  

Methodological quality of the included studies was independently evaluated using the modified Jadad scale [24]. There were  

3 items for Jadad scale: randomization (0–2 points), blinding $(0-$ 2 points), dropouts and withdrawals (0–1 points). The score of Jadad Scale varied from 0 to 5 points. An article with Jadad score $\leq~2$ was considered to be of low quality. If the Jadad score $\geq3$ , the study was thought to have high quality [25].  

# 2.4. Statistical analysis  

We estimate the odd ratio (OR) with $95\%$ CIs for dichotomous outcomes (symptomatic vasospasm, no or mild angiographic vasospasm, severe angiographic vasospasm, cerebral infarction, mRS score $\leq2$ , adverse events, hemorrhagic events, and cardiac events). The random-effects model was used regardless of heterogeneity. Heterogeneity was reported using the $\mathrm{I}^{2}$ statistic, and $\bar{1^{2}}>50\%$ indicated significant heterogeneity [26]. Whenever significant heterogeneity was present, we searched for potential sources of heterogeneity via omitting one study in turn for the metaanalysis or performing subgroup analysis. Publication bias was not evaluated because of the limited number $(<10)$ of included studies. All statistical analyses were performed using Review Manager Version 5.3 (The Cochrane Collaboration, Software Update, Oxford, UK).  

# 3. Results  

# 3.1. Literature search, study characteristics and quality assessment  

A detailed flowchart of the search and selection results was shown in Fig. 1. 403 potentially relevant articles were identified initially. Finally, four RCTs that met our inclusion criteria were included in the meta-analysis [20–22,27].  

The baseline characteristics of the four eligible RCTs in the meta-analysis were summarized in Table 1. The four studies were published between 2011 and 2018, and the total sample size was 405. Cilostazol was administered at the dose of $100\mathrm{mg}$ twice daily, and the treatment duration was 12 days or 14 days.  

Among the four studies included here, three studies reported symptomatic vasospasm [21,22,27], three studies reported no or mild angiographic vasospasm, and severe angiographic vasospasm [20–22], four studies reported cerebral infarction [20–22,27], two studies reported mRS score $\leq2$ [22,27], and two studies reported adverse events, hemorrhagic events and cardiac events [21,22]. Jadad scores of the four included studies varied from 3 to 5, and all four studies were considered to be high-quality ones according to quality assessment.

# 4. Discussion  

The antiplatelet drug cilostazol, was hypothesized to decrease angiographic vasospasm, DCI, and poor outcome in patients with subarachnoid hemorrhage [28]. However, another RCT revealed that cilostazol did not reduce angiographic vasospasm, but reduced DCI and poor outcomes in these patients [21]. These indicated that cilostazol had pleiotropic effects on the reduction in DCI with regard to the relief of angiographic vasospasm, improvement of the microcirculation [27,29], and suppression of spreading depolarization through microcirculatory vasodilatation mediated by both cyclic adenosine monophosphate (cAMP) and upregulation of endothelial NO synthesis [18]. In addition, the mechanisms of cilostazol to inhibit the pathogenesis of cerebral vasospasm mainly included several elements: suppressing lipid peroxidation [19], reducing ROS [16], inducing NO production [18], preventing endothelial damage [12,30], and inhibiting vascular smooth muscle proliferation [12]. These various actions may account for the amelioration of angiographic vasospasm.

Several clinical studies investigated the efficacy of cilostazol on the prevention of cerebral vasospasm for subarachnoid hemorrhage [22,27,31]. Their results were not necessarily been consistent. Our meta-analysis included four RCTs and 405 patients. The main findings of this meta-analysis concluded that cilostazol was associated with significantly reduced symptomatic vasospasm and cerebral infarction, improved no or mild angiographic vasospasm and mRS score $\leq2$ as compared to control intervention in patients with subarachnoid hemorrhage. There were similar incidence of adverse events, hemorrhagic events and cardiac events between two groups.

Although there was no significant heterogeneity for primary outcome, several factors may produce some bias. Firstly, basic management of subarachnoid hemorrhage were not standardized between groups, and they involved sodium ozagrel, calcium channel blockers, and/or statin. Secondly, the administration of cilostazol was initiated in different timing after subarachnoid hemorrhage, such as $48\mathrm{~h~}$ , 96 h or no clear description after the onset of subarachnoid hemorrhage. Thirdly, the treatment durations were 12 days, 14 days or no clear description, which may affect the pooling results. Fourthly, end-point assessment was not performed by double-blind evaluation in some studies, which led to some assessment bias.  

This meta-analysis has several potential limitations that should be taken into account. Firstly, our analysis is based on only four RCTs, and more RCTs with large samples should be conducted to confirm this issue. Next, the initiation time, duration and methods of cilostazol intervention in included RCTs are different, which may have an influence on the pooling results. Finally, the analysis of some important outcomes such mortality can not be performed in this meta-analysis because of the limited and included RCTs.  

# 5. Conclusions  

Cilostazol intervention may provide additional benefits to the treatment of subarachnoid hemorrhage.
