# Abstract  

Background: Multiple attempts of thrombectomy have been linked to a higher risk of intracerebral hemorrhage and worsened functional outcomes, potentially influenced by blood pressure (BP) management strategies. Nonetheless, the impact of intensive BP management following successful recanalization through multiple attempts remains uncertain.  

Aims: This study aimed to investigate whether conventional and intensive BP managements differentially affect outcomes according to multiple-attempt recanalization (MAR) and first-attempt recanalization (FAR) groups.  

Methods: In this secondary analysis of the OPTIMAL-BP trial, which was a comparison of intensive (systolic BP target: ${<}140\mathrm{mm}\mathsf{H}g,$ ) and conventional (systolic BP target $=140{-}180\mathrm{mm}\mathsf{H}g)$ BP managements during the $24\mathsf{h}$ after successful recanalization, we included intention-to-treat population of the trial. Patients were divided into the MAR and the FAR groups. We examined a potential interaction between the number of thrombectomy attempts (MAR and FAR groups) and the effect of BP managements on clinical and safety outcomes. The primary outcome was functional independence at 3 months. Safety outcomes were symptomatic intracerebral hemorrhage within $36\mathsf{h}$ and mortality within 3 months.  

Results: Of the 305 patients (median $=75$ years), 102 $(33.4\%)$ were in the MAR group and 203 $(66.6\%)$ were in the FAR group. The intensive BP management was significantly associated with a lower rate of functional independence in the MAR group (intensive, $32.7\%$ vs conventional, $54.9\%$ , adjusted odds ratio $(\mathsf{OR})=0.33$ , $95\%$ confidence interval $(\mathsf{Cl})=0.12-0.90$ , $p=0.03$ ). In the FAR group, the proportion of patients with functional independence was not significantly different between the BP managements (intensive, $42.5\%$ vs conventional, $54.2\%$ adjusted $O{\sfR}=0.73$ , $95\%$ $\mathsf{Cl}=0.38-1.40\$ . Incidences of symptomatic intracerebral hemorrhage and mortality rates were not significantly different according to the BP managements in both MAR and FAR groups.  

Conclusions: Among stroke patients who received multiple attempts of thrombectomy, intensive BP management for $24\mathsf{h}$ resulted in a reduced chance of functional independence at 3 months and did not reduce symptomatic intracerebral hemorrhage following successful reperfusion.

# Introduction  

Endovascular thrombectomy (EVT) is the standard care in acute stroke patients with large vessel occlusion.1 Technical advancements in EVT have led to higher rates of recanalization.2–4 Successful reperfusion often requires multiple thrombectomy attempts. The frequency of first-attempt recanalization (FAR) varies between $37.1\%$ and $61.9\%$ .5 However, in comparison with recanalization with a single thrombectomy attempt (FAR), recanalization with multiple thrombectomy attempts (multiple-attempt recanalization (MAR)) was associated with worse outcomes.6,7 Multiple thrombectomy attempts increase the risk of endothelial damage and distal embolization. In addition, MAR may enhance vascular permeability as indicated by hyperintense acute reperfusion marker (HARM), which has been proposed as a marker of reperfusion injury.8–11 Consequently, MAR may increase the risk of reperfusion injury or intracerebral hemorrhage (ICH).  

Control of blood pressure (BP) may affect outcomes in patients with successful reperfusion following EVT. Excessive BP lowering may exacerbate ischemic injury while persistently elevated BP may increase the risk of ICH. Recent evidence from two randomized clinical trials suggested that intensive BP lowering subsequent to successful EVT resulted in worse functional outcomes compared with conventional BP management.12,13 Given the potential differences in the risk of reperfusion injury and ICH between MAR and FAR, the impact of BP lowering following EVT may differ between patients with MAR and those with FAR.  

In this study, we performed a secondary analysis of the Outcome in Patients Treated with Intra-arterial thrombectomy—Optimal Blood Pressure control (OPTIMAL-BP) $\mathrm{trial}^{13}$ to investigate whether there were differences in outcomes based on targeted-BP managements between the MAR and FAR groups.  



# Methods  



# Standard protocol approvals, registrations, and patients consents  

This was a secondary subgroup analysis of the OPTIMAL-BP trial (ClinicalTrials.gov Identifier:  

NCT04205305). The study protocol was approved by the institutional review board of each participating hospital, and written informed consents were obtained from all participants or their approved surrogate. This study followed the Consolidated Standards of Reporting Trials (CONSORT) statement to report the findings.14  



# Study design and population  

We analyzed the data of the OPTIMAL-BP trial,13 which compared intensive BP management (systolic BP (SBP) target: ${<}140\mathrm{mmHg}$ ) and conventional BP management (SBP target $=140{-}180\mathrm{mmHg}$ ) during the first $^{241}$ after successful reperfusion in patients who received EVT. Briefly, the OPTIMAL-BP trial was a multicenter, randomized, open-label trial with a blinded end-point evaluation, conducted across 19 stroke centers in South Korea from June 2020 to November 2022. The trial enrolled patients with acute ischemic stroke due to large vessel occlusion who attained successful reperfusion of the occluded artery and exhibited an elevated SBP $(\gtrsim140\mathrm{mmHg})$ within $^{2\mathrm{h}}$ after successful reperfusion. The details of the protocol, statistical analysis plan, and the results of the trial were reported elsewhere.13,15 The intention-to-treat population of the OPTIMAL-BP trial were eligible for this study.  



# Study group  

Patients were dichotomized into two groups based on the total number of passes to achieve successful recanalization: FAR group and MAR group. The FAR group was composed of patients who underwent a single pass of the device during the thrombectomy procedure, while the MAR group included patients who required two or more passes of the thrombectomy device. The count of passes was recorded each instance a stent retriever or aspiration catheter was utilized, either individually or in combination. Rescue therapies such as stenting, balloon angioplasty, and concomitant intra-arterial drug infusion were not counted as the passes.11 Device passing number was collected by local investigators. Two interventional neurologists and one neuroradiologist independently performed central adjudication of neuroimaging without knowledge of the treatment allocation.  



# BP monitoring and management  

Patients were managed in a stroke unit or similar facility equipped with continuous BP monitoring. BP was consistently recorded using non-invasive methods. To achieve and sustain the target SBP for each BP management group, intravenous BP medications were used. Nicardipine was predominantly used for BP reduction, although physicians had the option to select alternative drugs if necessary. Both  

BP management groups aimed to achieve the target SBP within $^{1\mathrm{h}}$ following randomization.  



# Outcomes  

The primary efficacy outcome was a binary analysis of the modified Rankin Scale (mRS) score at 3 months, categorizing scores as either 0–2 for functional independence or 3–6 for functional dependence or death. The primary safety outcomes included symptomatic intracerebral hemorrhage (sICH) within $36\mathrm{h}$ and mortality related to the index stroke within 3 months. The definition of sICH was from the European Cooperative Acute Stroke Study III.16  

Secondary outcomes included (1) shift analysis of the distribution of mRS scores; (2) any type of ICH within $36\mathrm{h}$ ; (3) National Institutes of Health Stroke Scale (NIHSS) score at $^{241}$ ; (4) proportion of patients who achieved excellent recovery (a NIHSS score of 0–1 or an improvement of ${>}8$ points at $^{241}$ ; (5) successful reperfusion at $^{241}$ which was defined as modified Thrombolysis in Cerebral Infarction grade of 2b or better, based on follow-up computed tomography angiography or magnetic resonance angiography at $24(\pm12)\mathrm{h}$ ; (6) functional independence at 1 month; (7) 3-level EuroQoL 5-Dimension Self-Report Questionnaire (EQ-5D-3L) score at 3 months (The EQ-5D-3L score can fall between $-0.171$ and 1, in which 1 represents a full health state (best outcome) and $-0.171$ is equivalent to death (worst outcome));17 (8) infarction volume at $^{241}$ ; and (9) frequency of malignant cerebral edema (defined as a rapidly worsening neurological condition marked by significant swelling on brain imaging) within $36\mathrm{h}$ . Malignant cerebral edema was determined by local investigators.  



# Statistical analysis  

Baseline characteristics were compared between the intensive BP management and the conventional BP management within the MAR and FAR groups. Continuous variables were presented as a mean $\pm$ standard deviation (SD) or median (interquartile range (IQR)), and categorical variables as numbers $(\%)$ . The $t\cdot$ -test, Wilcoxon rank sums test, chi-square test, or Fisher’s exact test was used for comparing baseline characteristics as appropriate. To show the difference of BP more than 24-hour period between the intensive and conventional BP managements according to the MAR and FAR groups, we used linear mixed model in each group’s stratum.  

The primary efficacy and safety outcomes were tested using binomial logistic regression to estimate the odds ratios (ORs) and $95\%$ confidence intervals (CIs). In addition, risk differences with $95\%$ CI were calculated. Multivariable logistic regression analyses were performed to adjust for age, sex, time from stroke onset to randomization, and NIHSS score immediately before EVT which were adjusted in the OPTIMAL-BP trial. For the secondary outcomes, the common OR representing a shift in mRS scores was calculated using an ordinal logistic regression analysis. Linear regression analyses were conducted for the NIHSS score at $^{241}$ , the EQ-5D-3L score at 3 months, and infarction volume at $^{241}$ . Interaction analyses were conducted to determine whether the impact of BP management group differed according to the group, either MAR or FAR, on the outcomes. We performed a subgroup analysis of the primary efficacy outcome to assess how intensive versus conventional BP management affected proportion of patients with 3-month functional independence across the prespecified subgroups, which included age, sex, hypertension, diabetes, atrial fibrillation, NIHSS score, onset to puncture time, intravenous tissue-type plasminogen activator, Alberta Stroke Program Early CT Score, collateral grade, and modified Treatment In Cerebral Infarction immediate. Treatment effect consistency across subgroups was assessed using a logistic regression model. The results of these secondary group analyses are considered exploratory, given the potential for type I errors due to multiple comparisons. A two-sided $p<0.05$ was considered statistically significant. All data were analyzed using SAS version 9.4 (SAS Institute Inc.) and R version 4.2.2 (R Foundation for Statistical Computing).

# Results  

Of the 306 patients who were enrolled in the OPTIMAL-BP trial, one patient was excluded due to immediate withdrawal of consent after randomization, and 305 were included in the group analyses (median age $=75$ years $\mathrm{IQR}=66-82\$ years), 182 $(59.7\%)$ men). Among the 305 included patients, the MAR group comprised 102 $(33.4\%)$ patients (49 intensive BP management and 53 conventional BP management) and the FAR group comprised 203 $(66.6\%)$ patients (106 intensive BP management and 97 conventional BP management) (Supplemental Figure 1).  



# Baseline characteristics  

Baseline demographics, clinical, imaging, and procedural characteristics were similar between the MAR and FAR groups (Supplemental Table 1). Patients in the MAR group had a median age of 75 years $(\mathrm{IQR}=67-82\$ years); 62 $(60.8\%)$ were men. Patients in the FAR group had a median age of 75 years $(\mathrm{IQR}=66-82\$ years); 120 $(59.1\%)$ were men. In the MAR group, baseline characteristics were comparable between the BP managements except for the variables of smoking, onset to reperfusion time, and onset to randomization time. Onset to reperfusion time and onset to randomization time were longer in the intensive management compared with the conventional management in the MAR group. In the FAR group, there were no significant differences in baseline characteristics between the BP managements (Table 1).  



# BP measurements  

At the time of enrollment, in both groups, the mean SBP did not significantly differ between the intensive BP management and the conventional BP management. In both groups, the intensive management had a significantly lower 24-h-mean SBP than the conventional management (Supplemental Table 2). Within the MAR group, the intensive management arm maintained target SBP for a median of $79.2\%$ $(\mathrm{IQR}=66.7-91.7)\$ of the study period, while the conventional management arm maintained target SBP for a median of $39.6\%$ $\mathrm{IQR}{=}20.8{-}70.8;$ of the time. In the FAR group, the intensive management arm maintained target SBP for a median of $87.5\%$ $(\mathrm{IQR}=75.0–91.7\$ of the time, while the conventional management arm maintained target SBP for a median of $33.3\%$ $\mathrm{\Delta}\mathrm{IQR}=11.5{-}66.7\$ of the study period (Supplemental Table 3). Within the MAR group, the mean between-BP management difference in SBP more than $^{241}$ was $-13.9\mathrm{mmHg}$ ( $95\%$ $\mathrm{CI}{=}{-}16.4$ to $-11.5$ , $p{<}0.001$ ). In the FAR group, the mean between-BP management difference in SBP more than $24\mathrm{h}$ was $-6.5\mathrm{mmHg}$ ( $95\%$ $\mathrm{CI}{=}{-}8.1$ to $-4.9$ , $p{<}0.001$ ) (Figure 1). In addition, regarding BP drop (at least one event of $\mathrm{SBP}<100\mathrm{mmHg})$ , both the MAR and FAR groups showed a higher proportion of medication-induced BP drops in the intensive BP management compared with the conventional BP management (Table 2).  



# Primary efficacy outcome  

The intensive management was significantly associated with a lower rate of functional independence (mRS score of 0–2) at 3 months in the MAR group (intensive, $32.7\%$ vs conventional, $54.9\%$ , adjusted $\mathrm{OR}=0.33\$ , $95\%$ $\mathrm{CI}{=}0.12{-}$ 0.90, $p{=}0.03,$ , but not in the FAR group (intensive, $42.5\%$ vs conventional, $54.2\%$ , adjusted $\mathrm{OR}{=}0.73,95\%\mathrm{CI}{=}0.38-$ $1.40,p{=}0.34)$ (Table 3 and Figure 2).

# Secondary outcomes  

In the MAR group, we found that a shift in the distribution of the mRS score at 3 months toward worse outcomes in the intensive management than in the conventional management. In addition, the intensive management was associated with a higher NIHSS score at $^{241}$ and a lower EQ-5D-3L score at 3 months compared with the conventional management. The outcomes of any type of ICH, excellent recovery in NIHSS score at $^{241}$ , successful reperfusion at $^{241}$ , functional independence at 1 month, infarction volume at $^{241}$ , and malignant cerebral edema were not significantly different between the intensive and conventional BP managements in the MAR group (Supplemental Table 4).  

In the FAR group, the proportion of malignant cerebral edema was significantly higher in the intensive management compared with the conventional management. Apart from the outcome of malignant cerebral edema, there were no significant differences in secondary outcomes between the intensive and conventional BP managements (Supplemental Table 4).  



# Subgroup analysis  

No significant interactions were detected within any specified subgroup regarding functional independence at 3 months in both MAR and FAR groups. In addition, point estimates consistently favored conventional BP management across all strata in both groups (Figure 3).  



# Discussion  

We found that intensive BP management for $^{241}$ after successful reperfusion was associated with a lower rate of functional independence at 3 months in the MAR group, but not in the FAR group. The intensive BP management in the MAR group was also associated with a shift to worse functional outcomes, lower NIHSS score at $^{241}$ , and lower EQ-5D-3L score at 3 months. Regardless of MAR and FAR groups, sICH and any type of ICH did not significantly differ between the BP managements.  

In this study, intensive BP management was associated with worse clinical outcomes in the MAR group, but not in the FAR group. Intensive BP management could exacerbate the oligemic state in patients who have sustained endothelial injury from multiple thrombectomy attempts. In addition, the MAR group showed longer duration of onset to reperfusion time compared with the FAR group. Several studies indicated that a longer duration of onset to reperfusion time was associated with delayed recovery time and poor outcomes.18,19 Intensive BP management may exacerbate the aggravated oligemic condition due to delayed recovery in the MAR group, potentially leading to an expansion of the ischemic lesion. In addition, the MAR group experienced a larger scale BP reduction from intensive management than the FAR group. This may also be related to the poor functional outcomes in the MAR group. Finally, a recent post hoc analysis of the OPTIMAL-BP trial indicated that medication-induced BP drops were linked to worse functional outcomes at 3 months.20 This suggests that the more pronounced medication-induced BP drops in the MAR group may have contributed to a lower rate of functional independence at 3 months.  

Our study showed that the incidence of sICH or any type of ICH was comparable across BP management strategies, irrespective of group stratification. EVT causes direct vessel injury in the intima and median of the arterial wall.21,22 In addition, multiple attempts of thrombectomy to achieve recanalization lead to higher rates of blood–brain barrier breakdown at 24-h post-EVT.9 Given the heightened risk of ICH in patients with disruption of blood–brain barrier,23 strict BP management may be advisable following multiple thrombectomy attempts. However, our findings imply that despite possible endothelial damage from multiple device passes, conventional BP management does not lead to a higher incidence of sICH compared with intensive BP management. The comparable incidence of sICH between the conventional and intensive BP managements might be attributed to adherence to guidelines that recommend maintaining SBP below $180\mathrm{mmHg}$ in both BP management strategies.

In clinical practice, as the number of thrombectomy attempts is affected by factors such as the tortuosity of the cerebral artery, the clot characteristics, and the choice of device or technique for EVT,24–27 it is hard to predict the number of attempts required for successful recanalization prior to completing EVT. The number of thrombectomy attempts may be the result of procedure before randomization. So, it is crucial to investigate whether intensive or conventional BP management after a successful EVT has an impact on clinical outcomes that varies with the number of thrombectomy attempts. Although no interaction in treatment effect was noted between the FAR and MAR groups, our findings indicated that intensive BP management in the MAR group was correlated with poor clinical outcomes without increasing ICH. These findings suggest that clinicians should approach BP lowering with caution in patients who underwent multiple attempts during EVT.  

This study has several limitations. First, the reduced sample size as partitioned between the two groups may have limited our ability to detect a difference. The reduced sample sizes for both the FAR group and the MAR group might be insufficient to ascertain the safety or efficacy of intensive BP management. Given the small size of study population, it should be interpreted with caution. Furthermore, the small sample size in the subgroup analysis may limit the interpretability of the results. Second, the device passing number was determined by local investigator without central adjudication. However, central adjudication is challenging since the operator possess the most precise understanding of details related to the procedure. There are constraints in making determinations based on digital subtraction angiography images. Third, this study may have been underpowered to detect differences in secondary outcomes like malignant cerebral edema, indicated by wide CIs and unstable regression models. This may be attributable to the infrequency of events, which has the potential to compromise the model’s robustness. Therefore, results of secondary outcomes should be interpreted as exploratory. Fourth, significant cross-over in SBP values was observed in both FAR and MAR groups. Especially in the conventional BP management group, SBP often fell below the target range of $140{-}180\mathrm{mmHg}$ . This is related to the OPTIMAL-BP trial protocol, which did not use vasopressors to raise SBP to the target range if the SBP fell below $140\mathrm{mmHg}$ . Further research is needed on using vasopressors to maintain target SBP to minimize cross-over. Fifth, the uneven distribution of stroke subtypes among MAR patients by BP managements may confound outcome comparisons. Further research on focusing on stroke etiology is needed. Finally, considering that all participants in the OPTIMAL-BP trial were from South Korea, the results should be interpreted with considering potential ethnic differences.

# Conclusion  

In this secondary analysis of the OPTIMAL-BP trial, patients who underwent multiple-attempt thrombectomy showed that intensive $^{24-\mathrm{h}}$ BP management decreased the likelihood of 3-month functional independence. However, intensive BP management did not reduce sICH rates within 36-h post-reperfusion in acute ischemic stroke due to large vessel occlusion.
