# Clinical and Imaging Indicators of Hemorrhagic Transformation in Acute Ischemic Stroke After Endovascular Thrombectomy  

Bing Tian , MD\*; Xia Tian, MD\*; Zhang Shi , MD; Wenjia Peng , MD; Xuefeng Zhang , MD; Pengfei Yang , MD; Zifu Li, MD; Xiaoxi Zhang, MD; Min Lou , MD; Congguo Yin, MD; Yongwei Zhang , MD; Jianping Lu , MD, Jianmin Liu, MD; for the DIRECT-MT Investigators  

BACKGROUND: Prior studies have investigated the clinical and imaging factors for hemorrhagic transformation (HT), especially symptomatic intracranial hemorrhage (sICH); however, whether alteplase increases the risk of HT after endovascular thrombectomy (EVT) is unknown. This study aimed to assess clinical and imaging features associated with HT, sICH, and parenchymal hematoma (PH) in patients with acute ischemic stroke after EVT, with and without intravenous alteplase in DIRECT-MT (Direct Intraarterial Thrombectomy to Revascularize Acute Ischemic Stroke Patients with Large Vessel Occlusion Efficiently in Chinese Tertiary Hospitals: a Multicenter Randomized Clinical Trial).  

METHODS: The DIRECT-MT trial is a randomized trial of EVT alone versus intravenous thrombolysis combined with EVT. HT, sICH, and PH was evaluated on follow-up computed tomography. Multivariable ordinal logistic regression analysis was used to test the association of stepwise selected determinants with HT, sICH, and PH.  

RESULTS: In total, 633 patients were analyzed; 261 $(41.2\%)$ had HT; 34 $(5.4\%)$ ) had sICH; and 85 ( $13.4\%)$ had PH. The median age was 69, and $56.7\%$ were men. The median National Institutes of Health Stroke Scale score was 18, and 320 patients were in combination-therapy group. Symptomatic intracranial hemorrhage was associated with higher baseline National Institutes of Health Stroke Scale score (adjusted odds ratio [OR], 1.06 $[95\%$ CI, 1.10–1.12]) and higher glucose level at hospital arrival (adjusted OR, 1.14 $[95\%$ CI, 1.00–1.29]). No association was found between alteplase treatment and HT, sICH, or PH. The independent predictor of sICH was higher baseline National Institutes of Health Stroke Scale score (adjusted OR, 1.09 $[95\%$ CI, 1.01–1.18]) in EVT alone group, and history of anticoagulant drugs (adjusted OR, 3.75 $[95\%$ CI, 1.07–13.06]), higher glucose level at hospital arrival (adjusted OR, 1.19 $[95\%$ CI, 1.03–1.38]), ${>}3$ passes of device (adjusted OR, 4.42 $[95\%$ CI, 1.36–14.32]) in combination-therapy group.  

CONCLUSIONS: In DIRECT-MT, independent predictors of sICH were baseline National Institutes of Health Stroke Scale score and glucose level at hospital arrival. Alteplase treatment did not increase the risk of HT, sICH, or PH after EVT. The independent predictor of sICH was different in EVT alone group and combination-therapy group.

emorrhagic transformation (HT) is a frequently encountered spontaneous complication of acute ischemic stroke, especially after thrombolytic treatment.1 HT can cause acute neurological deterioration and even minor HT can influence long-term functional outcome.2 According to the Heidelberg criteria and the European Cooperative Acute Stroke Study classification, HT can be classified as either asymptomatic intracranial hemorrhage (ICH) or symptomatic ICH (sICH).3,4 sICH is associated with a higher risk of clinical complications, prolonged hospitalization, death, and worse clinical outcome at discharge. Furthermore, patients with asymptomatic ICH also have a higher chance of worse clinical outcomes at discharge.5  

Several clinical, laboratory, and radiographic factors have been reported to be associated with the risk HT, especially sICH, after alteplase treatment. Moreover, studies of the risk factors associated with HT, sICH, and parenchymal hematoma (PH) after endovascular thrombectomy (EVT) are also reported. The evidence supporting these associations is variable, with some evidence consistently demonstrated across multiple studies and other evidence shown only in small, singlecenter cohort studies.6 Whether alteplase treatment increases the risk of HT, sICH, and PH after EVT is also unknown. Besides, compared with EVT alone, whether risk factors of sICH were different when bridging intravenous alteplase is administered, are still unclear. Therefore, the risk factors associated with HT, sICH, and PH in patients with acute ischemic stroke after EVT with and without alteplase treatment need to be addressed.  

In this study, we aimed to assess the clinical and imaging features associated with HT, sICH, and PH in patients with acute ischemic stroke after EVT, with and without intravenous thrombolysis. Besides, the risk factors of sICH in EVT alone group and combination-therapy group were also evaluated.  

# METHODS  

The authors declare that all supporting data are available within the article and its Supplemental Material. The DIRECT-MT (Direct Intraarterial Thrombectomy to Revascularize Acute Ischemic Stroke Patients with Large Vessel Occlusion Efficiently in Chinese Tertiary Hospitals: a Multicenter Randomized Clinical Trial) is a randomized, controlled, open-label, and noninferior trial of mechanical thrombectomy alone versus intravenous thrombolysis combined with mechanical thrombectomy in the Chinese population, in terms of patient outcome on the modified Rankin Scale score at 3 months. Patients with acute ischemic stroke from large vessel occlusion in the anterior circulation were randomly assigned in a 1:1 ratio to undergo EVT alone or EVT preceded by intravenous alteplase, at a dose of 0.9 mg per $\mathsf{kg}$ of body weight, administered within 4.5 hours after symptom onset. The DIRECT-MT design and outcomes have been previously presented in detail.7,8 Ethics approval was obtained from the local institutional review board and written informed consent was obtained from patients.  

The study is a secondary (cohort-base) analysis of RCT data and has been prepared in accordance with the STROBE guidelines. All patients with follow-up non-contrast computed tomography (NCCT), groin puncture, and large artery occlusion were eligible for inclusion in this study. Baseline NCCT and computed tomography angiography (CTA) were performed before EVT. Radiological follow-up with NCCT and CTA was performed at 24 to 72 hours, with an additional NCCT at 5 to 7 days in the DIRECT-MT. All imaging was evaluated at an independent imaging core laboratory by trained staff who were blinded to the treatment assignments.  

An independent imaging core laboratory detected HT and classified the HT as 1a (hemorrhagic infarction1, HI1); 1b (hemorrhagic infarction 2, HI2); 1c (parenchymal hematoma 1, PH1); 2 (parenchymal hematoma 2, PH2); 3a (parenchymal hematoma remote from infarcted brain tissue, rPH); 3b (intraventricular hemorrhage); 3c (subarachnoid hemorrhage); and 3d (subdural hemorrhage). sICH was evaluated by an adverseevent committee according to the Heidelberg classification based on the core laboratory assessment in combination with the clinical event.3 The clinical and imaging parameters expected to influence the HT, sICH, and PH (PH1/2) were analyzed. Clinical data included age, sex, modified Rankin Scale score of 1 or 2 before stroke onset, previous ischemic stroke, a history of atrial fibrillation, diabetes, and hypertension, cause of stroke (cardioembolism, intracranial atherosclerosis, ipsilateral extracranial obstruction, and undetermined), history of anticoagulant drugs and antiplatelet drugs, use of alteplase, the National Institutes of Health Stroke Scale score (NIHSS), systolic blood pressure and glucose level at hospital arrival, and time factors (time from stroke onset to randomization, from randomization to start of alteplase, from randomization to groin puncture, from randomization to revascularization, from stroke onset to revascularization, and from groin puncture to revascularization). EVT procedure including number of device passes $(>3)$ and degree of recanalization (eTICI≥2b) were also collected and analyzed.  

Imaging data included hyperdense artery sign and the Alberta Stroke Program Early CT Score (ASPECTS) on baseline NCCT, as well as occlusion location (internal carotid artery, M1 or M2 of the middle cerebral artery), clot burden score, and collateral score on baseline CTA.9 Imaging factors were also evaluated by the imaging core laboratory. The collateral score was classified according to Tan et $a^{|^{10}}$ as follows: grade 0, absence of collateral filling of the occluded area; grade 1A, collaterals filling ${<}25\%$ of the occluded area; grade 1B, collaterals filling ${\ge}25\%$ , but ${<}50\%$ of the occluded area; grade 2, collaterals filling ${\ge}50\%$ , but $<100\%$ of the occluded area; and grade 3, collaterals filling $100\%$ of the occluded area.  

# Statistical Analyses  

All analyses were performed using SAS software, version 9.2 (SAS Institute). The pure risk of HT, sICH, and PH in the EVT-alone and combination-therapy groups was calculated. The difference in the HT, sICH, and PH rates between the 2 groups was compared using $\times2$ test. Clinical and imaging characteristics of the patients were compared between the HT and non-HT groups, sICH and non-sICH groups, as well as PH and non-PH groups. Quantitative data with a normal distribution were analyzed and compared by the t test between groups (HT and non-HT, sICH and non-sICH, as well as PH and non-PH). Otherwise, the data were analyzed by Wilcoxon signed-rank test. The enumerated data were analyzed using the $\chi^{2}$ test or the Cochran-Mantel-Haenszel (CMH) test.  

We performed a univariable analysis of all the clinical and imaging factors with a $P{<}0.15$ . Variables with $P{<}0.15$ in univariate analysis were considered as candidates for the stepwise logistic regression analysis in which the entry level probability was set at 0.05 and the removal level at 0.1. Multivariable logistic analysis was used to test the clinical and imaging factors associated with HT, sICH, and PH, with non-HT, non-sICH and PH as the reference levels, respectively. Besides, multivariable logistic analysis was also used to evaluate the risk factors of sICH in EVT alone group and combination-therapy group, separately.  

# RESULTS  

From February 23, 2018, through July 2, 2019, a total of 656 patients were enrolled in the DIRECT-MT trial. Of the 656 patients, 17 patients (11 in the thrombectomyalone group and 6 in the combination-therapy group) were excluded as they had not undergone catheter angiography, and 6 patients (3 in the thrombectomy-alone group and 3 in the combination-therapy group) were excluded as they did not have large vessel occlusion in the anterior circulation. In the end, a total of 633 patients (313 in the thrombectomy-alone group and 320 in the combination-therapy group) were included in this analysis (Figure S1). Patients form 2 groups are combined into a single cohort of 633 patients, and the characteristics of this cohort are compared across HT, sICH, and PH in Table 1. The median age of the patients was 69 years (interquartile range, 61–76), and 359 patients $(56.7\%)$ were men. In all 633 patients, 261 $(41.2\%)$ patients had HT; 34 patients had sICH $(5.4\%)$ ; and 85 patients had PH ( $13.4\%)$ . There was no significant difference between the EVT-alone and combination-therapy groups for the risk of HT, sICH, and PH ${\bf\nabla}39.3\%$ versus $43.1\%$ $\chi^{2}\mathrm{=}0.96$ , $\mathrm{\it{P=0.33}}$ ; $4.5\%$ versus $6.3\%$ , $ \chi^{2}{=}0.98$ , $\widetilde{P=}0.32$ ; $10.9\%$ versus $16.3\%$ , $\chi^{2}{=}1.00$ , $\scriptstyle{\stackrel{\scriptstyle\sum}{=0.32}}.$ ). The number of HI1, HI2, PHI, PH2, $\mathsf{rPH},$ , intraventricular hemorrhage, subarachnoid hemorrhage, and subdural hemorrhage in each group can be found in Table S1.  

The results of the multivariate logistic analysis for all 633 patients revealed that higher glucose level at hospital arrival (adjusted odds ratio [OR], 1.18 $[95\%$ CI, $1.09-$ 1.27]), longer time from stroke onset to revascularization (adjusted OR, 1.01 $[95\%$ CI, 1.00–1.01]), hyperdense artery sign on baseline CT (adjusted OR, 1.82 $[95\%$ CI, 1.20–2.77]), and lower collateral score on baseline CTA (adjusted OR, 0.73 $[95\%$ CI, 0.57–0.93]) were significantly associated with HT (Table  2). The multivariable model showed that higher baseline NIHSS score (adjusted OR, 1.06 $[95\%$ CI, 1.01–1.12]) and higher glucose level at hospital arrival (adjusted OR, 1.14 $[95\%$ CI, 1.00–1.29]) were associated with sICH (Table 2). Higher glucose level at hospital arrival (adjusted OR, 1.15 $[95\%$ CI, 1.05–1.25]) was significantly associated with PH in multivariate logistic analysis (Table  2). The trend of the odds of HT over time from stroke onset to recanalization is shown in Figure S2. As time from stroke onset to recanalization increased to ${>}280$ minutes, the odds ratio of HT also increased.  

There was no significant difference regarding alteplase use between patients with HT and those without HT $(\chi^{2}{=}0.78$ , $_{P=0.38;}$ ), patients with sICH and those without sICH $(\chi^{2}=1.02$ , $\widetilde{P=}0.31$ ), as well as for patients with PH and those without PH $(\upchi^{2}=3.62$ $_{P=0.06}$ ). No association was found between use of alteplase and HT, sICH or PH both in univariate (HT: OR, 1.15 $[95\%$ CI, 0.84–1.58]; sICH: OR, 1.43 $[95\%$ CI, $0.71-2.89]$ ; PH: OR, 1.57 $[95\%$ CI, 0.98–2.50]) and multivariate logistic analysis.  

The independent predictor of sICH was higher baseline NIHSS score (adjusted OR, 1.09 $[95\%$ CI, 1.01–1.18]) in EVT alone group, and history of anticoagulant drugs (adjusted OR, 3.75 $[95\%$ CI, $1.07\mathrm{-}13.06\mathrm{]},$ ), higher glucose level at hospital arrival (adjusted OR, 1.19 $[95\%$ CI, $1.03\mathrm{-}1.38]_{\cdot}$ ), ${>}3$ passes of device (adjusted OR, 4.42 $[95\%$ CI, 1.36–14.32]) in combination-therapy group (Table S2).

Several studies have investigated the relationship between hyperglycemia at admission and HT, especially sICH after intravenous recombinant tissue-type plasminogen activator treatment or EVT. For patients treated with intravenous recombinant tissue-type plasminogen activator, hyperglycemia was independently associated with sICH.11,12 Similar results were found for the relationship between high glucose values at admission and sICH after thrombectomy.13,14 However, some studies found no link between glucose levels at admission and the incidence of sICH after thrombectomy.15,16 The inconsistency between hyperglycemia and sICH may be caused due to the different definitions of sICH in previous studies. Our results found that high blood glucose level at hospital arrival can be a predictive factor for HT, sICH, and PH for all patients included in DIRECT-MT trial, as well as an independent predictor of sICH in combination-therapy group.  

Time is one of the most important factors that influences the clinical outcome of acute ischemic stroke. In 2 studies, Raychev et al17,18 concluded that prolonged time from onset to groin puncture increases the risk of PH and basal ganglia hemorrhage after neurothrombectomy within 8 hours. Hao et $a^{|^{19}}$ enrolled 632 patients treated with endovascular treatment within 6 hours of stroke onset and found that symptoms onset to groin puncture time ${>}270$ minutes was associated with an increased risk of sICH. In our study, we found that longer time from stroke onset to revascularization was associated with HT; further, time form stroke onset to recanalization increased to ${>}280$ minutes, the odds ratio of HT also increased. However, other time, such as the time from stroke onset to randomization was not associated with HT in the multivariable analysis. This may be because patients included in the DIRECT-MT were all eligible for intravenous thrombolysis and mechanical thrombectomy within 4.5 hours after symptom onset; therefore, the influence of different time intervals from stroke onset to revascularization was not as obvious as that of the entire period.  

The imaging factors associated with HT was hyperdense artery sign on baseline NCCT and lower collateral score on baseline CTA in our study. Zou et al20 analyzed 182 consecutive patients treated with intravenous thrombolysis therapy and found that hyperdense middle cerebral artery sign was independently predictive of HT after thrombolytic therapy. Moreover, hyper dense cerebral artery sign has been shown to be one of the most important factors in the SEDAN score to assess the risk for sICH in patients with anterior and posterior circulation ischemic stroke treated with intravenous thrombolysis.21 Patients with hyperdense artery sign may provide a large substrate for HT, which had an increased volume of early reperfusion to ischemic tissue. Previous study showed that poor collateral circulation may increase the risk of HT and sICH.19 We also found that lower collateral score on baseline CTA was an independent predictor of sICH.22  

Previous studies have shown the relationship between ASPECTS and HT. Hao et $a|^{23}$ conducted a systematic review and meta- analysis and found that lower ASPECTS score on admission was one of the predictors of sICH after mechanical thrombectomy. However, in our multivariable analysis, ASPECTS were not associated with HT, sICH, and PH. We think that the bias in the ASPECTS of the enrolled patients is the main reason for the negative result. The patients enrolled in our study had relatively high ASPECTS $(7.96\pm2.16,$ , median, 9), although the ASPECTS were not a part of the inclusion criteria. Qian et $a|^{24}$ reported that admission NIHSS score significantly affected the prognosis of sICH. Consistent with previous studies, our analysis identified the baseline NIHSS score as an independent predictor of sICH in all patients and EVT group alone.

Several studies have reported a positive association between increasing systolic blood pressure and increased risk of sICH.25,26 However, other studies have reported no relationship between blood pressure and sICH.13,19 The results of our univariable and multivariable analyses showed that there was no significant association between higher systolic blood pressure at hospital arrival and either HT sICH or PH. The reason for this may be that the systolic blood pressure at admission was influenced by many factors, and the clinical and imaging factors at baseline also influenced each other. Another reason may be lower systolic blood pressure at hospital arrival of the patients included in our study.  

Intravenous thrombolytic therapy with alteplase is the mainstay of treatment for acute ischemic stroke when administered within 4.5 hours of symptom onset.27 Despite its efficacy, the most feared complication of intravenous alteplase is HT, particularly sICH. Late administration of intravenous alteplase is associated with a higher risk of large intracerebral hemorrhages and death. Multiple randomized controlled studies have shown that EVT has superior efficacy and safety without showing an increase incidence of clinically relevant HT in comparison to exclusive conservative therapy.28 However, some studies have concluded that intravenous alteplase increases the risk of PH after neurothrombectomy.15 Many studies have suggested that pretreatment with intravenous alteplase does not result in higher rates of sICH after acute stroke endovascular therapy.29,30 In our study, no association was found between use of alteplase and HT, sICH or PH both in the univariate and multivariate logistic analyses. But the independent predictor of sICH was different in EVT alone group and combination-therapy group.  

In a multicenter analysis of 1122 patients undergoing EVT within 8 hours of symptom onset, TICI 2b or 3 reperfusion was independently associated with lower rates of ICH.31 However, eTICI, no. $(\%)\geq2\mathrm{b}$ was not an independent predictor of HT, sICH, or PH in our study. The main reason for the inconsistency between our results and those reported in the literature may be the difference in the included patients. Previously study concluded that ${>}3$ passes of stent retriever was an increased risk of PH and worse clinical outcome.32 Our study also found that ${>}3$ passes of device was the independent risk factor of sICH in combination-therapy group.  

# Limitations  

Our study has some limitations. First, although 261 patients had HT, only 34 were classified as sICH by the adverse-event committee. This small number of sICH patients may have affected the results of the multivariable analysis. Second, in addition to baseline blood pressure, blood pressure during and after thrombectomy has also been shown to be associated with a worse functional outcome and sICH.33,34 Unfortunately, researchers were not required to record blood pressure during and after thrombectomy in the DIRECT-MT trial. Thus, further randomized studies are needed to evaluate the association of blood pressure during the after thrombectomy with sICH, as well as the effect of EVT. Third, although ASPECTS, or blood pressure were not a part of inclusion criteria, the patients enrolled in our study had high ASPECTS, favorable collateral, and lower systolic blood pressure at hospital arrival; therefore, the patients may not have been well represented. Hence, we suggest that the results should be carefully interpreted, because HT and sICH may have been influenced by these factors.  

# Conclusions  

This study shows that higher baseline NIHSS score and higher glucose level at hospital arrival were associated with sICH in patients with acute ischemic stroke after EVT. Alteplase treatment did not increase the risk of HT, sICH, or PH after EVT. The independent predictor of sICH was different in EVT alone group and combinationtherapy group.
