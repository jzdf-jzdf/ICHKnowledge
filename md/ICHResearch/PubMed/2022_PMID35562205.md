# ABSTRACT  

Purpose: CN-105 is an IV, apolipoprotein E– mimetic pentapeptide. Preclinical studies have reported that CN-105 effectively down-regulates neuroinflammatory responses in microglia and mitigates neuronal excitotoxicity following acute brain injury. The CN105 Phase I and II trials that have been done in the United States have demonstrated that CN-105 was well tolerated in US participants. Thus, the main objective of the present Phase I study was to investigate the tolerability and pharmacokinetic (PK) profiles of CN105 in healthy Chinese participants.  

Methods: This randomized, double-blind, placebocontrolled, dose-escalation study was performed in healthy participants using sequential 30-minute IV administration of single and multiple doses of CN105 (four times daily for 13 doses). Forty volunteers were randomly assigned, in an 8:2 ratio, to one of four dosing groups, receiving either CN-105 (0.03, 0.1, 0.3, or $1~\mathrm{mg/kg)}$ , or placebo. Serial blood samples were collected for the measurement of plasma concentrations of CN-105. Tolerability was also assessed.  

Findings: After single-dose administration, the plasma CN-105 concentration rapidly reached the peak by the end of infusion. The mean elimination halflife of CN-105 ranged from 2.3 to 3.6 hours. During single- and multiple-dosing paradigms, exposure to  

CN-105 (AUC) exhibited linear dependency on dose. Steady state was reached by the fourth dose, with minimal accumulation. The PK properties of CN105 with single and multiple dosing were comparable to those observed in US participants. CN-105 was generally well tolerated in Chinese participants. A total of 13 adverse events were reported in $30\%$ of subjects (12/40) at the $0.03~\mathrm{mg/kg}$ (6/8), $0.1~\mathrm{mg/kg}$ (1/8), 0.3 $\mathrm{mg/kg}$ (2/8), $1~\mathrm{mg/kg}$ (0/8) doses and with placebo (3/8). All adverse events were mild or moderate in severity and self-limited, with no dose relationship observed.  

Implications: CN-105 was well tolerated in these healthy Chinese participants at doses of 0.1 to 1 $\mathrm{mg/kg}$ with single and multiple IV administrations. The PK characteristics of CN-105 were comparable among Chinese and Western subjects. A Phase II study in patients with intracranial hemorrhage is being planned in China. # INTRODUCTION  

The global burden of stroke continues to increase as a function of the aging population. In high-income countries, stroke represents the most frequent cause of permanent disability, the second most frequent cause of death in people aged ${>}60$ years, and the second most common cause of dementia.1 In China, stroke remains a leading cause of death and disability.2 Thus, there is a pressing need for new treatments for stroke, especially for intracranial hemorrhage (ICH), which is associated with higher mortality and poorer outcomes than ischemic stroke. Many neuroprotective strategies have been attempted to preserve brain tissue and improve function in the setting of acute ischemia. However, numerous neuroprotective drugs have failed to demonstrate efficacy in stroke, making the search for new treatments imperative.3–5  

CN-105 is a pentapeptide designed to mimic the receptor-binding region of apolipoprotein E. It interacts with low-density lipoprotein receptor–related protein 1 and other low-density lipoprotein receptor– family receptors, which dampens microglial activation and subsequent neuroinflammation.6–10 CN-105 has exhibited substantial neuroprotective activity in several preclinical models of acute and chronic neuronal injuries.11–15 In previous clinical studies, ${>}85$ subjects have received CN-105, including 48 subjects in a Phase I study.16,17  

The intrinsic characteristics of the drug recipient and extrinsic characteristics associated with environment and culture may affect drug disposition and/or response in different races. The previous Phase I trial of CN-105 in the United States enrolled only 1 participant of Asian ethnicity. Thus, it remained unclear whether the pharmacokinetic (PK) and tolerability profiles of CN-105 would differ between US participants and those in an Asian population. Moreover, the dose dependency of the PK parameters of CN-105 with the clinically relevant dosing regimen (four times daily for 13 infused doses, each separated by 6 hours) had not been established. As such, Phase I single and multiple ascending–dose studies of CN-105 we conducted in healthy Chinese volunteers.  



# Subjects and Methods  

The study was performed at Beijing Tiantan Hospital (Beijing, China) from December 23, 2020, to April 28, 2021. The protocol was approved by the National Medical Products Administration under an investigational new drug application and by the institutional review board of Beijing Tiantan Hospital, which was certified by the Association for the Accreditation of Human Research Protection Programs. The study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and the International Council for Harmonisation of Technical Requirements for Pharmaceuticals for Human Use guideline for Good Clinical Practice.  

All procedures involving the participants were performed in accordance with the ethics standards of the institutional research committee and with the 1964 Declaration of Helsinki and its amendments or comparable ethics standards (see Supplemental Table.  



# Study Design  

In this Phase I, single-center, randomized, doubleblind, placebo-controlled, dose-escalation study, participants were sequentially assigned, in a 8:2 randomization ratio, to receive IV administrations of CN-105 or matching vehicle, either as a single ascending dose (SAD) or as multiple ascending doses (MAD) for a total of 13 doses separated by 6-hour intervals. In total, four groups $(\mathrm{N}=40$ ) were enrolled.  

Subjects who provided written informed consent were screened within 14 days prior to study drug administration, using physical examination, clinical laboratory tests, and collection of medical history, vital sign measurements, and ECG. Subjects who fully met the inclusion criteria were admitted to the Phase I Unit of Beijing Tiantan Hospital on the day prior to the first dosing day and were confined to the Clinical Research Unit until 24 hours after the administration of the last study dose. Blood samples were collected for PK analysis, and tolerability assessments were performed. The study duration was 6 days for each participant, excluding the screening session.  



# Study Population  

Healthy Chinese men and women aged 18 to 45 years, with a body mass index of 18 to $28~\mathrm{kg}/\mathrm{m}^{2}$ and a body weight of at least $50\mathrm{kg}$ (men) or $45~\mathrm{kg}$ (women), were recruited into the study. Subjects who met any of the following criteria were excluded from the study: (1)  

evidence or a history of clinically significant disease or allergic disorder; (2) an abnormal result on physical examination, ECG, or laboratory testing during the screening visit; (3) donation of ${>}400\mathrm{mL}$ of blood within 3 months prior to the first study dose; (4) a history or evidence of drug or alcohol abuse within 1 year prior to the first study dose; (5) the receipt of any medication $<5$ half-lives before the administration of the first study dose; and/or (6) ongoing pregnancy or breast-feeding in women.  

Participants were required to avoid intense physical exercise during the study. Standardized meals and plain water were provided to the participants during their stay. Smoking and the consumption of alcohol or tea, coffee, or other caffeinated beverages were not allowed during the study.  



# Treatments  

Four dose-escalation groups (0.03, 0.1, 0.3, and 1 $\mathrm{mg/kg)}$ and a placebo group were set up in this study. Single dosing was followed by a multiple-dose regimen to determine the linear PK properties of CN-105 in humans.  

The investigational medication (white/off-white lyophilized powder) was dissolved, by nonblinded research nurses, in $0.9\%$ saline to the appropriate concentration and administered intravenously, by blinded research nurses, over 30 minutes at each dosing point. SAS Proc Plan version 9.4 (SAS Institute Inc, Cary, North Carolina) was used to generate a randomization table with a block size of 5. One dosing group corresponded to 2 blocks (1 of each block corresponded to the placebo, and the other 4, to CN105). The first SAD was given on day 0. After 24 hours, the MAD regimen was begun, and 13 doses of the investigational medication were administered at intervals of 6 hours each. The last dose was administered on the morning of day 4. Participants were discharged from the trial facility at 24 hours after the last study dose administration.  



# Pharmacokinetic Assessments  

The SAD pharmacokinetic assessments were performed at 0.5 hour predose and at 0.25, 0.5, 0.75, 1, 2, 3, 4, 8, 12, and 24 hours postdose. For MAD, blood samples were collected at 5 minutes prior to start of the fifth dose on day 2 and ninth dose on day 3. Blood samples were also collected at 0.5 hour predose and at  

0.25, 0.5, 0.75, 1, 2, 3, 4, 8, 12, and 24 hours after the last dose administration, on day 4.  

Blood samples were collected into a $\mathrm{K}_{2}$ EDTA tube preinjected with $100~\times$ Halt protease inhibitor (Thermo Fisher Scientific, Waltham, Massachusetts), reaching a final concentration of $1\%$ Halt inhibitor. The plasma samples were then stored at $-70^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$ for future analysis. The analytical protocol for CN-105 was established by United-Power Pharmatech (Beijing, China). Briefly, either CN-105 or isotopically labeled CN-105 (CN-105iso: $\mathrm{Ac-VSR[^{15}N_{4}}$ ， $^{13}\mathrm{C}_{6}]\mathrm{RR-NH}_{2})$ was extracted from plasma samples using an Oasis WCX 96-well $\mu$ Elution solid phase extraction plate (Waters, Milford, Massachusetts), with $2\mathrm{mg}$ of sorbent per well. LC-MS/MS analysis was performed on an ExionLC AD HPLC system (AB Sciex, Foster City, California) with an Xbridge BEH $\mathrm{C}_{18}$ column $(2.5\mathrm{mm}$ internal diameterx, $4.6\times75\mathrm{mm}$ ; Waters), followed by a Triple Quad $6500+$ mass spectrometer (AB Sciex). The lower and upper limits of quantitation of CN-105 were 1.00 and $1000\mathrm{ng/mL}$ , respectively, with precision values of $<15\%$ for all non–lower limit of quantitation samples and $<20\%$ for lower limits of quantitation.  



# Tolerability Assessment  

Tolerability was assessed using the frequency and severity of adverse events (AEs), defined as abnormalities on vital sign assessment, clinical laboratory testing (hematology, biochemistry, urinalysis), and/or ECG. The tolerability follow-up visit was scheduled for 24 hours after the administration of the last dose. AEs were monitored throughout the study.  



# Statistical Analysis  

PK analysis was performed using noncompartmental analysis with Phoenix WinNonlin software version 8.3 (Pharsight Corp, Mountain View, California). Statistical analysis was performed using SAS software version 9.4 (SAS Institute Inc).  

The following two PK analysis sets were defined to address the objectives of the PK analysis: (1) the PK concentration set included randomized subjects who received at least one dose of the study drug and had at least one measurable postdose plasma concentration available (this dataset was used to summarize the concentration data); (2) the PK parameter set included randomized subjects who received at least one dose of study drug and had at least one obtainable PK parameter (this dataset was used to summarize the PK parameters).

# RESULTS Demographic Characteristics  

A total of 40 subjects were enrolled into this study. All of the subjects completed the study per protocol. Eight subjects in the placebo group were excluded from the PK concentration set and PK parameter set after data unblinding. One data point from a subject receiving study drug was excluded from the PK concentration set and the PK parameter set, because the time of blood collection was 2 minutes beyond the preset time window. The demographic and clinical characteristics of the subjects by dose group at baseline are presented in Table I. The mean age of all of the subjects was 30.5 years (range, 24.0–35.3 years), and $77.5\%$ of the participants were men. Baseline characteristics were similar among groups.  



# PK Results SAD  

Table II shows the pharmacokinetic properties after a single dose of CN-105. Figure 1 shows the mean concentration–time profile of CN-105 after 30- minute infusion, by dose group. After IV infusion for 30 minutes, $\mathrm{CN}{-}105\mathrm{C}_{\mathrm{max}}$ reached 151.4 to 3780.0 $\mathrm{ng/mL}$ directly by the end of administration. The concentration of CN-105 declined with time in a multiphase pattern in the semilogarithmic graph. The mean $\mathrm{t_{1/2}}$ was 2.3 to 3.6 hours. The clearance rate (CL) of CN-105 was 6.5 to $7.5~\mathrm{L}/\mathrm{h}$ , and the volume of distribution was 21.0 to $38.3\mathrm{~L~}$ . The exposure parameters of CN-105 (ie, $\mathrm{{C}_{\mathrm{{max}}}}$ , $\mathrm{AUC}_{\mathrm{0-t}}$ , and $\operatorname{AUC}_{0-\infty}$ ) were increased in a dose-proportional manner. The slope of $\mathrm{log_{10}C_{\mathrm{max}}}$ was 0.9518 $95\%$ CI, 0.8992– 1.0043), of $\mathrm{log_{10}AUC_{0-t}}$ was 0.9623 $95\%$ CI, 0.9114– 1.0132) and of $\mathrm{log_{10}AUC_{0-\infty}}$ was 0.9576 $95\%$ CI, 0.9072–1.0080) $(\boldsymbol{p}<0.0001)$ .

# Tolerability Results  

Single- and multiple-dose IV administration of CN105, at doses ranging from 0.03 to $1.0\mathrm{mg/kg}$ , was well tolerated in these healthy Chinese subjects. A total of 13 AEs were reported in 12 subjects $\mathrm{0.03mg/kg},6$ of 8 subjects; $0.1\mathrm{mg/kg},1$ of 8; $0.3\mathrm{mg}/\mathrm{kg},2$ of 8; $1\mathrm{mg/kg},$ 0 of 8; placebo, 3 of 8). All reported AEs were mild or moderate in severity. Twelve AEs, corresponding to 12 subjects, were deemed as possibly related to the investigational drug. One case was considered as possibly related and was due to high-intensity physical activities prior to enrollment. The most common AE was increased serum bilirubin (6 of 12 subjects) and was not dose dependent. Except for the few abnormal laboratory values and ECG abnormalities that were reported as AEs, there were no clinically significant physiologic abnormalities or changes in laboratory test values (Table V).

# DISCUSSION  

This study investigated the tolerability and PK properties of CN-105 with single and 6-hourly repeated IV administration in healthy Chinese participants.  

Consistent with findings from previous studies, there were no major safety signals identified in this study. The prevalence of AEs was $30\%$ in this study compared with $47\%$ in the US study. The AEs in the present study mostly pertained to laboratory abnormalities that were not clinically significant. One subject in the $0.03\mathrm{mg/kg}$ group experienced transient headache compared to 2 of 36 subjects reported in the corresponding US trial. One subject developed rash that was not aggravated with repeated dosing. None of the 40 enrolled participants had concerning abnormalities on 12-lead ECG.  

One intrinsic shortcoming of peptide-based therapeutics is a lack of stability in vivo, which often manifested as a short serum half-life.18,19 In the present study, the clearance half-lives were 2.3 to 3.6 hours and 3.0 to 4.6 hours with SAD and MAD, respectively; these values are similar to those from the US trial and significantly greater than those in rodents $\mathord{\left[\sim\infty\right.}30$ minutes).20 Based on these findings, the 13-dose regimen with 6-hour interval is appropriate for producing a steady drug exposure as early as the first day of dosing. Based on preclinical pharmacodynamic findings in rodent ICH models, a dose-dependent therapeutic effect was found with CN105, with significantly reduced ipsilateral brain edema and improved neurologic function found at a dose of $0.2~\mathrm{mg/kg.}^{21}$ 1 In the PK study in Sprague-Dawley rats, approximate exposure levels (AUC) of 125 and 504 $\mathrm{h\cdotng/mL}$ were reported with single CN-105 doses of 0.1 and $0.3~\mathrm{mg/kg.}^{2}$ 1 Considering the results from the Phase I trial of CN-105 from the United States, the initial dose was set at $0.03\mathrm{mg/kg}$ , with which there was an effective exposure of $280\mathrm{h\cdotng/mL}$ in US volunteers, roughly corresponding to the exposure required for significant efficacy in the rodent model. The 14-day No Observed Adverse Effect Leve (NOAEL) of CN-105 in Sprague-Dawley rats was $30\mathrm{mg/kg/d}$ , equivalent to $4.8\mathrm{mg/kg/d}$ in humans. A dose range of 0.03 to 1 $\mathrm{mg/kg}$ would give rise to steady-state exposure values of 336.8 to $8620.9\mathrm{~h~}\cdot\mathrm{~ng/mL}$ , which are likely to produce desirable therapeutic outcomes according to the findings from preclinical efficacy studies in animal models.20  

CN-105 did not show significant accumulation in plasma with repeated IV administration at 6- hour intervals. Findings from a preclinical study of the distribution and excretion of $\mathrm{CN}{-}105^{20}$ in mice suggested that the elimination of CN-105 in plasma was two-phase, with a peak at 5 minutes and a slow elimination after 4 hours. CN-105 was metabolized into amino acids in blood and was excreted in urine in the vast majority. The distribution, excretion, and metabolism of CN-105 were not analyzed in the present study. However, a predictable linear PK profile, dose proportionality, and minimal accumulation indicate a favorable clinical pharmacologic profile applicable to disease scenarios.

To address the question of interethnic differences, the PK properties of CN-105 were compared between the subjects in the present study and those reported in the US participants, with similarity found between the two populations (Table IV). Moreover, the pharmacokinetic parameters with respect to dose of CN105 were linear with SAD and MAD administration in all dose-escalation groups. The consistency of the pharmacokinetic parameters of CN-105 between the Asian and Western populations support further clinical development of CN-105 simultaneously in Asian and US participants.  

There are two possible placebo options for CN105: $0.9\%$ saline (solvent) or mannitol (excipient). A significant amount of mannitol is required for adequate lyophilization of the CN-105 drug product, mainly due to the hygroscopic nature of the triple-arginine moiety. In the present study, the dose range of CN-105 (0.03– $1~\mathrm{mg/kg)}$ corresponded to a mannitol dose range of 0.48 to $16~\mathrm{mg/kg}$ —far below the dose range of 500 to $2000\mathrm{mg/kg}$ typically administered for the mitigation of edema and control of intracranial pressure. Mannitol involved in the present study was thus considered to be of little biological effect. Normal saline was used as the placebo in this trial for consistency with all previous and ongoing clinical trials of CN-105 in the United States and Singapore.  

The multifaceted role of apolipoprotein E in the CNS has been well established. CN-105, as a mimetic peptide of apolipoprotein E, has been reported to have neuroprotective effects in animal models of ICH, ischemic stroke, and subarachnoid hemorrhage.21–26 The present study and early clinical trials from the United States have demonstrated adequate tolerability and a broad therapeutic window of CN-105, which fully warrant further evaluation of the therapeutic potential CN-105 in ICH and other acute brain injuries.  



# CONCLUSIONS  

The tolerability and PK characteristics of CN-105 in these Chinese participants were consistent with those in US subjects. IV administration of CN-105 0.03 to 1 $\mathrm{mg/kg}$ for 13 doses, administered at 6-hour intervals, was well tolerated and covers the effective doses that could be recommended for future proof-of-concept studies.
