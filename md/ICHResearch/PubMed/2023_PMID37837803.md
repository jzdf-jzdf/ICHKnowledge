# ABSTRACT  

Objectives: A post-hoc analysis of the ICH Deferoxamine (i-DEF) trial was performed to examine any associations pre-ICH statin use may have with ICH volume, PHE volume, and clinical outcomes.   
Materials and methods: Baseline characteristics were assessed. Various ICH and PHE parameters were measured via a quantitative, semi-automated method at baseline and follow-up CT scans 72-96 h later. A multivariable logistic regression model was created, adjusting for the variables that were significantly different on univariable analyses $(p<0.05)$ , to assess any associations between pre-ICH statin use and measures of ICH and PHE, as well asgoodclinicaloutcome $(\mathrm{mRS}\leq2)$ ,at90and180days.   
Results: 262 of 291 i-DEF participants had complete data available for analysis. 69 $(26.3~\%)$ used statins prior to ICH onset. Pre-ICH statin users had higher prevalences of hypertension, diabetes, and prior ischemic stroke; higher concomitant use of antihypertensives and antiplatelets; and higher blood glucose level at baseline. On univariable analyses, pre-ICH statin users had smaller baseline ICH volume and PHE volume on repeat scan, as well as smaller changes in relative PHE (rPHE) volume and edema extension distance (EED) between the baseline and repeat scans. In the multivariable analysis, none of the ICH and PHE measures or good clinical outcome was significantly associated with pre-ICH statin use.   
Conclusion: Pre-ICH statin use was not associated with measures of ICH or PHE, their growth, or clinical outcomes. These findings do not lend support to either overall protective or deleterious effects from statin use before or after ICH.  

# Introduction  

Besides lowering cholesterol level via inhibition of hydroxymethylglutaryl-CoA (HMG-CoA) reductase, statins have other putative pleiotropic effects. Several preclinical studies suggest that statins exert neuroprotective effects after intracerebral hemorrhage (ICH) by reducing brain edema, accelerating hematoma clearance, and improving neurologic function.2-6 These benefits have been atributed to statin's effects on increased neurogenesis, decreased endothelial inflammatory response and inflammatory cell infiltration in the hematomal and perihematomal brain tissues, increased hematoma reabsorption, protection and maintenance of the blood-brain barrier, and various pathways involved in the mechanisms of ICH-induced secondary brain injury.5.7-12 Likewise, clinical studies suggest potential benefits of statins in ICH patients. Pre-ICH statin use has been associated with smaller perihematomal edema (PHE) volumes on admission brain computed tomography (CT) scans, lower mortality, and improved recovery.13,14  

However, the effects of statins in ICH are inconsistent across various studies.15 While some studies have reported that pre-ICH statin use has been associated with a $69\%$ decrease in baseline infratentorial ICH volume, others have reported increased hematoma volume on admission scan and its progresson on follow-up scans with statin use.16.17 In a post-hoc analysis of the ICH ADAPT study, statin-treated patients had similar median baseline ICH volumes but larger median PHE volumes compared to non-treated patients; statin use, along with ICH volume, was independently associated with increased PHE volume.18 The inconsistent effects of statins in ICH may be attributed to variabilities in study design, definitions used for PHE, timing of imaging scans, and extent of accounting for various confounding variables. In addition, most of these studies were single center, had a relatively small number of patients, and were retrospective or observational. Given conflicting evidence of the statin's association with outcomes in ICH, we undertook this study to examine any associations pre-ICH statin use may have with baseline volumes of ICH and PHE, their growth on 72-96 h follow-up scans, and functional outcomes among participants in the ICH Deferoxamine (i-DEF) trial.19  

# Methods  

# Study design and participants  

The i-DEF trial was a multicenter, randomized, placebo-controlled, double-blind, futility-design, phase 2 clinical trial in the United States and Canada. The trial was funded by the National Institute of Neurological Disorders and Stroke (U01 NS074425) and approved by the US Food and Drug Administration (IND #77306) and Health Canada (CTA $\#160713)$ . Details of study design, methods, and results have been previously published.19 Briefly, patients aged 18-80 years with spontaneous supratentorial ICH were recruited and randomly assigned at 1:1 ratio to receive deferoxamine (DFO) ( $32~\mathrm{mg/kg}$ per day) or placebo (saline) infusions for 3 consecutive days within $24\mathrm{~h~}$ of ICH onset. Brain CT scans were performed at baseline and within $24\mathrm{~h~}$ of completion of the study drug infusion (i.e. after 72 to $96\mathrm{{h}}$ 0. The modified Rankin Scale (mRS) was assessed at 90 and 180 days. All assessments were done by qualified investigators who were certified in mRS administration and masked to treatment assignment. The trial was approved by the institutional review board at each participating site, and written informed consent was obtained from each participant or legally authorized representative according to local regulations.  

A total of 294 patients were randomized in the i-DEF trial. However, the study infusions were initiated in only 291 participants. Key randomization covariates were clinical site, baseline ICH score ( ${\bf\zeta}_{<3}$ Vs. ${\geq}3)$ ,ICH-onset-to-treatment time( $\leq12$ h vs. ${>}12\mathrm{h}$ 0,warfarin use at ICH onset, National Institute of Health Stroke Scale (NIHSS) $\leq10$ Vs. ${>}10.$ ， and ICH volume ( ${\bf\beta}.<10\mathrm{mL}$ Vs. ${\geq}10\mathrm{mL}$ at presentation.We defined preICH statin use as ongoing use of a statin at the time of ICH onset - irrespective of the dose, agent, or indication - based on patient or family report. Compliance with statin was not assessed. For the purposes of this analysis, only participants who had complete data were included. Out of the 291 participants who had study infusions initiated, 10 (2 from preICH statin use group; 8 in no pre-ICH statin use group) were excluded because post-infusion CT scans were not performed; 5 (1 in pre-ICH statin use group; 4 in no pre-ICH statin use group), for missing mRS assessments at 90 days; 12 (in no pre-ICH statin use group only), for missing mRS at 180 days; and 2 (in no pre-ICH statin use group only), for not having post-infusion EED measured.  

# Imaging analyses  

The images were sent to the i-DEF core imaging laboratory (Beth  

Israel Deaconess Medical Center, Boston, MA, USA) for central review and volumetric measurements. Experienced assessors, who were masked to clinical data and treatment assignment, determined the location of the ICH and performed quantitative measurements of parenchymal hematoma, intraventricular hemorrhage (IVH), and PHE volumes using an imaging analysis software (Analyze 11.o Visualization and Analysis Software for Medical Imaging; AnalyzeDirect, Overland Park, KS,USA). Details of the methodologies used for these measurements and inter- and intra-rater reliability have been previously published.20-22 The sums of the hematoma and IVH volumes were added to calculate the total ICH volume. The following parameters of PHE were calculated: absolute PHE (aPHE) defined as the sum total volume of pixels of the perihematomal lesion; relative PHE (rPHE), the ratio of aPHE to hematoma lesion; and edema extension distance (EED), the difference between the radius of a sphere equal to the aPHE volume and the radius of a sphere equal to the ICH volume alone.23  

# Statistical analyses  

Although the primary analysis for the i-DEF trial was based on the intent-to-treat population, all analyses in the current study were performed according to treatment received (i.e., as treated). Continuous variables were summarized with the mean and standard deviation (SD) for normally distributed variables, and median and interquartile range (IQR) for variables not following a normal distribution. Categorical variables were reported as absolute counts and percent of total. In the univariable analyses, we used Student's t-test and Wilcoxon rank-sum test to compare normally and non-normally distributed continuous variables, respectively. For univariable comparisons of categorical variables, the Chi square test was used. We built a multivariable logistic regression model with various measures of ICH and PHE volumes, as well as good clinical outcome (defined as mRS ${\le}2\$ )at 90 and 180 days, as the primary outcomes of interest. A multivariable logistic regression model was created, adjusting for the variables that were significantly different $(p<0.05)$ on the univariable analysis. To account for multiple testing, the significance threshold for the multivariable analyses was set to 0.006 using the Bonferroni correction. Statistical analyses were performed using STATA 13 (College Station, TX, USA) and $\mathbf{JMP}(\widehat{\mathbf{R}})$ Pro 15.0.0.

# Discussion  

We found no significant associations between pre-ICH statin use and baseline hematoma or PHE volumes, as well as their growth within $72–96\mathrm{h}$ , after adjustment for confounding variables among participants in the i-DEF trial. We also did not find any significant association between pre-ICH statin use and long-term good clinical outcome. Our findings add to existing and conflicting literature on the relationship between statin use and ICH and do not lend support to either overall protective or deleterious effects from statin use during the acute phase of ICH.13-17

In our study, those with pre-ICH statin use were older and had higher rates of other cardiovascular comorbidities, such as hypertension, prior ischemic stroke, and diabetes. Those with pre-ICH statin use were also more likely to be on antihypertensive and antiplatelet medications. This likely represents the association between older age and increased prevalence of cardiovascular risk factors, previously noted in the literature.24 The higher prevalence of diabetes in those with pre-ICH statin use also likely explains the elevated blood glucose level in the same group.  

The relationship between statin use and PHE has been inconsistent in the literature with some studies showing mechanistic and clinical benefits of statins in reducing PHE, while others showed no significant relationship.,1325 It is posible that this inconsistency may be econdary tointer-rater variability in identification and assessment of PHE,as well as varying methods of measurement, timing of imaging, and analytical adjustments for confounding factors. In contrast, our analysis was conducted within the context of a prospective trial with standardized imaging and clinical assessment procedures by experienced and blinded assessors. In addition, analyzing participants in a randomized controlled trial likely reduces the variability in patient health-seeking behavior and physician perceptions of patient health relative to hospital cohort observational studies, thereby minimizing the potential for healthy user bias.

Pre-ICH statin use was associated with smaller baseline hematoma volume on univariable analysis. However, this association was not shown to be significant in adjusted multivariable analysis. While it only trended towards significance, greater baseline rPHE (the ratio of aPHE to hematoma volume) among those with pre-ICH statin use is likely the result of smaller baseline hematoma volumes in these patients. Mechanistically, the increase in hematoma growth with statin use is in line with the purported effects of statins on the coagulation pathway.26 There were also marginal reductions in EED and aPHE growth on follow-up scans among pre-ICH statin users. These findings are difficult to reconcile with the noted simultaneous increase in hematoma volume from a pathophysiological standpoint and may be related to artifacts of our small numbers. Alternatively, it is possible that continued imaging past the $96~\mathrm{{h}}$ may have detected later increases in PHE volume measures, as it has been previously reported that PHE continues to increase until about 14 days after the onset of ICH.27 Unfortunately, the i-DEF trial protocol did no require scans beyond day 4 to fully assess the peak PHE.  

Approximately half of the patients in both pre-ICH statin use and no pre-ICH statin use groups received DFO in the i-DEF trial. Previous clinical studies reported conflicting results regarding the effect of DFO on PHE. One study reported that DFO reduced the growth of rPHE and slowed the resolution of the hematoma.28 We found no significant difference in our measures between DFO-treated and placebo groups in this analysis. Furthermore, treatment with DFO had no effect on rPHE growth or hematoma resolution in the i-DEF trial.19

In one study, prior statin users withserum total cholesterollevels $\leq150\mathrm{mg/dL}$ hadgreaterbaseline ICH volume and worse 30-day mRs.29 The Helsinki ICH study, a large single-center observational registry of 964 ICH patients, found that while pre-ICH use of statins did not affect clinical outcome, lower LDL-C levels were associated with higher in-hospital mortality.30 Our post-hoc analysis may not have been powered appropriately to detect the effect of prior statin use in ICH, and the difference in sample sizes, potentially exacerbated by those excluded due to incomplete follow-up data, between the pre-ICH statin and no pre-ICH statin groups may have further impaired the study's ability to discern any significant effect of statins on ICH or introduced bias. There could also have been additional confounders, beyond the variables that were significantly different in the univariable analyses, that we did not account for. However, the strengths our study lie in the vigorous methodology of the main trial and its standardized assessments within a prespecified protocol, and central blinded analyses of all imaging data by experienced raters.  

# Conclusion  

In this post-hoc analysis of a randomized controlled trial with standardized imaging and functional assessments, we did not find any significant associations between statin use at the time of ICH onset and various measures of hematoma andperihematomaledema,as well as clinical outcome. This finding adds to the existing conflicting literature on the relationship between statin use and ICH.
