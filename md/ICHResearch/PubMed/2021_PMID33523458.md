# Abstract  

Background: Mechanical thrombectomy (MT) is effective in treating ischemic strokes due to large vessel occlusion. However, the risk-benefit ratio of intravenous thrombolysis (IVT) prior to MT is still unclear. Aim of the study was to provide a pooled analysis of only randomized controlled trials (RCTs) comparing direct MT (dMT) vs bridging treatment $(|\mathsf{VT}+\mathsf{MT}.$ ).  

Methods: PubMed, EMBASE and Cochrane Central were searched only for RCTs comparing $\scriptstyle|\vee\top+\mathsf{MT}$ vs dMT in ischemic stroke patients. Primary endpoint was functional independence at 90 days $(\mathsf{m}\mathsf{R}\mathsf{S}<3)$ ), while secondary endpoints were represented by successful recanalization $(\mathsf{TICI}>2a)$ ), mortality at 90 days and symptomatic intracranial hemorrhage (slCH). Odds ratios for endpoints were pooled with meta-analysis and compared between reperfusion strategies.  

Results: The pooled analysis comprised 5 studies (n. patients $=1657.$ . The rates for the primary endpoint were $39\%$ and $34.5\%$ for dMT and $\scriptstyle|\vee\top+\mathsf{MT}$ , respectively (OR 1.06; $95\%\mathsf{C}|0.80\smallskip1.40_{.}$ ). For the secondary endpoints, we did not observe significant differences between groups, even if the rate of successful recanalization was higher in $\scriptstyle|\vee\top+\mathsf{MT}$ treated patients (OR: 0.58; $95\%\mathrm{C}$ 0.26-1.30;Pneterogenity 0.002), without a significant increase in sICH rates $(4.3\%$ vs $5.5\%$ ; OR: 0.96; $95\%\mathrm{C}$ 0.43-2.13;Pheterogenity $=0.26.$ ). Finally, mortality rates were $19.8\%$ and $15.9\%$ for dMT and $\mathsf{IVT+MT}$ , respectively.  

Conclusions: In this meta-analysis including only RCTs, dMT and bridging treatment were substantially equivalent for good functional outcome. $\scriptstyle|\vee\top+\mathsf{MT}$ was associated to higher rates of successful recanalization, even if not significant. Therefore, further adequately powered RCTs comparing dMT vs $\scriptstyle|\vee\top+\mathsf{MT}$ are warranted.

# 1  |  INTRODUCTION  

The association of intravenous thrombolysis (IVT) and mechanical thrombectomy (MT) is more effective than thrombolysis alone in patients with large vessel occlusion (LVO).1,2 However, the benefit of IVT in patients treated with only dMT is still debated. Previous meta-analyses found no significant differences in functional outcome after mechanical thrombectomy depending on previous IVT.3,4 Nevertheless, the results were partially influenced by the fact that few trials available and by possible confounding factors, including early recanalization after IVT. Recent findings support a similarity between dMT and bridging treatment, potentially suggesting IVT may not be necessary.5 Here we provide the most updated systematic review and meta-analysis of randomized controlled trials (RCTs) comparing dMT versus bridging treatment $(|\mathsf{VT}+\mathsf{MT}\$ .

# 2  |  METHODS  

The methods and guidelines of this meta-analysis followed PRISMA guidelines. The study protocol was registered on the Open Science Framework (https://doi.org/10.17605/​OSF.IO/P82RU). Pubmed, EMBASE and Cochrane Central register of Controlled Trials were systematically reviewed by two authors, searching for studies comparing dMT vs combined treatment and published between 1990 and May 2020. Search strategy included the combination of terms, such as “cerebrovascular disorder,” “stroke,” “thrombolysis,” “thrombectomy,” as either keywords or MeSH terms. Reference lists and citing articles were also reviewed to increase the identification of relevant studies. In this pooled analysis, we included only RCTs comparing the effectiveness and the safety of dMT or bridging therapy among adults $(\ge18$ years old) affected by acute cerebral ischemia. We limited the studies to English language and excluded conference proceedings and reviews. The patients treated with dMT and the bridging therapy represented the interventional and control group, respectively. The primary endpoint was the functional independence at 90  days from stroke onset (modified Rankin Scale of less than 3). Secondary endpoints were represented by the rate of good recanalization according to TICI grade of 2b or 3, the mortality at 90  days from stroke onset, and the occurrence of symptomatic intracranial hemorrhage (sICH) as defined by ECASS 2 criteria.6 The baseline and outcome data of each included study were systematically reviewed by two authors. Lack of data on outcome was recorded, when appropriate. Risk of bias was evaluated and reported according to the recommendations of the Cochrane Handbook for Systematic Reviews of Intervention and represented graphically by the funnel plots. A statistical analysis pooling strategy for both intervention and control group was performed, calculating outcome heterogeneity with Cochrane's $\mathsfQ$ test and $|^{2}$ and considering an overall $p$ value of $<0.05$ as statistically significant. Random-effects models (DerSimonian and Laird method) were applied. Odds ratio (OR) and $95\%$ confidence interval (CI) values were calculated for primary and secondary endpoints. # 3  | RESULTS  

Five studies with a total of 1657 patients were included in the analysis (PRISMA flowchart, Appendix  S1: Figure S1).1,5,7,8 Main characteristics of each study are reported in Table 1. We registered a total of 590 and 1067 patients for the interventional and control groups, respectively. We observed no statistically significant differences in age, vascular risk factors (hypertension and diabetes mellitus), or clinical severity between groups, considering only RCTs reporting these data, with the exception of atrial fibrillation $47.5\%$ in the interventional group vs $33\%$ in the control group; $p<0.001$ ; Table S1). Studies were overall of high quality and presented low risk of bias (Appendix S1: Figure S2).  

For the primary endpoint we observed a similarity between dMT and bridging treatment (OR 1.06, $95\%\mathrm{C}$ : 0.80-1.40, $|^{2}=20\%)$ (Figure  1). A suitable outcome was reported in $39\%$ and $34.5\%$ of patients treated with dMT and bridging therapy, respectively.  

Successful recanalization rates did not differ between groups, although bridging treatment correlated to a higher rates $(76.4\%)$ than the dMT $(70.2\%)$ (OR: 0.58; $95\%\mathrm{C}$ : 0.26-1.30, Pheterogeneity $=0.002$ , $|^{2}=84\%$ ; Appendix S1: Figure S3).  

The mortality at 90 days was non-significantly higher in the interventional group (OR 1.59; $95\%\mathrm{C}$ ; 0.61-4.17, Pheterogeneity $=0.001$ , $|^{2}=81\%$ ; Appendix S1: Figure S4), occurring in $19.8\%$ and $15.9\%$ of dMT and $\scriptstyle|\vee\top+\mathsf{MT}$ groups, respectively. Finally, no significant difference was observed also for sICH in the two groups (OR 0.96; $95\%\mathrm{C}$ : 0.43-2.13, $\mathsf{p}_{\mathsf{heterogeneity}}=0.26$ , $|^{2}=27\%$ ; Appendix  S1: Figure S5), showing rates of $4.3\%$ and $5.5\%$ in dMT and ${\mathsf{IVT}}{\mathsf{T}}{\mathsf{+MT}},$  

For all endpoints we observed no publication bias with the exception for the recanalization outcome, as represented in the funnel plots (Appendix S1: Figures S6-S9).  

# 4  |  DISCUSSION  

In this meta-analysis on study-level data from RCTs comparing dMT with bridging therapy in large vessel occlusion, the two treatments were substantially equivalent in terms of good functional outcome at 3 months, mortality rates, and rates of recanalization and sICH.

For the primary endpoint, our findings differ from the results of previous meta-analyses that compare the two strategies, primarily because we included RCTs only, excluding retrospective and prospective studies that contributed to increasing the rate of good outcome in bridging group.49.1013  

In our meta-analysis, which is the most comprehensive and recent study to include all the current evidence from RCTs, the successful recanalization rate was slightly higher in the bridging group. This finding is plausibly attributed to early recanalization after IVT, without a significant increase of sICH rate.14 However, the fact that no significant difference was found despite the large sample included and the longer onset-to-treatment time reported in dMT compared to bridging suggests a substantial comparability between the two strategies.  

The study has several limitations. First, this was an only studylevel analysis rather than a patient-level pool. Second, data from some RCTs derived primarily from post-hoc analyses and for this reason susceptible to potential selection bias. Third, no imaging endpoints were considered with the exception of the presence of LVO. However, the inclusion in this pooled analysis of only RCTs with very low risk of bias supports the robustness of the findings. Finally, subgroups analyses have not been performed due to the lack of data. However, only one RCT presented subgroup analyses considering age, clinical severity at onset, occlusion location and different framework times without significant differences between the two treatment groups.5 The results from two further ongoing RCTs exploring the clinical advantage of IVT prior endovascular treatment (SWIFT DIRECT and the MR CLEANNOIV) might provide important informations to improve the treatment of ischemic strokes due to large vessel occlusion.
