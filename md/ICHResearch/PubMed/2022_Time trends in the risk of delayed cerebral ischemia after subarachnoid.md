OBJECTIVE  Delayed cerebral ischemia (DCI) contributes to morbidity and mortality after aneurysmal subarachnoid hemorrhage (aSAH). Continuous improvement in the management of these patients, such as neurocritical care and aneurysm repair, may decrease the prevalence of DCI. In this study, the authors aimed to investigate potential time trends in the prevalence of DCI in clinical studies of DCI within the last 20 years.  

METHODS  PubMed, Embase, and the Cochrane library were searched from 2000 to 2020. Randomized controlled trials that reported clinical (and radiological) DCI in patients with aSAH who were randomized to a control group receiving standard care were included. DCI prevalence was estimated by means of random-effects meta-analysis, and subgroup analyses were performed for the DCI sum score, Fisher grade, clinical grade on admission, and aneurysm treatment method. Time trends were evaluated by meta-regression.  

RESULTS  The search strategy yielded 5931 records, of which 58 randomized controlled trials were included. A total of 4424 patients in the control arm were included. The overall prevalence of DCI was 0.29 $(95\%\mathbb{C}|0.26\mathrm{-}0.32)$ . The event rate for prevalence of DCI among the high-quality studies was 0.30 $95\%\mathbb{C}|0.25–0.34)$ and did not decrease over time $(0.25\%$ decline per year; $95\%$ CI $-2.49\%$ to $1.99\%$ , $\mathsf{p}=0.81\mathsf{9}$ ). DCI prevalence was higher in studies that included only higher clinical or Fisher grades, and in studies that included only clipping as the treatment modality.  

CONCLUSIONS  Overall DCI prevalence in patients with aSAH was 0.29 $95\%$ CI 0.26–0.32) and did not decrease over time in the control groups of the included randomized controlled trials.

Despite improvements in neurocritical care and endovascular and surgical aneurysmal repair, it is unclear whether this has led to a decrease in DCI prevalence and its consequences over time. In fact, no data on time trends in the prevalence of DCI have been reported.4,5 The aim of this study was to investigate potential time trends in the prevalence of DCI in clinical studies of DCI within the last 20 years.  

# Methods  

# Search Strategy and Selection Criteria  

This study was performed according to the PRISMA statement.6 Search strategies were developed in consultation with a research librarian with systematic review expertise. Literature was reviewed through the PubMed, Embase, and Cochrane Central Register of Controlled Trials from 2000 to March 31, 2020, using keywords specific to aSAH, cerebral vasospasm, and DCI. Complete search syntaxes for the PubMed, Embase, and Cochrane databases are included in the Supplemental Appendix. Citations used in articles that were included in the present review were hand searched.  

We included randomized controlled trials (RCTs) that investigated patients with aSAH in a control group that underwent standard preventive therapy for DCI, which consisted of administration of nimodipine and/or maintaining normovolemia.  

Patients randomized to the intervention arms were excluded from the analysis to avoid bias due to the possible effect on DCI prevalence. We included only RCTs that reported clinical signs of DCI, or any other synonym of DCI, as an outcome measure and included more than 10 patients in the control group. In the full-text screening, we used the following exclusion criteria: 1) studies not reporting on DCI; 2) articles for which the full text was not available; 3) animal studies; 4) non-RCTs; 5) articles written in languages other than English and Dutch; and 6) studies reporting only on angiographic vasospasm as a measure of DCI. If several articles used the same or overlapping patient populations, we included only the report with the largest population or with the most relevant information.  

Two authors (L.R. and C.H.S.V.G.) independently assessed titles and abstracts according to the predefined inclusion criteria. If an abstract was judged by both authors to meet the requirements, the full article was reviewed; discrepancies were solved with a third author (H.D.B.).  

# Outcome Measures and Quality Assessment  

The primary outcome was the prevalence of DCI. Because heterogeneous definitions of DCI were expected, the quality of the DCI definition was graded. We designed a DCI definition sum score based on the separate entities used in the definition of DCI formulated by Vergouwen et al.: “the occurrence of focal neurological impairment (such as hemiparesis, aphasia, apraxia, hemianopia, or neglect), or a decrease of at least 2 points on the Glasgow Coma Scale (either on the total score or on one of its individual components [eye, motor on either side, verbal]). This should last for at least 1 hour, is not apparent immediately after aneurysm occlusion, and cannot be attributed to other causes by means of clinical assessment, CT or MRI scanning of the brain, and appropriate laboratory studies” (Supplemental Table 1).4 Additionally, the quality of the trials was assessed using the Jadad score.7  

# Data Extraction  

Titles, abstracts, and keywords of the obtained reports were screened by two authors (C.H.S.V.G. and L.R.), and duplicates were eliminated. The included articles were assessed for eligibility in full text. Endnote (X9, Clarivate) was used as the citation manager. Control groups of included studies were used to extract data on the prevalence of DCI. Data extracted for each study were: authors and publication year; median year of data collection, study population/control group size, sex, clinical grade of aSAH; Fisher grade and type of treatment (clipping or coiling); and DCI definition and occurrences.  

# Statistical Analysis  

Stata software version 14 (StataCorp LLC) was used for statistical analysis. Results are presented as the event rate (ER) with $95\%$ CI. The $\mathrm{I}^{2}$ value was used to present between-study heterogeneity.  

Random-effects model meta-regression was used to determine the overall DCI prevalence over time. The median year of each data collection period was used. When not mentioned in the studies, the data collection year was considered the mean of the difference between the median data collection year and the year of publication of all other trials. Only studies with a high-quality definition (DCI sum score $\geq3$ ) and high quality (Jadad score $\geq3$ ) were included for the quantitative meta-analysis.  

In accordance with known risk factors for DCI, subgroup analyses for the DCI sum score, clinical grade, Fisher grade, and aneurysm repair modality were performed whenever possible.8,9 To assess the linear trend in incidence rate across subgroups, univariable meta-regression analysis was performed separately for each variable.  

# Results  

# Included Studies  

The search results are presented in the PRISMA flow diagram (Fig. 1). Our search strategy identified 5931 articles after removing duplicates. Two articles were identified after a hand search of the references and were evaluated in full text.10,11 Discrepancies were resolved in accordance. Of the 227 articles selected for full-text review, 169 were excluded for not meeting the inclusion criteria. Fifty-eight RCTs were included, describing a total of 4424 patients in the control arm. These references can be found at the end of the Supplemental Data.  

The characteristics and quality assessment of the included studies are listed in Supplemental Table 2. The attribution of the DCI sum score is described in Supplemental Table 3, and the quality assessment of the studies by the mean Jadad score is described in Supplemental Table 4.

# Analysis of Overall DCI Prevalence  

The overall DCI prevalence was 0.29 $95\%$ CI $0.26-$ 0.32). Heterogeneity between trials (I2) was $72\%$ $\mathbf{\tilde{p}}<0.001)$ (Supplemental Fig. 1). The DCI prevalence among highquality studies was 0 $.30(95\%\mathrm{CI}\dot{0.25}-0.34)$ . Heterogeneity between trials (I2) was $78\%$ $\mathbf{\rho}_{\mathrm{{p}}}<0.001,$ (Supplemental Fig. 2). Moreover, the prevalence of DCI among high-quality studies did not significantly decrease over time $(0.25\%$ decline per year; $95\%$ CI $-2.49\%$ to $1.99\%$ , $\mathrm{p}=0.819,$ ) (Fig. 2).  

# Subgroup Analyses  

No trend in change in DCI prevalence was found among studies with different DCI sum scores (Table 1). DCI prevalence was not significantly higher in studies that included only higher clinical grades (ER 0.40, $95\%$ CI 0.24–0.56) compared with studies including all grades (ER 0.28, $95\%$ $\operatorname{CI}{\hat{0.25}}\mathbf{-}0.31)$ and in studies including only higher Fisher grades $(\mathrm{ER}0.31,95\%\mathrm{CI}0.26-0.37)$ compared with studies including all Fisher grades (ER 0.26, $95\%$ CI 0.22–0.29). Regarding treatment modality, there was no significantly higher prevalence of DCI in studies that included only clipping (ER 0.28, $95\%$ C $\mathrm{~T~}0.22\substack{-0.33})$ compared with studies that included only coiling (ER 0.23, $95\%$ CI 0.17–0.28).

# Discussion  

The aim of this systematic review was to determine time trends in the prevalence of DCI in patients with aSAH. Only control groups of RCTs were considered in order to obtain the most reliable comparison among studies over time and to have a valid estimate of DCI prevalence for future research. The overall DCI prevalence in our review was 0.29 $95\%$ CI 0.26–0.32), and DCI prevalence among high-quality studies was 0.30 $95\%$ CI 0.25– 0.34). Our meta-regression analysis showed no significant change in DCI prevalence over time among high-quality studies.12–14  

The population analyzed in our study was the control group undergoing standard treatment for DCI (nimodipine and/or normovolemia) irrespective of the treatment modality, to eliminate any influence on DCI prevalence due to non–evidence-based preventive treatments. A previous meta-analysis on treatment modality that compared coiling versus clipping showed a lower prevalence of DCI in the endovascular (0.32) versus the surgical group (0.38) (risk ratio 0.84, $95\%$ CI 0.74–0.96). We also found a lower prevalence in studies that included patients who underwent only coiling (ER 0.23, $95\%$ CI 0.17–0.28) compared with studies that included patients who underwent only clipping (ER 0.28, $95\%$ CI 0.22–0.33). However, the confidence intervals were overlapping, and the number of studies included in each subgroup was limited; therefore, we were unable to draw any conclusion.15  

The case-fatality rate of SAH in population-based studies has declined over past decades, most likely due to a pronounced reduction in aneurysmal rebleeding resulting from earlier treatment and endovascular treatment.1–3,16 Additionally, such decline cannot be attributed to the constant prevalence of DCI over time estimated by our meta-analysis, although we included only RCTs and not population-based studies.

Despite this correction, analysis of high-quality studies showed the same results as low-quality studies, indicating that the DCI definition did not influence the prevalence of DCI. Nevertheless, despite the development of the DCI sum score, a changing definition over time might have influenced the results in any given study.  

We could not find a significant association between the clinical and Fisher grades and DCI; the subgroups were not suitable to make a distinction between high and low grades due to overlap. In the literature, however, an association between higher clinical grades and high modified Fisher grades and DCI has been reported and used for prediction models such as the VASOGRADE scale.19  

# Conclusions  

In our systematic review, the overall DCI prevalence in high-quality studies did not change over time within the last 2 decades. Despite many attempts to improve the diagnosis and prevention of DCI, no reduction in prevalence was achieved. Given the large heterogeneity of the definition of DCI among studies, we advocate for a uniform definition in future studies to reduce interobservational bias. This is particularly relevant given the critical importance of DCI in determining the outcome of aSAH, and as a target for clinical trials.  

# Study Limitations  

Heterogeneity between studies is a limitation of our study that must not be overlooked. It was caused by methodological differences between the studies and biological or social differences between the populations studied. By only selecting studies with control groups undergoing standard preventive therapy for DCI and, nevertheless, reaching a large number of patients, heterogeneity between studies was reduced. Additionally, as mentioned, the low number of studies that included only coiling as the treatment modality was a limiting factor for data interpretation.
