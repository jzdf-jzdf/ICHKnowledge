# Sequential Expression of Chemokines in Chronic Subdural Hematoma Fluids after Trepanation Surgery  

Koji Osuka,1,\* Yusuke Ohmichi,2,\* Mika Ohmichi,2 Takahiro Nakura,2 Kenichiro Iwami,1 Yasuo Watanabe,3 and Shigeru Miyachi1  

# Abstract  

Chronic subdural hematoma (CSDH) is considered an angiogenic and inflammatory disease. Chemokines attract leukocytes, and invading neutrophils and monocytes/macrophages play important roles in wound healing. However, no studies have been reported regarding changes in expression of chemokines in CSDH fluid after trepanation surgery. We randomly divided patients who underwent trepanation surgery into two groups. One was the irrigation group, in which irrigation of CSDH fluids was performed and a drainage tube was placed $(n=10)$ . The other was the non-irrigation group, in which a drainage tube was inserted without irrigation $(n=10)$ ). CSDH fluids were collected during the trepanation surgery, immediately after surgery and on day 1 through the drainage tube. The concentrations of interleukin-8 (IL-8), growth-regulated oncogene- $\cdot\alpha$ (GRO- $\cdot\alpha$ ), epithelial neutrophil-activating peptide 78 (ENA-78), monocyte chemoattractant protein-1 (MCP-1), interferon- $\cdot\gamma$ -induced protein-10 (IP-10), tissue plasminogen activator (tPA), von Willebrand factor (vWF), eotaxin-3, and myeloperoxidase (MPO) in each CSDH fluid sample were measured using enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay kits. After irrigation, concentrations of all chemokines decreased. However, concentrations of IL-8, GRO- $\alpha$ , ENA-78, MCP-1, and MPO were significantly increased on day 1 compared with concentrations during surgery with or without irrigation. In contrast, there were no changes in concentrations of IP-10, eotaxin-3, tPA, or vWF after trepanation surgery. Moreover, there were significant relationships among concentrations of IL-8, GRO- $\boldsymbol{\cdot}\alpha$ , ENA-78, and MCP-1 during the surgery and on day 1. In CSDH fluids, chemokines that attract neutrophils, such as IL-8, GRO- $\boldsymbol{\cdot}\alpha$ , ENA-78, and macrophage-attracting MCP-1, appear first after trepanation surgery, whereas lymphocyte-attracting IP-10 and eosinophil-attracting eotaxin-3 levels do not change within 1 day of surgery. These findings suggest that neutrophils and macrophages may play important roles in the healing process of CSDH at an early stage.  

Keywords: chronic subdural hematoma; ENA-78; GRO- $\alpha$ ; interleukin-8; MCP-1  

# Introduction  

hronic subdural hematoma (CSDH) is considered to be an angiogenic and inflammatory disease. Chemokines recruit leukocytes to sites of inflammation and trigger firm adhesion of leukocytes to activated endothelial cells as well as subsequent diapedesis. Previous studies have reported that there were significantly higher levels of the chemokines interleukin-8 (IL-8), monocyte chemoattractant protein-1 (MCP-1), interferon- $\cdot\gamma$ -induced protein10 (IP-10), and monokine induced by $\gamma$ interferon (MIG) in CSDH fluids compared with serum,1 suggesting that IL-8 and MCP-1 promote angiogenesis, whereas a compensatory angiostatic activity of IP-10 and MIG may contribute to the repair of this disorder. Eosinophils were found to have infiltrated the outer membrane of CSDH and are thought to play a role in the growth of CSDH membranes.2 We have revealed that eotaxin-3, a potent eosinophil chemoattractant chemokine, is expressed in CSDH fluids.3,4  

Myocardial infarction is also associated with an inflammatory response. IL-8 and MCP-1 are rapidly expressed in ischemic areas. Later, after the infarct is debrided, IP-10 and transforming growth factor- $\cdot\beta$ (TGF- $\cdot\beta$ ) are induced to encourage the resolution of inflammation and transition to fibrosis.5 In human wound healing, IL-8 and growth-regulated oncogene- $\alpha$ (GROa) mRNA are expressed on day 1, which is associated with neutrophil infiltration. On day 2, MCP-1 mRNA appeared to be associated with migration of macrophages. Finally, on day 4, IP-10 and MIG mRNAs were observed with lymphocyte migration.6,7 However, the time course of the expression of the above-mentioned inflammatory chemokines in CSDH fluids after trepanation surgery still remains unknown.  

Burr-hole craniotomy with closed-system drainage is currently the most acceptable method for the treatment of CSDH. During burr-hole craniotomy, we wash the CSDH fluids with artificial cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) until the CSDH fluids become as clear as possible. Twist-drill craniotomy and continuous closed-system catheter drainage without irrigation of CSDH fluids have been reported to be effective.8,9 Slow continuous drainage allows the brain to re-expand sufficiently to eliminate the subdural space and decreases the risk of recurrence.10 A randomized controlled trial revealed that drainage after surgical evacuation leads to a better outcome.11 A systematic review and meta-analysis of these surgical procedures showed that there were no significant differences in outcomes between twist-drill and burr-hole surgeries.12 Trepanation surgery against CSDH is the turning point when stages change from growing to healing of the CSDH outer membrane. We speculate that the investigation of sequential expression of chemokines in CSDH fluids with or without irrigation will tell us the role of chemokines after trepanation surgery toward the healing process. Therefore, the present study was performed to elucidate the changes in concentrations of these chemokines in CSDH fluids after trepanation surgery.  

# Methods  

# Patients and subjects  

This study included 20 patients who underwent surgical trepanation surgery for CSDH at Aichi Medical University Hospital (Aichi, Japan). These patients, thirteen men and seven women, ranged in age from 50 to 86 years (mean, 74.5; Table 1). The computed tomography (CT) findings were categorized into four groups according to Nakaguchi’s classification.13 These patients were randomly divided into two groups using a block randomization method. One was the irrigation group, in which CSDH fluids were washed with artificial CSF. After this procedure, a drainage tube was placed into the cavity of the CSDH. The other was the non-irrigation group, in which a drainage tube was inserted into the cavity of the CSDH without irrigation. CSDH fluid samples were collected three times: during the operation, after the dura was incised crosswise, within $30\mathrm{min}$ after the operation, and day 1 after the operation. The drainage tube was removed to avoid infection on day 1 after sufficient drainage of CSDH fluids had been confirmed by CT scan. No patients suffered from infection in this study. One patient suffered from recurrence (non-irrigation group, case 6). All samples were immediately centrifuged upon collection, and the supernatants were stored at $-80^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$ until analysis. The ethics committee of Aichi Medical University approved this clinical experiment (17-H047).  

# Analysis of chemokines in chronic subdural hematoma fluids  

Concentrations of IL-8 (ThermoFisherScientific, Inc., Waltham, MA), GRO- $\alpha$ (ThermoFisherScientific), epithelial neutrophilactivating peptide 78 (ENA-78; R&D Systems, Inc., Minneapolis, MN), MCP-1 (R&D Systems), IP-10 (R&D Systems), eotaxin-3 (Abcam, Cambridge, UK), tissue plasminogen activator (tPA; Assaypro, St. Charles, MO), and von Willebrand factor (vWF; ThermoFisherScientific) were measured in duplicate using enzymelinked immunosorbent assay (ELISA) kits according to the manufacturer’s instructions. Minimum detectable doses of the assays were $2\mathrm{pg/mL}$ for IL-8, $7.6\mathrm{pg/mL}$ for GRO- $\alpha$ , $15\mathrm{pg/mL}$ for ENA78, $1.7\mathrm{pg/mL}$ for MCP-1, $1.67\mathrm{pg/mL}$ for IP-10, $0.01\mathrm{ng/mL}$ for tPA, $0.13\mathrm{ng/mL}$ for vWF, and $1.54\mathrm{pg/mL}$ for eotaxin-3. Concentrations of myeloperoxidase (MPO) in samples obtained during the operation and day 1 after surgery were measured in duplicate using an ELISA kit (R&D Systems). The minimum detectable dose was $0.014\mathrm{ng/mL}$ for MPO.  

# Statistical analysis  

Data are expressed as the mean $\pm$ standard error of the mean. Significant differences between groups were assessed using oneway analysis of variance (ANOVA), followed by the Bonferroni/ Dunn test for multiple comparisons. Correlations between cytokine and chemokine levels were assessed by Pearson’s correlation coefficient. Significant differences between the groups were assessed by paired $t\cdot$ -test. Significance was set at $p<0.05$ .  

# Results  

Changes in interleukin-8, growth-regulated oncogene-a, epithelial neutrophil activating peptide 78, monocyte chemotactic protein-1, interferon-c-induced protein-10, tissue plasminogen activator, von Willebrand factor, and eotaxin-3 levels after surgery  

Concentrations of lL-8, GRO- $\alpha$ , ENA-78, MCP-1, IP-10, tPA, vWF, and eotaxin-3 were decreased immediately after trepanation surgery in the irrigation group (Fig. 1A, C, E, G, I, K, M, and O, respectively), but not in the non-irrigation group (Fig. 1B, D, F, H, J, L, N, and P, respectively). However, concentrations of lL-8, GRO- $\alpha$ , ENA-78, and MCP-1 were significantly increased on day 1 whether or not irrigation was performed (Fig. 1A, B, C, D, E, F, G, and H). Regarding IP-10, tPA, vWF, and eotaxin-3, concentrations on day 1 were not significantly changed compared with those during the surgery (Fig. 1I, J, K, L, M, N, O, and P).  

Concentrations of lL-8, GRO- $\alpha$ , ENA-78, and MCP-1 during surgery and on day 1 were positively correlated with each other according to Pearson’s correlation coefficient (Fig. 2). However, no other correlations were noted with respect to concentrations of IP-10, tPA, vWF, and eotaxin-3 during surgery and on day 1.  

# Changes in myeloperoxidase levels after surgery  

It is intriguing that the concentration of MPO in CSDH fluids on day 1 $3625.7\pm730.9\mathrm{ng/mL})$ was significantly increased compared with that during surgery ( $1513.6\pm310.8\mathrm{ng/mL}$ , as shown by paired $t\cdot$ -test $p<0.01$ ; Fig. 3).  

# Discussion  

In this study, we revealed that the concentrations of IL-8, GRO- $\alpha$ , ENA-78, and MCP-1 in CSDH fluids significantly increased on day 1 after surgery, regardless of irrigation, compared with concentrations during surgery, whereas those of IP-10, tPA, vWF, and eotaxin-3 did not. Moreover, the concentration of MPO in CSDH fluids on day 1 was also significantly increased. There were significant correlations among levels of lL-8, GRO- $\alpha$ , ENA-78, and MCP-1 during trepanation surgery.  

Vascular endothelial cells play a critical role in maintaining blood flow in response to rapid injury. Several hemostatic and inflammatory mediators are secreted from endothelial cells as an instantaneous response. Chemokines are inducible, secreted, proinflammatory cytokines that act as chemoattractants of leukocytes, macrophages, and lymphocytes. Weibel-Palade bodies are the most prominent of the regulated secretory granules. Endothelial cells store IL-8 in WeibelPalade bodies.14–17 IL-8 is known to be a potent promoter of angiogenesis18 and induces chemotaxis in neutrophils, causing them to migrate into sites of inflammation.19 In vascular injury, vWF plays an important role in hemostasis through platelet adhesion. vWF, a multi-meric protein, is also the major constituent of WeibelPalade bodies of endothelial cells.17,20,21 We have previously confirmed that endothelial cells of vessels within the outer membrane of CSDH are immunopositive for vWF.22 Eotaxin-3 (CCL26) is produced locally from epithelial, mesenchymal, and endothelial cells and induces the shear-dependent, transendothelial migration of eosinophils.23 Moreover, eotaxin-3 is present in Weibel-Palade bodies in endothelial cells.15 Eotaxin-3 in CSDH fluids induces migration of eosinophils into the outer membrane and activates the Smad pathway in fibroblasts through TGF- $\beta$ , suggesting a potential mechanism for CSDH growth.3,4 Weibel-Palade bodies in endothelial cells include IL-8, vWF, and eotaxin-3. However, our data revealed that neutrophil-attracting chemokine IL-8 is significantly elevated only on day 1 compared with levels during surgery, regardless of irrigation, whereas vWF and eotaxin-3 are not. Extracellular acidification to levels of $\mathrm{pH}6.8$ or lower significantly alters the behavior of secreted vWF, whereas rapid release of the inflammatory mediators IL-8 and eotaxin-3 is essentially unaltered in human endothelial cells.24 Considering previous data and our current data, exocytosis of the contents of Weibel-Palade bodies may be precisely regulated after surgery. Local conditions in the CSDH cavity after surgery may determine the dispersal of neutrophil-attracting chemokines from Weibel-Palade bodies.

tPA is a protein involved in thrombolysis. It is a serine protease found on endothelial cells, and it plays a role in inducing fibrinolysis and thrombolysis. Endothelial cells of capillaries in the outer membrane of CSDH showed strong immunostaining for tPA.25 High concentrations of tPA in CSDH fluids during the initial surgery have a relatively high probability of recurrence.26 tPA in endothelial cells is stored in secretory granules different from the Weibel-Palade bodies in which vWF is stored.27,28 $\mathrm{Ca}^{2+}$ triggers different secretion of tPA and vWF in endothelial cells, suggesting a balance of different physiological actions.28 From our data, neither thrombosis nor fibrinolysis appears to be involved in the healing processes immediately after trepanation surgery.  

IP-10 belongs to the $\alpha$ or CXC family of chemokines and was originally identified as an interferon- $\gamma$ -induced protein. IP-10 stimulates migration of monocytes and T-lymphocytes to inflammatory tissues. However, it does not induce chemotactic activity in neutrophils. IP-10 showed synergistic induction in endothelial cells by inflammatory mediators, which plays an important role in the immune response.29 After subarachnoid hemorrhage (SAH), the concentration of MCP-1 in CSF increased from days 1 to 5, peaking on day 3, and decreased thereafter. Concentrations of IP-10 and MIG, members of the CXC chemokine family that activate lymphocytes, progressively increased after the increase in MCP-1, peaked on day 5, and then gradually decreased in CSF after SAH.30 Lymphocytes may be involved at a later time, and longer follow-up studies are needed to explore the role of IP-10 after trepanation surgery.  

ENA-78 has chemotactic and activating functions on neutrophils, especially during acute inflammatory responses.31 Human endothelial cells express not only IL-8, but also ENA-78, with each accounting for $\sim40\%$ of the chemotactic activity of neutrophils.32 Neutrophils play an essential role in anti-type II collagen antibody- and lipopolysaccharide-induced arthritis. ENA78 is one of the most abundant chemokines in rheumatoid arthritis (RA) synovial fluid. ENA-78 recruits neutrophils into the inflamed joints of RA.34 GRO- $\alpha$ and MCP-1 reside mainly in smaller vesicles in endothelial cells.15 GRO- $\alpha$ is synthesized by human umbilical vein endothelial cells (HUVECs), and its synthesis is inducible by shear stress, IL-1, TNF-a, LPS, and thrombin.35,36  

GRO- $\alpha$ possesses potent neutrophil-stimulating activity; however, its chemoactivity is less than that of IL-8.37 GRO- $\alpha$ synthesized by endothelial cells contributes to neutrophil chemotaxis in chronic rhinosinusitis.38 MCP-1 belongs to the $\beta$ or CC family of chemokines and stimulates the migration of monocytes to inflamed tissues.39 MCP-1 is expressed by endothelial cells, including HUVECs, in response to $\operatorname{IL}{-1\beta}$ .40 After transient middle cerebral artery occlusion, neutrophil infiltration occurs at an early stage ( $1\sim3$ days), followed by peak expression of MCP-1 at 2 days, resulting in the infiltration of macrophages at a later stage $(2\sim7$ days).41 Together, these previous data indicate that infiltration of neutrophils occurs at early stages, followed by infiltration of macrophages, in CSDH fluids after trepanation surgery. More frequent samplings through day 1 may reveal the changes over time more precisely. These previous findings, together with our data showing that significant correlations exist among levels of IL8, GRO- $\alpha$ , ENA-78, and MCP-1 during the surgery and on day 1, suggest that induced neutrophils and macrophages cooperate together to initiate the healing process after trepanation surgery. Further, we have examined whether changes in concentrations of chemokines depend upon the procedure of irrigation during surgery. Levels of chemokines lL-8, GRO- $\alpha$ , ENA-78, and MCP-1 significantly increased on day 1 regardless of irrigation, compared with their levels during surgery, which suggests that these chemokines play significant roles in the sustainable repair of CSDH after surgery.  

Localization of MPO was confirmed in azurophil granules of neutrophils.42,43 MPO, with its ability to promote lipoprotein oxidation by pathways involving hypochlorous acid (HOCl) and tyrosyl radicals, plays a role in atherogenesis by catalyzing oxidative reactions in the vascular wall. Synovial fluid samples of patients with early osteoarthritis have elevated levels of MPO, suggesting that MPO serves as a potential biomarker of the disease.44 In our study, MPO levels significantly increased on day 1 compared with those during surgery, suggesting that the earlier-acting chemokines, IL-8, GRO- $\alpha$ , and ENA-78, attract neutrophils within CSDH fluids and may induce lipoprotein oxidation within CSDH fluids. Expression of MPO is a footprint of the existence of neutrophils within CSDH fluids.  

In this study, we conducted a serial analysis of the levels of chemokines in CSDH fluids after trepanation surgery. There were several limitations to this study. We enrolled only a small number of patients and examined the results over a limited time after surgery. A longer follow-up study with sampling from more patients will give us a more precise view of the changes of these chemokines, especially for lymphocyte-attracting IP-10, which is involved in the healing process several days after surgery. Moreover, our results are purely observational, given that we performed no animal experiments to corroborate our findings, and the precise mechanism of expression of these chemokines still remains unknown. However, our preliminary data confirmed that neutrophils and macrophages may play an important role in the healing process of CSDH at an early stage.

# Conclusions  

This study showed, for the first time, the changes that occur over time in chemokine levels in CSDH fluids after trepanation surgery with or without irrigation. Our finding regarding the sequential expression of chemokines in CSDH fluids after surgery was particularly interesting, given that it suggests that neutrophiland macrophage-attracting chemokines may play an important role in the healing process of CSDH at an early stage. Expression levels of these chemokines are independent of the irrigation procedure. These chemokines are discharged from Weibel-Palade bodies and type II granules, whereas some other chemokines remain unchanged within endothelial cells. Further studies are necessary to explore a precise mechanism of exocytosis of these chemokines. IL-8, GRO- $\alpha$ , and ENA-78 belong to the CXC chemokine family. These chemokines elicit their effects by signaling through the chemokine receptors CXCR1 or CXCR2. Therapies targeting these receptors may provide a means of treating intractable CSDH.
