# Non–High-Density Lipoprotein Cholesterol Predicts Adverse Outcomes in Acute Ischemic Stroke  

Guangyao Wang, MD; Jing Jing , MD, PhD; Anxin Wang, PhD; Xiaoli Zhang, MD; Xingquan Zhao , MD, PhD; Zixiao Li , MD, PhD; Chunjuan Wang, MD, PhD; Hao Li, MD, PhD; Liping Liu , MD, PhD; Yongjun Wang , MD; Yilong Wang , MD, PhD; on behalf of the China National Stroke Registry II Investigators  

BACKGROUND AND PURPOSE: Non–high-density lipoprotein cholesterol (non–HDL-C) was significantly related to adverse outcomes in patients with cardiovascular disease. We aim to investigate the associations of non-HDL-C and adverse outcomes in acute ischemic stroke.  

METHODS: Among 19 604 patients with acute ischemic stroke admitted to the China National Stroke Registry II, 16 113 with both total cholesterol and HDL-C were analyzed. Patients were classified into 5 groups by quintiles of non-HDL-C. The outcomes included recurrent ischemic stroke, intracranial hemorrhage, and all-cause death within 1 year. The relationship of non-HDL-C with the risk of outcomes was analyzed by Cox regression models.  

RESULTS: Among the 16 113 patients, the median (interquartile range) of non-HDL-C was 3.41 (2.78–4.10) mmol/L. After adjustment for confounding variables, patients in the top quintile of non-HDL-C were associated with higher risk of recurrent ischemic stroke within 1 year (adjusted hazard ratio, 1.46 $[95\%$ CI, $1.20\mathrm{-}1.77]$ ), compared with those in the third quintile. Patients in the bottom and top quintile of non-HDL-C were associated with higher risk of all-cause death within 1 year (adjusted hazard ratio, 1.22 $[95\%$ CI, 1.01–1.47] and adjusted hazard ratio, 1.40 $[95\%$ CI, 1.15–1.70], respectively), compared with those in the third quintile. However, non-HDL-C levels were not significantly predictive in intracranial hemorrhage.  

CONCLUSIONS: Non-HDL-C may be a qualified predictor for recurrent ischemic stroke and all-cause death within 1 year in patients with acute ischemic stroke.

# METHODS  

# Data Availability  

All data are available to researchers on request for purposes of reproducing the results or replicating the procedure by directly contacting the corresponding author.  

# Study Cohort  

CNSR II initiated by the Ministry of Health of China in 2012 was a nationwide, multicenter, prospective registry study for evaluating the stroke care delivery in clinical practice.12 A total of 219 hospitals were selected by a steering committee by means of the same criteria used in CNSR I.13 CNSR II was conducted in China from June 2012 to January 2013. CNSR II was approved by the Ethics Committee at Beijing Tiantan Hospital. Each participant offered written informed consent before participating in this study.  

# Study Participants  

Patients in the CNSR II met the following eligibility criteria: (1) age 18 years or older; (2) diagnosis of acute ischemic stroke, transient ischemic attack, spontaneous intracerebral hemorrhage, or subarachnoid hemorrhage within 7 days; (3) direct hospital admission from emergency departments or physician’s clinics; and (4) written informed consent provided by the patients or their legally authorized representatives. Among 25 018 patients with acute cerebrovascular events enrolled in the CNSR II study, 19 604 of them were acute ischemic stroke. A total of 998 patients were excluded from the 19 604 patients because of lacking of TC or HDL-C.  

# Baseline Data Collection  

Baseline information including demographics, medical history of taking agents, medical history of ischemic stroke, myocardial infarction, transient ischemic attack, dyslipidemia, hypertension, diabetes, angina, atrial fibrillation, medications during hospitalization and at discharge, and National Institutes of Health Stroke Scale (NIHSS) on admission were collected by trained coordinators via face-to-face interviews at each center.  

The venous blood samples were obtained from fasting patients within the first 24 hours of admission for the purpose of routine laboratory tests including TC and HDL-C. The collection, preservation, and processing of the blood samples was in a manner according to the clinical site laboratory’s policies and procedures in every center. Concentrations of TC and HDL-C on admission were extracted from medical records. NonHDL-C was calculated by deducting HDL-C from TC.14  

# Clinical Outcomes and Follow-Up  

Follow-up was performed at 3, 6, 12 months after acute ischemic stroke in the CNSR II study. Patients were contacted through telephone at each interview by trained research coordinators. The detailed procedure of follow-up has been previously published.15 In this study, we reported the 1-year follow-up data. The outcomes included recurrent ischemic stroke, all-cause death, and intracranial hemorrhage. Ischemic stroke recurrences related to rehospitalization were sourced to the corresponding hospitals for purpose of ensuring reliable diagnosis during the follow-up. In the event of suspected recurrent cerebrovascular events without hospitalization, adjudication was made by the principal investigator together with the trained research coordinators. Totally, 1424 $(7.7\%)$ and 2200 $11.8\%)$ patients with acute ischemic stroke were lost to follow-up at 3 months and 1 year, respectively.  

# Statistical Analysis  

Patients were divided into 5 groups by non-HDL-C quintiles according to a previous study.16 We described continuous variables as medians with interquartile ranges and categorical variables as proportions. Baseline characteristics stratified by non-HDL-C quintiles were compared by χ2 test for categorical variables and Kruskal-Wallis test for continuous variables for difference between the 5 groups.  

The relationship of non-HDL-C and recurrent ischemic stroke, intracranial hemorrhage and all-cause death was investigated with Cox proportional hazards models. The third quintile of non-HDL-C was taken as a reference in accordance with a previous study for the event of recurrent ischemic stroke and all-cause death.16 The bottom quintile of non-HDL-C was taken as a reference in accordance with a previous study for the event of intracranial hemorrhage.17 We performed 2 models. We adjusted for age and sex in the first model. We adjusted for all the variables in the baseline in the second model, except for those with collinearity. Pearson correlation coefficients were used to evaluate the potential for collinearity between pairs of covariates in the baseline. The correlation coefficient ${>}0.5$ was considered as a threshold for collinearity.18 Adjusted hazard ratios and their $95\%$ CIs were calculated.  

Stratified analyses were conducted among patients with or without prescription of statins at discharge. The interactions of non-HDL-C levels and prescription of statins at discharge on the outcomes were investigated by using Cox proportional hazards models.  

To visualize the relationship between non-HDL-C and recurrent ischemic stroke and all-cause death, restricted cubic splines with 5 knots (at the 5th, 25th, 50th, 75th, and 95th centiles)19 were constructed. The reference point was the median of nonHDL-C, and the hazard ratio was also adjusted for all the potential confounders in the baseline, except for those with collinearity. The potential linear or nonlinear relationships of non-HDL-C levels with recurrent ischemic stroke and all-cause death were explored.20 Test result for nonlinearity was checked first. If test for nonlinearity was not significant, test result for linearity was checked, with the significant result indicating the linear association.  

A 2-tailed $P$ of ${<}0.05$ was considered as statistical significance. All statistical analyses were carried out by SAS software, version 9.3 (SAS Institute, Inc, Cary, NC).  

# RESULTS  

# Baseline Characteristics  

In total, 16 113 patients with acute ischemic stroke were included in this study. The flowchart of this study is shown in Figure I in the Data Supplement. The baseline characteristics of patients included and not included in this analysis are shown in Table I in the Data Supplement. The median (interquartile range) level of non-HDL-C was 3.41 (2.78–4.10) mmol/L. The median (interquartile range) duration of follow-up was 383 (352–404) days. Baseline characteristics of patients with acute ischemic stroke stratified by the quintiles of non-HDL-C are shown in Table  1. Patients in the top quintile of non-HDL-C were more likely to have slightly higher body mass index, a higher proportion of history of taking antihypertensive agents before admission, higher proportions of history of dyslipidemia, diabetes, and hypertension, higher NIHSS on admission, and more likely to have antihypertensive agents, lipid-lowering statins, and antidiabetic agents at discharge. Patients in the bottom quintile of non-HDLC were more likely to be older, male, and smoker, and have slightly lower body mass index, lower blood pressure, higher proportions of history of ischemic stroke, and atrial fibrillation, higher NIHSS on admission, higher proportions of history of taking antiplatelet and lipidlowering agents before admission, a higher proportion of anticoagulant agents at discharge, and a lower proportion of antiplatelet agents at discharge. Baseline characteristics of patients with and without prescription of statins at discharge are shown in Tables II and III in the Data Supplement, respectively. Pearson correlation between each pair of covariates is shown in Table IV in the Data Supplement.  

# Non-HDL-C and Clinical Outcomes  

The incidence rate of recurrent ischemic stroke was 70.1 per 1000 person-years within 1-year follow-up. After adjusting for potential variables, patients in the top quintile of non-HDL-C were associated with higher risk of recurrent ischemic stroke within 1 year (adjusted hazard ratio, 1.46 $[95\%$ CI, 1.20–1.77]; Table 2), compared with those in the third quintile. We found a linear association between non-HDL-C levels and the risk of recurrent ischemic stroke within 1 year by restricted cubic splines (Figure). Similar results were also observed at the 3-month follow-up (Table 2; Figure).  

The incidence rate of all-cause death was 76.7 per 1000 person-years within 1-year follow-up. Patients in the bottom (hazard ratio, 1.22 $[95\%$ CI, 1.01–1.47]) and top (HR, 1.40 $[95\%$ CI, $1.15\mathrm{-}1.70]$ ) quintile of non-HDLC were associated with higher risk of all-cause death within 1 year, compared with those in the third quintile (Table 2). We found a nonlinear (U-shaped) association between non-HDL-C and all-cause death within 1 year by restricted cubic splines (Figure).  

When stratifying patients into those with and without prescription of statins at discharge, patients with and without prescription of statins at discharge in the top quintile of non-HDL-C were associated with higher risk of recurrent ischemic stroke within 1 year. No interaction between non-HDL-C and prescription of statins at hospital discharge for the risk of adverse outcomes was observed (Tables V and VI in the Data Supplement).  

# DISCUSSION  

In this present study, we found that elevated levels of non-HDL-C were associated with an increased risk of recurrent ischemic stroke within 1 year in patients with acute ischemic stroke. Compared with intermediate level of non-HDL-C, patients with both bottom and top levels of non-HDL-C were associated with higher risk of allcause death within 1 year.  

Although higher non-HDL-C level was significantly related to higher risk of ischemic stroke in women21 and was a significant predictor of cerebrovascular disease after myocardial infarction,8 the predict value of nonHDL-C for recurrent ischemic stroke after acute ischemic stroke remains limited and controversial.2 Data from the VISP trial (Vitamin Intervention for Stroke Prevention) indicated that non-HDL-C was not significantly linked with recurrent stroke in patients with noncardioembolic stroke.2 However, our study found that elevated levels of non-HDL-C were related to higher risk of recurrent ischemic stroke in patients with acute ischemic stroke.  

Several reasons may account for this phenomenon. First, the VISP trial included patients who experienced a nondisabling ischemic stroke within the preceding 120 days, and patients in our study were acute ischemic stroke within 7 days. The risk of recurrent ischemic stroke was higher in the early stage after an index stroke or transient ischemic attack.22–24 Second, concentration of non-HDLC has been shown as a predictor for atherosclerotic disease.25 Elevated levels of non-HDL-C were related to large artery atherosclerotic stroke.26,27 Intracranial arterial stenosis was common in patients with stroke of Asian population.28–31 The Chinese Intracranial Atherosclerosis Study showed that recurrent stroke at 1 year occurred in $5.2\%$ of patients with $70\%$ to $99\%$ intracranial stenosis and $7.3\%$ of those with total occlusion after acute cerebral ischemia.31 The risk of recurrent ischemic stroke was high $(6.4\%)$ in our study, which probably reflected a high proportion of intracranial stenosis.

Our study found a nonlinear (U-shaped) relationship between levels of non-HDL-C and all-cause death within 1 year. That is, patients with higher $(\ge4.30$ mmol/L) and lower $(\pm2.63\mathrm{mmol/L)}$ levels of non-HDL-C had a higher risk of all-cause death within 1 year after acute ischemic stroke. These findings are partially in consist with previous findings in acute ischemic stroke, in which lower levels of non-HDL-C were significantly associated with higher risk of death.9,32 Elevated levels of non-HDLC were associated with all-cause death in patients with cardiovascular diseases.5,33 A recent study from UK Biobank also showed that the lipids and lipoproteins were usually nonlinearly associated with fatal cardiovascular disease,16 which was in line with our study.  

Several reasons may explain for the nonlinear (U-shaped) association of non-HDL-C and death after acute ischemic stroke. Patients with lower levels of nonHDL-C were more likely to be older, lower weight, lower blood pressure, and higher proportions of history of ischemic stroke, dyslipidemia, and atrial fibrillation, smoker, higher NIHSS on admission, which seemed to indicate that patients with the lowest quintile of non-HDL-C were more at risk for death attributed to a combination of these factors and/or overall frailty.34,35 Therefore, lower level of non-HDL-C may be a marker of these aggregated risk factors and other nonmeasured confounders. Non-HDL-C level was on behalf of the atherogenic effects of multiple apolipoprotein B-containing lipoproteins, low-density lipoproteins, very low-density lipoproteins and their metabolic remnants, intermediate-density lipoproteins, and lipoprotein(a).4 Intracranial plaques and stenoses were highly prevalent in fatal stroke at autopsy.36 Atherosclerosis was demonstrated as a potential pathological cause of death from stroke.37 Elevated levels of non-HDL-C were related to higher risk of all-cause death after acute ischemic stroke in this study, suggesting that higher levels of non-HDL-C might be a vital factor for atherosclerosis.

A strength of this study was that the blood samples were drawn within the first 24 hours after admission. However, there are several limitations in our study. First, the TOAST (Trial of ORG 10172 in Acute Stroke Treatment) classification of acute ischemic stroke was not collected in the study, and we could not estimate the associations of non-HDL-C and outcomes by TOAST classification. Patients included in our study had a higher proportion of male $(63.5\%)$ and smokers $(44.5\%)$ ). Only Chinese patients who are known to have a higher rate of large artery atherosclerosis disease associated with higher risk of stroke recurrence were enrolled in the CNSR II study.31,38 Given that elevated levels of nonHDL-C would in theory be more predictive of recurrent vascular events in a population with atherothrombotic strokes,39 the predictive ability of non-HDL-C may differ in other populations. Second, only baseline non-HDL-C was available in our study. Dynamic changes of nonHDL-C during the follow-up were not available. However, Framingham Offspring cohort showed that non-HDL-C concentrations were usually stable over the 30-year life course.40 Heterogeneity of equipment at 219 sites might lead to biased estimates of these associations; however, this effect was minimized, on account of strict quality control and daily practice in each hospital. Third, the baseline characteristics between patients included and not included in our study were not entirely balanced. Patients excluded from this study were older and had lower body mass index and blood pressure, higher proportion of history of ischemic stroke and atrial fibrillation, higher NIHSS on admission, lower proportion of medications at discharge. Fourth, follow-up was only by telephone in this study, and end point assessment could not be validated by hospital records in all individuals. Further large-scaled cohort studies in other populations are needed to verify the generalizability of our findings.

In patients with acute ischemic stroke, elevated levels of non-HDL-C an increased risk of recurrent ischemic stroke within 1 year, while both bottom and top levels of non-HDL-C were associated with an increased risk of all-cause death within 1 year. Non-HDL-C may be a qualified predictor for recurrent ischemic stroke and allcause death in acute ischemic stroke.
