# Basilar Artery Occlusion Chinese Endovascular Trial: Protocol for a prospective randomized controlled study  

Chuanhui Li1, Chuanjie $\mathbf{\Delta}\mathbf{w}_{\mathbf{u}}\mathbf{\Xi}^{\mathsf{I}}$ , Longfei $\mathbf{\Delta}\mathbf{w}_{\mathbf{u}}\mathbf{\Delta}$ , Wenbo Zhao1 , Jian Chen2, Ming Ren3, Chen Yao4, Xiaoyan Yan4, Chongya Dong Haiqing Song1, Qingfeng Ma1, Jiangang Duan5, Yunzhou Zhang1, Hongqi Zhang2, Liqun Jiao2, Yuping Wang1, Tudor G Jovin6 and Xunming Ji2 $\circledcirc$ ; on behalf of the BAOCHE Investigators  

# Abstract  

Rationale: There are no randomized trials examining the best treatment for acute basilar artery occlusion in the 6– 24-hour time window.  

Aims: To assess the safety and efficacy of thrombectomy for stroke due to basilar artery occlusion in patients randomized within $6{-}24\mathrm{h}$ from symptom onset or time last seen well.  

Sample size: For an estimated difference of $20\%$ in proportions of the primary outcome between the two groups, 318 patients will be included for $5\%$ significance and $90\%$ power with a planned interim analysis after two-thirds of the sample size (212 patients) have achieved the 90 days follow-up.  

Methods and design: A prospective, multi-center, randomized, controlled, open-label and blinded-endpoint trial. The randomization employs a 1:1 ratio of mechanical thrombectomy with the detachable Solitaire thrombectomy device and best medical therapy (BMT) vs. BMT alone.  

Study outcomes: The primary outcome will be the proportion of patients achieving modified Rankin Scale (mRS) 0–3 at 90 days. Key secondary outcomes are: dramatic early favorable response, dichotomized $\mathsf{mRS}$ score (0–2 vs. 3–6 and $\--$ 4 vs. 5–6) at 90 days, ordinal (shift) $\mathsf{mRS}$ analysis at 90 days, infarct volume at $24\mathsf{h}$ , vessel recanalization at $24\mathsf{h}$ in both treatment arms, and successful recanalization in the thrombectomy arm according to the modified thrombolysis in cerebral infarction (mTICI) classification defined as mTICI 2 b or 3. Safety variables are mortality at 90 days, symptomatic intracranial hemorrhage rates at $24\mathsf{h}$ , and procedure-related complications.  

Discussion: Results from this trial will indicate whether mechanical thrombectomy is superior to medical management alone in achieving favorable outcomes in subjects with acute stroke caused by basilar artery occlusion presenting within $6{-}24\mathrm{h}$ from symptom onset.  

Trial registration: URL: http://www.clinicaltrials.gov. ClinicalTrials.gov Identifier: NCT02737189.

# Introduction and rationale  

Multiple randomized controlled trials have provided strong evidence that endovascular revascularization treatment improves clinical outcomes in selected patients with acute ischemic stroke (AIS) caused by proximal occlusions in the anterior circulation up to $24\mathrm{{h}}$ from time last seen well (TLSW).1–3 The natural history of basilar artery occlusion (BAO) is worse compared with that seen in the anterior circulation, and carries an exceptionally high morbidity and mortality rate.4,5 Reperfusion of the basilar artery has been associated with improved clinical outcomes compared to historical outcomes.6–9 However, level one evidence in support of endovascular treatment (EVT) for patients with acute BAO is lacking. The purpose of the study is to evaluate the hypothesis that thrombectomy (performed with a detachable stentriever) plus best medical management (BMT) leads to superior clinical outcomes as compared to BMT alone in appropriately selected subjects experiencing an acute BAO when patients are randomized within $6{-}24\mathrm{h}$ from symptom onset or TLSW.  

# Methods  

# Design  

Basilar Artery Occlusion Chinese Endovascular Trial (BAOCHE) is a prospective, multi-center, randomized, controlled, open-label, and blinded-endpoint trial. The randomization employs a 1:1 ratio of mechanical embolectomy with the detachable stentriever Solitaire FR or AB device with or without adjunctive manual aspiration vs. BMT alone.  

# Patient population  

Patients presenting with posterior circulation AIS due to BAO and who can be randomized within $6{-}24\mathrm{h}$ from symptom onset or TLSW are enrolled. Eligibility requires confirmation of BAO or intracranial segments of both vertebral arteries, age 18–80 years old, baseline National Institutes of Health Stroke Scale $\mathrm{(NIHSS)}\geq6$ , and pre-stroke modified Rankin Scale $(\mathrm{mRS})\leq1$ . Neuroimaging exclusion criteria includes posterior circulation Acute Stroke Prognosis Early CT score (pc-ASPECTS) $<6$ and Pons-midbrain-index of ${\geq}3$ ,10 intracranial hemorrhage, complete cerebellar infarct with significant mass effect, and complete unilateral or bilateral thalamic infarction as evidenced by baseline neuroimaging.  

Inclusion and exclusion criteria are detailed in the supplemental material.  

# Randomization  

Central randomization system will be used to randomize subjects. Subjects will be randomized on the basis of a minimization process to balance between the two groups the 1:1 ratio both overall as well as within every category of the baseline factors: age ( $\leq70$ or $>70$ years), baseline NIHSS $(6-20\mathrm{or}>20)$ , therapeutic window $_{6-12\mathrm{h}}$ or ${>}12\mathrm{h}$ ).  

# Intervention  

Patients in both arms will be admitted at acute stroke units (or neurological intensive care unit if needed) and treated following the latest Chinese guidelines for AIS management (which are very similar to the guideline from the American Heart Association/American Stroke Association). Any intra-arterial intervention or administration of intravenous thrombolytics that occurs after randomization in a control arm subject will be considered a major protocol violation.  

For Solitaire arm, balloon angioplasty and/or stenting of the vertebral artery or basilar artery will be allowed in case there is persistent occlusion or high grade stenosis after embolectomy and in the opinion of the interventionalist the culprit lesion is underlying atherosclerotic plaque.  

# Primary outcome  

The primary outcome will be the proportion of patients achieving favorable outcomes defined as mRS 0–3 at 90 days as evaluated by independent certified central assessors who are blinded to treatment.  

# Secondary outcomes  

1. Dramatic early favorable response as determined by an NIHSS of 0–2 or NIHSS improvement ${\ge}8$ points at 24 $(-2/+12)$ hours from randomization or before discharge if patient is discharged prior to the above time limit.   
2. Dichotomized mRS score (0–2 vs. 3–6) at 90 days.   
3. Dichotomized mRS score (0–4 vs. 5–6) at 90 days.   
4. The mRS shift analysis at 90 days.   
5. Barthel Index at 90 days.   
6. NIHSS at 90 days.   
7. Montreal Cognitive Assessment test (MOCA) at 90 days.   
8. Quality of life analysis as measured by EuroQol/ EQ-5D and SF-36 at 3 months, 6 months and 12 months, between interventional therapy vs. medical therapy alone.  

9. Core lab adjudicated neuroimaging endpoints: (1) Follow-up infarct volume evaluated on CT/MR at $24\mathrm{{h}}$ $(-2/+12\mathrm{h})$ and the change of infarct volume compared with baseline. (2) Vessel recanalization at $24\mathrm{{h}}$ $(-2/+12$ hours) in both treatment groups assessed by using Arterial Occlusive Lesion (AOL) grades.11,12 (3) Immediate Post-Endovascular Treatment Recanalization (for the Solitaire arm only). Successful recanalization is defined as mTICI (Modified Thrombolysis in Cerebral Infarction) 2 b or 3 in the post-procedure angiography.  

10. Safety endpoints: (1) Mortality at 90 days; (2) Symptomatic intracranial haemorrhage (ICH) rates at 24 $(-2/+12)$ h. Symptomatic ICH will be defined as per the SITS-MOST definition: deterioration in NIHSS score of $\geq4$ points within $24\mathrm{{h}}$ from treatment and evidence of intraparenchymal hemorrhage type 2 in the $22\mathrm{-}36\mathrm{h}$ follow-up imaging scans;13 (3) Procedural related complications.  

All secondary endpoints will be evaluated by local blinded assessors to treatment arm (for clinical scales) or by independent Clinical Events Committee and Imaging Core Labs (for medical complications, parenchyma and vessel imaging variables).  

# Sample size estimates  

In the previous versions of the study protocol, the proportion of patients achieving $\mathrm{mRS~}0{\-}{\-}4$ at 90 days was used as the primary outcome. For an estimated difference of $20\%$ in proportions of of patients achieving mRS 0–4 at 90 days between the two groups $(60\%$ in the Solitaire arm vs. $40\%$ in the control arm),4,9,14 318 patients will be included for $5\%$ significance and $90\%$ power with a planned interim analysis after two-thirds of the sample size (212 patients) have achieved the 90 days follow-up.  

On February 23, 2021, before the planned interim analysis and before any data unblinding, the executive committee of BAOCHE decided to implement a change in the primary outcome of the study from the proportion of patients achieving mRS 0–4 to 0–3 at 90 days. Owing to inclusion criteria specific to BAOCHE, the absolute values representing the proportion of mRS 0–3 in the two groups remain difficult to estimate with precision. For this reason and also in order to deviate as little as possible from the initial statistical plan, the assumptions leading to the initial sample size calculation of total sample size of 318 patients with interim analysis at 212 patients have been not changed.  

# Discussion  

While advances in EVT technologies have led to promising results in recent published single arm studies of endovascular therapy for BAO,6–9 there is still no consensus whether patients with an acute BAO benefit from EVT because data from randomized clinical trials definitively confirming the benefit of this approach are lacking.15,16 BAOCHE will provide evidence on mechanical thrombectomy for treatment of BAO in the 6–24-h time window.  

# Summary and conclusions  

There is a lack of data from randomized clinical trials to establish the best treatment for acute BAO in the extended time window. BAOCHE aims to address this question by comparing outcomes in patients selected based on extent of baseline infarct between mechanical thrombectomy using the Solitaire device in conjunction with BMT to BMT alone.
