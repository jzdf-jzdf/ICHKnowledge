# Increased intracranial hemorrhage of mechanical thrombectomy in acute ischemic stroke patients with atrial fibrillation  

Kangmo Huang1 $\circledcirc$ · Mingming Zha2   · Jie Gao1 $\cdot$ Juan Du1 $\cdot$ Rui Liu1 $\bullet$ Xinfeng Liu1,2  

Published online: 12 September 2020

# Abstract  

The impact of atrial fibrillation (AF) on outcomes of mechanical thrombectomy (MT) for acute ischemic stroke (AIS) is controversial, and with a paucity of evidence base. This study aimed to investigate the potential association between AF and outcomes after MT in AIS patients. A post-hoc analysis of a multi-center prospective clinical trial was conducted. Before and after propensity score matching (PSM), the clinical features were compared between patients with and without AF. Multivariable logistic regression and mediation analyses were performed to assess the relationship between AF and ICH. Of the total 245 patients, 123 patients were included in the AF group. After PSM, the AF group showed more retrieval attempts $\scriptstyle{\calP}=0.004,$ ), comparable favorable outcome ( $\scriptstyleP=0.493,$ ), and mortality ( $\scriptstyle{P=0.362})$ at 90 days. Multivariate analysis revealed that AF was significantly associated with increased risk for ICH (OR 2.198; $95\%\mathrm{CI}1.099{-}4.395$ ; $P{=}0.026$ ). INR and retrieval attempts were found to act as partial mediations. In the subgroup with lower INR, AF still had a significant association with ICH (OR 2.496; $95\%\mathrm{CI}$ 1.331–4.679; $P{=}0.004.$ ). In AIS patients undergoing MT, AF was associated with more retrieval attempts and higher risk of any ICH. Of note, the effect of AF on the increased risk of ICH was partly attributable to the adjusted anticoagulation status and more retrieval attempts. It is crucial to elaborately prevent ICH after thrombectomy for stroke patients with AF.  

Keywords  Atrial fibrillation $\cdot$ Ischemic stroke $\cdot$ Mechanical thrombectomy $\cdot$ Intracranial hemorrhage

# Introduction  

Atrial fibrillation (AF) is a major risk factor for acute ischemic stroke (AIS) and is associated with about a five fold increased risk of ischemic stroke [1–3]. AIS patients with AF are characterized by older, more frequently female, more comorbidities than those without AF [4, 5]. Previous studies have demonstrated that AF is probably an independent risk factor of poor clinical outcomes and hemorrhagic transformation in AIS patients undergoing intravenous thrombolysis(IVT) [6–9]. Owing to its well-established efficacy, mechanical thrombectomy (MT) with stent retrievers served as a mainstay of reperfusion therapy for AIS [10]. However, there are scant data to prove the specific impact of AF on the safety and efficacy of MT for AIS [11, 12]. Due to the incomplete understanding of the impact exerted by AF, more appropriate intensive care and systemic therapies seems hard to achieve. Therefore, we performed a detailed analysis of prospectively collected data to determine the influence of AF on the clinical outcomes after MT in patients with AIS.  



# Methods  



# Data and study design  

This study was a post-hoc analysis based on a multi-center head-to-head clinical trial (the Captor trial, comparing Solitaire™ FR and Captor™ stent retriever, register code: ChiCTR1900025256). Participants were initially confirmed with large intracranial vessel occlusion by computed tomography angiography (CTA), magnetic resonance angiography (MRA) or digital subtraction angiography, and eligibility to initiate MT within $^{8\mathrm{~h~}}$ after stroke onset. The other detailed inclusion and exclusion criteria of the patients receiving intravascular therapy were in accordance with the 2018 AHA/ASA Guideline [10]. Subjects were divided into the AF-group and the non-AF group, according to the history of AF or electrocardiogram (ECG) recorded at admission. Data collection was conducted by an independent contract research organization, and image analysis was performed by an independent core laboratory in a blinded manner.  



# Baseline and clinical assessment  

Baseline demographic parameters, medical history, the National Institutes of Health Stroke Scale (NIHSS) score, laboratory tests, radiographic evaluation (CTA or MRA), and 12 lead electrocardiography were obtained.  

Revascularization was performed by MT with stent retrievers or bridging therapy with prior IVT in accordance with the guidelines at that time [10, 13]. IVT was administered within $4.5\mathrm{~h~}$ of stroke onset after excluding any contraindications. Time intervals from onset to treatment, from puncture to revascularization and the operation duration were documented. On the final angiogram, reperfusion status was classified according to the modified thrombolysis in cerebral ischemia (mTICI) scale, and a mTICI scale score of 2b or 3 was defined as successful revascularization [14]. Operative information such as the number of retrieval attempts and remedy with intra-arterial thrombolysis or other devices were recorded in detail. Retrieval attempt was recommended within three times, while it was actually at the discretion of operators. Postoperative conditions of patients were assessed by neurological function scale (the NIHSS) and follow-up imaging examinations performed within $24\pm6$ h after MT. Any ICH was ascertained by CT/MRI scan, and symptomatic intracranial hemorrhage (SICH) was defined as ICH with an increase of $\geq4$ points on the NIHSS within $^{24\textrm{h}}$ . [15, 16]  

Medical therapy with antiplatelet, anticoagulation therapies and symptomatic treatment was at the discretion of the attending physician according to the clinical conditions of patients. On day $7\pm2$ or hospital discharge if earlier, assessments of neurological function were performed on the participants again. All the adverse events were recorded whether related or not to stroke. Serious adverse events were defined as any events that resulted in life-threatening illness, prolonged hospitalization, or significant disability. A long-term follow-up was conducted through outpatient visits or telephone calls (median follow-up time, 90 days after thrombectomy, range from 76 to 104 days).  



# Clinical outcomes  

The primary effective outcome was set as the proportion of patients with 90-day modified rankin scale (mRS) 0–2 after MT. Secondary effective outcomes included the mRS score and the proportion of patients with 90-day mRS 0–1. The incidence of any ICH was defined as the primary safety outcome. Secondary safety outcomes were SICH and the allcause mortality at 7 days and 90 days after thrombectomy.  



# Statistical analysis  

Differences were analyzed with Student t-test, Mann–Whitney U, $\chi^{2}$ , or Fisher exact test, as appropriate.  

To balance heterogenicity in demographics, we used two propensity score (PS) methods for further analyses, namely PS adjustment and PS matching (PSM). Variables used for calculating PS included sex, age, hypertension, coronary heart disease, smoking, baseline NIHSS score, prothrombin time (PT) and international normalized ratio (INR). The 1:1 PSM was applied to create the matched AF group and non-AF group with a caliper distance of 0.1. After PSM, the differences between the two groups were compared again using the aforementioned statistical methods.  

Multivariate logistic regression analysis was performed in three models using the enter method. Model 1 was adjusted for demographics as age, sex, diabetes and smoke. Model 2 included additional adjustments for coagulation indexes (activated partial thromboplastin time [APTT], INR and PT). Model 3 was built upon Model 1 with adjustments for other variables significant in univariate analysis $(P\leq0.1)\$ . Besides, the predictive value of adding AF into models was estimated by the area under curve (AUC) and net reclassification index (NRI) of which value above 0 was regarded as significant [17].  

Mediation analysis was conducted by using the mediation package in R statistical software. Multiple imputation with chain equations was performed to account for missing values (Glu, $10[4.1\%]$ ; APTT, $10[4.1\%]$ ; PT, $6[2.4\%]$ , INR, $6[2.4\%],$ ). $P$ values of $\leq0.05$ were statistically significant. The statistical analyses were performed using the SPSS software package, version 25 (IBM-Armonk, NY) and R statistical software, version 3.6.3 (R Core Team (2020)]. R: A language and environment for statistical computing. R Foundation for Statistical Computing, Vienna, Austria. URL https://www.R-project.org/).  



# Results  



# Clinical characteristics  

Detailed demographics were displayed in Supplemental Table 1. A total of 245 patients enrolled in this study, comprising 123 patients in the AF group and 122 in the nonAF group. Compared with the non-AF group, patients in the AF-group had less frequently men ( $43.9\%$ vs. $68.9\%$ , $P<0.001\$ ), more advanced age (median [interquartile range, IQR], 74[67–69] years vs. 64[54–71] years, $P{<}0.001$ ), less smokers ( $16.3\%$ vs. $41.8\%$ , $P<0.001\$ ), higher proportions of hypertension ( $68.3\%$ vs. $45.9\%$ , $P{<}0.001$ ) and coronary heart disease $23.6\%$ vs. $13.1\%$ , $P{=}0.034.$ ). With respect to the severity of stroke onset, the median baseline NIHSS scores were higher in the AF group (16 vs. 14, $P=0.013$ ).  

After 1:1 PSM analysis, each of the 70 patients in the AFgroup and the non-AF group were extracted (Supplemental Fig. 1), with a good balance in the demographic characteristics and medical history as shown in Supplemental Table 1.  

Detailed treatment profiles and clinical outcomes before and after PSM are shown in Table 1. The matched patients with AF showed a trend for lower rate of thrombolysis $30.0\%$ vs. $42.9\%$ , $P{=}0.114.$ ). More retrieval attempts were performed in the AF group (2 vs. 1, $P{=}0.004;$ , while the successful recanalization rates were similar in both groups $88.6\%$ vs. $94.3\%$ , $P{=}0.227.$ ). There were no significant differences between the groups in time intervals, medication and follow-up NIHSS scores (all $P$ values $>0.05$ ).  



# Clinical outcomes  

Comparison of 90-day mRS distribution before and after PSM between the two groups was also illustrated in Fig. 1. In all datasets, less patients with AF achieved favorable outcomes (90-day mRS score 0–2: $32.5\%$ vs. $49.2\%$ , $P{=}0.008 $ ) and excellent outcomes (90-day mRS score 0–1: $22.8\%$ vs. $36.9\%$ , $P{=}0.016$ ). In addition, mortality at 90 days of the AF group was $30.1\%$ , higher than $16.4\%$ of the non-AF group $(P=0.011$ ). However, there were no longer statistically significant differences in the aforementioned outcomes between the two groups after PSM and PS adjustment (Table 1 and Supplemental Table 2).  

Notably, a significantly higher incidence of ICH was detected in the AF group after PSM $(47.1\%$ vs. $28.6\%$ , $P=0.023,$ ) and PS adjustment (OR 1.967; $95\%\mathrm{CI}$ 1.063–3.639, $P=0.031,$ . Therefore, the binary logistic regressions were conducted to further investigate the association between AF and ICH risks (Table 2). AF remained significantly associated with higher risk for ICH in adjusted models, as in Model 1 (OR 2.535; $95\%\mathrm{CI}$ 1.360–4.726, $P=0.003)$ and Model 2 (OR 2.334; $95\%\mathrm{CI}$ 1.232–4.419, $P{=}0.009.$ ). Furthermore, the association still reached statistical significance (OR 2.198; $95\%\mathrm{CI}$ 1.099–4.395, $P{=}0.026,$ in the Model 3 after adjusting for confounding factors, baseline blood glucose, INR, baseline NIHSS scores, anesthesia, ICA occlusion and retrieval attempts. Table 2 elucidated the improved AUC and NRI after adding AF into models for predicting ICH.  



# Mediation analyses  

Variable was considered as potential mediation if AF was associated with the variable and the variable was associated with the incidence of any ICH as well. After mediation analyzes in the potential mediations, two variables, INR value and retrieval attempts, were found to act as partial mediations. In the model which INR served as mediation, the indirect effect of AF on ICH accounted for $15.7\%$ ( $95\%\mathrm{CI}$ [0.012, 0.560], $P{=}0.038,$ of the total effect. When the number of retrieval attempts acted as mediation, it explained $15.6\%$ ( $95\%\mathrm{CI}$ [0.003, 0.570], $P{=}0.043,$ ) of the total effect (Fig. 2).  



# Subgroup analyses  

To detect possible interactions between AF and certain features in governing the incidence of ICH, we conducted subgroup analyses on the full data set, as shown in Fig. 3. The subgroup analyses revealed a significant impact of the INR value on the association between AF and the risk of ICH (OR 2.496 vs. 0.733, $P$ for interaction $=0.032\$ ). AF remained a significant association with ICH in patients with INR value lower than 1.125, but not in those with higher INR (OR 2.496 vs. 0.733). Significant interactions were not observed between AF and other predefined factors including age, sex, diabetes, hypertension, CHD, prior stroke, baseline NIHSS scores, operation duration, retrieval attempts and administration of thrombolysis.

Compared with patients without AF, patients with AF underwent significantly higher thrombectomy maneuver counts, despite the comparable finial recanalization rates. There are several possible reasons for more retrieval attempts in stroke patients with AF. First, due to higher proportions of fibrin/platelets, cardioembolic thrombi has greater hardness and toughness resulting in more attempts of thrombus withdrawal than non-cardioembolic thrombi [19]. Besides, fewer patients with AF receive thrombolysis because of the adjusted coagulation state and less benefit [7], so that preoperative emboli is not effectively dissolved in the absence of thrombolytic drugs.

This study indicated an increased incidence of ICH in AF patients with MT and the effect of AF on ICH was partly explained by the mediations, INR value and retrieval attempts. In previous studies, AF was considered as a wellrecognized risk factor for intracranial hemorrhage events following thrombolysis therapy in AIS patients [6, 20–23]. It was conventionally speculated that the increased risk for ICH in AF patients was mainly attributable to the adjusted coagulation state as a result of chronic anticoagulation. Nevertheless, the increasing number of studies elucidates that MT in acute ischemic stroke patients on oral anticoagulants seems to be safe without a significant increase in ICH [24–29]. According to this study, even in patients with lower INR value, a significant association was observed between AF and the increased ICH events. These findings challenge the conventional notion that the adjusted anticoagulation function accounts for the main effect of AF on ICH.  

As mentioned above, AF patients experience more retrieval attempts, which improve final recanalization rates along with an increased risk of ICH [30, 31]. Furthermore, it has been reported that AF patients are associated with greater volumes of hypo-perfused territory [23], which are validated to be especially vulnerable for hemorrhagic transformation due to the subsequent territorial damaging of the blood–brain barrier [10, 32, 33]. Therefore, altered coagulation status and the damaged blood–brain barrier are two potential mechanisms by which AF increases the ICH risk.  

In our cohort, AF was not an independent risk factor of worse functional outcomes in AIS patients undergoing MT, despite the impact of AF on the prognosis of AIS patients remained controversial. A previous metaanalysis showed that AF may increase the risk of SICH and decrease the achievement of a favorable outcome for patients after thrombolysis [9]. Whereas, Seet et al. and Frank et al. identified the presence of AF had no independent impact on stroke prognosis [34, 35]. Another study based on a large national database with AIS also reached similar conclusions, suggesting that MT for AIS results in comparable in-hospital mortality in individuals with and without AF [12]. Thus, it was conjectured that advanced age and accumulated comorbidities rather than lone AF might be related to poor outcomes.  

Given the unconvincing straightforward association between AF and stroke, an updated model emphasized that abnormal systemic and atrial substrate in addition to rhythm were involved in the pathophysiological process of stroke in AF [36]. Our findings strengthen the assumption that the effect of AF on the clinical prognosis and ICH after MT is achieved by multiple factors and the interaction among intricate mechanisms. This suggests that treatment strategies for stroke patients with AF should be carefully developed, especially, thoroughly monitoring to prevent ICH after thrombectomy.  

Despite various efforts, this study still had some limitations. First, due to design flaws of the original clinical trial, it was difficult to achieve an accurate TOAST classification to reflect the stroke mechanism. Second, there was a lack of detailed records of prior anticoagulants for patients with AF before stroke, resulting in neglecting certain heterogeneity in AF patients. Third, a relatively small sample size was not enough to generalize the association between AF and clinical outcomes of AIS patients undergoing MT. Overall, future studies with considerable sample sizes are warranted to comprehensively determine the substantial mechanisms by which AF increases the risk of hemorrhagic transformation after MT.

# Conclusion  

In AIS patients undergoing MT, AF was associated with more retrieval attempts and comparable finial recanalization rates. Multivariate analysis revealed that AF was an independent risk factor for any ICH, but not associated with poor stroke prognosis. Of note, the association between AF and increased risk of ICH might be attributable to not only the adjusted anticoagulation status, but also more retrieval attempts. Therefore, it is crucial to develop elaborate treatment strategies for stroke patients with AF, especially, thoroughly monitoring to prevent ICH after thrombectomy.
