# Safety and Efficacy of Reteplase Versus Alteplase for Acute Ischemic Stroke: A Phase 2 Randomized Controlled Trial  

Shuya Li , MD\*; Xuechun Wang , MD\*; Aoming Jin , PhD; Gaifen Liu , PhD; Hongqiu Gu PhD; Hao Li, PhD; Bruce C.V. Campbell , PhD; Marc Fisher , MD; Yi Yang , MD; Yan Wei , MD; Junhai Wang , MD; Yilong Wang, MD; Xingquan Zhao , MD; Liping Liu , MD; Zixiao Li , MD; Xia Meng, MD; Yongjun Wang , MD  

BACKGROUND: Reteplase is a more affordable new-generation thrombolytic with a prolonged half-life. We aimed to determine the safety dose range of reteplase for patients with acute ischemic stroke within 4.5 hours of onset.  

METHODS: This is a multicenter, prospective, randomized controlled, open-label, blinded-end point phase 2 clinical trial. Patients with acute ischemic stroke aged between 18 and 80 years who were eligible for standard intravenous thrombolysis were enrolled from 17 centers in China and randomly assigned (1:1:1) to receive intravenous reteplase $12+12\mathrm{mg}$ , intravenous reteplase $18+18$ mg, or intravenous alteplase $0.9\mathsf{mg}/\mathsf{kg}$ . The primary safety outcome was symptomatic intracranial hemorrhage (SITS definition) within 36 hours. The primary efficacy outcome was the proportion of patients with the National Institutes of Health Stroke Scale score of no more than 1 or a decrease of at least 4 points from the baseline at 14 days after thrombolysis.  

RESULTS: Between August 2019 and May 2021, 180 patients were randomly assigned to reteplase $12+12\mathrm{mg}$ $\left(\mathsf{n}{=}6\mathsf{1}$ ), reteplase $18+18~\mathrm{mg}$ $\scriptstyle(\mathtt{n}=67$ ), or alteplase $(\mathsf{n}{=}52)$ . Four patients did not receive the study agent. Symptomatic intracranial hemorrhage occurred in 3 of 60 $(5.0\%)$ in the reteplase $12+12$ mg group, 1 of 66 $(1.5\%)$ in the reteplase $18+18~\mathrm{mg}$ group, and 1 of 50 $(2.0\%)$ in the alteplase group $(P=0.53.$ ). The primary efficacy outcome in the modified intention-to-treat population occurred in 45 of 60 $(75.0\%)$ in the reteplase $12+12$ mg group (odds ratio, 0.85 $[95\%$ CI, 0.35–2.06]), 48 of 66 $(72.7\%)$ in the reteplase $18+18$ mg group (odds ratio, 0.75 $[95\%$ CI, 0.32–1.78]), and 39 of 50 $^{'}78.0\%)$ in alteplase group.  

CONCLUSIONS: Reteplase was well tolerated in patients with acute ischemic stroke within 4.5 hours of onset in China with a similar efficacy profile to alteplase. The efficacy and appropriate dosage of reteplase for patients with acute ischemic stroke need prospective validation.

Reteplase is a nonglycosylated variant of alteplase, which was approved in Europe and the United States in 1996 and in China in 2007 for acute myocardial infarction $^{8,9}|\boldsymbol{\mathfrak{n}}$ contrast to alteplase, the epidermal growth factor and fibronectin finger domains are absent in reteplase.10,11The result of these deletions is extended plasma half-life that permits a double-bolus regimen with fixed dosage. In the preclinical study, reteplase showed an ability to penetrate into blood clots but also had reduced fibrin specificity versus alteplase.12 In addition to ease of administration, reteplase is more affordable at $\$385$ perpatientversus $\$735$ for alteplase in China.  

Few data on the appropriate dosage of reteplase and its safety and efficacy compared with alteplase in AIS are available. The aim of this study was to compare the safety and efficacy of 2 doses of reteplase with standard alteplase in patients with acute ischemic stroke who are eligible for IVT within 4.5 hours from symptom onset.  

# METHODS  

# Data Availability  

Data from the study are available from the China National Clinical Research Center for Neurological Diseases. Maintenance of data availability is guaranteed by the corresponding author.  

All procedures performed in studies involving human participants were in accordance with the ethical standards of the institutional research committee and the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki.  

# Study Design  

This trial was a phase 2, multicenter, prospective, randomized controlled, open-label, blinded-end point clinical trial. The trial was conducted at 17 primary and comprehensive stroke centers across China (Table S1). The trial was ethically approved by the institutional review board at each local center and was done in accordance with the guidelines for Good Clinical Practice, the Declaration of Helsinki, and the Consolidated Standards of Reporting Trials. The trial was prospectively registered with https://www.clinicaltrials.gov (NCT04028518).  

# Participants  

Participants were eligible if they were aged between 18 and 80 years; were diagnosed with AIS; were eligible for thrombolysis within 4.5 hours onset according to guidelines; had a modified Rankin Scale (mRS) score of no more than 1 before enrollment; and with a National Institutes of Health Stroke Scale (NIHSS) score of 4 to 24. Patients were ineligible for participation if they had received or intended to proceed to endovascular thrombectomy. Additional information on inclusion and exclusion criteria is presented in Table S2. Written informed consent was provided by all participants or their representatives before their enrollment.  

# Randomization and Masking  

A simple stratified randomization was performed in a 1:1:1 ratio to receive intravenous reteplase (China Resources Angde Biotech Pharma Co, Ltd, China; $18~\mathsf{mg}$ per vial) $12+12\mathsf{mg},$ $18+18$ mg, or alteplase (Boehringer Ingelheim, Germany; 50 mg, 20 mg per vial) $0.9\mathrm{{mg}/kg}$ (to a maximum of $90~\mathrm{mg}$ ), and the onset to start of treatment time was used as a stratification factor $\stackrel{\prime}{\leq}3$ hours versus 3–4.5 hours). After each subject was screened and qualified, the investigator logged into the randomization and experimental drug management system (Randomisation and Trial Supply Management eBalance, version 4.0; Zhejiang Taimei Medical Technology Co, Ltd, China) to obtain a treatment allocation. All other treatments were guided by the standard of care for ischemic stroke.  

The intravenous thrombolytic treatment was open-labeled. Outcomes were assessed by an independent investigator who was blinded during all procedures and trained in mRS, NIHSS, and other clinical evaluations.  

# Procedures  

Reteplase was given immediately after randomization as a double, intravenous bolus (over 2 minutes for each bolus) with $12+12$ or $18{+}18\mathrm{mg}$ at 30-minute intervals. Alteplase was given at a dose of $0.9\mathrm{{mg/kg}}$ (maximum dose $90~\mathrm{mg},$ ), $10\%$ of the dose was given as a bolus within 1 minute, and the residual dose was administered via intravenous infusion in the subsequent 60 minutes. Reteplase should be stored and transported at a temperature between $2^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$ and $8^{\circ}{\mathrm{C}},$ in a sealed container protected from light, while ensuring it does not freeze. Alteplase, on the other hand, should be stored as lyophilized activase at room temperature below $25^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$ and also protected from light. It is recommended to use the prepared solution immediately after preparation. Studies have demonstrated that the prepared alteplase solution remains stable for up to 24 hours when stored at temperatures between $2^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$ and $8^{\circ}\mathrm{C}$ without freezing. Subjects were closely monitored within the first 24 hours of administration. The following drugs were prohibited during the study: other thrombolytic drugs such as urokinase, human tissue plasminogen kinase, and antifibrinogen drugs. Antiplatelet drugs and anticoagulants were prohibited within 24 hours after thrombolysis. Other treatments were performed with adherence to established clinical principles and medical practice guidelines.13 Non-contrast computed tomography imaging or magnetic resonance imaging was done to detect any hemorrhage at 24 to 36 hours after randomization. The mRS and Barthel index score at 90 days were assessed in people or by telephone. Serious adverse events and adverse events were categorized according to standard terminology.  

Venous blood samples were collected and tested at local hospitals. Detailed laboratory methods for coagulation assays are listed in Table S3.  

# Outcomes  

Safety outcomes were incidences of symptomatic intracranial hemorrhage (sICH) with 3 definitions: (1) NINDS criteria (The National Institute of Neurological Disorders and Stroke rt-PA Stroke Study): a hemorrhage was considered symptomatic if it had not been seen on a previous CT scan, but there was subsequently either a suspicion of hemorrhage or any decline in neurological status. To detect intracranial hemorrhage, CT scans were required 24 hours and 7 to 10 days after the onset of stroke and when clinical findings suggested hemorrhage.14 (2) ECASS-II criteria (Second European-Australasian Acute Study): any hemorrhage with neurological deterioration, as indicated by an NIHSS score that was higher by 4 points or more than the value at baseline or the lowest value in the first 7 days, or any hemorrhage leading to death.15,16 (3) SITS-MOST criteria (Safe Implementation of Thrombolysis in Stroke-Monitoring Study): parenchymal hematoma type 2 or remote parenchymal hematoma on the imaging scan obtained 22 to 36 hours after treatment, plus neurological deterioration, as indicated by a score on the NIHSS that was higher by 4 points or more than the baseline value or the lowest value between baseline and 24 hours or hemorrhage leading to death.17 The primary safety outcome was sICH defined by SITS. The other safety outcomes included all causes of death within 90 days, intracranial hemorrhage, extracranial bleeding, the proportion of adverse events, or serious adverse events within 90 days.  

The primary efficacy outcome was the proportion of subjects with NIHSS score $\leq1$ or a decrease of ${\ge}4$ points between baseline and day 14 after treatment. Secondary efficacy outcomes included the proportion of subjects with an NIHSS score of $\leq1$ or a decrease of ${\ge}4$ points between baseline and 72 hours after treatment, the proportion of subjects with an mRS score of 0 to 1 at 30 and 90 days, the proportion of subjects with mRS score of 0 to 2 at 30 and 90 days, ordinal distribution of mRS at 30 and 90 days, and the proportion of subjects with the Barthel index score ${\ttz95}$ points at 30 and 90 days.  

# Statistical Analysis  

Efficacy and safety outcomes were analyzed in the modified intention-to-treat population that included all patients who received the allocated thrombolytic. The per-protocol population was defined as patients who completed the assigned treatment without major violation of the trial protocol or missing data for primary efficacy end points. Mean (SD) or median (interquartile range) was used for continuous variables, and the number $(\%)$ was used for categorical variables. The mean level and variation of the prothrombin time, international normalized ratio, activated partial thromboplastin time, fibrinogen, thrombin time, and platelet at baseline, 24 hours of follow-up, and  

15 days of follow-up in 3 groups were reported. Differences in efficacy outcomes among the 3 groups were assessed using logistic regression models. Odds ratios (ORs) and $95\%$ CIs were reported. The rates of safety outcomes were reported by 3 groups and were tested by the $\chi^{2}$ test or the Fisher exact test. All tests were 2-sided, and $P{<}0.05$ was considered to be statistically significant. Statistical analyses were conducted with SAS software, version 9.4 (SAS Institute).  

# RESULTS  

# Baseline Characteristics  

Between August 2019 and May 2021, a total of 180 subjects were randomized. Four subjects did not receive a study agent and were excluded from the modified intention-to-treat analysis. Among 176 patients, 60 were assigned to receive $12+12\mathrm{mg}$ of reteplase, 66 were assigned to receive $18+18$ mg of reteplase, and 50 were assigned to receive $0.9\mathrm{{mg/kg}}$ of alteplase (Figure 1). The characteristics of the patients at baseline were similar between the 2 groups (Table 1). The mean age of the patients was 62.6 $(\pm9.71\$ ) years, and $74.1\%$ were men. The median NIHSS score was 6 (interquartile range, 5–9), and the median time from stroke onset to the needle was 213.0 minutes (interquartile range, 162.5–240.0). The characteristics of the patients at baseline were similar between the 2 groups (Table 1).  

Four patients in the reteplase $12+12$ mg group, 2 patients in the reteplase $18+18$ mg group, and 2 patients in the alteplase group were excluded from the per-protocol analysis due to missing primary efficacy outcome data at 14 days. Nine patients in the reteplase $12+12$ mg group, 10 patients in the reteplase $18+18$ mg group, and 3 patients in the alteplase group who had major deviations from the protocol were excluded from the per-protocol analysis (Table S4).  

# Safety Outcomes  

No safety outcome data were missing. The SITS definition of sICH occurred in 5 patients $(4.5\%)$ , including 3 $(5.0\%)$ patients in the low-dose group of reteplase, 1 $(1.5\%)$ patients in the high-dose group of reteplase, and 1 $(2.0\%)$ patient in the alteplase group. The incidence of sICH defined by NINDS and ECASS-II was similar in the 3 groups. No significant difference was found in serious adverse events, death, or other extracranial bleeding events (Table 2). The reteplase $18+18$ mg group caused significant hypofibrinogenemia $\left(0.91~9/\mathsf{L}\right.$ versus $2.42{\mathfrak{g}}/{\mathsf{L}},$ ), prolonged prothrombin time ( $13.34~\varsigma$ versus $11.60~\mathrm{s},$ ), thrombin time (25.45 s versus 18.17 s), and international normalized ratio (1.16 versus 1.01) at 24 hours after administration of thrombolytics versus alteplase group. The reteplase $18+18$ mg group also numerically consumed more platelet and prolonged activated partial thromboplastin time than alteplase without significant difference (Figure 2). Serious adverse events and adverse events within 90 days according to the MedDRA organ class are presented in Tables S5 and S6.

# Efficacy Outcomes  

In the intention-to-treat analysis, 45 of 60 $(75.0\%)$ patients in the reteplase $12+12$ mg group (OR, 0.85 $[95\%$ CI, 0.35–2.06]), 48 of 66 $72.7\%)$ patients in the reteplase $18+18$ mg group (OR, 0.75 $[95\%$ CI, $0.32-$ 1.78]), and 39 of 50 $(78.0\%)$ patients in the alteplase group achieved the primary outcome (Table 3). The perprotocol analysis of primary efficacy outcome showed similar results (Table S5). The proportion of patients with excellent functional outcome (mRS, 0–1) in the reteplase $12+12$ mg group was $66.1\%$ (OR, 1.03 $[95\%$ CI, $0.45-$ 2.24]) and, in the reteplase $18+18$ mg group, was $73.4\%$ (OR, 1.42 $[95\%$ CI, 0.64–3.19]) compared with $66.0\%$ in alteplase group on day 90 (Table 3; Figure 3). The distribution of the 90-day mRS scores in the intention-to-treat and per-protocol analysis is depicted in Figure 3. No significant differences were observed in all other secondary outcomes among the 3 groups. Sensitivity analysis in the per-protocol analysis was shown in Table S7 and was consistent with the intention-to-treat analysis. Subgroup analysis of primary efficacy outcome according to age, sex, baseline NIHSS scores, and onset-to-needle time was shown in Table S8, and no significant differences were observed in the 3 treatment groups for the subgroups of sex, baseline NIHSS scores, and onset-toneedle time.

# DISCUSSION  

In this clinical trial, no statistically significant differences were found for the rate of death and sICH among the $12+12$ mg of reteplase group, the $18+18$ mg of reteplase group, and the alteplase group in patients with AIS within 4.5 hours onset who were eligible for IVT treatment. Patients treated with $12+12$ mg of reteplase and $18+18$ mg of reteplase had more extracranial bleeding than those treated with $0.9\mathrm{{mg/kg}}$ of alteplase, and the proportions of early neurological improvement at 14 days or excellent neurological functional outcome at 90 days among patients treated with reteplase were similar to those treated with alteplase. The same result was obtained after adjusting the imbalance in the baseline NIHSS score between the treatment groups. This result suggested that the evaluation of reteplase as an alternative to alteplase for thrombolytic therapy in AIS warrants investigation in a phase 3 trial.  

Previous studies on reteplase for IVT were mainly focused on patients with acute myocardial infarction (AMI) $9,18\substack{-20}|\boldsymbol{\mathrm{n}}$ the single-blind randomized trials of reteplase compared with alteplase infusion in AMI (RAPID I [More Rapid, Complete, and Stable Coronary Thrombolysis With Bolus Administration of Reteplase Compared With Alteplase Infusion in Acute Myocardial Infarction]),18 the $10+10$ MU $\mathrm{\:18+18~mg)}$ reteplase group achieved better 90-minute and $5^{-}$ to 14-day early restoration and maintenance of normal (Thrombolysis in Myocardial Infarction [TIMI] grade 3) flow than the alteplase group ( $63\%$ $[90\%$ CI, $55\%{-}71\%]$ versus $49\%$ $[90\%$ CI, $41\%{-}57\%]$ ; $\mathrm{\it{P=0.019}}$ and $88\%$ $[90\%$ CI, $82\%{-}94\%]$ versus $71\%$ $[90\%$ CI, $63\%\mathrm{-}79\%]$ ; $P\mathrm{<}0.001.$ ) with the same profile of safety. In RAPID II research,19 infarct-related coronary artery patency (TIMI grade 2 or 3) and complete patency (TIMI grade 3) at 90 minutes after the start of thrombolytic therapy were significantly higher in the reteplase-treated patients than alteplasetreated patients (TIMI grade 2 or 3: $83.4\%$ versus $73.3\%$ for front-loaded alteplase-treated patients; $\widetilde{\calP}{=}0.03$ ; TIMI grade 3: $59.9\%$ versus $45.2\%$ ; $\widetilde{P=}0.01$ ). There was no significant difference between reteplase and alteplase in bleeding requiring a transfusion ( $12.4\%$ versus $9.7\%$ ) or hemorrhagic stroke ( $1.2\%$ versus $1.9\%$ ). The mortality rate in the GUSTO III trial (Global Utilization of Streptokinase and t-PA for Occluded Coronary Arteries III) between 30 days and 1 year in 13 883 patients did not differ between the reteplase group and tPA (tissue-type plasminogen activator) group.21,22 GUSTO IV and V indicated that combined use of reduced-dose reteplase and abciximab was safe and effective in AMI thrombolysis and percutaneous coronary intervention.20,23Also, in the INJECT trial (International Joint Efficacy Comparison of Thrombolytics) research,19 6010 patients with AMI received reteplase at a dose of $10+10$ MU or streptokinase 1.5 MU for IVT, and there was no significant difference in mortality at 35 days $9\%$ versus $9.5\%$ ) and 180 days ( $11\%$ versus $12\%$ ), but reteplase allergy was less and adverse reactions were fewer compared with streptokinase. In brief, reteplase ( $\mathrm{\:18+18~mg})$ ) does not increase the risk of severe bleeding and mortality versus standard alteplase in patients with AMI.24 Based on the evidence for the use of reteplase in AMI, we selected $18+18$ mg as the high dose for AIS indication, which is consistent with the use in AMI. The low dose of reteplase for AIS was chosen based on the dosage of alteplase in patients with AIS being about $60\%$ of that for patients with AMI,2,25 and considering practicalities, $12+12\mathrm{mg}$ was selected as the low-dose group in this clinical trial.

Tenecteplase, as a variant of alteplase, was noninferior to alteplase in patients with ischemic stroke.26,27 Up until now, no studies comparing directly tenecteplase with reteplase have been reported. Llevadot et $a|^{28}$ had indirectly compared reteplase to tenecteplase in patients with AMI (meta-analysis) and found no significant differences among them in terms of death and disabling stroke in patients with AMI. As one of the bolus thrombolytics, reteplase may be another alternative to alteplase in patients with acute ischemic stroke.  

Fibrin affinity was a double-edged sword for thrombolysis. The balance lies in greater potency for the thrombolysis effect while preserving the integrity of systemic coagulation.29,30 Previous studies supported the concept that early fibrinogen degradation was a relevant cause of major bleeding complications.29 Nevertheless, administration of fibrinogen concentrate resulted in no significant difference in the amount of intraoperative blood loss compared with placebo for patients with intraoperative bleeding during high-risk cardiac surgery.30 The lack of the finger domain reduced the fibrin specificity of reteplase and improved the ability to penetrate blood clots.10,11 We found that reteplase induced dosedependent fibrinogen degradation. Fibrinogen was 1.65 ${\mathfrak{g}}/{\mathsf{L}}$ in the $12+12$ mg group while $0.91{\:}9/{\mathsfL}$ in the $18+18$ mg group. The proportion of intracranial hemorrhage also numerically increased with the dose increase $(6.7\%$ in the $12+12$ mg group versus $10.6\%$ in the $18+18~\mathrm{mg}$ group), as well as other extracranial bleeding $30\%$ in the $12+12$ mg group versus $42.4\%$ in the $18+18$ mg group). However, the proportion of sICH, which affected prognosis, did not increase ${\it\Delta\phi}(5.0\%$ in the $12+12$ mg group versus $1.5\%$ in the $18{+}18\mathsf{mg}\mathsf{group},$ ). The proportion of patients who had an mRS score of 0 to 1 on day 90 also numerically increased in a dose-dependent manner $(66.1\%$ in the $12+12\mathrm{mg}$ group versus $73.4\%$ in the $18+18~\mathrm{mg}$ group). Therefore, the risk-benefit balance of reteplase compared with alteplase requires validation in a larger RCT study.

# Limitations  

This study had some limitations. First, the small sample size limited the robust recommendation of optimal dose. Second, bias due to the open-label design cannot be excluded although outcomes were assessed by blinded investigators. Third, the index of fibrinogen degradation was not prespecified; thus, the dynamic changes in fibrinogen level within 24 hours were unknown. However, we still observed a similar profile of clinical efficacy outcomes in the reteplase group and the alteplase group without a significant increase in the sICH rate. Moreover, the limited representation of females in this study may impede its generalizability to female patients.  

# Conclusions  

Both $12+12\mathrm{mg}$ and $18+18~\mathrm{mg}$ of reteplase were tolerated in patients with acute ischemic stroke within 4.5 hours of onset in China with a similar efficacy profile of standard alteplase. The efficacy and appropriate dosage of reteplase for patients with acute ischemic stroke need further prospective validation.
