# ABSTRACT  

Background  Middle meningeal artery (MMA) embolization has been proposed as a treatment of chronic subdural hematoma (CSDH). The benefit of the procedure has yet to be demonstrated in a randomized controlled trial. We aim to assess the efficacy of MMA embolization in reducing the risk of CSDH recurrence 6 months after burr-hole surgery compared with standard medical treatment in patients at high risk of postoperative recurrence.  

Methods  The EMPROTECT trial is a multicenter open label randomized controlled trial (RCT) involving 12 French centers. Adult patients $(\geq18$ years) operated for CSDH recurrence or for a first episode with a predefined recurrence risk factor are randomized 1:1 to receive either MMA embolization within 7 days of the burr-hole surgery (experimental group) or standard medical care (control group). The number of patients to be included is 342.  

Results  The primary outcome is the rate of CSDH recurrence at 6 months. Secondary outcomes include the rate of repeated surgery for a homolateral CSDH recurrence during the 6-month follow-up period, the rate of disability and dependency at 1 and 6 months, defined by a modified Rankin Scale (mRS) score ${\ge}4$ , mortality at 1 and 6 months, total cumulative duration of hospital stay during the 6-month follow-up period, directly or indirectly related to the CSDH and embolization procedure-related complication rates.  

Conclusions  The EMPROTECT trial is the first RCT evaluating the benefit of MMA embolization as a surgical adjunct for the prevention of CSDH recurrence. If positive, this trial will have a significant impact on patient care. Trial registration number  NCT04372147.

Middle meningeal artery (MMA) embolization has been proposed as a potential treatment to prevent CSDH recurrence.9 Embolization can be considered either as a first line option or as an adjunct to surgery.? Preliminary non-randomized data suggest that postoperative MMA embolization may reduce the recurrence rate of CSDH in patients at risk of recurrence.10  

The aim of the EMPROTECT (Embolization of the Middle Meningeal Artery for the Prevention of Chronic Subdural Hematoma Recurrence in High Risk Patients) trial is to assess the efficacy of MMA embolization, performed within 7 days of CSDH burr-hole surgery, in reducing the risk of CSDH recurrence at 6 months as compared with standard medical treatment in patients at high risk of postoperative recurrence.

# Study population  

The population is that of adult patients ( $\stackrel{\prime}{=}18$ years old) at high risk of recurrence following burr-hole surgery for a CSDH or a CSDH recurrence. The literature regarding risk factors of postoperative recurrence suffers from a low level of evidence and low reproducibility.11–16  

Because there is poor consensus in the literature on the risk factors of CSDH recurrence,11–13 16 the definition of patients considered at higher risk of recurrence was elaborated by consensus among the study investigators. Patients meeting the eligibility criteria (table 1) will be included and randomized after signing an informed consent form (by the patient or a delegated close relative or a support person) obtained by an interventional neuroradiologist or a neurosurgeon investigator.

# Randomization and blinding  

Patients will be screened and included (table  2) within a maximum of 7 days following index surgery. Intervention will be allocated 1:1 via a central web-based randomization system (CLEANWEB). The randomization sequence is by random permuted blocks of small size with stratification on the center, treatment with antiplatelet or anticoagulant medication (vs no such treatment), and unilateral/bilateral CSDH status.  

This is an open trial. There will be no blinding of the procedure. The primary and secondary endpoints will be evaluated by a local neurosurgeon or interventional neuroradiologist investigator. An evaluation of the primary endpoint will be performed by an independent adjudication committee, blinded to the randomization arm.  

# Intervention in the experimental group  

# MMA embolization  

In the experimental group, patients will receive MMA embolization after having undergone CT angiography of the supra-aortic vessels and intracranial vessels, within 7 days of surgery. In the first version of the protocol, the embolization procedure was authorized under local anesthesia or conscious sedation. In the second version of the protocol (taking effect on June 10, 2020) general anesthesia was also authorized. In the third version of the protocol (taking effect on April 30, 2021), the CT angiography is not mandatory anymore if the patient has undergone a CT angiography scan of the supraaortic vessels in the past year, deemed to be of sufficient quality by the investigator. The aim of the CT angiography is to check the feasibility of the arterial catheterization and embolization procedure. The following specific points are to be checked on the CT angiography: the presence of an arterial thrombus in the aortic arch precluding aortic arch navigation, and assessment of supra-aortic vessel anatomy in order to plan for radial or femoral access for the procedure. In addition, specific anatomical variants contraindicating MMA embolization will be checked for on the brain CT angiography: MMA originating from the ophthalmic artery with agenesis of the foramen spinosum, and absence of an MMA originating from the internal maxillary artery.  

The MMA embolization procedure can be performed by radial or femoral access. The MMA is catheterized with a Progreat 2.4 (Terumo, Shibuya, Tokyo, Japan) microcatheter and occluded by free-flow injection of $300{-}500\upmu\mathrm{m}$ EmboSphere (Merit Medical,  

South Jordan, UT) tris-acryl gelatin microspheric (TAGM) particles. If a dangerous anastomosis is identified on the superselective angiogram of the MMA, either the artery is embolized downstream of the anastomosis with a sufficient security margin or the anastomosis is secured by coiling using Optima coils (Balt, Montmorency, France) deployed through a Vasco 10 (Balt) coiling microcatheter. The technical endpoint of the embolization procedure is occlusion of both anterior and posterior branches of the MMA by the microparticles, either through injection from the common trunk or by selective catheterization of each individual branch. In case a patient is included following the surgery for a one-sided recurrence of an initially bilateral CSDH operated bilaterally, the embolization procedure must be performed on both right and left MMAs.  

EmboSphere (Merit Medical) particles are non-resorbable, calibrated, biocompatible, hydrophilic TAGM microspheres impregnated with porcine gelatin. They are an acrylic polymer derivative in a sodium chloride suspension solution. Particles are provided in a prefilled syringe.  

# Best medical treatment  

In addition to the MMA embolization procedure, the patient will receive the best medical treatment, which can include:  

► Standard post-surgical care   
► Standard nursing care   
► Symptomatic treatments including medical treatment of pain if required Corticosteroid medication if deemed necessary by the attending physician Clinical and imaging follow-up as deemed necessary by the attending physician depending on patient status   
► Rehabilitation if necessary.  

# Intervention in the control group  

In the control group, the patients will receive the best medical treatment alone.  

# Follow-up visits  

There will be two follow-up visits: one M1 intermediate visit at one month (T1; spanning from T1-7 days to $\mathrm{T}1+15$ days) and one final M6 visit at 6 months $\ensuremath{\left(\ensuremath{\mathrm{T}}2\pm28\right)}$ days). These visits are part of usual care and will be performed by a neurosurgeon or an interventional neuroradiologist.  

Each visit will include:  

► A non-contrast head CT scan ► Clinical examination with a scoring of the mRS ► Recording of all primary and secondary outcomes ► Recording of all adverse events and serious adverse events.  

If for any reason the follow-up visits are not performed, the investigator will call the patient, a legal representative or a healthcare giver for a phone interview.  

# Outcomes  

Primary outcome  

Rate of chronic subdural hematoma recurrence at 6 months, defined as:  

$\blacktriangleright$ Reappearance of a homolateral CSDH with a midline shift $\ge5\mathrm{mm}$ or a symptomatic homolateral CSDH, including leading to death   
$\blacktriangleright$ Or, the presence of a homolateral CSDH ${>}10\mathrm{mm}$ in maximal thickness on the 6 months control head CT scan   
$\blacktriangleright$ Or, the need for repeated surgery for a homolateral CSDH recurrence Or, the need for a new hospital admission in relation to a homolateral CSDH recurrence.  

# Secondary outcomes  

The secondary endpoints are the following:  

$\blacktriangleright$ Rate of repeated surgery for a homolateral CSDH recurrence during the 6-month follow-up period Rate of disability and dependency at 1 and 6 months, defined by an mRS score $\geq4$ . The mRS is an ordered scale coded from 0 (no symptoms at all) through 5 (severe disability) to 6 (death)   
► Mortality rate at 1 and 6 months   
► Total cumulative duration of hospital stay, during the 6-month follow-up period, directly or indirectly related to the CSDH Minor and major embolization procedure-related complication rates.  

# Adjudication committee  

An independent adjudication committee blinded to the treatment group will evaluate the primary endpoint. The adjudication committee is composed of three members, neurosurgeons or interventional neuroradiologists, independent from the trial and from the investigating centers. The committee will adjudicate the following cases (the list has been modified in the fourth and fifth versions of the protocol taking effect on July 27, 2021 and June 7, 2022, respectively; the final list is provided below):  

►	 A recurrence following the definition reported in this protocol according to the local investigator   
► A hospital admission potentially related to the CSDH   
► A medical visit other than the M1 and M6 planned visits (see follow-up section), potentially related to the CSDH A head CT or MRI scan, other than those planned at M1 and M6 endpoints (see follow-up section), potentially related to a CSDH recurrence   
$\blacktriangleright$ Any new surgery potentially related to the CSDH   
$\blacktriangleright$ Any neurological symptoms.  

# Sample size calculation  

In order to show a reduction of the primary outcome event (CSDH recurrence) from $1{5}\%$ (control arm) to $5\%$ (intervention arm) with an $80\%$ power and a bilateral overall alpha (α) risk of $5\%$ , 142 patients per arm are necessary. The sample size has been calculated considering two sequential tests planned according to the Lan and Demets method with an O’Brien and Fleming $\upalpha$ -spending function with an interim analysis after the end of the 6-month follow-up period of the first 129 patients, representing a $37.5\%$ information fraction, at a nominal $\upalpha$ threshold of 0.001 and an analysis at the end of the study at the threshold of 0.05. Supposing a $20\%$ rate of lost to follow-up, the total population was calculated to be 342 patients, 171 in each arm.  

# Statistical analyses  

The statistical analysis will be performed by a biostatistician of the Clinical Research Unit of Pitié-Salpêtrière University Hospital, under the supervision of the study methodologist Professor Agnès Dechartres.  

Data will be analyzed using SAS software (SAS Institute, Cary, NC) or R with the latest version available (R Core Team (2017). R: A language and environment for statistical computing. R Foundation for Statistical Computing, Vienna, Austria. https:// www.R-project.org/). All statistical tests will be two-sided.  

The selection process will be described using a flow chart following the Consolidated Standards of Reporting Trials (CONSORT) statement. Baseline characteristics will be described in each group with mean $(\pm\mathrm{SD})$ or median (IQR) as appropriate for quantitative variables and as numbers and percentages for qualitative variables.  

We planned a sequential analysis of the primary outcome with two analyses: an interim analysis after the evaluation of the primary outcome for the first 129 patients, and a final analysis at the end of the study (end of follow-up for the last included participant). This sequential analysis is planned to be able to stop the trial in case of a large difference between the two groups. It is planned according to the Lan and DeMets method, with the O’Brien and Fleming α spending function. The trial can be stopped after the interim analysis in case of a difference between the two groups attested at the threshold of 0.001 or for futility in case of a low conditional power.  

The interim and final analyses will be the comparison of the rate of CSDH recurrence at 6 months between the two groups in the intent-to-treat population using a logistic mixed model taking into account stratification factors. Randomization arm, anticoagulant/antiplatelet medication versus none and unilateral/ bilateral CSDH status will be included as fixed effects and study center as a random effect.  

The significance threshold of the intermediate analysis has limited impact on the $\upalpha$ risk. The final analysis will therefore be at a threshold of 0.05 despite the interim analysis.  

The primary analysis will be carried out in the intent-to-treat population (defined as all included patients in the group in which they were randomized) on the primary outcome as adjudicated by the adjudication committee. Missing data will be imputed using multiple imputation and patients deceased without recurrence will be considered as treatment failure.  

We will conduct a sensitivity analysis on the primary outcome without adjudication of the recurrence of CSDH (evaluation of the primary outcome by the local investigators) and a sensitivity analysis excluding patients in the intervention arm who will not have undergone the embolization procedure.  

Two subgroup analyses for the primary outcome will be conducted: according to the unilateral versus bilateral CSDH status and anticoagulant/antiplatelet therapy medication status (yes vs no).  

Reporting of adverse events, independent data safety monitoring board related information, data collection and monitoring, data access and sharing, quality control, and authorship rules are provided as online supplemental methods.

# DISCUSSION  

The EMPROTECT trial aims to determine whether MMA particle embolization as an adjunct to surgery reduces recurrence risk in the management of symptomatic CSDH in patients at risk of recurrence.  

Particle embolization was chosen because of previous nonrandomized preliminary data pointing to the potential benefit on patient outcome,9 10 and because particles are the most versatile and simple to use embolic agents. Smaller caliber particles, ethylene vinyl alcohol and N-butyl cyanoacrylate liquid embolic agents are expected to provide for a more distal embolization and devascularization, although with a potentially higher risk of non-target embolizations and clinically significant consequences. The choice was made to prioritize safety in the EMPROTECT trial design, in turn guiding the choice of the embolic agent for the trial MMA embolization procedure (TAGM $300{-}500\upmu\mathrm{m}$ calibrated microspheres). More specifically, the meningeal arterial network is highly anastomotic, with primary anastomotic arteries (PAA; $100{-}300\upmu\mathrm{m}$ in diameter) in turn giving rise to secondary anastomotic arteries (SAA; $50{-}90\upmu\mathrm{m}$ in diameter).17 TAGM $300{-}500\upmu\mathrm{m}$ calibrated microspheres are not expected to penetrate PAA or SAA, therefore greatly reducing the risk of nontarget embolization. This may come at the expense of reduced devascularization and potentially reduced clinical benefit. We expect future trials comparing different embolization agents will tackle this issue.  

On September 9, 2023, 13 other randomized trials are recorded on ClinicalTrials.gov under the headings ‘chronic subdural hematoma’ and ‘embolization’ along with five other non-randomized studies. The different trials registered aim to demonstrate the benefit of MMA embolization in CSDH management using different embolic agents (particles and liquid) in different settings of CSDH management (operated and nonoperated patients). The benefit of MMA embolization in CSDH patient management has not yet been demonstrated in any RCT, regardless of patient management (surgically operated or not) or embolic agent used. We chose to focus on a population which stands to benefit most from a reduction of CSDH recurrence risk given that these patients are, among those surgically managed, most likely to present with recurrence and subsequent complications. This does not preclude further investigation to extend indications for CSDH embolization in other patient groups if the procedure indeed demonstrates to be beneficial in the EMPROTECT study population.  

The main limitation of the trial is that it will not inform on the utility of MMA embolization in all CSDH patients.  

# SUMMARY AND CONCLUSIONS  

EMPROTECT is the first RCT assessing MMA particle embolization as a postoperative adjunct to burr-hole surgery for the treatment of CSDH at risk of recurrence. The EMPROTECT trial is poised to inform clinical decision-­making with high quality evidence on an important aspect of CSDH management. If embolization is shown to reduce CSDH postoperative recurrence rate, the trial will dramatically modify management of a significant number of patients in neurosurgical practice.
