# ABSTRACT  

Background  The influence of leukoaraiosis in patients with acute ischemic stroke (Als) given intra-arterial treatment (IAT) with or without preceding intravenous thrombolysis (IVT) remains unknown.  

Objective  To assess the clinical and radiological outcomes of IAT in patients with or without leukoaraiosis.  

Methods  Patients of the direct mechanical thrombectomy trial (DIRECT-­MT) whose leukoaraiosis grade could be assessed were included. DIRECT-­MT was a randomized clinical trial performed in China to assess the effect of direct IAT compared with intravenous thrombolysis plus IAT. We employed the Age-Related White Matter Changes Scale for grading leukoaraiosis (ARWMC, 0 indicates no leukoaraiosis, 1–2 indicates mild-to-moderate leukoaraiosis, and 3 indicates severe leukoaraiosis) based on brain CT. The primary outcome was the score on the modified Rankin Scale (mRS) assessed at 90 days.  

Results  There were 656 patients in the trial, 649 patients who were included, with 432 patients without leukoaraiosis, and 217 $(33.4\%)$ patients with leukoaraiosis divided into mild-to-moderate $(\mathsf{n}{=}139)$ and severe groups $(\n{=}78)$ . Leukoaraiosis was a predictor of a worse mRS score (adjusted OR $(\mathsf{a}0\mathsf{R}){=}0.7$ $(95\%$ CI 0.5 to 0.8)) and higher mortality $\left(\mathsf{a}\mathsf{OR}=1.4\right.$ (1.1 to 1.9)), but it was not associated with symptomatic intracranial hemorrhage (sICH) $\scriptstyle(\partial\mathrm{OR}=0.9$ (0.5 to 1.5)). IVT preceding IAT did not increase sICH risk for patients with no ( $\mathsf{aOR}{=}1.4$ (0.6 to 3.4)), mild-to-moderate $\cdot\mathsf{a}\mathsf{OR}{=}1.5$ (0.3 to 7.8)), or severe $:a0R=1.5$ (0.1 to 21.3)) leukoaraiosis.  

Conclusion  Patients with leukoaraiosis with AIS due to large vessel occlusion are at increased risk of a poor functional outcome after IAT but demonstrate similar sICH rates, and IVT preceding IAT does not increase the risk of sICH in Chinese patients with leukoaraiosis.

# METHODS  

DIRECT-­MT (ClinicalTrials.gov, number NCT03469206) was a randomized controlled trial conducted at 41 centers. The study methods and patient eligibility criteria have been reported previously.8 9 The trial screened 1586 patients; 656 underwent randomization, and 654 were included in the final analysis. In this subgroup analysis, we included all patients with brain CT of sufficient image quality to allow the leukoaraiosis severity to be graded.  

The imaging criteria for inclusion were a CT scan ruling out hemorrhage and CT angiography (CTA) showing occlusion of the intracranial internal carotid artery or middle cerebral artery (M1 or M2 segments). All patients or their legal representatives provided written informed consent before randomization. The study protocol was approved by a Shanghai Changhai Hospital ethics committee (ID CHEC2018-003) and the research board of each participating center.

Readers collected data on leukoaraiosis from the first CT scan for each patient at presentation using the Age-­ Related White Matter Changes (ARWMC) rating scale.10 The ARWMC was graded on the contralateral hemisphere of the known LVO. This technique has been used by previous studies to avoid mislabeling ipsilateral acute stroke-related hypodensities on CT scans as leukoaraiosis.3 6 The ARWMC method of leukoaraiosis grading has been deemed comparable with other CT imaging and MRI-based grading systems.11 The included patients were divided into three groups based on the ARWMC rating score: ARWMC score 0 (no leukoaraiosis), ARWMC score 1-2 (mild-to-moderate leukoaraiosis), and ARWMC score 3 (severe leukoaraiosis).  

The primary clinical outcome was the score on the modified Rankin scale (mRS) assessed at 90 days after randomization. Secondary outcomes included favorable outcome (defined as a 90-day mRS score of 0 to 2), mortality at 90 days, asymptomatic intracranial hemorrhage (asICH) and sICH according to the Heidelberg criteria,12 neurological assessment with the National Institutes of Health Stroke Scale (NIHSS) at 24 hours and at 5–7 days or at discharge if patients were discharged before 5–7 days.  

The radiological outcomes were successful reperfusion before and after IAT as assessed with the extended Thrombolysis in Cerebral Infarction (eTICI) score on the first and final digital subtraction angiography13 and successful reperfusion defined as eTICI score $\geq2\mathrm{~b~}50$ ; the percentage of patients with recanalization at 24 to 72 hours as assessed by CTA; large or malignant middle cerebral artery (MCA) infarction; contrast extravasation; infarction in a new territory at 5–7 days; and the final infarct volume with non-contrast CT at 5-7 days. Futile recanalization was also analyzed, which was defined as successful recanalization without favorable functional outcome $\mathrm{'mRS}{\le}2$ at 90 days).  

# Statistical analysis  

Baseline data are presented stratified by ARWMC group (no, mildmoderate, severe leukoaraiosis), with descriptive statistics as appropriate. Descriptive statistics are presented as the absolute number (percentage) for discrete variables and as the mean (SD) or median (25th–75th centiles) for continuous variables, as appropriate. The $\chi^{2}$ , Fisher exact test, Student’s t test, and Mann–Whitney tests were used, as appropriate, for the univariable analyses.  

To examine the correlation between leukoaraiosis and the primary outcome, we used multivariable ordinal logistic regression to calculate the adjusted common odds ratio (acOR) of a shift toward a better functional outcome on the mRS for IAT compared with $\mathrm{IVT+IAT}.$ . Adjusted factors included patient age, the NIHSS score at baseline, the time from stroke onset to randomization, the mRS score before stroke onset, and cerebral collateral blood flow status, which were recorded according to the original DIRECT-­MT statistical analysis plan. Secondary outcomes were examined using linear or logistic regression analyses, as appropriate, with the same adjustments as used for the primary outcome. Interaction terms were entered into the models to test the modification of treatment effect by the severity of leukoaraiosis (no vs mild-to-moderate vs severe) in primary or secondary outcomes.  

We report acORs and unadjusted common ORs with $95\%$ confidence intervals (CIs). We analyzed binary outcomes with logistic regression and reported them as adjusted and unadjusted ORs with $95\%$ CIs. We analyzed continuous outcomes with linear regression and reported adjusted and unadjusted $\upbeta$ with $95\%$ CIs.  

All analyses were performed with SAS software, version 9.2 (SAS Institute). All p values were two sided, with significance at less than 0.05.  

# RESULTS Subgroup population for the leukoaraiosis study  

From February 23, 2018, through July 2, 2019, a total of 1586 patients were assessed for eligibility for the DIRECT-­MT trial at 41 sites; a total of 656 patients were enrolled in the DIRECT-­MT trial.9 A total of 649 patients were entered into this post hoc subgroup analysis, seven DIRECT-­MT patients were excluded because they did not have brain CT images suitable for grading the severity of leukoaraiosis.  

# Leukoaraiosis prevalence, severity, and baseline characteristics  

A total of 217 patients $(33.4\%)$ had leukoaraiosis, with 139 patients $(21.4\%)$ classified as mild-to-moderate leukoaraiosis (ARWMC 1–2) and 78 $(12.0\%)$ classified as severe leukoaraiosis (ARWMC 3). Table  1 outlines the baseline characteristics according to leukoaraiosis group. Compared with patients with mild-to-moderateor severe leukoaraiosis, patients without leukoaraiosis were more likely to be male ( $\left\{61.1\%\right.$ vs $42.4\%$ vs $52.6\%$ , $\mathrm{p}{<}0.001)$ and less likely to have a previous ischemic stroke ( $10.4\%$ vs $17.3\%$ vs $26.9\%$ , $\mathsf{p}{<}0.001\$ , or to have atrial fibrillation $(41.4\%$ vs $53.2\%$ vs $59.0\%$ , $\mathsf{p}{=}0.003;$ , hypertension $(54.9\%$ vs $71.9\%$ vs $69.2\%$ , $\mathsf{p}{<}0.001\$ ), diabetes mellitus $(16.4\%$ vs $19.4\%$ vs $30.8\%$ , $\mathrm{p}{=}0.012$ ), cardioembolic stroke $(39.6\%$ vs $51.1\%$ vs $59.0\%$ , $\mathtt{p}{=}0.019$ ), or to be receiving periprocedural antiplatelet regimens $13.4\%$ vs $22.3\%$ vs $15.4\%$ , $\mathrm{p}{=}0.04)$ . Patients with mild-to-moderate or severe leukoaraiosis were older $(\mathrm{p}<0.001)$ , had higher baseline systolic blood pressure $(\mathrm{p}<0.001)$ and higher baseline NIHSS scores $(\mathsf{p}{=}0.019)$ than patients without leukoaraiosis (see table 1 for the details).  

# Clinical outcomes

The median NIHSS score at 5–7 days (6 (no leukoaraiosis) vs 10 (mild-­moderate leukoaraiosis) vs 12 (severe leukoaraiosis), a $\upbeta{=}1.6$ (0.2 to 3.1)) was elevated in patients with mild-to-moderate or severe leukoaraiosis compared with patients without leukoaraiosis.

# DISCUSSION  

This post hoc subgroup analysis of DIRECT-­MT trial participants— patients with proximal occlusion of the anterior circulation given intra-arterial treatment with or without IVT—indicated that leukoaraiosis was associated with worse 90-day mRS scores and worse functional outcomes after IAT but with similar rates of sICH. Moreover, no significant treatment-by-trichotomized ARWMC interaction was detected for the primary endpoint (ordinal mRS), and  

IVT preceding IAT did not increase the sICH risk regardless of the leukoaraiosis severity.  

Leukoaraiosis is a radiological phenomenon that reflects cerebral small-vessel disease in aging individuals. Known risk factors for leukoaraiosis include hypertension,15 diabetes mellitus,16 smoking,17 and age.18 All these risk factors, especially hypertension, can thicken the wall of the small penetrating arteries and arterioles, narrowing their lumen through the replacement of smooth muscle cells with fibrohyaline material.19 Due to these changes, the intrinsic distensibility of the penetrating arteries decreases, and this is accompanied by a decreased capacity to sustain collateral blood flow above infarction thresholds.20 Therefore, collateral flow tends to be poor in patients with leukoaraiosis, and their rates of infarct progression are typically fast.20–22 T hus, to obtain a similarly favorable clinical outcome for patients with a high leukoaraiosis burden, it is necessary to start IAT earlier than in patients with a low leukoaraiosis burden.21  

In line with previous studies,1 3 6 21 23–25 leukoaraiosis was associated with poor functional outcome. However, notable differences from previous studies are worth noting. First, our investigation was a post hoc analysis of a randomized controlled trial (RCT)—DIRECT-­MT participants, whereas other studies were multicenter/monocenter retrospective cohorts. Second, the study population was distinct: the DIRECT-­MT study examined patients whose IVT could be administered within 4.5 hours post-ictus, so the median onset-to-reperfusion time (OTR) was within 5 hours in our study, whereas the OTR surpassed 5 hours in the other studies.21 23–25 Third, the size of the study population differed from that of other studies. To the best of our knowledge, our DIRECT-­MT participant analysis was to date the largest $\scriptstyle(\mathrm{n}=649$ ) investigation of leukoaraiosis in patients who received IAT. Fourth, the group classifications used in our study included three groups based on the ARWMC scale (ARWMC, 0 indicates no leukoaraiosis, 1-2 indicates mild-to-moderate leukoaraiosis, and 3 indicates severe leukoaraiosis), whereas other studies divided patients into two groups (absent-to-mild or moderate-to-severe25 or used a bipartite classification based on the van Swieten scale (VSS) 0–2 or VSS 3–4.21 23 24  

Leukoaraiosis is associated with increased platelet activation26 and hypercoagulability.27 Activated platelets and fibrin also contribute to microvascular obstructions in both capillary and non-capillary sectors of the vessel tree, and loss of microvascular patency can cause focal no-reflow28 and futile reperfusion. Severe leukoaraiosis is a significant predictor of futile reperfusion,3 14 23 a result replicated in our study. Our study also indicates that compared with no leukoaraiosis, more periprocedural antiplatelet regimen(s) are used in patients with mild-to-moderate and severe leukoaraiosis.  

One study showed that leukoaraiosis does not influence the outcome of mechanical thrombectomy,5 but this finding differs from our results and from several other studies.21 23–25 A plausible explanation for these apparently contradictory findings is that the median OTR in that study was shorter (a median time of $233\mathrm{min}$ ) than that in our study $(281\mathrm{min})$ and in other studies.21 23–25 Notably, the presence and severity of leukoaraiosis are consistently associated with sICH after IVT or after intra-arterial thrombolysis for AIS.7 The increased risk of sICH after thrombolysis associated with leukoaraiosis may be attributable to damage to the cerebral microcirculation, which can cause increased small-vessel fragility and susceptibility to vessel rupture and bleeding complications, as well as the focal 'no-reflow’ phenomenon, which has also been linked to increased hemorrhagic transformation of the infarct.28  

Data on risks for patients with leukoaraiosis receiving IAT are sparse and conflicting. 1 3 23 29 We did not detect an association between leukoaraiosis and sICH or asICH in patients who received IAT. Given the reported risk of sICH after IVT in patients with leukoaraiosis, we hypothesized that IVT before IAT would increase the sICH risk in patients with leukoaraiosis. However, our study—which focused on this point specifically—did not detect such an association. One possible explanation might be that DIRECT-­MT used revascularization techniques, which might be more advanced than those used in previous studies. If so, we can anticipate that the puncture-torecanalization time may be shorter, and the successful reperfusion rate may be greater for DIRECT-­MT participants than for patients in previous studies. All these advantages can avoid massive infarction, which is an important factor related to sICH.  

We also noted a trend toward more patients achieving excellent outcomes (mRS score 0–1) in the $\mathrm{IVT+IAT}$ group than those in the IAT-alone group among patients with mildto-­moderate leukoaraiosis. This may be due to more patients achieving successful reperfusion in the $\mathrm{IVT+IAT}$ group among those with mild-to-moderate leukoaraiosis; this is consistent with a previous study reporting that preceding IVT facilitates endovascular mechanical recanalization in intracranial LVO and can decrease procedure times by approximately $20\mathrm{min}$ .30 However, we did not detect a better prognosis trend for the $\mathrm{IVT+IAT}$ group among the patients with severe leukoaraiosis. This may be related to the relatively lower proportion of patients with severe leukoaraiosis who achieved successful reperfusion because such patients tend to have tortuous vessels, which may be an indirect indicator of larger vessel atherosclerosis and may predict complex aortic and cervical access for $\mathrm{IAT}^{6}$ ; all these factors can influence the achievement of successful reperfusion.  

# Strengths and limitations  

Our study has the inherent limitations of an observational study. Without a placebo arm, it was impossible to know whether the effect of IAT was truly attenuated in patients with leukoaraiosis. Additionally, our findings must be interpreted in light of the fact that the entire study population was from a single-race Asian country, so there could be bias when trying to generalize these findings globally. Another possible limitation is the use of the ARWMC Scale to assess the extent of leukoaraiosis on CT, which is less accurate than volumetry using MRI (although this may be less applicable in clinical practice). CT is still widely used to evaluate patients with a stroke, and ARWMC is broadly approved as a visual rating tool. The strengths of our study include its relatively large sample size from a multicenter analysis, blinded imaging assessment, and systematic ascertainment of clinical patient characteristics. Although the sample sizes of our study are the largest thus far, the sample is still relatively small, and large RCTs are required to confirm the result.  

# CONCLUSION  

Our study provides evidence that patients with AIS due to LVO with leukoaraiosis are at increased risk of poor functional outcome after IAT but demonstrates similar rates of sICH and shows that IVT preceding IAT does not increase the risk of sICH in Chinese patients with leukoaraiosis. Given our findings that approximately $20\%$ of patients with severe leukoaraiosis achieved favorable 90-day outcomes and that sICH occurrence did not increase compared with patients without leukoaraiosis, it appears that severe leukoaraiosis may not be a good criterion on which to withhold IAT. Future analyses of randomized trial data are needed to understand whether the treatment effect of IAT is truly diminished in patients with leukoaraiosis and to determine if direct IAT is superior to IAT $+\mathrm{IVT}$ for patients with severe leukoaraiosis.
