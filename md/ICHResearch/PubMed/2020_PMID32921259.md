# CLINICAL AND POPULATION SCIENCES  



# Is Endovascular Treatment Still Good for Ischemic Stroke in Real World?  

A Meta-Analysis of Randomized Control Trial and Observational Study in the Last Decade  

Zixu Zhao, MD; Jiarui Zhang, MD; Xin Jiang, MD; Li Wang, PhD; Zixiao Yin, MD; Michael Hall, PhD; Yang Wang, MD; Lingfeng Lai , MD  

BACKGROUND AND PURPOSE: Although endovascular treatment (EVT) for acute ischemic stroke is classified as I evidence, outcomes after EVT in real-world practice appear to be less superior than those in randomized clinical trials (RCTs). Additionally, the effect of EVT is unclear compared with medical treatment (MT) for patients with mild symptoms defined by National Institutes of Health Stroke Scale score ${<}6$ or with severe symptoms defined by Alberta Stroke Program Early CT Score ${<}6$ .  

METHODS: Literatures were searched in big databases and major meetings from December 6, 2009, to December 6, 2019, including RCTs and observational studies comparing EVT against MT for patients with acute ischemic stroke. Observational studies were precategorized into 3 groups based on imaging data on admission: mild stroke group with National Institutes of Health Stroke Scale score ${<}6,$ severe stroke group with Alberta Stroke Program Early CT Score ${<}6$ or ischemic core ${\ttz50}$ mL, and normal stroke group for all others. Outcome was measured as modified Rankin Scale score of 0 to 2, mortality at 90 days, and symptomatic intracranial hemorrhage (sICH) at 24 hours.  

RESULTS: Fifteen RCTs $\mathsf{\Lambda}_{\mathsf{n}=3694}^{\prime}$ ) and 37 observational studies $\scriptstyle\mathtt{\tilde{n}=}9090$ ) were included. EVT was associated with higher modified Rankin Scale 0 to 2 rate and lower mortality in RCTs and normal stroke group, whereas EVT was associated with higher sICH rate in normal stroke group, and no difference of sICH rate appeared between EVT and MT in RCTs. In severe stroke group, EVT was associated with higher modified Rankin Scale 0 to 2 rate and lower mortality, whereas no difference of sICH rate was found. In mild stroke group, there was no difference in modified Rankin Scale 0 to 2 rate between EVT and MT, whereas EVT was associated with higher mortality and sICH rate.  

CONCLUSIONS: Evidence from RCTs and observational studies supports the use of EVT as the first-line choice for eligible patients corresponding to the latest guideline. For patients with Alberta Stroke Program Early CT Score ${<}6.$ , EVT showed superiority over MT, also in line with the guidelines. On the contrary to the guideline, our data do not support EVT for patients with National Institutes of Health Stroke Scale score ${<}6$ .

Additionally, the set of criteria for eligibility of EVT is based on RCTs in which patients with either National Institutes of Health Stroke Scale (NIHSS) score ${<}6$ or Alberta Stroke Program Early CT Score (ASPECTS) ${<}6$ were excluded.10 Thus, still controversy remains relating to these margins of eligible criteria for the usage of EVT in the latest guideline.18 The purpose of this study was to make direct comparison of the therapeutic effect between EVT and MT in both RCTs and observational studies (OSs) published in the last decade and investigate the effectiveness of EVT in treating marginal patients.  



# METHODS  



# Literature Search  

The authors declare that all supporting data are available within this article and its Data Supplement. Literatures were systematically searched by 2 reviewers in PubMed, EMBASE, Cochrane Library (Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews, Cochrane Central Register of Controlled Trials, Cochrane Methodology), Grey Literature (OpenGrey), and major neurovascular meetings from December 6, 2009, to December 6, 2019. Search strategies are listed in the Data Supplement. This study was conducted corresponding to the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analysis guidelines,19 the standards set by the Meta-Analysis of Observational Studies in Epidemiology,20 and was registered at PROSPERO, number CRD42020157456.  



# Selection Criteria  

We included studies comparing outcomes between EVT and MT for treating patients $\geq18$ years of age with acute ischemic stroke. All studies were checked by title and abstract following the inclusion criteria. RCTs and prospective and retrospective cohort studies were included. Those with $\mathtt{<}10$ participants in either group or those lacking outcome variables especially modified Rankin Scale (mRS) at 90 days were excluded. Additional details for inclusion/exclusion criteria are listed in the Data Supplement.  



# Quality Assessment  

The risk of bias was assessed by 2 reviewers independently. Publication bias of RCTs was evaluated by Cochrane risk of bias assessment to reduce the risk of selection bias, performance bias, detection bias, attrition bias, reporting bias, and other sources of bias.21 The quality of cohort studies was assessed by using the Newcastle-Ottawa Scale.22  



# Data Extraction  

Accomplished by 2 authors separately, titles and abstracts of the studies were retrieved from all databases mentioned previously. Full texts of eligible studies were reviewed according to the criteria of inclusion and exclusion outlined above. Disagreements were resolved by consensus. The following data were extracted: (1) study characteristics: study design, institution, sample size, and quality of study; (2) patients’ characteristics: admission baseline; (3) data relating to treatment: devices for EVT and treatment methods for MT; (4) initial imaging data: NIHSS23 and ASPECTS.24 Given the variable data forms in OSs, the highest quality data were extracted for meta-analysis (using the following rank order: propensity matched>adjusted>unadjusted). For all studies, we used data from intention to treatment rather than from preprotocol for data analysis.  



# Outcome Measures  

The proportion of patients with mRS score 0 to 2 at 90 days after intervention was considered the primary measure of outcome.25 The secondary outcome used measures of mortality at 90 days and symptomatic intracranial hemorrhage (sICH) at 24 hours.26,27  



# Statistical Analysis  

We conducted a separate analysis of RCTs and OSs due to the intrinsic differences between these 2 types of study designs. In OSs, all studies were precategorized into 3 groups based on their initial imaging data on admission: mild stroke group (MSG), severe stroke group (SSG), and normal stroke group (NSG). MSG was defined as studies only recruiting participants with NIHSS score ${<}6$ or with median NIHSS in baseline ${<}6$ . SSG was defined as studies whose participants were with ASPECTS ${<}6$ or with median ASPECTS in baseline ${<}6$ or ischemic core under imaging devices ${\ge}50~\mathrm{mL}$ . Other studies were included in NSG. A 2-step analysis was performed. In the main analysis, we compared RCTs to NSG for the investigation of the differences between clinical trials and real-world practice, as RCTs and NSG shared the same inclusion standard of EVT. In the additional analysis, integral and separate analysis was conducted in OSs to evaluate the effect of EVT among MSG, SSG, and NSG.  

The Comprehensive Meta-Analysis 3.0 (Biostat, Englewood, NJ) was used to perform the data analysis. Odds ratios (ORs) with $95\%$ CIs were calculated and pooled for each outcome of interest. As clinical diversity and methodological differences among the studies were assumed, a random-effects model was used to pool outcomes for all meta-analyses.28 The statistical heterogeneity between studies was assessed using the Q test and the calculation of I2. We considered $R{\cdot}0.10$ or $P_{\mathrm{~\it~\geq~}50\%}$ as an indication of substantial heterogeneity. Each pooled OR between different subgroups was assessed by $Z$ test, and 2-tailed $R{<}0.05$ was regarded as statistically significant. Publication bias was evaluated visually by funnel plots and quantified by Egger regression28 and the BeggMazumdar test.29 Trim-and-fill method was applied when significant publication bias was observed.30 Subgroup and sensitivity analyses were conducted to analyze the source of heterogeneity when $|^{2}\ge40\%$ , as $0\%$ to $40\%$ of $|2$ may be irrelevant according to the Cochrane Handbook.31 Meta-regression was implemented to further evaluate heterogeneity. Random model was used for metaregression, and covariate with $P\mathrm{{<}O.1}$ was considered significant.  



# RESULTS  

A total of 15 RCTs reporting data from 3694 participants and 37 OSs of 9090 enrolled participants were qualified and included for meta-analysis. A flowchart showing selection strategy and the baseline characteristics of the included participants is displayed in Figure I in the Data Supplement. In RCTs, NIHSS on admission was reported in all 15 studies, $_{1}^{1-5,15,32-40}$ and the median score in all studies was ${>}6$ (Table I in the Data Supplement). ASPECTS in the baseline was reported in 12 studies whose ASPECTS were all ${>}6$ . Ischemic core in one study was ${<}50$ mL. No relevant indication of the severity of stroke was reported in 2 studies. Among the OSs, 37 studies were categorized into MSG, SSG, and NSG, which included 11,42–52 $4,^{53-56}$ and 22 studies, $\cdot^{57-78}$ respectively (Tables II through IV in the Data Supplement).  



# mRS Score 0 to 2  

In the main analysis, EVT in RCTs was associated with higher odds of mRS score of 0 to 2 (OR, 1.97 $[95\%$ CI, 1.48–2.63]; $1^{2}{=}72.8\%$ ), which was parallel with the effect of EVT in NSG (1.72 $[95\%$ CI, 1.46–2.02]; $1^{2}{=}47.7\%$ ; Figure 1). A statistical significance of OR was not observed between RCTs and NSG ( $\mathrm{\Delta}P$ for interaction, 0.40). Substantial heterogeneity was detected in RCTs $(1^{2}{=}72.8\%)$ ) and NSG $(1^{2}{=}47.7\%)$ ). Therefore, multiple subgroup and sensitivity analyses and meta-regression were performed. In RCTs, mRS score of 0 to 2 was significantly favorable among patients with anterior circulation only (2.47 $[95\%$ CI, 1.89–3.23]; $P$ for interaction, ${<}0.001$ ), among patients treated with late-generation devices (2.13 $[95\%$ CI, 1.95–2.74]; $P{<}0.001$ ), and in studies published after 2015 (2.13 $[95\%$ CI, $1.95-$ 2.74]; $P{<}0.001$ ; Figure II in the Data Supplement). By using meta-regression, high recanalization rate in RCTs, high hypertension rate, and low cardioembolic stroke rate in NSG were found to be associated with better mRS score of 0 to 2 (Table V in the Data Supplement).  

In the additional analysis, EVT in OSs was associated with higher mRS score 0 to 2 ratio than MT $(\mathsf{OR},$ 1.56 $[95\%$ CI, 1.32–1.84]). EVT showed higher odds of mRS score 0 to 2 in SSG (5.33 $[95\%$ CI, $2.22\substack{-12.76}$ ; $1^{2}{=}27.85\%$ ), whereas there was no significant difference between EVT and MT in MSG (0.91 $[95\%$ CI, $0.61-1.37]$ ; $|^{2}{=}51.6\%$ ; Figure  2). Meta-regression and subgroup analyses were conducted in MSG. No covariate either for meta-regression or subgroup analysis was found to be significantly associated with mRS score of 0 to 2 in MSG (Table V in the Data Supplement; Figure II in the Data Supplement).  



# Mortality  

In the main analysis, EVT was found to be associated with lower mortality than MT in RCT (OR, 0.82 $[95\%$ CI, 0.69–0.99]; $1^{2}{=}0.00\%\cdot$ ), which was consistent with the result in NSG (0.71 $[95\%$ CI, $0.55\substack{-0.92}$ ; $|^{2}{=}51.6\%$ Figure  3). No significant difference was observed between these groups $\cdotP$ for interaction, 0.35). In the meta-regression for NSG, low mortality was significantly associated with high hypertension rate and low cardioembolic rate (Table VI in the Data Supplement). In the additional analysis, EVT was associated with lower mortality rate than MT in OSs (OR, 0.77 $[95\%$ CI, $0.63\substack{-0.95]},$ , which was also found in SSG (0.53 $[95\%$ CI, 0.34–0.82]; $\scriptstyle1^{2}=0.00$ ; Figure 4). In contrast, EVT was associated with higher mortality than MT in MSG (2.22 $[95\%$ CI, $1.26\substack{-3.89}]$ ; $1^{2}{=}0.00$ ).

# DISCUSSION  

In the main analysis, we made a direct comparison showing that, relative to MT, EVT was associated with higher rate of mRS score of 0 to 2 at 90 days and lower mortality rate in both RCTs and NSG. Significant difference of effect size was not discovered in these outcomes between the 2 groups. However, discrepancy of sICH appeared between RCTs and NSG, as EVT was associated with higher sICH rate in NSG, whereas there was no significant difference of sICH rate between EVT and MT in RCTs. This phenomenon was also found in a multicenter registry study, as the incidence of sICH after EVT was higher in real-world practice than that in RCTs.79 On the top of factors including delay of treatment or lower ASPECTS that could be attributed to the difference, we propose that strict inclusion/exclusion criteria in RCTs might also reduce the incidence of sICH after EVT.  

In the subgroup and meta-regression analysis of RCTs, higher mRS 0 to 2 rate was associated with anterior circulation only, with participants treated with second-generation devices, with high recanalization rate, and publication year after 2014. Due to some limitations (eg, broad spectrum of inclusion criteria, high percentage of patients with posterior circulation stroke, low reperfusion rate, and lack of imaging guidance), unfavorable results of EVT were shown in the early RCTs published in 2013.1,39,80,81  

In the meta-regression analysis of NSG, high hypertension rate and low cardioembolic rate in NSG were found to be associated with better mRS 0 to 2 rate and lower mortality rate. In the latest guideline, a blood pressure threshold of $185/110\mathrm{mm}\mathsf{Hg}$ for EVT candidates was recommended, which was based on the inclusion criteria of most RCTs.18 However, a specific range for preprocedural blood pressure management was not outlined in the guideline. A post analysis of an RCT demonstrated that hypertension did not affect the benefit or safety of EVT in patients with acute ischemic stroke.82 In a multicenter prospective study, a systolic blood pressure of $157\mathrm{mm}\mathsf{Hg}$ on admission was identified to predict the lowest mortality rate at 3 months after EVT.83 Based on the findings of our study and previous studies, we speculate that hypertension with a reasonable range of systolic blood pressure is a potential favorable variable associated with better mRS results, as it could increase blood reperfusion to ischemic tissues.84 Cardioembolism is an important pathogenesis as nearly half of large vessel occlusions are caused by embolus arising in the heart.85 The data from a multicenter registry supported that cardioembolic stroke was more favorable to EVT than noncardioembolic stroke.86 However, the aforementioned study lacks suitable control of the variables that might produce a biased conclusion. In our studies, lower percentage of cardioembolism on admission was associated with better mRS score of 0 to 2 and lower mortality rate in EVT compared with MT, which is parallel with the result from 2 retrospective studies.87,88 In the subgroup analysis of both RCTs and NSG, there was no significant difference between EVT alone versus EVT in combination with MT in terms of mRS score of 0 to 2 and mortality rate, which is in line with the latest released RCT conducted by Suzuki et al.89  

In the additional analysis, we compared the outcomes among SSG, MSG, and NSG to evaluate the therapeutic effects of EVT. In contrast to the findings of a previous study,90 our study suggests that patients with ischemic core volume ${\ge}50~\mathsf{mL}$ or ASPECTS ${<}6$ could benefit from EVT compared with MT treatment, as EVT was associated with higher mRS 0 to 2 rate and lower mortality rate. This could be explained by a significant increase in recanalization rate and reperfusion in EVT.54 For patients with large ischemic core treated by EVT, a reduction in likelihood of more favorable mRS score of 0 to 2 for each hour of delay in the first 24 hours was identified.56  

Clinically, patients with NIHSS ${<}6$ are regarded to exhibit minor-to-mild symptoms, which is not rare in stroke as around two-thirds of patients with stroke presented with minor-to-mild symptoms in a populationbased study.91 The latest guideline outlined that for patients with NIHSS ${\mathrm{<}}6,$ , it is reasonable to choose EVT over MT.18 On the contrary, our result in the additional analysis highlighted the inferiority of EVT compared with MT, as there was no significant difference in mRS score of 0 to 2 between EVT and MT, whereas EVT was associated with higher sICH rate and lower mortality than MT in MSG. In a meta-analysis of 5 studies, there was no significant difference in any of the outcomes mentioned above. However, this meta-analysis was limited by its small size and the absence of heterogeneity analysis.43 We identified limitations in the available data in this area. For example, only few studies primarily focused on this area, and most were restricted by the design of studies, as those studies have small samples and were retrospectively designed.42,47,50–52 Additionally, many studies treated patients with low rate of thrombolysis in MT group, which might reduce the effect of MT.45,46,52 What’s more, most studies were limited by broad inclusion criteria of patients, for example, occlusion sites were variable in many studies, as some studies recruited large proportion of patients with proximal intracranial (internal carotid artery and M1 middle cerebral artery) occlusions42,43,45 and some did not.44,47,50 For acquiring more credible clinical evidence, occlusion sites in future studies should be specified. Additional clinical trials and large prospective researches are necessary for further investigation of this field.  



# Limitations  

Certain limitations of this study include the variable design and reporting. For this reason, the randomeffects model and heterogeneity test including subgroup study and sensitivity test was applied to reduce the statistics bias. In addition, the majority of studies included patients with anterior circulation only, whereas there are 4 RCTs, 6 studies in NSG and 4 studies in MSG also included a small portion of posterior circulation patients. We performed sensitivity and subgroup analyses showing that all results remained stable when we excluded those studies with posterior circulation (Figure II in the Data Supplement).  

In addition, publication bias was observed in several analyses of outcomes. We attempted to mitigate this effect by applying the following methods. First, after implementing the trim-and-fill method, the effect size of all the outcomes with potential risk of publication bias remained robust (Tables VIII and IX in the Data  

Supplement; Figures III and V in the Data Supplement). Second, we minimized publication bias by using a comprehensive search strategy and range including Grey Literature (OpenGrey) and major neurovascular meetings. Third, in the subgroup analysis of mRS score of 0 to 2 in NSG, after excluding studies with low quality, effect size remained stable showing a favorable outcome associated with EVT (Figure II in the Data Supplement). Last, we contacted 10 esteemed researchers in this field to discuss whether potentially unpublished articles showing negative results existed, of which none were reported. For other types of bias, we tried to avoid any reporting bias by using a predefined searching algorithm, without restriction of languages, with paper screening and data extraction by 2 independent reviewers, by including registries and studies’ subanalysis. Overall, it is unlikely that a greater volume publicly reported data would have substantially altered our findings.  

Due to the lack of relevant studies in this field, our result in SSG might be limited by the included studies. Thus, we reanalyzed all the outcomes by joining subgroup analysis from other studies that were initially excluded in the additional analysis.41,63,92 Unsurprisingly, the result was in line with our previous result as EVT was associated with higher mRS 0 to 2 rate and lower mortality, and no significant difference in sICH was observed compared with MT (Figures VI through VIII in the Data Supplement).  



# Conclusions  

Evidences from both RCTs and OSs support that EVT should be chosen as the first-line choice for eligible patients corresponding to the level I evidence of the latest guideline, as EVT is associated with higher mRS 0 to 2 rate and lower mortality over MT. On the contrary to the latest guideline, the data in this study do not support EVT for patients with NIHSS score ${<}6$ as it showed no superiority of mRS 0 to 2 rate over MT, while increasing the possibilities of mortality and sICH. For patients with ASPECTS ${<}6$ , EVT shows significant superiority over MT as it was associated with higher mRS score of 0 to 2 and lower mortality rate supporting the guideline. However, more clinical trials and large prospective cohort studies are required for further investigation of this topic.
