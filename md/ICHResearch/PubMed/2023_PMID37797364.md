# Endovascular therapy versus medical management for acute ischemic stroke with large infarct core: Systematic review and meta-analysis of randomized controlled trials☆

# ABSTRACT  

Objective: To compare the efficacy and safety of endovascular therapy (EVT) versus best medical management (BMM) in patients with acute ischemic stroke (AIS) with large infarct core.  

Methods: We searched Pubmed, Embase and Cochrane Central Register of Controlled Trials for published ran­ domized clinical trials (RCTs) from inception to February 18, 2023. We defined patients with large core infarcts as having an Alberta Stroke Program early computed tomography score (ASPECTS) of 3–5. The primary outcome was functional independence, defined as a score of 0–2 on the modified Rankin scale (mRS) at 90 days. Sec­ ondary outcome was independent ambulation defined as $\mathrm{mRS}0{-}3$ at 90 days. Safety outcomes were mortality at 90 days, symptomatic intracranial hemorrhage (sICH) and any intracranial hemorrhage (ICH). Results: The overall treatment effect was more favourable to EVT group. EVT was significantly correlated with improvement of functional independence at 90 days $(\mathrm{mRS}0{-}2)$ $(\mathrm{RR}=2.40\$ ; $95~\%$ CI, 1.82–3.16; $\mathrm{P}<0.01$ ; $\operatorname{I}^{2}=$ $0\%$ ) and independent ambulation $(\mathrm{mRS}0-3)$ $(\mathrm{RR}=1{,}78$ ; $95~\%$ CI, 1.28–2.48; $\mathrm{P}<0.01$ ; $\mathrm{I}^{2}=58\%$ ) at 90 days. 90-day mortality was not significantly different between the two groups $\mathrm{RR}=0.95$ ; $95~\%$ CI, 0.78–1.16; $\mathbf{P}>$ 0.05; $\mathrm{I}^{2}=0\%)$ . The risk of sICH and any ICH was higher in EVT group than in BMM group. Conclusion: Compared with BMM, EVT may improve functional outcomes in patients with ASPECTS 3–5, despite being associated with an increased risk of sICH and any ICH.  



# 1. Introduction  

Stroke is the second leading cause of disability and death worldwide, with 13.7 million new cases in 2016; $87\%$ were ischemic strokes, with a conservative estimate of about $10{-}20~\%$ being large vessel occlusion (LVO) [1]. Stroke is the leading cause of death in China. Deaths due to stroke are three times that of coronary heart disease [2]. Rapid assess­ ment and selection of appropriate treatment modalities are essential to reduce disability and death. Endovascular therapy (EVT) has become a first-line treatment strategy for acute ischemic stroke (AIS) due to acute LVO [3]. Many studies have demonstrated the effectiveness of EVT [4, 5]. However, the guidelines recommend EVT for patients with a small infarct core [6]. Patients with an Alberta Stroke Program early computed tomography score (ASPECTS) of less than six or an ischemic core volume of larger than $70~\mathrm{mL}$ were also excluded from most trials that used EVT as a strategy of care, leading to the exclusion of some patients who might benefit from EVT from this highly effective treat­ ment approach.  

There are two common definitions for large infarct core: the AS­ PECTS score and core infarct volume. ASPECTS 3–5 are widely used in current clinical trials. Although these patients are not at the highest level of EVT recommended in the guidelines, they can also benefit from EVT. Therefore, we conducted this meta-analysis to investigate the efficacy and safety of EVT in patients with LVO with ASPECTS score of 3–5.  



# 2. Methods  



# 2.1. Protocol and guidance  

The meta-analysis was registered through the international database of prospectively registered systematic reviews PROSPERO (CRD42023398823) and prepared with reference to the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic reviews and Meta-Analyses (PRISMA).  



# 2.2. Eligibility criteria  

A commonly used evidence medicine framework, the PICOS (Patient population, Intervention, Control, Outcome, Study design) framework, was used to search for relevant articles: (1) the patient population enrolled patients aged ${\geq}18$ years who were diagnosed with AIS; (2) the intervention was EVT(No restrictions on the use which kinds of EVT, thrombectomy can be performed using $a$ stent-retriever or contactaspiration system, as well as balloon angioplasty, stent implantation, or intraarterial thrombolysis if needed); (3) the control condition was best medical management (BMM); (4) the outcomes included functional outcomes, mortality at 90 days, symptomatic intracranial hemorrhage (sICH) and any intracranial hemorrhage (ICH); (5) the study design included randomized clinical trials (RCTs). We accept different defini­ tions of sICH.  



# 2.3. Information sources and search strategy  

We identified potentially eligible studies using Pubmed, Embase and Cochrane Central Register of Controlled Trials. These trials compared EVT with BMM in treating patients with AIS. We also cross-checked references to potentially eligible RCTs. ASPECTS can be measured by any means, such as non-contrast CT imaging (NCCT), CT angiography source images (CTA-SI), CTP maps of cerebral blood volume (CTP-CBV), diffusion-weighted imaging (DWI), or any other means. The predefined search strategy is “((((((("Thrombectomy"[Mesh]) OR (Thrombectomy [Title/Abstract])) OR (mechanical intervention[Title/Abstract])) OR (endovascular treatment[Title/Abstract])) OR (endovascular therapy [Title/Abstract])) OR (endovascular reperfusion[Title/Abstract])) AND (((("Stroke"[Mesh]) OR ("cerebral infarction"[Mesh])) OR (Stroke[Title/ Abstract])) OR (cerebral infarction[Title/Abstract]))) AND ((((((((((large infarct[Title/Abstract]) OR (large infarcts[Title/Ab­ stract])) OR (large infarction[Title/Abstract])) OR (large infarctions [Title/Abstract])) OR (large core[Title/Abstract])) OR (large ischemic region[Title/Abstract])) OR (large region[Title/Abstract])) OR (large ischemic core[Title/Abstract])) OR (large ischemic stroke[Title/Ab­ stract])) OR (large infarct core[Title/Abstract]))”. No language re­ strictions on the documents searched.  



# 2.4. Study selection  

Two authors (WQW, JZ) independently screened the titles and ab­ stracts of retrieved reports for potential eligibility. The full text of potentially relevant trials was then screened. Differences of opinion were resolved by consensus. Given the lack of randomization in obser­ vational studies and the fact that baselines tend to differ between intervention groups, which may lead to bias in study results, we excluded observational studies.We also excluded non-controlled, or nonrandomized trials, unpublished studies and conference abstracts. Studies that included duplicate patient data reported by other studies, lacked data on our outcomes of interest or had fewer than 10 patient cases were also excluded.  



# 2.5. Data extraction  

Two authors (WQW and JZ) independently extracted data for all baseline information, functional outcomes and safety outcomes indicators for each eligible study: (1)General information: authors’ names, year of publication, enrolment criteria, and patient demographic characteristics; (2)The primary outcome: functionally independent pa­ tients defined as a 90-day mRS score of 0–2; (3)The secondary outcome: independent ambulation patients defined as a 90-day mRS score of 0–3; (4)Safety outcomes: mortality at 90 days, sICH and any ICH.  



# 2.6. Risk of bias assessment  

Two authors evaluated all the included literature. Considering that one of the publications we included was a meta-analysis of individual patient-level data from RCTs [7], we used the Assessing the Methodo­ logical Quality of Systematic Reviews (AMSTAR 2) tool to assess it [8]. The Cochrane Risk of Bias for RCTs (RoB 2) tool was used for quality control and bias assessment of eligible RCTs [9].  



# 2.7. Synthesis methods  

Meta-analysis was performed using RevMan 5.4 ( Review Manager 5.4 package) provided by the Cochrane Collaboration Network. We performed a cumulative meta-analysis of functional and safety outcomes in patients treated with EVT. We chose RR as the effect measure owing to the remarkable similarity of relative estimates between studies with different designs, populations, and lengths of follow-up. We generated RR at $95~\%$ ci using dichotomous data from published studies and per­ formed meta-analysis using the Mantel-Haenszel random effects model. When using the Mantel-Haenszel method, we considered sample size and event rates. The difference was statistically significant at $\mathtt{p}\le0.05$ . The heterogeneity of the data was assessed by $\chi^{2}$ test and Higgins index $(\Gamma^{2})$ , with $\mathrm{I}^{2}>50\%$ defined as a high level of heterogeneity.  



# 3. Results  



# 3.1. Literature review and study selection  

We excluded duplicate records from the 1048 literature retrieved (n $=187\$ . We identified 52 potentially eligible studies after we removed records identified in the database search that did not meet our inclusion criteria $(\mathtt{n}=809)$ ) after browsing. After evaluating the full text of the studies, we included one meta-analysis of RCTs [7] and three RCTs (RESCUE-Japan LIMIT [10], ANGEL-ASPECT [11], and SELECT2 [12]). Our literature search is shown in the supplementary online Table.S1 and Fig. S1. The excluded studies and reasons for exclusion are shown in supplementary online Table.S2.  



# 3.2. Study characteristics  

All studies were multicenter, randomized, open trials (Table 1). The meta-analysis we included studied patients with different ASPECTS, but we only selected patients with ASPECTS 3–5 scores for inclusion. The included studies ranged in sample size from 182 to 455 between 2018 and 2023, with 1197 patients, of whom 606 received EVT and 591 received BMM. One trial defined a large infarct core as a low ASPECTS score [10] and two defined it as a low ASPECTS score or large core infarct volume [11,12]. The characteristics of included patients are shown in Table 2. The average age of the patients was 66–76 years old, the male-to-female ratio was about 1:1, and the severity of the stroke ranged from 15 to 22 on the National Institutes of Health Stroke Scale (NIHSS). Before randomization, all patients who met the criteria for intravenous thrombolysis(IVT) were eligible for IVT. The rates of IVT in each trial can be seen in Table 2. HERMES 2018 and RESCUE-Japan LIMIT 2022 used rt-PA as IVT drug. The IVT agents used in ANGEL-ASPECT 2023 were rt-PA or urokinase, and rt-PA or TNK were used in SELECT2 2023. The proportion of patients receiving IVT was similar in both groups. All trials had a follow-up period of 90 days and included the required functional outcomes and safety outcomes data.

The definition for sICH in SELECT2 was SITS-MOST, and in ANGEL-ASPECTS was Heidelberg bleeding classification. Other known predictors of the outcome, such as the baseline medical history, mRS before stroke onset, blood pressure, glucose level, cause of stroke, location of intracranial artery occlusion, and NIHSS, did not statistically differ between the groups in any of the studies.  



# 3.3. Risk of bias in the included studies  

We evaluated the risk of bias for the included IPD meta-analysis using the AMSTAR 2 tool and is presented in supplementary online Table.S3. The specific reasons for study exclusion, the sources of funding for included trials, and the evaluation of publication bias were not thoroughly discussed. The overall quality was evaluated at $81\%$ (13 out of 16 criteria were fulfilled).  

The risk of bias of the included RCTs was assessed by the Cochrane RoB 2 tool. The assignment of trial groups in RESCUE-Japan LIMIT 2022 was not concealed from patients or treating physicians. All three RCTs were open-label, with a high risk of performance bias. Overall, the included RCTs were considered to be of moderate quality with a large risk of bias. Our risk of bias assessment is shown in supplementary online Table.S3 and Fig. S2.  



# 3.4. EVT effect meta-analysis  



# 3.4.1. Functional outcomes  

606 and 591 patients received EVT and BMM, respectively. In the primary outcome analysis, the EVT group of 605 patients with 149 (24.6 $\%$ function independently, and EVT was associated with a significant increase in functional independence $(\mathrm{mRS~}0{-}2)$ $(\mathrm{RR}=2.40\$ ; $95~\%$ CI, 1.82–3.16; $\mathrm{~P~}<0.01$ ; $\mathrm{I}^{2}=0~\%$ ) (supplementary online Fig. S3). In the analysis of secondary outcome $(\mathrm{mRS~}0{-}3)$ ), EVT was also superior to BMM $\mathrm{{\bf{RR}}}=1{,}78$ ; $95~\%$ CI, 1.28–2.48; $\mathrm{~P~}<0.01$ ; $\mathrm{I}^{2}=58~\%$ ) (supple­ mentary online Fig. S4). The results suggest that EVT achieved better  

results than BMM within 90 days.  



# 3.4.2. Safety outcomes  

In 90 days, 136 of 507 patients $(26.8~\%)$ died. In general, 90-day mortality was not significantly different between the two groups (RR $=0.95$ ; $95\%\mathrm{CI}$ , 0.78–1.16; $\mathrm{P}>0.05$ ; $\mathrm{I}^{2}=0\%\mathrm{\Omega}$ ). The risk of sICH in EVT group was higher than that in BMM group $(\mathrm{RR}=2.31\$ ; 95 $\%\mathrm{CI}$ : 1.30–4.09; $\mathrm{~P~}<0.01$ ; $\mathrm{I}^{2}=0\%$ ). The risk of any ICH was also higher in EVT group than in BMM group $\mathbf{\Delta}\mathbf{R}\mathbf{R}=2.22\$ ; $95\%$ CI, 1.50–3.29; $\mathrm{P}<0.01$ ; $\mathrm{I}^{2}=52\%$ ) (supplementary online Fig. S5、S6、S7).  



# 4. Discussion  

Our meta-analysis showed significant improvement in functional independence (mRS0–2) and independent ambulation $(\mathrm{mRS~}0{-}3)$ be­ tween patients treated with EVT and those treated with BMM without increased mortality. Many studies have also shown improved functional outcomes with EVT in patients with low ASPECTS score [13–15]. These updated data included in our meta-analysis reinforce the results of a previous analysis of the effect of EVT in patients with low ASPECTS by providing up-to-date estimates of the outcomes of patients after EVT. All these results suggest that patients with low ASPECTS score also benefit from EVT. Although intracranial hemorrhage was more common in the EVT group, the net beneficial effect of EVT remained. In addition, the benefit of EVT is related to the ASPECTS score, and the lower the AS­ PECTS score, the more limited the benefit. In the stratified comparative analysis of ASPECTS, the benefit of patients with ASPECTS score of 0–2 was less pronounced [7]. In our study, patients with a 3–5 ASPECTS score were most likely to benefit from EVT. These results provide evi­ dence for expanding the indications for EVT. So some patients with large infarct core may also benefit from EVT.  

Regarding the use of IVT in trials, Suzuki et al.[16]proposed that EVT alone failed to demonstrate noninferiority regarding favorable func­ tional outcome in patients with acute large vessel occlusion stroke compared with IVT plus EVT. However, the low proportion of patients receiving IVT in the three RCTs may have disadvantaged the BMM group.  

ASPECTS can be evaluated in a variety of ways, such as NCCT, CTPSI, and CTP-CBV, of which CTP-CBV is the most commonly used. Chu et al. [17] reported that CTP-CBV had a stronger correlation with the final ASPECTS than NCCT and CTA-SI (0.717 vs. 0.711 vs. 0.565; $\mathrm{~P~}<$ 0.05). CTP-CBV ASPECTS might be more reliable for delineating early ischemic changes and predicting final infarction. In ANGEL-ASPECT 2023 and SELECT2 2023, most patients adopted CTP-CBV as the eval­ uation method for ASPECTS, while in RESCUE-Japan LIMIT 2022, most patients adopted DWI as the evaluation method. Some studies suggest [18] that for patients with hyperacute stroke, DWI-ASPECTS score is about 1 point lower than CT-ASPECTS, and the difference between DWI-ASPECTS and CT-ASPECTS should be considered in the interpre­ tation of trial results.  

Although we mainly used the ASPECTS score to assess patients with large infarct core, patients with large core infarct volume may also benefit from EVT. In eligible patients with large core $(\ge70\mathrm{mL}$ ) AIS, EVT may still increase the incidence of functional outcomes compared to IVT [19]. Similarly, the magnitude of benefit from EVT in patients with large infarct core is also related to core infarct volume. Yoshimoto et al. ’s study [20] suggest that core infarct volume between 70 and $100\mathrm{mL}$ may benefit from EVT. In patients with a core infarct volume of $70{-}300~\mathrm{mL}$ , the upper core limit that can be treated is approximately $120~\mathrm{mL}$ . The study by Campbell et al. also showed that EVT has a better probability of achieving functional outcomes than BMM at each level of core infarct volume [21].  

There are different views on the difference between semiquantitative (ASPECTS) and quantitative (core volume) imaging mo­ dalities in evaluating the ischemic core area; the study of Turc et al. [22] suggested that DWI - ASPECTS and VolDWI can be highly correlated (p $=-0.82)$ . DWI-ASPECTS ${_{,<4}}$ or $\geq7$ can be a reliable surrogate for $\scriptstyle{\mathrm{VolDWI}}>100$ or ${<}70~\mathrm{mL}$ , respectively.  

Most studies define technical success of EVT as cerebral infarction thrombolysis (TICI) revascularization grade 2b or higher. The successful vascular recirculation rate in the EVT group was $79.8~\%$ in SELECT2 2023 and $85.8\%$ in ANGEL-ASPECTS 2023. It is generally believed that a greater degree of recanalization is associated with a higher rate of good functional outcomes. Dargazanli et al. [23] demonstrated that patients with modified TICI 3 were more likely to have good functional outcomes than patients with modified TICI 2b $(71.7~\%$ vs. $50.5~\%$ , $\mathbf{P}=.001)$ . Ghozy et al. [24] also proposed that among TICI, modified TICI (mTICI) and extended TICI (eTICI), patients with TICI 3 recanalize had the highest rate of good functional outcomes, followed by patients with TICI 2c and TICI 2b recanalize. In addition, good collateral status is associ­ ated with functional outcomes in patients with large infarct core [25]. Kaesmacher et al. found a low incidence of sICH with successful reper­ fusion after EVT in patients with ASPECTS ${\le}5$ [26]. This also provides a certain guarantee for patients with large infarct core to choose EVT.  

The recommended time window for EVT is within $^{6\textrm{h}}$ . The effect of EVT more than $^{6\textrm{h}}$ after onset in AIS patients with large infarct core is uncertain. Patients with large infarct core were also excluded from the extended time window trial of EVT (Thrombectomy $6{-}24\mathrm{~h~}$ after Stroke with a Mismatch between Deficit and Infarct [DAWN] [27] excluded patients with ischemic cores over $50~\mathrm{mL}$ , and Thrombectomy for Stroke at $^{6-16\mathrm{~h~}}$ with Selection by Perfusion Imaging [DEFUSE 3] [28] excluded patients with ischemic cores over $70~\mathrm{mL}$ ). In addition, more rigorous patient selection, higher study site selection levels, and inter­ vention specialists’ proficiency in RCTs may have exaggerated the magnitude of the effect compared to the real world. Therefore, how to translate the obtained results into clinical practice needs to continue to be explored.  

Our meta-analysis had limitations. First, we included a limited number of studies and were subject to bias owing to the open-label nature of the included trials. Despite the use of data from multicenter RCTs, more information is needed to assess the safety of EVT.  

Observational studies may be more adequate than RCTs in assessing safety because these studies may include patients who are usually excluded from RCTs, and the follow-up period is usually longer. Un­ fortunately, we did not include observational studies. Second, there may be heterogeneity among different imaging tools and scanning modal­ ities. Some studies have shown differences in the effect size of different ASPECTS score [7,29], but our study included patients with ASPECTS score of 3–5 only. We did not conduct further stratification analysis of the ASPECTS score. It may be more beneficial to determine the boundary values of the ASPECTS of EVT to make a specific stratified study of the ASPECTS score.  



# 5. Conclusions  

Overall, the pooled analysis of the current data showed that EVT had a more significant benefit than BMM in patients with low ASPECTS score, despite a higher risk of intracranial hemorrhage than BMM. In the future, more RCTs and observational studies are still needed to deter­ mine EVT’s efficacy and safety in patients with large infarct core.
