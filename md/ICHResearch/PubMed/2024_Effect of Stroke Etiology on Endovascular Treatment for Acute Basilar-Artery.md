# Effect of Stroke Etiology on Endovascular Treatment for Acute Basilar-Artery Occlusion: A Post Hoc Analysis of the ATTENTION Randomized Trial  

Guangxiong Yuan, MS\*; Thanh N. Nguyen , MD\*; Lei Liu, MS\*; Rui Li , MD; Hong Xia, MD; Chen Long, MS; Junxiong Wu, MS; Jun Xu, BS; Feng Huang, BS; Bo He, MS; Derong Wu, MS; Hailing Wang, BS; Can Feng, BS; Yong Liang, MS; Xianghong Zhou, MS; Zhenhua Xiao, BS; Li Luo, BS; Yanjuan Hu, BS; Bin Liu, MS; Weibo Peng , MS; Chao Zhang, MD; Tao Cui, MD; Gaoshan Zhao, MD; Lihua Xu, MD; Gaoting Ma , MD; Wei Hu , MD; for the ATTENTION Investigators  

BACKGROUND: Stroke etiology could influence the outcomes in patients with basilar-artery occlusion (BAO). This study aimed to evaluate the differences in efficacy and safety of best medical treatment (BMT) plus endovascular treatment (EVT) versus BMT alone in acute BAO across different stroke etiologies.  

METHODS: The study was a post hoc analysis of the ATTENTION trial (Trial of Endovascular Treatment of Acute BasilarArtery Occlusion), which was a multicenter, randomized trial at 36 centers in China from February 2021 to September 2022. Patients with acute BAO were classified into 3 groups according to stroke etiology (large-artery atherosclerosis [LAA], cardioembolism, and undetermined cause/other determined cause [UC/ODC]). The primary outcome was a favorable outcome (modified Rankin Scale score of 0–3) at 90 days. Safety outcomes included symptomatic intracranial hemorrhage and 90-day mortality.  

RESULTS: A total of 340 patients with BAO were included, 150 $(44.1\%)$ had LAA, 72 $(21.2\%)$ had cardioembolism, and 118 $(34.7\%)$ had UC/ODC. For patients treated with BMT plus EVT and BMT alone, respectively, the rate of favorable outcome at 90 days was $49.1\%$ and $23.8\%$ in the LAA group (odds ratio, 3.08 $[95\%$ CI, 1.38–6.89]); $52.2\%$ and $30.8\%$ in the cardioembolism group (odds ratio, 2.45 $[95\%$ CI, 0.89–6.77]); and $37.5\%$ and $17.4\%$ in the UC/ODC group (odds ratio, 2.85 $[95\%$ CI, 1.16–7.01]), with $\widetilde{\widetilde{\Gamma{=}0.89}}$ for the stroke etiology×treatment interaction. The rate of symptomatic intracranial hemorrhage in EVT-treated patients with LAA, cardioembolism, and UC/ODC was $8.3\%$ , $2.2\%$ , and $3.2\%$ , respectively, and none of the BMT-treated patients. Lower 90-day mortality was observed in patients with EVT compared with BMT alone across 3 etiology groups.  

CONCLUSIONS: Among patients with acute BAO, EVT compared with BMT alone might be associated with favorable outcomes and lower 90-day mortality, regardless of cardioembolism, LAA, or UC/ODC etiologies. The influence of stroke etiology on the benefit of EVT should be explored by further trials.

troke is the leading cause of disability and death S in the world.1 Acute basilar-artery occlusion (BAO) stroke accounts for $\approx1\%$ of all ischemic stroke and $5\%$ to $8\%$ of large vessel occlusion stroke.2,3 Acute BAO is one of the most severe forms of stroke, resulting in up to $80\%$ of patients with severe disability or death.4,5 Two randomized trials suggested that the best medical treatment (BMT) combined with endovascular treatment (EVT) significantly reduces disability severity in patients with stroke with BAO compared with BMT alone.6,7 A study-level meta-analysis of 4 randomized controlled trials confirmed this benefit for patients with moderate to severe presenting BAO stroke.8  

Prior studies had assessed the relationship between stroke etiology and prognosis after EVT and have shown that patients with acute BAO due to different stroke etiologies may have a heterogeneous response to EVT.4,9 Some studies showed that patients with acute BAO with cardioembolism and undetermined cause (UC) achieved better functional outcomes than patients with large-artery atherosclerosis (LAA) after EVT,10,11 whereas a systematic analysis showed that patients with atrial fibrillation had worse outcomes after BAO recanalization therapy.4 One study indicated that patients with BAO and embolism from tandem vertebral artery stenoocclusion were associated with a lower rate of recanalization and higher mortality compared with in situ atherosclerotic thrombosis and embolism without vertebral artery steno-occlusion.12 Also, another study found no differences in outcomes between patients with BAO from intracranial atherosclerotic disease or embolic etiology.13 However, these studies provided insufficient information on the absolute benefit of EVT versus BMT alone for patients with different etiologies. Whether all acute BAO due to different etiologies should be treated with EVT remains unclear.  

Herein, we compared the efficacy and safety between EVT and BMT in patients with acute BAO across different stroke etiologies (LAA, cardioembolism, and UC/ other determined cause [ODC]) via a post hoc analysis of the ATTENTION (Trial of Endovascular Treatment of Acute Basilar-Artery Occlusion).7  

# METHODS  

# Data Availability  

Data that reproduced the results are available to researchers from the corresponding author upon reasonable request.  

# Study Design and Patient Selection  

This study was a post hoc analysis of the ATTENTION trial, which was a multicenter, prospective, randomized, openlabel trial at 36 comprehensive stroke centers in China from February 2021 to September 2022, to determine whether BMT plus EVT would be superior to BMT alone for patients with acute BAO stroke. ATTENTION was registered at https:// www.clinicaltrials.gov/, number NCT04751708. ATTENTION was approved by the ethics committee of all participating sites. Each patient or their legally authorized representative signed the informed consent.  

The safety, ethics, and oversight of the trial were overseen by an independent safety and data monitoring board. An independent clinical research organization was responsible for monitoring trial quality. The modified Rankin Scale (mRS) was obtained using a structured phone interview by a locally certified neurologist or nurse who was unaware of the trial assignment group, and adjudicated by an independent clinical-event adjudication committee. The safety outcomes were assessed by an independent adverse-events committee. All imaging data, such as the occlusion site, posterior circulation Acute Stroke Prognosis Early CT Score, and reperfusion, were evaluated by an Imaging Core Laboratory blinded to the patient outcomes. There were 340 patients with acute BAO included in the present cohort. BAO was defined by magnetic resonance angiography, computed tomography angiography, or digital subtraction angiography within 12 hours after the estimated time of stroke onset. The estimated occlusion time was defined as the time of sudden onset of symptoms of basilar-artery stroke, regardless of minor prodromal symptoms, as adjudicated by 2 neurologists. The main inclusion criteria included the following: age $\geq18$ years, baseline National Institutes of Health Stroke Scale (NIHSS) score $\geq10$ , and patient presentation within 12 hours of estimated time of BAO. The main exclusion criteria were prestroke mRS score ${\ge}3$ and cerebral hemorrhage on neuroimaging. Details of the methods, patient selection criteria, and the primary results of the ATTENTION trial have been reported.7,14 This study followed the Strengthening the Reporting of Observational Studies in Epidemiology reporting guideline.15  

# Classification of Stroke Cause  

Stroke etiology in the ATTENTION trial was evaluated based on the patient’s clinical, laboratory examination, imaging features, and categorized into LAA, cardioembolism, and UC/ ODC subtypes according to the TOAST (Trial of ORG 10172 in Acute Stroke Treatment) criteria.16 Stroke investigations were conducted according to local site stroke protocols and national guidelines.17 Patients had a carotid artery ultrasound, computed tomography angiography, magnetic resonance angiography, or digital subtraction angiography after hospital admission to evaluate for the possibility of large-artery atherosclerotic disease. The LAA subtype was defined as the presence of a localized lesion with significant stenosis of the basilar or vertebral artery V4 segment $(>50\%)$ ) after EVT or occlusion in the basilar-artery or the vertebral artery V4 segment due to atherosclerosis. ECG, transthoracic echocardiography, and 24-hour Holter monitoring were performed on patients. The cardioembolism subtype was defined as a sudden onset arterial occlusion event with evidence of embolism from a cardiac source: valvular heart disease, atrial fibrillation, mitral or aortic valve replacement, infective endocarditis, atrial or ventricular thrombus on echocardiography, cardiomyopathy, cardiac catheterization, cardiac anomaly, but without proximal carotid stenosis or intracranial stenosis. The UC subtype was defined as an unexplained or UC after a comprehensive evaluation. Patients with 2 or more causes in whom a definitive etiology could not be determined were classified as UC. The ODC subtype was defined as patients with evidence of specific causes (eg, hematologic disorders, hypercoagulable states, nonatherosclerotic vasculopathy). As the proportion of patients in the UC and ODC groups was low, they were merged into 1 category. Therefore, patients in the present cohort were divided into 3 groups: LAA, cardioembolism, and UC/ODC.  

# Treatments  

Patients in the BMT group received intravenous thrombolysis, anticoagulants, antiplatelet therapy, or any combination based on the management guidelines of acute ischemic stroke.17 Patients in the EVT group received BMT plus EVT. The EVT approach included use of stent retriever, contact aspiration, balloon angioplasty, stenting, intraarterial thrombolytic, or a combination of these approaches, and was performed at the discretion of the neurointerventionalist.  

# Outcomes  

The primary outcome was favorable outcome defined as a mRS score of 0 to 3 at 90 days. The secondary outcomes were excellent outcome (90-day mRS score $0{-}1$ ), functional independence (90-day mRS score 0–2), the mRS shift analysis at 90 days, NIHSS score at 24 to 72 hours, and at 5 to 7 days (or discharge), successful reperfusion (defined as grade 2b [perfusion with ${>}50\%$ distal filling] or 3 [complete perfusion]) on the modified Thrombolysis in Cerebral Infarction after EVT, recanalization on computed tomography angiography or magnetic resonance angiography at 24 to 72 hours (defined as modified Arterial Occlusive Lesion score of 2 to 3, 0 [no recanalization], and 3 [complete recanalization]).  

The safety outcomes included symptomatic intracranial hemorrhage (sICH) and intracranial hemorrhage (ICH) within 24 to 72 hours, all-cause death within 7 days and 90 days. sICH was assessed according to the modified SITS-MOST (Safe Implementation of Thrombolysis in Stroke-Monitoring Study) criteria, which was defined as local or distal type 2 parenchymal hemorrhage, intraventricular hemorrhage or subarachnoid hemorrhage within 24 to 72 hours after treatment, accompanied by an increase in NIHSS score ${\ge}4$ points compared with baseline NIHSS, or leading to death.18  

# Statistical Analysis  

Categorical data are expressed as percentage and frequency, and continuous variables data are reported as median and interquartile range (IQR). The primary efficacy outcome of good functional status (mRS score of $_{0-3}$ at 90 days) was analyzed with the use of binary logistic regression, and odds ratios (ORs) and $95\%$ CIs are reported. A similar approach was used for the secondary outcomes of mRS score of 0 to 2 at 90 days, mRS score of 0 to 1 at 90 days, and artery patency at 24 to 72 hours on computed tomography angiography or magnetic resonance angiography. Shift analysis was performed for comparison of the mRS score at 90 days with the use of an ordinal logistic regression after verification of the proportional odds assumption, and the common OR and $95\%$ CI were calculated. The NIHSS score at 24 to 72 hours and 5 to 7 days of discharge were conducted with the BreuschPagan test to assess heteroskedasticity and the Jarque Bera test to evaluate normality. If homoscedasticity or normality was not satisfied, $\mathsf{BOX^{-}CoX}$ transformation was applied to the NIHSS score, and linear regression was used to calculate the beta coefficient and $95\%$ CI.  

We also examined the heterogeneity of EVT in patients with different etiologies by including the allocated arm (EVT versus BMT), stroke etiology, and the interaction of stroke etiology×treatment into the regression models. All statistical analyses were performed with SPSS 26.0 (IBM Corp, Armonk, NY). All $P$ values were 2-sided, and statistical significance was set at $R{<}0.05$ .  

# RESULTS  

# Baseline Characteristics  

There were 340 patients in the ATTENTION trial included in this post hoc analysis (226 received BMT plus EVT, 114 received BMT alone). # Outcomes of EVT Versus BMT in the LAA, Cardioembolism, and UC/ODC Groups  

Compared with patients who received BMT alone, patients receiving EVT had higher rates of favorable outcome in the LAA $(49.1\%$ versus $23.8\%$ ; OR, 3.08 $[95\%$ CI, 1.38–6.89]; $\widetilde{P}\widetilde{=}0.01$ ), UC/ODC groups $(37.5\%$ versus $17.4\%_{!}$ OR, 2.85 $[95\%$ CI, $1.16{-}7.01]$ ; $_{P=0.02}$ ) but was not significant in the cardioembolism group $(52.2\%$ versus $30.8\%$ ; OR, 2.45 $[95\%$ CI, 0.89–6.77]; $_{P=0.08.}$ ), with $\widetilde{P{=}0.89}$ for the stroke etiology×treatment interaction effect (Figure 2). The distribution of the mRS according to EVT versus BMT in the LAA, cardioembolism, and UC/ODC groups is shown in Figure 3. There was a shift in the 90-day mRS distribution in favor of EVT in LAA (common OR, 3.05 $[95\%$ CI, $1.55\substack{-6.02}$ ; $P\mathrm{=}0.001$ ). Patients in the LAA and UC/ODC groups treated with EVT had higher proportions of functional independence (LAA: $38.0\%$ versus $9.5\%$ ; OR, 5.81 $[95\%$ CI, $1.93-$ 17.48]; UC/ODC: $29.2\%$ versus $8.7\%$ ; OR, 4.32 $[95\%$ CI, 1.38–13.58]) and excellent outcome (LAA: $22.2\%$ versus $7.1\%$ ; OR, 3.71 $[95\%$ CI, 1.05–13.08]; UC/ODC: $20.8\%$ versus $4.3\%$ ; OR, 5.79 $[95\%$ CI, 1.26–26.65]) compared with those treated with BMT (Figures 2 and 3). Patients with cardioembolism after EVT had a lower NIHSS at 24 to 72 hours (13 versus 35, beta coefficient, −2.30 $[95\%$ CI, $-3.96$ to $-0.631$ ). Among all stroke etiologies, EVT patients had lower NIHSS scores at 5 to 7 days or discharge compared with BMT patients (LAA, 15 versus 38; beta coefficient, $-1.65$ $[95\%$ CI, $-2.92$ to −0.38]; cardioembolism, 8 versus 23; beta coefficient, −1.64 $[95\%$ CI, $-3.07$ to $-0.21]$ ; UC/ODC, 21 versus 35; beta coefficient, −1.90 $[95\%$ CI, $-3.49$ to $-0.30]$ ; Figure 2). There were also no significant interaction effects of stroke etiology×treatment for all secondary efficacy outcomes.

# DISCUSSION  

This post hoc analysis from the ATTENTION trial assessed the efficacy and safety of EVT compared with BMT in patients with acute BAO across different stroke etiologies. The results indicated that EVT might be associated with improved functional outcomes compared with BMT, regardless of stroke etiology. Similar results were observed in terms of 90-day mortality benefit in different stroke etiologies treated with EVT.  

In this cohort of patients with acute BAO, the most common cause of stroke was LAA $(44.1\%)$ followed by UC/ODC $(34.7\%)$ and cardioembolism $(21.2\%)$ ). This is consistent with previous studies showing that

LAA is the leading subtype of posterior circulation ischemic stroke in China.10,19–23 Our study confirmed findings in previous studies that acute BAO with different etiologies have some distinct characteristics.10,11 Patients with acute BAO caused by LAA often present later but had lower NIHSS score at admission, possibly due to favorable collaterals. Acute BAO due to cardioembolism had atrial fibrillation in up to $83.3\%$ of patients, and cardioembolism accounted for $59.7\%$ of occlusions of the distal basilar-artery. These variables are well-known factors associated with prognosis,24–26 which may explain the different responses to treatment and hence, the importance of assessing the EVT effect by stroke etiology.  

A previous study based on a nationwide prospective cohort of acute BAO from the BASILAR registry (The EVT for Acute Basilar Artery Occlusion Study) had demonstrated that both patients with LAA and cardioembolism treated with EVT were more likely to achieve functional independence at 90 days than BMT alone.11 Our study assessed the heterogeneity of EVT effect among 3 etiology groups in the ATTENTION randomized trial and found that EVT was also associated with a better prognosis than BMT alone in the LAA and UC/ODC groups. For the cardioembolism etiology, the EVT group achieved a numerically higher rate of favorable outcomes than the BMT group $(52.2\%$ versus $30.8\%$ ), but the difference was not significant, which was probably related to the small sample size. Importantly, the interaction analysis between etiology and treatment was not significant, suggesting the etiology did not modify the EVT effect in the ATTENTION trial. In addition, the NIHSS score at 5 days or discharge was better in patients treated with EVT compared with BMT alone across 3 etiology groups. We thus speculated that regardless of the stroke cause, patients with acute BAO could benefit from EVT than BMT.

Moreover, some studies have shown that a longer procedure time was an independent predictor of poor functional dependence.27,28 For embolic clots, such as those originating from the heart, contact aspiration, stent retrievers, or their combination are currently used to remove these types of emboli,29 which are a relatively simple procedure without the need for rescue treatment, such as balloon angioplasty or stenting with concomitant antiplatelet administration. Thus, the puncture to revascularization time of patients with the cardioembolism subtype was significantly shorter, while patients with the LAA subtype had a more complicated rescue treatment and longer revascularization time. In our study, the proportion of intracranial angioplasty/stenting and extracranial angioplasty/stenting in the LAA group was $66\%$ and $15.1\%$ , respectively; while the proportion of intracranial angioplasty/stenting and extracranial angioplasty/ stenting in the cardioembolism group was $6.5\%$ and $0\%$ , respectively. Differences in technique used for EVT were reflective of the suspected underlying pathophysiology of the stroke. Of note, the etiology of BAO is often unknown at the time of initial patient presentation. Although some scores can predict the relevant etiology,30,31 there may be challenges in clinical implementation. Our study shows that EVT improved clinical outcomes in patients with acute BAO regardless of the stroke subtype, thus identification of the etiology in patients with acute BAO is mainly helpful in choosing the appropriate technique of EVT, reducing procedural duration, reducing potential endothelial damage caused by EVT, and improving the patient’s outcome.

With regards to safety, the mortality benefits of EVT versus BMT were consistent in patients with different stroke etiologies. Of note, the risk of intracranial hemorrhage is usually lower in acute BAO than in anterior circulation occlusion.32 In this study, no patient treated with BMT alone suffered from sICH, and when treated with EVT, the frequency of sICH was also low in the cardioembolism and UC/ODC groups, but increased to $8.3\%$ in the LAA group. The explanation was unclear but might be due to the more aggressive rescue treatment with intracranial angioplasty/stenting $66\%$ in LAA, $6.5\%$ in cardioembolism, and $21.7\%$ in UC/ODC), rescue angioplasty/ stenting followed by intensive antiplatelet therapy, and higher proportion of vascular re-occlusion after angioplasty/stenting in LAA group, leading to increased risk of bleeding.33,34 Nevertheless, mortality was significantly decreased in patients with BAO treated with EVT than BMT in the LAA group. The increased risk of bleeding by EVT was acceptable given the overall improved functional outcome and reduced mortality in patients with LAA.  

While this study was an analysis derived from a multicenter randomized trial, we acknowledge limitations. First, the study was a post hoc exploratory analysis, and the randomization was not stratified by stroke etiology because there was no reliable indicator to determine the etiology in the acute phase. Second, the sample sizes in the 3 etiology groups were small, which may lead to an underpowered analysis. Hence, the generalizability of our research results to other patients with BAO may be limited. Third, the high number of patients in the UC group (115 patients) was a significant limitation, possibly related to incomplete stroke workup or overlapping stroke etiologies. Also, the ATTENTION trial only recruited patients who presented within 12 hours after estimated time of BAO. Hence, the relationship between stroke etiology and treatment beyond 12 hours is unclear. Last, the participating centers of the ATTENTION trial tended to favor aggressive rescue angioplasty/stenting, and the generalizability of the findings among other hospitals is uncertain. However, as similarly high rates of rescue angioplasty/stenting were observed in the BAOCHE trial (The Basilar Artery Occlusion Chinese Endovascular Trial), this suggests use of this technique may be common in clinical practice among patients with underlying intracranial atherosclerotic disease.6  

# CONCLUSIONS  

In patients with acute BAO presenting within 12 hours of estimated time of symptom onset, EVT might be associated with improved functional outcomes compared with BMT, regardless of the stroke etiology. The 90-day mortality benefit of EVT versus BMT was consistent in patients with different stroke etiologies. A patient-level meta-analysis on stroke etiology as related to clinical outcomes will be of interest to confirm these findings.
