- BACKGROUND: There are still some patients with acute anterior circulation large vessel occlusion stroke experienced unsuccessful recanalization after endovascular thrombectomy (EVT). The efficacy of intravenous alteplase before thrombectomy for such patients is unknown. We performed this study to investigate whether prior intravenous alteplase could affect the outcome of patients with unsuccessful recanalization.  

- METHODS: This is a post-hoc analysis of the DIRECT-MT trial (direct intraarterial thrombectomy in order to revascularize acute ischemic stroke patients with large vessel occlusion efficiently in Chinese Tertiary Hospitals: a multicenter randomized clinical trial). Patients with final extended thrombolysis in cerebral infarction score of 0e2a were included. Patients with successful recanalization (modified treatment in cerebral ischemia 2be3) or could not be assessed for modified treatment in cerebral ischemia were excluded. The primary outcome was the 90- day modified Rankin Scale (mRS) score, while secondary outcomes included 90-day functional independence (mRS 0e2), recanalization (modified arterial occlusive lesion) rates at 24e72 hours by computed tomography angiography, infarction volume on computed tomography, and rates of symptomatic intracranial hemorrhage and asymptomatic intracranial hemorrhage.  

- RESULTS: Among 656 randomized patients, 92 patients with unsuccessful recanalization were included, of which 55 patients underwent EVT alone (EVT group) and 37 patients underwent EVT preceded by intravenous alteplase (combination group). No statistically significant difference in the 90-day mRS score was observed between the 2 groups (adjusted common odds ratio $=1.65;$ $95\%$ confidence interval, 0.76 to 3.59). The volume of infarction, the proportion of 90-day functional independence, recanalization rates at 24e72 hours, rates of symptomatic intracranial hemorrhage, and rates of asymptomatic intracranial hemorrhage were similar in both groups.  

- CONCLUSIONS: We found no evidence that prior intravenous alteplase could affect the clinical or imaging outcome in patients with acute anterior circulation large vessel occlusion stroke and unsuccessful recanalization after EVT.

In our study, the overall intracranial hemorrhage rate was relatively higher as compared to published trails. It should be noted that we only included patients with unsuccessful recanalization, which has been reported to be a risk factor associated with the hemorrhagic transformations after EVT.18,19 The alteplase administration is known to increase the risk of SICH as compared to placebo or open control in patients with acute ischemic stroke.11,20 In addition, there are currently no management standards for patients with contrast extravasation during digital subtraction angiography before successful recanalization, and in some cases, the EVT process would be stopped to avoid further bleeding. There may also be a reason why the overall intracranial hemorrhage rate in our study was relatively higher as compared to published trails.  

Some retrospective studies have recently looked into the safety and efficacy of thrombolytic agent administration as rescue therapy in patients who had failed recanalization.11,21,22 Kaesmacher et al. evaluated the safety and efficacy of that intra-arterial urokinase administration in 100 patients with anterior circulation LVOS who had experienced the failed, unsuccessful, or incomplete  

EVT. The study found that giving intra-arterial urokinase after EVT did not increase the risk of SICH or 90-day mortality, and it improved angiographic reperfusion. Zaidi et al.22 also showed a trend toward higher rates of reperfusion and functional independence, without increase in risks of SICH. In addition, studies about rescue stenting therapy have also achieved promising results in published studies.23,24 Our findings suggest that prior intravenous alteplase administration may not be the best option for patients with failed recanalization and that trying another rescue strategy may be beneficial.  

There are some limitations to the study. The sample size in our study was relatively small, and the CIs are wide when analyzing the patients’ primary or secondary outcomes, resulting in lower power for statistical analysis. In addition, the time between intravenous alteplase and EVT initiation was short, and the lack of alteplase action before EVT may have influenced the study results. The results of our study may not apply to the circumstance that the alteplase was administrated at one center and then transferred to another center for EVT. Some patients without eTICI scores from the core laboratory due to the poor imaging quality were excluded, which might also affect the true effect of the assessment. A pooled analysis of the 6 RCTs may provide greater clarity.  



# CONCLUSION  

Our findings showed that prior intravenous alteplase administration did not affect the clinical and imaging outcomes of patients with acute anterior circulation LVOS who had failed recanalization after EVT, with similar mRS scores at 90 days and a similar safety profile. Future RCTs are needed to confirm our findings.
