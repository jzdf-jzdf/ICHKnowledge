# Abstract  

Background  Previous randomized controlled trials (RCTs) and meta-analyses were underpowered to demonstrate the superiority of endovascular thrombectomy (EVT) over medical therapy (MEDT) in the treatment of acute ischemic stroke due to large vessel occlusion of the posterior circulation (PC-LVO) We performed an updated systematic review and meta-analysis after the publication of the BAOCHE and ATTENTION trials to determine whether EVT can benefit patients presenting with PC-LVO.  

Methods  Using Nested Knowledge, we screened literature for RCTs on EVT in PC-LVO. The primary outcome was 90-day modified Rankin Scale (mRS) score 0-3, and secondary outcomes included 90-day mRS score 0-2, 90-day mortality, and rate of symptomatic intracranial hemorrhage (slCH). A random-effects model was used to compute rate ratios (RRs) and their corresponding $95\%$ confidence intervals (CIs).  

Results  Four RCTs with 988 patients, 556 patients in the EVT arm and 432 patients in the MEDT arm, were included in the meta-analysis. EVT resulted in significantly higher rates of mRS score 0–3 $\mathrm{RR}{=}1.54\$ ; $95\%$ CI 1.16 to 2.04 $;\mathsf{P}{=}0.002$ ) and functional independence $\mathsf{\Lambda}^{\prime}\mathsf{RR}{=}1.83;95\%\mathsf{Cl}1.08$ to 3.08; $\mathsf{P}{=}0.024)$ , and lower rates of mortality $:R{\ttR}=0.76$ ; $95\%\mathsf{Cl}0.65$ to 0.90; $\mathsf{P}{=}0.002$ ) at 90-day follow-up compared with MEDT alone. However, EVT patients had higher rates of sICH $\mathrm{'RR=}7.48;95\%\mathrm{Cl}2.27$ to 24.61; $\mathsf{P}{<}0.001\$ ).  

Conclusions  EVT conferred significant patient benefit over MEDT alone in the treatment of PC-LVO. Future studies should better define patients for whom EVT is futile and determine factors that contribute to higher rates of sICH.  

# Introduction  

Following the five landmark trials of 2015, endovascular thrombectomy (EVT) gained widespread acceptance as the standard-of-care treatment in appropriately selected patients presenting with acute ischemic stroke (AIS) due to large vessel occlusion of the anterior circulation (AC-LVO).1-5 Major progress has since been made in proving the effectiveness of EVT in late time windows, and the superiority of EVT in patients with larger ischemic cores.6–8 However, patients with posterior circulation AIS have been largely excluded from these studies.  

Large vessel occlusion of the posterior circulation (PC-LVO) is an uncommon but clinically debilitating event due to the density of nuclei and axonal tracts in regions being supplied by the vertebrobasilar system .9–11 Although EVT is routinely performed in PC-LVO due to presumed clinical benefit, there remains a lack of high-level evidence to support its superiority over standard medical therapy (MEDT). Two randomized controlled trials (RCTs) on basilar artery AIS, and a meta-analysis pooling the data from these studies, were all underpowered and failed to show to a difference between groups, despite directionality favoring EVT.12–14 Focus was shifted towards the ongoing BAOCHE and ATTENTION trials at the time, as a greater pooled sample size was deemed necessary to confirm the superiority of EVT in PC-LVO.  

With the recent publication of the BAOCHE and ATTENTION trials, we performed an updated systematic review and meta-analysis of all RCTs comparing EVT and MEDT in basilar artery AIS.15 16 Our analysis was expedited with the aid of a novel semi-automated systematic review platform.  

# Methods  

The data that support the findings of this study are available from the corresponding author on reasonable request.  

# Search strategy  

On October 12, 2022, following the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses (PRISMA) guidelines, a systematic literature review of the English language literature was conducted within the Nested Knowledge Autolit software according to the drafted protocol, from inception, using PubMed, EMBASE, Web of Science, and Scopus.17 Based on each database, different combinations of possible keywords and/or MeSH terms were used for that purpose. Keywords and MeSH terms included: ‘basilar”, ‘stroke', ‘stent-triever', ‘aspiration', ‘thrombectomy’, ‘thrombolysis’, ‘randomized controlled trial’, and ‘RCT’.  

# Screening process  

We included all original studies fulfilling our pre-determined Population, Intervention, Comparator, Outcome (PICO). Population was patients with AIS of the basilar artery, Intervention was EVT by stent-triever or aspiration, Control group was standard MEDT, and the Outcomes of interest were primarily, mRS score 0–3 at 90 days and secondarily, mRS score 0–2 at 90 days, Thrombolysis in Cerebral Infarction (TICI) score $\geq2\mathrm{b}$ , mortality at 90 days, and symptomatic intracranial hemorrhage (sICH). We excluded papers that did not relate to basilar AIS, did not report an EVT arm, reported only an EVT arm without a MEDT comparison, did not report patient outcomes, were duplicate studies including the same patients presented in other included papers, or were not RCTs. We did not pose any limitations for sample size or patients’ characteristics.  

Two authors did the title and abstract screening against the predefined criteria. This was followed by a full text screening of any retained studies of the first screening step. In both stages, the senior author was consulted to resolve any conflicts in the decisions.  

# Data extraction  

Following a pilot extraction, an extraction sheet was built, and the extraction was performed by one author. The extracted data included study characteristics, baseline data of the included patients, and the aforementioned outcomes of interest. After performing the extraction, a second author completed an extensive revision of the extracted data to avoid any mistakes.  

# Risk of bias  

The risk of bias and levels of evidence of each study were scored using the Scottish Intercollegiate Guidelines Network (SIGN) checklists for randomized controlled clinical trials.18 One author evaluated the quality of each study and a second author completed a review to check concurrence. A senior author was consulted to resolve any conflicts in scoring.  

# Statistical analysis  

All data were analyzed using R software version 4.2.1. Using a ‘meta’ package, rate ratios (RRs) and their corresponding $95\%$ confidence intervals (CIs) were computed using a random-effects model. To calculate the CI of the random-effects estimate, we used a restricted maximum likelihood estimator with Jackson’s modification  of  the  Hartung-Knapp/Sidik-Jonkman  variance correction.19–22 S ince the mRS score is an ordinal variable, we analyzed the mRS score at its original scale to supplement our results. For that, we calculated the proportional OR of mRS score for each study by ordinal logistic regression.23 The proportional OR expresses the common odds for treatment benefit at each cut-off point across all seven scores of the mRs.23 Heterogeneity was assessed using $\mathrm{Q}$ statistics and the $\mathrm{I}^{2}$ test, where $\mathrm{I}^{2}{>}\bar{5}0\%$ or a $\mathrm{P}$ value $<0.05$ were considered significant.24 Due to the small number of the included studies $\mathrm{\chi}<10$ per each analysis), neither Egger’s regression test for assessing publication bias nor meta-­ regression was possible.25  

# Results Search results and risk of bias assessment  

Our initial search returned 288 results. After removing duplicates, a total of 275 studies were evaluated for inclusion. The four randomized controlled trials that assessed EVT efficacy in basilar artery occlusion (BEST, BASICS, BAOCHE, and ATTENTION) were ultimately included in the final meta-analysis (figure 1).

# Patient characteristics  

Compiling the four randomized controlled trials, we obtained data for 988 patients presenting with AIS of the basilar artery: 556 patients were assigned to the EVT arm and 432 to the MEDT arm. Baseline demographics for each study are presented in table 1 and were largely balanced between the studies. Except for a significantly higher rate of atrial fibrillation in the pooled EVT arm $(\mathrm{P}{<}0.05)$ , medical history was also balanced between the pooled EVT and MEDT arms.  

Considering the increased frequency of underlying intracranial atherosclerotic disease (ICAD) in PC-LVO, the cause of stroke was also pooled across the four studies (table 2).26  

# Functional outcomes  

The rate of 90-day mRS score 0-3 in the EVT group (251/556, $45\%$ ) was significantly higher than in the MEDT group (128/432, $30\%$ ), with a RR of 1.54 ( $95\%$ CI 1.16 to 2.04; $\mathrm{P}{=}0.002;$ . Similarly, the rate of 90-day functional independence in the EVT group (194/556, $35\%$ ) was significantly higher than in the MEDT group $(89/432,21\%)$ , with an RR of 1.83 $(95\%$ CI 1.08 to 3.08; $\mathrm{P}{=}0.024;$ ). However, there was a significant heterogeneity among the included studies with $\mathrm{I}^{2}$ of $79.5\%$ $(95\%$ CI 45.4 to 92.3), $\uptau^{2}$ of 0.22 ( $95\%$ CI 0.03 to 3.88, $\mathrm{P}{=}0.002;$ ). These findings are displayed in figure 2A,B.  

We additionally analyzed mRS score by its original scale to supplement our results. We found EVT to be associated with significant proportional treatment benefit across mRS scores in both unadjusted $\mathrm{^{\prime}OR}{=}1.66$ ; $95\%$ CI 1.16 to 2.37; $\mathrm{P}{=}0.005^{\mathrm{'}}$ and adjusted $(\mathrm{OR}=2.01\$ ; $95\%$ CI 1.34 to 3.01; $\mathrm{P}{<}0.001$ ) estimates (online supplemental figures 1,2).

# Reperfusion success  

TICI was scored in 475 patients in the EVT arm pooled across the four RCTs. Successful reperfusion, defined as a TICI score $\geq2\mathrm{b}$ , was achieved in 405 of these patients $(85\%)$ . TICI was not scored in the MEDT arm in three of the four studies, so a meaningful comparison could not be made. Similarly, the number of passes and first-pass effect was not reported in three of the four studies.  

# Discussion  

In this study of the BEST, BASICS, BAOCHE, and ATTENTION trials, we found that EVT has a significantly higher rate of good functional outcome and functional independence at 90 days compared with MEDT alone. With regards to safety outcomes, patients who underwent EVT were found to have significantly lower rates of mortality at 90-days and significantly higher rates of postprocedural symptomatic intracranial hemorrhage. These results underscore a significant treatment benefit for EVT in patients with PC-LVO.  

Neurointerventional technique and device development have seen rapid progress over the past decade, and there is a strong drive to produce high-level evidence to help guide clinical practice in insufficiently studied patient cohorts. EVT in PC-LVO is an example of clinical practice that has been adopted ahead of evidence, owing to presumed patient benefit after EVT success in AC-LVO. However, high-level evidence remains a prerequisite for EVT to become a standard-of-care treatment for PC-LVO.

14 more will achieve functional independence than with MEDT alone. Despite these high rates of favorable outcomes, $55\%$ of patients in the EVT arm had severe disability (mRS score 4–5) or had died by the 90-day follow-up visit. The density of nuclei and axonal tracts in the regions supplied by posterior circulation may contribute to a lower threshold for critical brain injury that makes EVT success futile. More refined imaging that identifies areas of irreversible brain injury in these patients before EVT may help to identify patients who are unlikely to benefit from treatment.  

Reperfusion success to TICI score $2\mathrm{b}/3$ was achieved in $85\%$ of EVT patients in this meta-analysis, which is higher than the $71\%$ TICI score $2\mathrm{b}/3$ reperfusion success reported for AC-LVO.27 These data confirm that technical and angiographic success can be readily achieved in PC-LVO. However, the discordance of reperfusion success with functional outcomes remains a topic of interest. It is possible that the high rates of ICAD in the PC-LVO studies influence these reperfusion rates.  

EVT resulted in lower mortality rates, but sICH occurred more frequently after EVT. This finding is discordant with the results in AC-LVO, in which EVT and MEDT have similar rates of sICH.1–5 There may be physiological differences in reperfusion after posterior circulation EVT, or mechanical factors of existing devices not optimized to PC-LVO that contribute to these higher sICH rates in EVT patients. Furthermore, the higher rate of ICAD, which requires adjunct treatment with angioplasty, cerebral artery stent placement, or glycoprotein (Gp) IIb/IIIa inhibitor infusion, may increase the risk of sICH. Future studies should evaluate patient-level data to track risk factors and outcomes in patients who have a post-treatment course compli cated by sICH after EVT for PC-LVO. These studies may then drive preventive measures or therapies to reduce sICH in this patient population.  

BEST and BASICS showed a trend toward better functional outcomes with EVT, but they failed to reach the significance that was demonstrated in BOACHE and ATTENTION. It is likely that the success of BOACHE and ATTENTION is due to differences in patient population and patient selection. ICAD was the cause of stroke in nearly half of the patients enrolled in BOACHE and ATTENTION, which is much higher than the rates of ICAD in BEST and BASICS. This difference probably reflects the Chinese population treated in BOACHE and ATTENTION, but additional knowledge is needed to cement EVT treatment efficacy in patients with a PC-LVO caused by other embolic or thromboembolic events. BOACHE and ATTENTION excluded patients with a posterior circulation Alberta Stroke Program Early CT Score (PC-ASPECTS) ${<}6$ (for patients aged $<80$ years in ATTENTION or all patients in BOACHE) or ${<}8$ (for patients $\geq80$ years in ATTENTION), whereas prior randomized trials lacked a quantitative assessment of ischemic injury on non-contrast CT. Further knowledge about how the severity of ischemic injury prior to EVT influences patient outcomes after EVT is also needed. Therefore, future studies should define which PC-LVO patient populations do and do not benefit from EVT in order to guide future studies designed to improved outcomes in patients who are unlikely to benefit from existing EVT techniques.  

Several study-specific limitations must be noted. Both the BASICS and BEST trials are limited by an overly broad selection of patients, without an estimate of ischemic injury on prethrombectomy imaging.12 13 BASICS and BEST also had high crossover rates, slow recruitment due to the perceived benefit of EVT, and high rates of patients treated outside of the trial. Three of the four trials, BEST, BAOCHE, and ATTENTION, were primarily conducted in China, which might limit the generalizability of these results to Western populations. For example, a minority of patients received intravenous thrombolysis in these trials as alteplase was limited or unavailable in many centers, or patients were recruited beyond the safe time window for intravenous thrombolysis.  

Our overall meta-analysis also has a notable limitation. The lack of individual patient-level data did not allow us to account for high crossover rates in BASICS and BEST or to rigorously control for differences in patient and study characteristics. For example, patients recruited to the BEST trial had a higher median baseline National Institutes of Health Stroke Scale (NIHSS) score than the other three trials, and patients in the BAOCHE trial had a median baseline NIHSS score in the moderate-tosevere category rather than in the severe category like the other three trials. These differences might certainly have affected our data, as patients with greater baseline deficits inherently have higher rates of futile thrombectomy. Despite this limitation of no patient-level data, we were able to report on the heterogeneity of studies by formal statistical testing.  

# Conclusions  

In conclusion, this meta-analysis provides additional clarity as to the efficacy of EVT for the treatment of AIS due to PC-LVO. We found that EVT was superior to MEDT alone, and patients treated by EVT had higher rates of good functional outcome and lower rates of mortality. However, patients were also found to have a greater risk of sICH. Future studies should understand how the severity of ischemic injury prior to EVT affects patient outcomes and whether non-ICAD causes of PC-LVO respond equally favorably to EVT.
